The sky was grey and the rain was thin and wheedling.

She put the kettle on the stove and waited by the window.

He counted the coins twice and put them back in the tin.

Outside, the street lamps came on one by one.

The dog slept under the table with its nose on its paws.

A letter lay unopened on the hall floor.

She read it standing up, her coat still on.

The clock in the hall struck seven and then stopped.

He said nothing for a long time, looking at his boots.

The kettle whistled and she did not move.

Rain ran down the glass in slow, uneven lines.
