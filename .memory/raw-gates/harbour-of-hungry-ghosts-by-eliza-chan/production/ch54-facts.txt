Over the next week, they toiled late into the night to make preparations. || 接下来一周，她们连夜赶工做准备。
The workers in the Pavilion of Spring Buds helped, sitting round the communal kitchen table filling folded paper parcels with spoonfuls of the salt and glutinous rice mix, like a family making dumplings for New Year. || 春芽亭的工人也来帮忙，围坐在公用厨房桌边，把盐和糯米粉一勺勺装进折好的纸包，像一家人包过年的饺子。
The room upstairs – the one sealed closed with fu, where Archie was held at the centre of a salt circle – burnt in the back of her head, but Kiamling resisted the urge to check on him. || 楼上那间用符封死、Archie 被扣在盐圈中央的屋子一直压在她脑后，她忍住了没去看他。
Barrister Ng sent his valet over with a small chest of silver coins. Not just British, but Spanish and Dutch too. || Barrister Ng 派男仆送来一小箱银币，不只英国的，还有西班牙和荷兰的。
The fae might fear iron, but it was silver that the British Empire craved. || 妖也许怕铁，但大英帝国贪的是银子。
Hoi used tongs to pull each coin from the flames, but Kiamling had to be the one to hammer the hole through the centre. || Hoi 用钳子把银币一枚枚从火里夹出，但中心那个孔必须由 Kiamling 亲手敲穿。
Only when she nearly hammered a nail through her own hand did Hoi force her to stop. || 直到她差点把钉子敲穿自己的手，Hoi 才强行让她停下。
“A hundred and eighteen.” She did not have to explain why. Eight was lucky in numerology, a homophone for prosperity. || 她要一百一十八枚，不必解释：八在数术里吉利，与「发」同音。
“There’s a way.” Hoi placed the hammer firmly into her hands, but it threatened to pull her arm out of the socket altogether. || Hoi 说有个办法，把锤子稳稳放进她手里，可锤子几乎要把她的胳膊从肩窝里拽脱。
He cupped his hand over the top of hers and guided her arm up, then took the heated silver from the fire and hammered the thick nail down into the centre. || 他用手掌盖住她的手背，托起她的手臂，从火里取出烧热的银币，把粗钉敲进正中。
When she was done, the sword was a hand-length longer than it had been before. || 做完时，剑比原先长了一掌。
With the spare coins, she made a dagger for Jingling, and even a protective amulet for Hoi. || 她用剩下的银币给 Jingling 打了一把匕首，还给了 Hoi 一枚护身符。
He accepted it with none of the derision she had expected, tying it to his belt with a double knot. || 他接了过去，没有她预想的嘲讽，打了个双结系在腰带上。
Hoi was not the only one who handled her like an injured bird because of what had happened. || 因为那件事，把她当受伤的小鸟照顾的不止 Hoi 一个。
The episode at St John’s had taught her that chicken’s blood alone would not be enough to stop the new monsters. || 圣约翰堂那一场让她明白，光靠鸡血挡不住新的怪物。
“Do you need to make such a mess of my house?” the wulei jing retaliated. || 狐精回敬她：你就有必要把我的屋子弄得这么乱吗。
She dumped the burning opium resin into another bowl, mixing it with chicken’s blood. || 她把烧着的鸦片烟膏倒进另一只碗，与鸡血调在一起。
“You want laudanum.” Madam Yau yawned as she curled up on the bench. || Madam Yau 蜷在长凳上打哈欠，说她要的是鸦片酊。
Jingling dumped a pile of tattered books by Kiamling’s elbow. Old hymn books from the cathedral. || Jingling 把一摞破旧的书撂在她肘边，那是大教堂的旧赞美诗集。
“When there is no one language in common, people end up using a mix – a bit of Cantonese, English, French, Spanish. || Hoi 说没有共同语言时，人就会混着用，一点粤语、英语、法语、西班牙语。
Jingling had not been far off the mark with the suggestion to use English in the fu. || 她意识到 Jingling 提议用英文写符并没有错。
“Enough chit-chat. We have work to do.” || 她叫停闲聊，说还有活要干。
