The noise was enough to wake the whole island. || 游行声响大得足以吵醒全岛。
A long tail of firecrackers sparked and snapped on the ground, hissing and spitting at those who got too close. || 一长串鞭炮在地上炸响，谁靠近就朝谁嘶嘶喷溅。
Red lanterns, squat as ripe persimmons, bobbed along as if held aloft on bamboo poles: except there were no poles, only ghosts. || 红灯笼像熟柿子般矮胖，看似举在竹竿上，其实没有竹竿，只有鬼。
The louder the noise, the easier it was to scare the man-eating nin sau. || 响声越大，越容易吓走吃人的年兽 nin sau。
At the head of the parade, Ox-Head and Horse-Face – the two guardians of the underworld – marched in their full ceremonial uniforms, setting a slow, even pace. || 队伍最前是牛头马面，阴间的两位差役穿全套礼服，步子缓慢均匀。
At the centre of the procession, a huge dragon puppet oscillated up and down, painted papier-mâché eyes swivelling left and right, held aloft not by human hands, but by emaciated lui gwei, their bloody tattered sleeves slipping down to their elbows. || 纸龙由枯瘦的女鬼高举，不是人抬的，鬼的破袖滑到肘上。
And bringing up the rear was a huge skeleton, a bat gwat jing, as tall as the two-storey buildings, green spheres burning in its eye sockets. || 队尾是一具两层楼高的骷髅，眼窝里烧着绿光。
The yiugwai hunter broke away from the main procession with a few of the other performers in their own disguises, heading towards the far side of the police station. || 猎妖人带着几个同样乔装的表演者离开大队，绕向警署远侧。
“Gong hei fat choi, sun tai geen hong!” Jingling said merrily, her voice somewhat muffled by the huge round mask on her head. || Jingling 戴着大圆面具，声音发闷地喊恭喜发财、新春吉祥。
“Lion dancers here to bless the station, ah seh!” || 她说他们是来给警署舞狮祈福的。
In response, Jingling dangled the key she had pilfered from the officers. || Jingling 晃了晃从军官那里顺来的钥匙。
The narrow metal bars were cold against her hand, already in place even though the gaol was not yet functional. || 牢房尚未启用，铁栅已经装好，摸着冰凉。
“Five li,” a disgruntled voice grumped in Cantonese. “Have to do everything myself.” || 一个不高兴的声音用广东话抱怨走了五里、什么都得自己来。
A squat little creature, as tall as Kiamling’s hip, stood in the middle of a semi-constructed gaol cell. || 一只只到 Kiamling 腰高的小东西站在半成品牢房中央。
“Famished I am. Missing my tai tai’s dinners.” || 它说饿坏了，想念老婆做的饭。
“It’s Leung gor!” Her words stalled Hoi’s downward strike. || 她认出是 Leung 哥，Hoi 已落下的刀停住。
That was when she grabbed it by the collar of its rough-hewn clothes and thrust a batgua mirror before its face. || 她揪住它粗布衣领，把八卦镜怼到它脸前。
It was not that he couldn’t see the transformation; it was that he did not want to. || 他不是看不见自己变了，是不想看见。
“Cheaper. More compliant. Less likely to complain,” Hoi finished. || Hoi 替他把话补完：更便宜、更听话、更少抱怨。
But now he was building something else entirely. A pillory. || 他眼下在造另一样东西：颈手枷。
“A noose.” || 问他英国人还要什么，他答：绞索。
It won’t bring your son back… || 她说这活干下去也换不回他儿子。
A gateway. It was reminiscent of the fairy ring, but there was nothing hidden about this one. || 地上开出一道门，像仙女环，却毫不遮掩。
