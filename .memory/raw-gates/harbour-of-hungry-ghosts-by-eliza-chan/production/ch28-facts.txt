Tai Ping Shan Street was named for the hill at the heart of Hong Kong island, the one the British had renamed Victoria Peak. || 太平山街得名于港岛中心那座山，英国人把它改叫 Victoria Peak。
It was infamous for the many brothels and opium dens tightly packed together there. || 这条街以密密挤在一起的妓院和鸦片馆出名。
It had been two days since Keilin had told her about Jingling working in one of the brothels on the island. || Keilin 告诉她 Jingling 在岛上妓院做事，已过了两天。
“Everyone is for sale.” Nevertheless he ambled over to the woman in the doorway instead || 那商人说「人人都是可以买的」，说完却晃到门口那女人身边去了。
Kiamling thought fast, stopping at one of the many flower shops to buy an armful of blooms, the basket too. || 她灵机一动，在街上一家花铺买了一抱花，连篮子一起买下。
The most opulent brothel was easy to spot: that would be the huge building that sat in the middle of the row. || 最气派的那家妓院最好认，就是并排房子正中间那栋大楼。
A sign painted in gold and red declared it to be the Pavilion of Spring Buds, a name so obtuse that Kiamling snorted. || 金红两色的招牌写着它叫「Pavilion of Spring Buds」，名字拗口得让她嗤了一声。
She had recognised him. Francis Peake, secretary to the governor, one of those who had sat in the front row during the Ghost Festival performance. || 她认出了他：总督秘书 Francis Peake，盂兰节演出时坐在前排的人之一。
Something was not right in here. || 她心里明白，这地方有什么不对。
Madam Yau Yuhuan was the most striking woman Kiamling had ever met. || Madam Yau Yuhuan 是她见过最夺目的女人。
Strapped to her back was a fabric-knotted bundle, long and bulky enough to conceal a weapon. || 她背上绑着一个布结的包袱，又长又鼓，足够藏下一件兵器。
Jingling wore a beautiful emerald-green top, a contrasting black floral border around the collar and wide sleeves, with a matching skirt beneath. || Jingling 穿着翠绿上衣，领口和宽袖镶一圈黑花边，下面配同色裙子。
Her hair had been styled into a low chignon. || 她的头发挽成一个低髻。
On the left side of her head, beads dangled from a familiar hair stick: the one Kiamling had bought her. || 她左侧发间垂着珠串，插的是 Kiamling 买给她的那支发簪。
She wore it still. || 她仍然戴着那支簪子。
A self-destructive streak obliterated the image. || 一股自毁的劲头把那个画面抹掉了。
“We’re safe now.” || 老板娘一边解包袱的结一边说：现在安全了。
Inside the parcel were four tattered paper umbrellas. || 包袱里是四把破旧的纸伞。
Something like salt sprinkled from the bamboo spokes, scattering over Madam Yau’s head and heaping into a mound on the floor. || 竹伞骨里撒出像盐一样的东西，落在 Madam Yau 头上，在地上堆成一小堆。
It was a lui gwei. A vengeful female ghost. || 那是一头 lui gwei，一个含怨的女鬼。
