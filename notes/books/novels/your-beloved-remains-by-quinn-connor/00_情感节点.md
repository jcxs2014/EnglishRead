# Your Beloved Remains · 情感节点

## 节点一　开放日的故居与楼上的禁忌（ch01–ch02）

开学派对的传闻被 Celia 当成背景音，她真正操心的是来客会不会在她的房子里留下杯痕；她向来客介绍「失踪的主人」Beatrice Donahue，却刻意不领人上楼。童年记忆随即接管：母亲读童话、揭开「Tristram 其实是 Beatrice」的那一页，而她五岁时已在主卧窗前把死者召来读书。

> "The rumors of Basin College's closure reached me at the start-of-year faculty mixer, but I was fretting too much over your things to care. The party should have been fun, a breath of social relief after another summer of mountain solitude."（ch01）

> "In the brief tour of the downstairs library, living room, formal dining room, and more, I avoided the mysteries of upstairs, the one piece of your biography everyone already knew. Upstairs ensured you would always be a spectacle of horror and delight."（ch01）

> "You stood by the window, your dark hair swept back from small ears, as delicate as the handles of teacups. You had a Gibson Girl's heavy hair and heavier eyes. Behind you, I could see the obelisks and iron gates of the historic cemetery peeking over your shoulder."（ch02）

## 节点二　门缝下的复写件与空掉的保险柜（ch06–ch07）

Harper 跪在档案馆门外把一封未签名的信推进门缝——「Donahue 遗产是这次过渡里较棘手的因素」「2 月 13 日投票」「开放原件以供核验」。同一天她打开保险柜，自己的归档秩序被翻乱，遗嘱不见了。

> "I will take your comments under advisement. The Donahue estate is one of the more difficult elements of the transition. In light of the concerns of your legal team over potential discrepancies with the copy, we will ensure reasonable access to the original for verification well ahead of the February 13 vote."（ch06）

> "Your will wasn't here. And there could only be one reason why. The college wasn't safe. You weren't safe."（ch07）

## 节点三　血泊里的玻璃罐（ch09）

被校长拍桌威胁之后，她跌回童年的崖边墓地与洞穴，在黑暗里哭喊「我将与你脱锚」，又亲手砸碎那只福尔马林罐；玻璃嵌进脚掌，她从血泊与粘液里捧出卷成贝壳形的纸——失传手稿的残页。

> "From out of the depression in the ground, covered in rocks like a makeshift grave, I lifted a grit-covered jar. I retraced my steps until I was close enough to the entrance to make use of that one edge of light. I tilted the jar, wiping away mud. It was full of murky, viscous liquid, faintly green in the single shaft of sunlight. And floating inside were rolled-up pages, so old and disintegrating they were practically fused together."（ch09）

> "Lightning jolted through my whole body. I knew what it was. The answer to my cries. A whisper through time from you to me. All along, you were just waiting for me to find it."（ch09）

## 节点四　Harriot 的来路与她的耳朵（ch15–ch16）

Act II 打开另一本日记：Joan 空手离开 Tulane，导师既不读她的稿，也不给她推荐信；她靠「词直接进耳」的天赋活下来——能演奏任何人的声音，包括伪造信件。她来 Basin 不是仰慕，是避风。

> "A year on the dime of one of the canon's most marginal eccentrics, Tristram Donahue, was all that stood between me and total academic ruin. I was offered the position of Donahue postdoc because she had been the topic of my first published article, and then the center of one of my dissertation case studies, the larger project exploring the theme of confinement, of suffocation, in nineteenth-century literature."（ch15）

> "When I read words on a page, they go straight to my ear, like a player sight-reading sheet music. Meaning falls away, lost to the melodious swell and shush of language. I feel my fingertips curling around the writer's heart, every tremble of the organ communicated to me."（ch16）

> "I'd been playing around with this talent for years, publishing satires, forging letters. To me, it was innate. But when I tried to teach this, it felt like there was a locked door between my students and me. Because it wasn't, after all, a desirable skill."（ch16）

## 节点五　楼梯半程之上的卧室（ch28）

「展览空间之外」的门被推开：Roland 一夜走进自己设计的迷宫再没出来，Beatrice 吞下整把乌头殉情被友人救回；此后十七年，她把他的尸体留在床上，器官腌在床下的坛里。「这从来不属于别人。」

> "There was an obvious shift, about halfway up the staircase, where the line of sight for visitors to the archive was broken. Above that, there were peels in the wallpaper, and a nick or two in the molding around the doorways. This was outside the exhibition space, beyond the public-facing purpose of the archive. This was private."（ch28）

> "He went into the maze one night and never came out. Beatrice found him in the morning." I could imagine a wild wailing, like wind, echoing over the mountains. "She swallowed fistfuls of hemlock to go with him."（ch28）

> ""For seventeen years, she kept his corpse here," you said. "Preserved him as best she could herself. Surrounded him with hay. His organs had to be removed." You nodded to the floor beneath us. "She kept them in pickling jars under the bed, right under his body. Like the pharaohs. Egypt was all the rage at the time.""（ch28）

## 节点六　温驯的野猪与梦中的一口（ch29）

野猪走到她膝前跪下、闭眼，然后被削皮刀割喉；她剖出心、咬进主动脉，再递给爱人。多年后 Celia 梦见同一颗心被递到自己面前，「紫罗兰与安全」的气味里，她毫不犹豫地咬下去——舌上炸开的是草莓。

> "With a thud felt through your ribs, the boar hit its knees. It laid its heavy head in your lap, keeping adoring eyes on you. You stroked its ears, the stubbly jut of its chin. Chuffing, the boar closed its eyes. You reached around with the paring knife you'd brought to harvest with and slit its throat."（ch29）

> "In your hands, you held a still-beating boar's heart that oozed out viscera over your wrists and fingers. And when you offered it to me, smelling of violets and safety, I leaned down without hesitation and took a bite."（ch29）

## 节点七　第一页伪造与撞破的门（ch36）

空白羊皮纸、旧墨与两种笔迹的舞步：她写下居中的标题，「那本可以是你的」；狂喜之中她看见死者含笑批准。而感恩节归来的 Jonathan 在地下室看见未干的墨——他认出的不是假货，是她。

> "Dreams of the Rose, I wrote, bold and centered on that first page, and it could have been yours. It was a kind of ecstasy. I could see you, smiling at me, a knowing light in your eyes."（ch36）

> "In our line of work, forgery was the shadow cast by every authentic piece of history. We archivists were often the final line of defense between overeager collectors and conmen."（ch36）

> ""Shut up." He was utterly cold as he looked at the page of fresh, smeared ink. Still wet and shining. And written to look just like your hand. Spidery and precise."（ch36）

## 节点八　泉效中的「我看见的是你」（ch41）

两人喝下洞中泉水，百万烛光里 Celia 请求 Joan 为她见证 Beatrice 的存在；Joan 拒绝，说「我看见的是你」，然后吻她。灯熄之后，Joan 反过来求她别睡：「我怕他会找到我——一个复仇之灵。」

> ""She brought him here." Truth spilled out of me. "They drank the water." I could see the shape of your pleasure. "They were the only two people in the world. All that mattered. Can you imagine loving someone so completely that you'd make your entire world a person?""（ch41）

> ""You know what I do. You must. Tell me you see it, Harriot." "No. I don't." Harriot's eyes scoped over me. "I see you." And then she closed the distance between us and kissed me."（ch41）

> "She paused so long I thought she'd fallen asleep. And when she spoke, it was through her teeth, as though she were drifting into a fitful nightmare. "The one I did it to." She swallowed. "A vengeful spirit.""（ch41）

## 节点九　雪夜里的「无路可出」（ch50）

血在蛋壳缎上摊开，旧伤已成危症；雪夜出逃，刹车失误撞进树丛，野猪立在路中央。Joan 终于听见「我看见它了」，却在同一分钟说出「我不能和你困在这里」——两个人都被困住，也都知道了。

> "Blood, a dirty spread of red brown over the president's eggshell satin. It was from a fraying, now soaked, bandage on your foot. My brain didn't process it at first. Your foot was still bleeding? How was an injury you'd suffered in September still an open wound?"（ch50）

> "Your eyes were fixed on the rearview mirror. Your chin trembled. "I see it.""（ch50）

> "Those words beat dully against my temples. There was no way out. There was no. way. out."（ch50）

## 节点十　掷进火里的结局与合葬（ch55–ch56）

她烧掉别人替她写成的完稿——「那本该是我」；洞穴里一声钝响，石封住入口；回身时房子已是一片火。最后一页，她推开了石棺的盖：冻住的春天里，死者攥着玫瑰，而她把头埋进对方的胸，拉过那只手臂环住自己。

> "It was. What had I done? Since I'd found this treasure in the cave, I'd fantasized about a completed Dreams of the Rose. But not like this. It was meant to be me."（ch55）

> "So it was over. I'd left Harriot to die. For nothing. I'd killed her. And lost you anyway. I watched, sitting vigil over the burning corpse, bright enough to rival the sun."（ch55）

> "I pushed at the marble slab that kept you from me. I didn't expect it to work. Like butter, it slid aside and crashed to the ground with enough force to shatter. I blinked down into the cold cavity. And there you finally were. You were a frozen springtime. Your perfect hands, each fingernail tapered and glossy, clutched a bouquet of roses."（ch56）

> "Maybe it was right that I'd only ever loved a dead woman. I'd doomed the one other person who might have dared to love me. Maybe this had always been my fate. I hitched over the side of the coffin and slumped inside it. Snow drifted down onto my eyelids, the taste of smoke dying on my lips. I tucked my head into your full, soft bosom, surrounded by the perfume of violets, and pulled your arm around me."（ch56）
