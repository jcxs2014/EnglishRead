# ch107. The Book

## 本章导航

## 精读

> **原句 1:** "Henry Samuel Strauss, this is bullshit." Bea slams the last page down on the coffee counter, startling the cat, who'd drifted off on a nearby tower of books. "You can't end it there." She's clutching the rest of the manuscript to her chest, as if to shield it from him. The title page stares back at him. The Invisible Life of Addie LaRue. "What happened to her? Did she really go with Luc? After all that?" Henry shrugs. "I assume so." "You assume so?" The truth is, he doesn't know.

"Henry Samuel Strauss，这是废话。"Bea 把最后一页摔在咖啡柜台上，吓到了猫，它在一旁的书塔上睡着了。"你不能在那里结束。"她把其余的手稿抱在胸前，好像要保护它不被他看到。标题页盯着他。《The Invisible Life of Addie LaRue》。"她发生了什么事？她真的和 Luc 走了吗？在这一切之后？"Henry 耸耸肩。"我想是吧。""你想是吧？"事实是，他不知道。

> **原句 2:** He's spent the last six months trying to transcribe the stories in the notebooks, to compile them into this draft. And every night, after his hands had cramped and his head had begun to ache from staring at the computer screen, he'd collapse into bed—it does not smell like her, not anymore—and wonder how it ends. If it ends. He wrote a dozen different endings for the book, ones where she was happy, and ones where she was not, ones where she and Luc were madly in love, and ones where he clung to her like a dragon with its treasure, but those endings all belonged to him, and not to her. Those are his story, and this is hers. And anything he wrote beyond those last shared seconds, that final kiss, would be fiction. But this is real—though no one else will ever know it.

他花了过去六个月试图抄写笔记本中的故事，把它们编译成这份草稿。每天晚上，当他的双手抽筋，他的头开始因为盯着电脑屏幕而疼痛时，他会倒在床上——它不再闻起来像她，不再——想知道它如何结束。如果它结束。他写了十几个不同的书结局，她快乐的，她不快乐的，她和 Luc 疯狂相爱的，他像龙守着宝藏一样抓住她的，但那些结局都属于他，而不是她。那些是他的故事，这是她的。他在那些最后共享的秒吻之外写的任何东西，那个最后的吻，都会是小说。但这是真的——虽然没有人会知道它。

> **原句 3:** He does not know what happened to Addie, where she went, how she is, but he can hope. He hopes she is happy. He hopes she is still brimming with defiant joy, and stubborn hope. He hopes she did not do it just for him. He hopes, somehow, one day, he'll see her again. "You're really going to method actor this shit, aren't you?" says Bea. Henry looks up. He wants to tell her it's all true. That she met Addie, just like he wrote, that she said the same thing every time. He wants to tell her that they would have been friends. That they were, in that first-night-of-the-rest-of-our-lives kind of way. Which was, of course, as much as Addie ever got. But she wouldn't believe him, so he lets it live for her as fiction.

他不知道 Addie 发生了什么事，她去了哪里，她怎么样，但他可以希望。他希望她快乐。他希望她仍然充满反抗的快乐，和固执的希望。他希望她不只是为他这样做。他希望，不知怎的，有一天，他会再见到她。"你真的要方法表演这个废话，不是吗？"Bea 说。Henry 抬头。他想告诉她这一切都是真的。她遇到了 Addie，就像他写的那样，她每次都说同样的话。他想告诉她他们本会成为朋友。他们是，以一种我们余生的第一夜的方式。当然，这是 Addie 曾经得到的最大的。但她不会相信他，所以他让它在她那里作为小说活着。

> **原句 4:** "Did you like it?" he asks. And Bea breaks into a grin. There is no fog in her eyes now, no shine, and he has never been more grateful to have the truth. "It's good, Henry," she says. "It's really, really good." She taps the title page. "Just make sure you thank me in the acknowledgments." "What?" "My thesis. Remember? I wanted to do it on the girl in those pieces. The ghost in the frame. That's her, isn't it?" And of course, it is. Henry runs his hand over the manuscript, relieved and sad that it is done. He wishes he could have lived with it a little longer, wishes he could have lived with her. But now, he is glad to have it.

"你喜欢它吗？"他问。Bea 露出笑容。她的眼睛里没有雾气，没有光泽，他从来没有更感激拥有真相。"很好，Henry，"她说。"真的，真的很好。"她轻拍标题页。"一定要在致谢中感谢我。""什么？""我的论文。记得吗？我想做那些作品中的女孩。画面中的幽灵。那是她，不是吗？"当然，它是。Henry 把手放在手稿上，既宽慰又悲伤它完成了。他希望他能和它一起生活更久一点，希望他能和她一起生活。但现在，他很高兴拥有它。

> **原句 5:** Because the truth is, he is already beginning to forget. It's not that he's fallen victim to her curse. She has not been erased in any way. The details are simply fading, as all things do, glossing over by degrees, the mind loosening its hold on the past to make way for the future. But he doesn't want to let go. He is trying not to let go. He lies in bed at night, and closes his eyes, and tries to conjure her face. The exact curve of her mouth, the specific shade of her hair, the way the bedside lamp lit against her left cheekbone, her temple, her chin. The sound of her laughter late at night, her voice when she was on the edge of sleep. He knows these details are not as important as the ones in the book, but he still can't bear to lose them yet.

因为事实是，他已经开始在忘记了。不是说他成为了她诅咒的受害者。她没有以任何方式被抹去。细节只是在消退，就像所有事情一样，逐渐模糊，头脑放松对过去的把握为未来让路。但他不想放手。他试着不放手。他晚上躺在床上，闭上眼睛，试图召唤她的脸。她嘴的精确曲线，她头发的具体色调，床头灯在她左颧骨、她的太阳穴、她的下巴上的光线。她深夜的笑声，她在睡眠边缘的声音。他知道这些细节不如书中的重要，但他仍然不能忍受失去它们。

> **原句 6:** Belief is a bit like gravity. Enough people believe a thing, and it becomes as solid and real as the ground beneath your feet. But when you're the only one holding on to an idea, a memory, a girl, it's hard to keep it from floating away. "I knew you were going to be a writer," Bea is saying. "All the trappings, you've just been living in denial." "I'm not a writer," he says absently. "Tell that to the book. You're going to sell it, right? You have to—it's too good." "Oh. Yeah," he says thoughtfully. "I think I'd like to try." And he will. He will get an agent, and the book will go to auction, and in the end he'll sell the work on one condition—that there is only one name on the cover, and it is not his—and in the end, they will agree. They'll think it some clever marketing trick, no doubt, but his heart will thrill at the thought of other people reading these words—not his, but hers, of her name carried from lips to lips, from mind to memory. Addie, Addie, Addie.

信念有点像重力。足够多的人相信一件事，它就变得像你脚下的地面一样坚实和真实。但当你是一个唯一一个抓住一个想法、一个记忆、一个女孩的人时，很难不让它飘走。"我就知道你会成为作家，"Bea 说。"所有的装饰，你只是生活在否认中。""我不是作家，"他心不在焉地说。"告诉那本书。你要卖它，对吧？你必须——它太好了。""哦。是的，"他沉思地说。"我想我会试试。"他会。他会找一个代理人，书会去拍卖，最后他会卖这部作品在一个条件下——封面上只有一个名字，而且不是他的——最后，他们会同意。他们会认为这是一个聪明的营销噱头，毫无疑问，但一想到其他人读这些话——不是他的，而是她的，她的名字从嘴唇到嘴唇，从头脑到记忆，他的心就会激动。Addie，Addie，Addie。
