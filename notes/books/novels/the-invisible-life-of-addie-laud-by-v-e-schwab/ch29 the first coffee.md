# ch29. The First Coffee

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：Henry 关店后，Addie 等他，两人去喝咖啡——这是三百年后第一次有人"记得"她并与她交谈。
- **情感弧线位置**：转折 —— Addie 第一次体验"被记住"的温暖，但也知道这可能会消失。
- **Tropes 兑现/反转**：First Date（第一次约会）的反转 —— 这不是普通的约会，这是三百年的第一次。
- **人物弧线**：Addie 从震惊到珍惜这一刻，Henry 从警惕到好奇。
- **叙事手法**：Henry 的"not a library"口头禅重复；Addie 的"Henry"名字 = Luc 的变体。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** Addie lingers on the bookstore steps for an hour until it closes. Henry locks up, and turns to see her sitting there, and Addie braces again for the blankness in his gaze, the confirmation that their earlier encounter was only some strange glitch, a slipped stitch in the centuries of her curse. But when he looks at her, he knows her. She is certain he knows her. His brows go up beneath his tangled curls, as if he's surprised that she's still there. But his annoyance has given way to something else—something that confuses her even more. It's less hostile than suspicion, more guarded than relief, and it is still wonderful, because of the knowing in it. Not a first meeting, but a second—or rather, a third—and for once she is not the only one who knows.

Addie 在书店台阶上徘徊一小时直到它关门。Henry 锁上门，转身看到她在那里坐着，Addie 再次为他的目光中的空白做准备，确认他们早些时候的遭遇只是某种奇怪的滑动，她诅咒的几个世纪中的一次滑针。但当他看她时，他认识她。她确信他认识他。他的眉毛在他纠结的卷发下抬起，好像他对她还在那里感到惊讶。但他的烦恼已经让位于其他东西——某种更让她困惑的东西。它比怀疑更不敌对，比解脱更警惕，它仍然是美好的，因为其中的知道。不是第一次见面，而是第二次——或者更确切地说，第三次——而她不是唯一知道的人。

**关键词**：lingers / braces again / blankness / confirmation / strange glitch / slipped stitch / knows her / tangled curls / annoyance has given way / less hostile than suspicion / more guarded than relief / wonderful / knowing in it / Not a first meeting / not the only one who knows

**为什么这样写**：Addie 的"等待"。"braces again for the blankness" —— 为空白做准备 = 她预期他会忘记。"knowing in it" —— 其中的知道 = 他认识她。"not the only one who knows" —— 不是唯一知道的人 = 她也知道。

**读者视角提示**：Addie 的"not the only one who knows" = 三百年后第一次有人与她共享知识。

> **原句 2:** "Well?" he says, holding out his hand, not for her to take, but for her to lead the way, and she does. They walk a few blocks in awkward silence, Addie stealing glances that tell her nothing but the line of his nose, the angle of his jaw. He has a starved look, wolfish and lean, and even though he's not unnaturally tall, he hunches his shoulders as if to make himself shorter, smaller, less obtrusive. Perhaps, in the right clothes, perhaps, with the right air, perhaps, perhaps; but the longer she looks at him, the weaker the resemblance to that other stranger. And yet. There is something about him that keeps catching her attention, snagging it the way a nail snags a sweater.

"嗯？"他说，伸出手，不是让她牵，而是让她带路，她走了。他们走了几个街区，尴尬的沉默，Addie 偷看，只告诉她他鼻子的线条，他下巴的角度。他有一种饥饿的样子，狼一般的瘦，即使他不高得不自然，他弓起肩膀让自己看起来更矮、更小、更不突兀。也许，穿着合适的衣服，也许，带着合适的气质，也许，也许；但她看他越久，与那个陌生人的相似就越弱。然而。他身上有某种东西不断吸引她的注意力，像钉子勾住毛衣一样勾住它。

**关键词**：holding out his hand / lead the way / awkward silence / stealing glances / line of his nose / angle of his jaw / starved look / wolfish and lean / hunches his shoulders / shorter, smaller, less obtrusive / right clothes / right air / weaker the resemblance / that other stranger / catching her attention / snagging it / nail snags a sweater

**为什么这样写**：Henry 的外貌。"starved look, wolfish and lean" —— 饥饿的样子 = 他的抑郁。"hunches his shoulders" —— 弓起肩膀 = 他想变小。"weaker the resemblance" —— 相似越弱 = 他不像 Luc。"nail snags a sweater" —— 钉子勾住毛衣 = 他吸引她。

**读者视角提示**：Addie 的"nail snags a sweater" = Henry 吸引她的注意力，但她不知道为什么。

> **原句 3:** "This isn't a library," he chides. "You can't just trade one book for another." Addie straightens. "Obviously," she says, a little indignant. "But like I said, I didn't buy it. My friend did, and I just heard you tell Ms. Kline that—" His face hardens, the flat regard of a door slammed shut. "Word of advice. Next time you try to return a book, don't return it to the same person you stole it from the first time."

"这不是图书馆，"他责备。"你不能只用一本书换另一本书。"Addie 挺直身体。"显然，"她说，有点愤慨。"但就像我说的，我没买。我的朋友买了，我刚才听到你对 Kline 小姐说——"他的脸变硬，像门砰地关上的平坦注视。"一句话建议。下次你试图还书时，不要还给你第一次偷它的同一个人。"

> **原句 7:** "What are you doing?" She blinks, and sees him standing in the open doorway, arms crossed. The sun has shifted lower in the sky, the light going thin. "Waiting for you," she says, cringing as soon as she says it. "I wanted to apologize," she continues. "For the whole book thing." "It's fine," he says curtly. "No, it's not," she says, rising to her feet. "Let me buy you a coffee." "You don't have to do that." "I insist. As an apology." "I'm working." "Please." And it must be something in the way she says it, the sheer mix of hope and need, the obvious fact it means more than a book, more than a sorry, that makes the boy look her in the eyes, makes her realize that he hadn't really, not until now. There's something strange, searching in his gaze, but whatever he sees when he looks at her, it changes his mind. "One coffee," he says. "And you're still banned from the shop." Addie feels the air rush back into her lungs. "Deal."

"你在做什么？"她眨眼，看到他站在开着的门口，双臂交叉。太阳已经移到天空较低处，光变薄。"在等你，"她说，一说出就畏缩。"我想道歉，"她继续。"为整本书的事。""没关系，"他简短地说。"不，不是，"她站起来。"让我请你喝咖啡。""你不必那样做。""我坚持。作为道歉。""我在工作。""求求你。"一定是她说的方式中的某种东西，希望和需求的纯粹混合，明显的事实它意味着比一本书更多，比一个道歉更多，让男孩看着她的眼睛，让她意识到他真的没有，直到现在。他的目光中有某种奇怪的、搜索的东西，但无论他看她时看到什么，它都改变了他的想法。"一杯咖啡，"他说。"你仍然被禁止进入商店。"Addie 感到空气涌回她的肺里。"成交。"
