# 05. West

## 本章导航

- **时空坐标**：Mojave 沙漠里 CCC 的工作间（studio），Binky 那间珊瑚色、几乎空着的病房，大窗同时朝东与朝西；一次探视从 Dean 抱着兔子进来开始，到 Bryan 起身离开、留下一堆兔粪为止
- **叙事视角**：第三人称限知，紧贴 Dean Konig；中段有两段第一人称口述（他对昏迷的 Binky 讲的噩梦与回忆），仍装在 Dean 的声音里，不是新的叙述者
- **核心事件**：Dean 照料怀孕七个月、自愿昏迷的驻留艺术家 Binky Stratford，把这只病床当成可以说话的对象；研究生 Bryan Sienna 拿着 Liza 给的钥匙进来，追问 Kelly 的传闻与 Voortelle 是否资助 CCC，Dean 用一个"身体不得不吃掉自己"的比喻回他
- **关键人物**：Dean Konig、Binky Stratford（三十九岁，孕七月，申请上写她想在沉睡里经历怀孕）、Una（CCC 的巨型 Flemish 治疗兔）、Bryan Sienna（论文写艺术与协作的研究生）、Kelly（已经离开 Collective，本节只以传闻与旁人的转述出现）、Vesta 与 Lars（在 Dean 的回忆里）、Vivienne Volker 与 Velour（外祖母与母亲，本章给出签字人）
- **章节主题**：照料一具不会回应的身体，与一个非要从外面确认"谁在主持这一切"的人被关进同一间房——本节里谁都能说话，只有被照料的那个人始终不开口
- **上章回顾**：上一节（East 线 ch04）末尾，Radko 查完屋顶、说定本周再来；Velour 站在门廊上说了句"他走了"，说不清是对谁说的，却感到四周有一种在场

## 精读

> **原句 1:** “Una wants to say hi to you,” Dean whispered as he plopped Una, the CCC’s giant Flemish therapy rabbit, down onto Binky Stratford’s bed.

**中文理解**："Una 想跟你打个招呼，" Dean 低声说着，把 Una——CCC 那只巨大的 Flemish 治疗兔——放到 Binky Stratford 的床上。

**关键词**：whispered、plopped、therapy rabbit

**为什么这样写**：plop 是把东西重重撂下的拟声动词：施动的是 Dean，被撂的是兔子，而"收件人"躺在床上动不了。therapy 这个定语把一只活物写成一件设备，本节此后的照料确实由活物与机器分摊（兔子、监护仪、氧气管）。第一句还让 Dean 替兔子开口问好，等于先声明这间屋子里所有的问候都得经他转达——这正是本节两段长独白能够存在的前提。

**读者视角提示**：上一节（ch04）结尾，Velour 说不清自己在对谁说话；本节第一场戏里听得见的只有一只兔子和一位昏迷者，而 Dean 把两者都当成谈话对象。

---

> **原句 2:** Binky was seven months pregnant. The child was to be acquired by a wealthy couple living in Europe. Beyond that, Dean did not know much about the project—this child—its fate and future. He animated Binky, moving her delicate, lifeless hand over the rabbit’s back. The window was open, a cool breeze blowing westward that sent ripples through the artist’s hair and the rabbit’s fur.

**中文理解**：Binky 怀孕七个月。这个孩子将由一对住在欧洲的富裕夫妇取得。除此之外，Dean 对这个项目——这个孩子——它的命运和将来并不了解多少。他让 Binky 动起来，把她那只纤细、没有生气的手在兔子背上移动。窗开着，一阵凉风向西吹去，在那位艺术家的头发和兔子的毛上吹出波纹。

**关键词**：seven months、acquired、animated、lifeless、westward

**为什么这样写**：He animated Binky 是全节最冷的一行——animate 的宾语是她那只 lifeless 的手，动词把"使有生命者活动"用在没有生命迹象的身体上；本节此后每一次 Binky 看似"回应"的时刻，都要按这一句来读。"this child" 被破折号插在 project 之后，项目和婴儿在同一句法位上互换，紧接着 Dean did not know much about the project—this child—its fate and future 又把知情范围划得极窄：集体内部也有人不知道自己在做什么，本节不解释这算不算失职。末句的 westward 把凉风写成一个有刻度的方向动作；这一节稍后还会交代那间病房的大窗同时朝东与朝西——方向在本节不是修辞，是可以指认的，而这一节本身就标着 West。

**读者视角提示**：acquired（取得）用在孩子身上，本节不会再用别的词替换它；后文宣传材料里那两个名词（孵化器、容器）是同一条语法的延伸。

---

> **原句 3:** Some of the artists had come to the program because they were already unconscious and not likely to wake up. This was the case with Vesta’s grandmother, Vivienne Volker. Velour had signed her into the Collective’s care all those years ago after an accident had rendered her brain-dead.

**中文理解**：有些艺术家之所以进这个项目，是因为她们本来就昏迷着，而且醒来的可能不大。Vesta 的外祖母 Vivienne Volker 就是这种情况。许多年前一场事故让她脑死亡，是 Velour 把她签字交给了 Collective 的照管。

**关键词**：unconscious、grandmother、rendered、brain-dead、signed

**为什么这样写**：三句话把两条线的地理关系拧成一层亲属关系：Vesta 的外祖母躺在 Mojave 的病房里，而当年把她签给 Collective 的人叫 Velour——就是 ch04 那个站在门廊上的女人。rendered her brain-dead 的冷在于用词：render 是"使成为"，事故于是读起来像一道文书手续，与紧邻的 signed waivers 同一语域。all those years ago 故意不给年份，本章也不交代那场事故是什么。把 ch04 里"Velour 是 Vivienne Volker 的女儿"与这里的"她签的字"合起来才见出她的全部：东线写她的冲动，西线写她的决断。

**读者视角提示**：注意"已经昏迷、不可能醒来"与"自愿睡去"是两类人，本节下一段就要把这两类并排放在一起；分类本身在这里就是伦理问题。

---

> **原句 4:** Dean found these voluntarily unconscious women to be brave and pioneering. It would frighten him to go away for so long, to change mental states, to place his life in the hands of others. The promotional materials had framed these VCs (voluntary comas) as vacations. One could log off and find peace within the vast emptiness of the Mojave with the added option of acting as an incubator, a vessel, for the special children of the future. It was a great art project. Great in the true sense of the word. Those were exciting times to be a Collective member. But now, things were different, and Dean had only given his consent to begin the Moon2 project very reluctantly.

**中文理解**：Dean 觉得这些自愿昏迷的女人勇敢、敢为人先。要他离开这么久、改变精神状态、把性命交到别人手上，会让他害怕。宣传材料把这些 VC（自愿昏迷）包装成度假。一个人可以下线，在 Mojave 的空无里找到安宁，还多一个选项：为未来那些特别的孩子当一台孵化器、一只容器。那是一个伟大的艺术项目。真正意义上的伟大。那曾是做 Collective 成员的激动人心的年月。可现在情况不同了，而 Dean 当初同意启动 Moon2 也极不情愿。

**关键词**：voluntarily、pioneering、incubator、vessel、reluctantly

**为什么这样写**：framed these VCs (voluntary comas) as vacations 与 log off 是同一套宣传语，本节先让叙述原样模仿它一遍，再用 But now, things were different 一句拆掉。incubator 与 vessel 一个来自工业、一个来自宗教语域，并列在 the special children of the future 之前，中间不加任何评论——这是本节对项目最直的判词写法：把两个不相容的名词挨着放，让读者自己听见摩擦。最后一句把 Dean 的立场钉死：他既不是发起人也不是反对者，只是勉强点头的人；本节稍后还有一个他在病床边摸口袋里那只手机的动作，两种不忠诚出同一个人。

**读者视角提示**：It was a great art project. Great in the true sense of the word. 这两句的重复是自辩式的；本节没有给出"现在是不是还伟大"，只写了"现在不同了"。

> **原句 5:** “I dreamed we were at a party and she was sick. She didn’t remember my name. She held her mouth open for me and I injected her with medicine. Before I injected her, I stood before her and looked into her mouth for a long time. It was hot in there and it made me feel queasy, like I was on a ride. It smelled like candy and chemicals. After I gave her the medicine, she closed her mouth and looked at me. She was very confused. And she started to make noises. Terrifying noises. Not quite human. Then, she fell asleep. Just like that. A deep sleep. She slept so deeply that I thought she was dead. I held a mirror up to her nose, you know, to see if she was breathing. I thought maybe I’d administered the wrong medicine. That it was actually poison. I woke up before I found out if she had lived or died. My hands were shaking.”

**中文理解**：我梦见我们在一个派对上，她病了。她不记得我的名字。她替我把嘴张开，我给她注射了药。注射之前，我站在她面前，往她嘴里看了很久。里面很热，让我恶心，好像我在坐一种游乐器械。那儿闻起来像糖果和化学品。给了药之后，她把嘴闭上，看着我。她非常困惑，然后开始发出声音。可怕的声音。不太算人的声音。接着她睡着了。就这么睡着了。睡得很深。她深到我以为她死了。我把一面镜子举到她鼻子那儿，你知道的，看她还有没有在呼吸。我以为我可能用错了药。以为那其实是毒药。我在我搞清楚她是活是死之前醒了过来。我的手在抖。

**关键词**：injected、queasy、Terrifying、mirror、poison

**为什么这样写**：独白的结构是一次医疗操作的正放与倒放：先是她主动张开口，然后他注射，最后她睡得太深以致他怀疑自己杀了她。梦里的"她"没有名字、没有身份，本节也不给——只写她不记得我的名字，于是这场梦真正的恐惧不是剂量错了，而是他被认不出。往口腔里看了很久、很热、像坐游乐器械、闻起来像糖果和化学品：四句连排把诊疗写成一次感官上的越界。收束只剩一个身体反应（My hands were shaking），叙述不评论，梦的解释权留给读者。

**读者视角提示**：这段是 Dean 对着一位无法睁眼的人说的；他刚讲完自己和 Vesta 的通话，紧接着就是这个"给药"的梦。本节不把这两件事连成因果，请也不要替它连。

---

> **原句 6:** “I remember when Vesta first came to the Center. I remember when she stepped out of the car wearing jean shorts, a white shirt, black boots, and an old-fashioned coat. She looked me up and down like she knew something about me. I remember feeling so stupid in my uniform, and irritated by her incessant stare. I remember Lars pacing outside in the desert, waiting. I remember the Collective wanted me to grill her. They were listening in on our conversation. I remember Vesta’s look—it was like she saw through me. She began asking me questions, though I was meant to be interviewing her. You know, for the mental health screening we give all visitors. How many visitors have passed that test? You might be wondering. As far as I know? None. Did I think Vesta Furio was nuts? Not really, no. It was a conversation. All conversations are different. I had to get a feel for her. What feeling did I get? Well, I had to keep my composure. I was uneasy. Vesta seemed to think I was full of shit. She was pissed, unimpressed. Rightfully so, I guess. She wanted to see her grandmother once more before she officially died. I mean, she made the long drive. It was their honeymoon, I remember. Lars and Vesta drove across the country after they got married. Vesta only eighteen. Lars, of course, much older. She kept asking me questions. Why was I part of the Collective? Did I have a boss? Did I ever behave inappropriately with the residents here? I don’t remember how I answered. But the questions made my heart pound. She was convinced, I remember, that my questions were scripted, so she kept turning it around. Maybe they were scripted—given to me by some invisible higher power. We likely have that transcript somewhere. We keep everything. Do I have a boss, Binky? And I just don’t know it? Can’t see it? The way that God’s existence doesn’t depend on us seeing him. But either way, he’s never going to come to the phone. Won’t answer our calls. I always thought it was just us. Just the Collective in the desert. Alone. Until Vesta. She made me wonder. Wonder who is calling the shots. Wonder. At one point, she went into a weird trancelike state, just kind of rambling. And sometimes I think she spoke this second moon into existence, or that somebody got the idea from her. She mentioned a second moon. I swear, she kind of mumbled it. Near the end of the conversation, when our time was almost up, and she was much more lucid, I told her we were almost done. She replied, and I do remember this part: “How do you know?” Her eyes narrowed. That was the last thing she said to me. I couldn’t answer. I sat there, silently sweating. How did I know? I didn’t. I don’t. And we weren’t almost done, were we? Then, I watched her walk away. Gone.”

**中文理解**：我记得 Vesta 头一回进 Center 的样子。记得她下车时穿的：牛仔短裤、白衬衫、黑靴子，加一件老式外套。她把我从头到脚打量了一遍，好像她对我知道些什么。我记得自己穿制服时蠢得难受，也被她没完没了的盯视弄得心烦。我记得 Collective 要我盘问她的，他们在旁听我们的谈话。我记得 Vesta 的那个眼神——她像是把我看穿了。原本该是我面试她，她倒问起我来了，你也知道，那是给所有来访者做的心理健康筛查。你也许会想，有多少来访者通过过这项测试？据我所知？一个没有。你觉得 Vesta Furio 疯了吗？也不全是。那就是一次谈话。所有的谈话都不一样，我总得摸清她的感觉。我摸到了什么感觉？我得稳住自己，我很不安。Vesta 好像认定我在满口胡言。她恼火，也不服气。我想她有理。她想在祖母正式离世之前再见她一次。我是说，她开了那么远的路。那是他们的蜜月，我记得。Lars 和 Vesta 婚后开车横穿了整个国家。Vesta 才十八，Lars 当然年长许多。她一直问我问题：我为什么做 Collective 的一员？我有没有上司？我有没有对这里的驻留者行为不检过？我不记得自己是怎么答的。可那些问题让我的心跳起来。我记得她咬定我的提问是照稿子念的，于是她一遍遍把话头反抛回来。也许真有稿子——是某个隐形的高层力量交给我念的。那份文字记录我们大概存在什么地方，我们什么都留着。Binky，我有上司吗？而我自己就是不知道？看不见？就像上帝的存在并不靠我们看见他。可无论怎样，他永远不会来接电话，不会接我们的电话。我一直以为世上就只有我们。就 Collective 在沙漠里。一个人。直到 Vesta。她让我开始想。想是谁在下命令。想。中间有一阵她进入一种古怪的出神状态，就那么絮絮地说。有时候我怀疑是她把这颗第二月亮说出来的，或者是有人从这儿得了这个主意。她提到过第二颗月亮。我发誓，她差不多是咕哝出来的。快结束的时候，她清醒多了，我跟她说我们快要谈完了。她回了一句，这一段我记得清清楚楚："你怎么知道？"她眯起眼睛。那是她对我说的最后一句话。我答不上来。我坐在那儿，一声不响地出汗。我怎么知道的？我不知道。现在也不知道。而我们并没有快要谈完了，不是吗？后来，我看着她走开。没了。

**关键词**：interviewing、screening、scripted、transcript、calling the shots

**为什么这样写**：这是一段对着昏迷者讲的回忆，所以它一边叙述一边向 Binky 提问（Do I have a boss, Binky?），再把答案推给上帝：God’s existence doesn’t depend on us seeing him 与"某个隐形的高层力量给的稿子"是同构的两句话，本节不区分神与雇主。审查关系的倒转写得极具体——原本该是我面试她，她倒问起我来了（though I was meant to be interviewing her），而 Collective 全程在旁听这场谈话；审与被审、听与被听在本节里是同一组动作。她那句反问 How do you know? 是整节的重心：Dean 承认自己答不上来，并用 I didn’t. I don’t. 的时态变化把当年那次会面一直拖到今天；紧接的一句反问 And we weren’t almost done, were we? 拆掉的不是谈话的结尾，而是"谈完了"这个说法本身——他当年无法确认结束，今天仍然无法。

**读者视角提示**：这段回忆一次性交代了 Lars 与 Vesta 已结婚、她当时十八岁、Lars 年长许多、外祖母临终在即——全部出自 Dean 一个人的转述，本书后面若展开这些，须另章取证。

> **原句 7:** A large, muscular man was sitting in the corner on a fluffy white faux-fur chair. It was the graduate student they’d allowed to access the desert compound. He’d been researching the CCC for months, as he prepared his thesis on art and collaboration. The man called Una over, and she took two hops toward him, then paused.

**中文理解**：一个高大、结实的男人正坐在角落那张毛蓬蓬的白色仿皮草椅上。就是那个他们允许进入沙漠院区的研究生。他为准备那篇关于艺术与协作的论文，已经研究了 CCC 好几个月。那人把 Una 叫过去，她朝他蹦了两下，然后停住了。

**关键词**：muscular、faux-fur、graduate student、thesis、two hops

**为什么这样写**：新人物先以坐姿出现在角落，权限由叙述用 they’d allowed 给出——不是他自己闯进来的，是集体放行的（本节稍后的对话里他说钥匙是 Liza 给的）。for months 只给时长不给起点。最后一句把兔子写成第三个有否决权的在场者：she took two hops toward him, then paused——接近之后停下。本节剩下的对话也按这个节奏走：Bryan 凑近一个话题，被挡回去，就换下一个。

**读者视角提示**：Bryan 是本章唯一被写明"在研究 CCC"的外部人；他的论文题目他后面会说还没定，别替他定。

> **原句 8:** “Voortelle is the government, so if people called on the government to stop Moon2, and CCC really did have backing from Voortelle, it would be like a body having to eat itself.”

**中文理解**："Voortelle 就是政府，所以如果人们真的去要求政府阻止 Moon2，而 CCC 又真有 Voortelle 的资助，那就好比一个身体不得不吃掉它自己。"

**关键词**：backing、government、a body having to eat itself

**为什么这样写**：这一行没有说话人标签，但按本节对话的交替次序只能归给 Dean——他上一轮刚对 Bryan 的追问回过一个"不"字。他这一次给了完整的句子，而且用的是身体意象：不是论证传闻为假，而是说它即便为真也无法被拆掉，因为资助者与要它停手的对象是同一个躯体。Bryan 立刻顺着形象而不是顺着逻辑接话（那是个坏形象，而 CCC 最擅长形象），于是一段关于利益结构的对话被改写成关于公关的对话——本节借此演示了集体的真正技能：把所有实质问题翻译成图像问题。

**读者视角提示**：注意这一段的分工：Bryan 负责提问，Dean 负责给出比喻——本节让外部人问、内部人答，读者因此比任何角色都更早听见集体的自我辩护。Bryan 走前留的那句"你该收拾一下"针对的是兔子排泄物，本节用它收尾，不再评论。
