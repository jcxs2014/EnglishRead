# ch16. Interlude — Alabeus Tells a Story

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：Alabeus 的 monologue —— St. Barothain 的生活、walls project (Project Horseshoe)、Tecuric 的 hostage-taking、Alabeus 的 discovery (walls are listening)、defection to Wickhills。
- **情感弧线位置**：revelation —— Alabeus's backstory; mission's true stakes (walls as living/intelligent, potential weapon).
- **Tropes 兑现/反转**：scientist's confession; walls are listening (fantasy twist); hostage-taking as control mechanism.
- **人物弧线**：Alabeus 从 grumpy old man 转为 sympathetic scientist; his fear of Tecuric and the Ssura.
- **叙事手法**：first-person monologue (Alabeus)；direct address to audience; historical exposition.

## 精读

> **原句 1:** "Oh, you want me to talk, eh? You want me to talk? That’s good. My skull smiles at your skull when we are both in the grave. No more of this Shut up, Alabeus, no more Keep quiet, do what you’re told, old man. Eh? No more of that?"

**中文理解**：Alabeus: "Oh, you want me to talk, eh? You want me to talk? That's good. My skull smiles at your skull when we are both in the grave. No more of this Shut up, Alabeus, no more Keep quiet, do what you're told, old man. Eh? No more of that?"

**关键词**：My skull smiles at your skull when we are both in the grave（骷髅对骷髅笑，当我们都在坟墓里——死亡后的和解）

**为什么这样写**：Alabeus's voice — grumpy, resentful, but opening up. "My skull smiles at your skull when we are both in the grave" = death as equalizer; his grievance (being silenced) now has audience. Direct address ("you" = Ferec/Hoepsachett/Maaz).

**读者视角提示**：Alabeus's perspective is resentful but informative; his "talking" is the mission's intelligence payoff.

---

> **原句 2:** "Anyway, we never thought after the revolution that there would be a leader like the one we had just deposed … you don't think so either, eh? Well, yours was a long time ago, your “civil war.” Ours, much more recent."

**中文理解**：Alabeus on St. Barothain's politics: "And isn't it strange that now we have what they call rule by the people. These people, the same ones. What does that mean? I am not sure yet. We are in untried days, shaky days... But manticores are longer lived, and the change feels as if it happened minutes ago. We do not trust it yet. Do you?"

**关键词**：rule by the people（人民统治）；

**为什么这样写**：Alabeus's political skepticism — "rule by the people" is new, untrusted. "manticores are longer lived" = Bardanes's perspective echoed (Alabeus speaks of manticores in third person, suggesting he's not one). "Do you?" = direct address to audience.

**读者视角提示**：Alabeus's political commentary parallels Bardanes's Civil War account; both distrust new governance.

---

> **原句 3:** "You want to know about the key that I disguised in the cap of my pen."

**中文理解**：Alabeus: "I made it to be so. You want to know if they can use it or cannot use it? The answer is both. If it is found without me, no random person could innocently pick it up and cause a disaster … but they are not innocent and they are not random, the ones looking for it and for me. No, they already know. They know how to try. The trying is what will end in disaster."

**关键词**：；The trying is what will end in disaster（尝试本身会导致灾难）

**为什么这样写**：asset's nature — "key" that is incomplete by design. "The answer is both" = dual-use (safe if found by innocent, dangerous if found by enemies). "The trying is what will end in disaster" = enemies' attempt to use it will cause catastrophe.

**读者视角提示**：asset is dangerous not in itself but in enemies' hands; Alabeus's design is a safety measure. This explains why recovery is urgent.

---

> **原句 4:** "Through all the centuries they have listened to one another, spoken to one another. This is a gross oversimplification. But what do you think happens if we can speak to them? If we can mimic their voices, give them orders? What do you think they would do?"

**中文理解**：Alabeus: "The walls are listening. Through all the centuries they have listened to one another, spoken to one another. This is a gross oversimplification. But what do you think happens if we can speak to them? If we can mimic their voices, give them orders? What do you think they would do?"

**关键词**：The walls are listening（墙壁在听）；mimic their voices（模仿它们的声音）

**为什么这样写**：the novel's central fantasy twist — walls are sentient/listening. "The walls are listening" = Alabeus's discovery. "mimic their voices, give them orders" = potential weapon (controlling walls). "What do you think they would do?" = rhetorical question with ominous implication.

**读者视角提示**：walls as living/intelligent entities — this is the novel's core fantasy element. Alabeus's research discovered this; enemies want to weaponize it.

---

> **原句 5:** "And then I thought of the family of Bardanes."

**中文理解**：Alabeus: "And then I thought of the family of Bardanes."

**关键词**：family of Bardanes（Bardanes 的家族）

**为什么这样写**：Alabeus's defection motive — he thought of Bardanes's family (Dossvern lineage, Carm Cadir connection). "family" = the snail-stone keepsake connecting lineages. Bardanes's family = refuge.

**读者视角提示**：Alabeus defected to Bardanes because of lineage connection (snail-stone keepsake from Carm Cadir). This ties back to Bardanes's monologue (ch08).

---

> **原句 6:** "I made myself calm down. You learn how to do that after a while, living the way I live. Or lived."

**中文理解**：Alabeus: "I am not a naturally optimistic man, I'm afraid; and even then what I had seen of human nature suggested that if there had been an unfettered ability to kill from a distance with the movement of a single finger, many, many, many people would have eagerly seized upon the chance."

**关键词**：unfettered ability to kill from a distance（不受限制的远距离杀人能力）；

**为什么这样写**：Alabeus's pessimism about human nature — people would eagerly seize the chance to kill from distance. "unfettered ability to kill from a distance with the movement of a single finger" = gun metaphor (or wall-control metaphor). Establishes Alabeus's moral urgency.

**读者视角提示**：Alabeus fears the asset in enemies' hands because human nature (eager to kill) makes it a weapon. This justifies his defection.
