# 14. Anne

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：一次搬进 `assisted living` 住处之后的减法生活里，Tobias 打电话来说存了二十年的仓库要拆、几个纸箱得送回来；Anne 开箱重读自己写给 Dr. Grassi 的旧信，从 Tom 与 Tom’s wife 那一夜一路读到一张她和 Louisa 争过归属的照片，最后把它扫描发给了女儿。

- **情感弧线位置**：起点是搬完家后的被动麻木（`Not “relief,” but at least a dumb numbness`），中段钻进旧信几乎不肯出来（`Anne was only startled back to the world`），到照片与那通电话时短暂回暖，末尾却落在一个没有回声的送达上——`the picture had gone to Louisa—was already there`。

- **人物弧线**：从把减法当作身体给的既成事实（`couldn’t comprehend people who lived for new things`），到在旧信里认出那个 `literary flowering` 的自己，再到被女儿一句 `a secret from me` 逼到必须承认：她丢掉的每一样东西里都留着女儿存在过的证据。

- **叙事手法**：三层套嵌：养老院当下的身体与算计、1966 年与 1977 年写给 Dr. Grassi 的复写纸信、以及信里复述的那一夜（`Anne’s letters to Dr. Grassi were far more voluble than Anne had ever been in Dr. Grassi’s presence`）。现时的动作只做开箱与发送两件事，判决交给被读出来的旧文本。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** Anne couldn’t remember when her energies had turned from accumulation to reduction. … Once her body went rogue, she’d not even bothered to shop for new clothes. … At some distant time in the past she must have been an accumulator, like everyone else. … “We had to downsize from a thirty-eight-hundred-foot home to this eight-hundred-square-foot apartment,” Bob at dinner would often introduce the favorite topic, for all the others to take up with gusto … The dining sets and credenzas and collections of China sacrificed to fit into a new home that was almost too roomy for Anne and that the others regarded as a cruel rehearsal for the ultimate unfurnished box

她记不清自己的力气是从「积攒」转向「削减」的；某个遥远的过去里，她必定也像别人一样是个积攒者。身体开始自行其事后，她连新衣服都懒得去买。饭桌上 Bob 常把这道 favorite topic 端上来——从三千八百平方英尺的大宅搬进八百平方英尺的公寓——在座的人兴高采烈地接话，把一辈子的积攒压向削减这件惨事；餐桌、餐边柜、成套的中国瓷器都被牺牲掉，只为塞进一个新家，那新家对她来说几乎太宽敞，旁人却把它看作通往那只空箱子的残酷彩排。

**关键词**：accumulation / downsize / unfurnished box

**为什么这样写**：削减在本章不是丢了东西，是身体的既成事实：`Once her body went rogue` 之后连买衣服这件事都被 `the fuss and anxiety` 拖上几个月，于是穿什么由不得自己——`what free T-shirts the fates sent her way`。作者接着把同一件事搬上饭桌，让 Bob 用两个具体尺寸（`a thirty-eight-hundred-foot home` 到 `this eight-hundred-square-foot apartment`）把它变成家庭谈资，而在座众人的反应不是同情，是 `for all the others to take up with gusto`。判决藏在这半句的对仗里：新家对 Anne 来说 `almost too roomy`，在别人眼里却是 `a cruel rehearsal for the ultimate unfurnished box`——宽敞被读成空，提前排练的那只箱子才是这场削减的终点。

**读者视角提示**：本章此后每一件「扔掉」的事都挂在这句上：先是 Walt 的书被 `consigning epochs of the world to Goodwill`，再是 `a murder of boxes`，连那只旧钱包都因为还能用而被留下。留意她 `couldn’t comprehend people who lived for new things`——羡慕的不是拥有，是还能买。

> **原句 2:** It all made her miss Walt terribly. Big Walt, who was supposed to have taken care of her to the end, who instead was the one who expired in a hospital bed while Anne sat sobbing and cursing in the visitor’s chair. “Goddammit, Walt!” she had sobbed. But by then he could not even hear her. They’d had barely ten years together, not much less than the time she’d been married to Serk. Strange how her marriage to Serk had seemed endless and the time with Walt felt like a blip. A bright, chiming blip like the noise from her computer when someone has recalled her existence and sent her a message. Yippee! You’ve got mail! Or as it had been: Yippee! You’ve got Walt!

Walt 的离去让她想念得更深：这个本该照顾她到最后的人，反而先在病床上走了；她在陪护椅上又哭又骂地喊出 `Goddammit, Walt!`，而他早已听不见。两人在一起差不多十年，并不比她嫁给 Serk 的时间短多少；奇怪的是，Serk 那一段婚姻在记忆里漫长得没有尽头，和 Walt 的十年却只是一个短促的亮点，像电脑收到消息时的那一声提示音：那一声原该是 `Yippee! You’ve got mail!`，落在她这里成了 `Yippee! You’ve got Walt!`。

**关键词**：Big Walt / Yippee! You’ve got mail / Yippee! You’ve got Walt

**为什么这样写**：作者先把 Walt 和 Serk 并排放上天平：`They’d had barely ten years together, not much less than the time she’d been married to Serk` 消掉了两人份量的差别，随即用 `Strange how` 把差别抽走——婚姻的时间感与感情的时间感在她这里互相错位：长的那场 `had seemed endless`，短的这十年 `felt like a blip`。把这个 blip 写成声音而不是长度，是本块的关键：`A bright, chiming blip like the noise from her computer when someone has recalled her existence and sent her a message`。于是句式完成替换——`Yippee! You’ve got mail!` 之后紧跟 `Or as it had been: Yippee! You’ve got Walt!`，提示音的轻快一分未减，落在「人没了」上的重量全在替换本身。

**读者视角提示**：Walt 是 Anne 如今这套减法生活的由来：搬进 `assisted living` 一类的住处，正是 Louisa 念叨了几年后她照办的事，而拿这件事开玩笑 `assisted dying` 的也是他。Serk 则主要由旧信与 Louisa 的讲述出现，在本章里的着墨集中在脾气与沉默两件事上。

> **原句 3:** Louisa had never forgiven her for getting rid of all the things they’d left in storage when they’d gone to Japan. … this had already been drawn out to a transparent thinness … Louisa had accused her of giving no thought to Louisa when she got rid of all of their things, and Anne had made no argument because this had been true. … It had never even occurred to her that Louisa ought to have a say … Now the unintentionally un-got-rid-of stuff mazed itself in heaps all around Anne not like a second chance but like the proof of an indictment. … Anne knew her daughter well enough to predict that the restoration of these things would only be a repetition of Louisa’s dispossession … Louisa would not be the wiser. But Tobias would know.

她当时太震惊，没对 Tobias 说、事后也没想起来：Louisa 从未原谅她在两人去日本前把寄存的东西清掉。Louisa 发现这件事并不是信任的终点（那份信任早被拉成 `a transparent thinness`），却是「信任还会回来」这个念想的终点。Anne 没有辩解，因为指控是真的；她甚至从没想过 Louisa 该有一份发言权。于是这批无意中被保留下来的东西在屋里堆成迷宫，不是第二次机会，而是一份起诉书的开头。她清楚女儿，知道这些东西回到 Louisa 手里只会再被夺走一次；她也想到自己仍可以把它们再处理掉一次——无人会知，只有 Tobias 会知道。

**关键词**：a transparent thinness / proof of an indictment / dispossession

**为什么这样写**：这一段把「归还」写成一次判决的送达。作者先排除一种误读——信任早已 `drawn out to a transparent thinness`，所以毁掉它的不是这批箱子，而是此前的一切；接着把女儿的原话与自己的沉默并置：`Louisa had accused her of giving no thought to Louisa` 与 `Anne had made no argument because this had been true`，同一行里两个 Louisa 同名并列，指控的准确与无从辩解挤在一起。真正的锋刃在 `not like a second chance but like the proof of an indictment`：东西回来了，证据也就回来了。而她的反应不是补做，是再扔一次——`Louisa would not be the wiser. But Tobias would know`，一句话把道德成本划给一个不在场的人。

**读者视角提示**：`the restoration of these things would only be a repetition of Louisa’s dispossession` 是 Anne 对自己下的判决，而她随后照这句执行：把箱子租进住处（`Anne hoped to never see the boxes again`），并且始终没有告诉 Louisa（`She still had not admitted their resurrection to Louisa`）。本块给的正是这个回避的动作。

> **原句 4:** Like the first one, from 1966, most were to Dr. Grassi. Just after her marriage to Serk, as they were readying to move to the town where Serk had a postdoc, Dr. Grassi had given her the gift of an electric typewriter. Now I see your clever stratagem, she’d written. By your extravagant gift you mean to turn me into a person who writes her friends letters. Despite the evidence you hold in your hands, don’t imagine you’ll ever succeed. … By contrast Anne’s letters to Dr. Grassi were far more voluble than Anne had ever been in Dr. Grassi’s presence. … They were, she realized with a mixture of pride and acute embarrassment when reading them again, literary—at least in their aspiration. … Dr. Grassi, who had loved literature so much, and had tried so hard to create it himself, seemed to have most succeeded in drawing forth a literary flowering from Anne, however briefly it had lasted. … Anne was only startled back to the world by her words’ melting into the purple background of the page, because the room had grown dim—it was past six and her fingers were stained from the carbons and she had forgotten to turn on a light.

她打开的第一个箱子里是自己旧信的复写纸存档，开头那封写于 1966 年，多数写给 Dr. Grassi——那时她刚嫁给 Serk，正要搬去他做 postdoc 的那个镇子，Grassi 送来一台电动打字机做礼物；她在信里当场识破这份用意（把不写信的人改造成写信的人），还断言不会成功。事实是她写得比在 Dr. Grassi 面前能说的多得多，重读时她既骄傲又难为情地认出这些信是文学的——至少是有这个企图的：Dr. Grassi 爱文学、也拼命想自己写出来，终于从她身上引出一场开花，只是不长。读着自己的字她忘了时间，直到字迹融进纸页的紫色底子里才被惊醒：房间已经暗了，指尖被复写纸染了色，她忘了开灯。

**关键词**：voluble / literary flowering / forgotten to turn on a light

**为什么这样写**：这一段的机关是「不在场比在场更丰产」。`By contrast Anne’s letters to Dr. Grassi were far more voluble than Anne had ever been in Dr. Grassi’s presence`——比较级 voluble 挂在 letters 上而不是人身上，于是作者把一叠复写纸写成了比面谈更能释放她的容器；而「文学」这个判断是写给自己的括注 `at least in their aspiration`，骄傲与难为情被压进同一个名词里。接着 Grassi 的位置被写成育种：`had tried so hard to create it himself` 的人，最终 `seemed to have most succeeded in drawing forth a literary flowering from Anne`。她读信读到的其实是自我唤醒的证据——`Reading her own words to him, Anne was actually transported`；而收尾把这份沉浸兑现成一次生理性的归来：叫醒她的不是闹钟也不是敲门，是 `the room had grown dim` 和 `she had forgotten to turn on a light`。

**读者视角提示**：这箱信的分量要看清：它是 Anne 亲手写下来的那一叠，而那批给 Louisa 的书她留了几十本（`The Korea books had been for Louisa`），信却只剩箱底这一叠。她读信的时刻紧接在六点那顿该吃的饭之后；再往后翻到的那封 1977 年的信，开头就是 `I must warn you in advance not to find this too interesting`。

> **原句 5:** As soon as she’d told Serk and me the above she excused herself to bed … Is that all you’re going to do?” I demanded. But, “She’s crazy,” Serk said. “Tom probably went to a girlfriend. … She thinks someone came and took him … and here Serk made a gesture like somebody snatching the mouth of a bag closed … Who would take him?? … The people who sent you that mailing? … And then Serk looked at me like he’d forgotten I’d been there all along. … That was nothing … Tom ran off on his wife. She’ll have to face it.

房东太太撂下话就回房了。Anne 追问「你就打算做这些？」Serk 的回答是「她疯了」，以及「Tom 一定是去找女朋友了」；可对方反复坚持的是另一件事——有人把 Tom 带走了，Serk 边说边做了一个像把袋口猛地扎紧的手势。Anne 问「谁会带走他？」还把话头接回那封来路不明的邮件，Serk 却像忘了她从头到尾都在场。最后两句才是判决：那封邮件「不算什么」，Tom 是自己跑掉的，而她「得面对」。

**关键词**：went to a girlfriend / snatching the mouth of a bag closed / been there all along

**为什么这样写**：场面全靠动作推进，判决被拆成两次下达。前一次是诊断：`“She’s crazy,” Serk said. “Tom probably went to a girlfriend.”`——妻子被解释成病态，于是她的证词自动作废；作者随即补一个手势替代她的语言（`like somebody snatching the mouth of a bag closed`），把「被带走」这件事在物理上做实。后一次更冷：听见「那封邮件是谁寄的」时 `And then Serk looked at me like he’d forgotten I’d been there all along`，一句话把提问者从现场划掉；判决则降成 `That was nothing` 与一句推给对方的 `She’ll have to face it`。Anne 在这一段里追问三次，每次都被同一句分类挡住——挡她的不是证据，是「她疯了」这个判断本身。

**读者视角提示**：这一夜是 Anne 后来拼不出全貌的那一夜。筋疲力尽之后她替自己下的结论是 `His story was only a mystery to someone like Anne who’d been left in the dark`；再往后她与 Louisa 的那通电话，母女俩同样越不过这一夜。

> **原句 6:** Louisa had even tried to steal Anne’s name from her—by suggesting that Anne was the thief. … “Why do you still have his name?” Anne remembered Louisa screaming, as if Serk had been some helpless mark Anne had killed and disposed of in order to make use of his name. … the same sort of innocuous lie? Wasn’t the actual reason that, if she gave up this name that she shared with Louisa, Anne gave up all proof that Louisa—who looked nothing like Anne, and appeared to despise her, for example screaming “Why do you still have his name”—was Anne’s own and no one else’s child?

Louisa 甚至试过把 Anne 的名字从她这里偷走——办法是反过来指认 Anne 才是贼：那句 `Why do you still have his name?` 在她记忆里是被尖叫着说出来的，仿佛 Serk 是某个无助的冤死鬼，被 Anne 杀了处理掉，只为把他的名字拿来用。Anne 当时给的理由是「留着更容易」，并且有几年真的信了：同一账户的支票、Serk 养老金的抚恤金、连房子和车都好卖。可她自己也反问，这些理由会不会都是同一种无害的谎；真正的理由不写心理、只写凭据：一旦交出这个与 Louisa 共用的名字，她就交出了全部证据——证明那个既不像她、看上去还鄙视她的 Louisa 确实是她的女儿，而不是别人的。

**关键词**：tried to steal / innocuous lie / Why do you still have his name

**为什么这样写**：「偷」在这里被拆成两种动作：Louisa 偷的是遗物（`as if Serk had been some helpless mark Anne had killed and disposed of`），偷不成之后改偷名字——把受害者改写成加害者，即 `by suggesting that Anne was the thief`。作者让 Anne 自己给出三条听起来都成立的功利理由（同一账户、抚恤金、卖房卖车），再用 `But weren’t all of these reasons the same sort of innocuous lie?` 一次性把它们降格；剩下的那条理由只写凭据：`Anne gave up all proof that Louisa`。名字在这里不是称呼，是所有权凭证。

**读者视角提示**：本章的两次「归还」在此对读：Anne 把箱子还给 Louisa 是物，Louisa 要 Anne 交出 Serk 的名字是名，作者把后者写成前者的镜像——`In Louisa’s view, what appeared to be Anne’s unsentimental willingness to part with anything made her an undeserving steward of everything`。

> **原句 7:** “I remember feeling as though Daddy and I had a secret from you, and I liked feeling that, and felt bad about liking it,” Louisa finally said. “But then I realized it was also a secret from me.”

电话那头 Louisa 说，她记得自己像是和爸爸背着 Anne 藏着一个秘密，她喜欢那种感觉，又为喜欢它而难过；紧接着她意识到，这个秘密同样是背着她自己藏的。

**关键词**：a secret from you / felt bad about liking it / a secret from me

**为什么这样写**：这通电话里 Louisa 说出的实质内容把主语排成一列：`a secret from you`，`I liked feeling that`，`felt bad about liking it`——喜欢与自责被她自己绑在一起，而真正的转折落在紧接的补充上：`But then I realized it was also a secret from me`。这一句把「父女同盟」这个想象当场撤销：那个秘密如果她连内容都想不起，就不可能是同盟，只可能是她一个人被排除在外的事实。此刻两人在这通电话里共享的不是信息，是同一种被排除。

**读者视角提示**：在此之前 Louisa 提到过一个女人（`I feel like it was a woman`），Anne 当即追问 `Was she a girlfriend of Daddy’s?`；被追到这一句之后，对话又走了二十来段才在这一类话题上收线（原文写的是 `a long call for them, anyway`）——而她挂断的理由写的是男孩们需要照料，并没有说记忆断了。

> **原句 8:** It felt terribly strange, sitting in Julie’s office having handed her the photo with its particular edges and creases and its sad otherworldly brightness, as if the past would always have more life than now, and seeing Serk and Louisa pop up on Julie’s sleek monitor, their image grainier, the colors muddier, the child and the man more convincingly invalidated. Anne felt a foolish stab of fear, that the photo might somehow be gone, but Julie lifted the lid of the scanner and handed it back to her and it was still, or even more, alive. Anne put it back in the stiff envelope, which she’d even gone so far as to furnish with two squares of cardboard to sandwich the photo between.

她把照片交给 Julie，坐在办公室里看着 Serk 和 Louisa 在屏幕上出现：影像更粗、颜色更浊，那个孩子和那个男人看起来更像被抹掉了。她一时害怕照片就此没了，Julie 掀开扫描仪把原件还给她，它还在，甚至更活。她重新把它放进那个硬信封，还特意垫了两块纸板。

**关键词**：sad otherworldly brightness / more convincingly invalidated / or even more, alive

**为什么这样写**：复制在这里被写成一次衰减加一次补偿。衰减用三个递进的名词短语完成——`their image grainier, the colors muddier, the child and the man more convincingly invalidated`：最后那个 invalidated（被证伪、被抹去）是抽象名词，放在两级视觉退化之后，就把「扫描件不如原件」这个直觉转写成了判决；而判决落点是照片里的两个人——本章里一个已经去世，另一个与她之间的信任已被拉成 `a transparent thinness`。补偿只有一个动作：`it was still, or even more, alive`，alive 落在被扫描之后仍然存在的那件东西上，于是复制不是损失。

**读者视角提示**：本章的末尾动作与开头那通电话形成对照：开头是 Julie 问 `Is your daughter on email?`，照片才有了去处；结尾换成 Anne 主动把地址报出来——`just like that, the picture had gone to Louisa—was already there`。而这张照片被翻出来之前，一直躺在一只从不使用的口袋的最里层（`Never use the pockets.`），她甚至为从没寄出过它道过歉（`I’m sorry I never sent you that photo`）。

## 一句话总结

它把一个人的几十年缩进一次搬家：她清掉 Walt 大部分的书、清掉女儿寄放的东西，也把 Serk 的名字据为己有；二十年后仓库要拆，纸箱一齐退回，她才重读自己写下的信，又听见女儿说那个只属于爸爸和她的秘密同样把她关在门外——这一回她没有再扔一次，而是把照片扫描了发过去，介绍它时只用了 `My daughter and my late husband`，而 `How simple it could sound`。
