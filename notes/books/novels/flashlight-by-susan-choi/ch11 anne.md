# 11. Anne

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：贴着 Anne 的一年：寄居兄嫂家的日子在 Louisa 上小学四年级那年结束，她搬进一处阳光充足的公寓，认识邻居 Walter，两人从互相嫌弃走到轮流做饭、开着一盘旧磁带听出一个给间谍听的电台；她拆开那只封了近十年的箱子，重新想起当年那句把她的症状定为器质损伤的诊断；章末她与 Walt 并坐，想起 Serk 当年盯着海面一闪而逝的亮光。（叙述明确把此刻定在 `unmoored in 1987`）

- **情感弧线位置**：长弧下沉，只在中途靠一段闪回抬起一次：开篇的寄居结束与当众处置（`an equally helpless child`）→ 草坪晚餐闪回里，Serk 显露出来的家务本领让她心疼（`how alone and lonesome Serk must be`）→ 猫失踪与 Louisa 电话里的爆发（`What kind of person does that?`）→ 旧箱子与那句诊断 → 数字电台把两段不相干的历史摆到一起 → 结尾并坐的安静。

- **人物弧线**：从「宁愿被女儿恨也不要被怜悯」（`rather be hated by her daughter than pitied`）的自我保护，走到能让 Walter 替她把常坐的那把椅子推进桌边、再拉出一把 `Louisa’s chair` 让她坐下；中间她仍会因为羞耻而躲开 Walter 好几天（`Anne found herself avoiding Walt`）。她的转变不在于学会道谢，而在于敢把那箱东西打开。

- **叙事手法**：第三人称限制视角贴紧 Anne，中段整块切到现在时（`Anne is deep in the past now, standing unaided with her bare feet sunk into that lawn, at 4:59 in real time`），章末的海边回忆同样回到现在时（`Serk has been saying to her`）。线索几乎全交给物件：信箱上她自己打上去的名字标签、邻居接手的橘猫、纸箱里的磁带与书包，以及海面上那个亮点。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** Anne’s brother and his wife waited until Louisa finished fourth grade to kick Louisa and Anne out, on the literal last day of school, when Louisa came home with a smile on her face and a messy stack of schoolwork in her arms she’d apparently been proud enough of to show off. … Anne couldn’t have been the only one of the four adults to wince, in guilty anticipation, as they sat together in the front parlor—Anne and her brother Gerald and his wife Dina and Louisa’s school psychologist Dr. Brickner, whom Gerald and Dina had called in as some sort of referee … But the way it all happened—the school psychologist appearing at the door, Dina pouring out her grievances to him as if he were her therapist, all of it no less an ambush on Anne than on Louisa, but Anne having to sit there, as she always now had to sit there, as if she were one of the powerful adults and not, herself, an equally helpless child—that part was especially hard to forgive.

兄嫂等到 Louisa 小学四年级的最后一天才动手；那天她带着笑和一份乱七八糟的作业回家，而两个大人和她那位 school psychologist Dr. Brickner 已经并排坐在 front parlor 里等着。作者随即点明：笑与「为作业骄傲」这两种表现，从日本那趟旅行之后就没再出现过。

**关键词**：fourth grade / some sort of referee / an equally helpless child

**为什么这样写**：这段把一次驱逐写成伏击，而请来的人只被称作裁判：`whom Gerald and Dina had called in as some sort of referee`——他负责听，不负责裁。真正的落点是叙述者临时更换了房间里每个人的位置：`as if she were one of the powerful adults and not, herself, an equally helpless child`。四个人里有一个其实还是孩子，那份 `especially hard to forgive` 才有对象。

**读者视角提示**：伏击不是当天才有的：那面墙脚板上的灰痕 `the many little gray marks that had come to decorate the baseboards`，是她自己的轮椅撞出来的，而她几乎不走前门。伤害早已在，只是今天被摆到台面上。

> **原句 2:** since the start of her current remission Anne’s hands worked okay at most times. “Okay at most times” was “great” in the Foundation context. … Many of the other MS people the Foundation liked to hire had crabbed useless hands paired with still-okay legs, instead of Anne’s combination—though how Anne would have traded the hands for the legs, those slender strong uncherished legs of her youth! Gliding her over the earth! … Sometimes she awoke with a phantom sensation of walking, residue of a dream. If Louisa had already left for the day, Anne would let herself cry, even scream. She never so much as misted an eye when Louisa could see. She was aware that Louisa regarded her as an unfeeling person, a sort of robot whose heart—if she even had one—must be made of the same dull aluminum, cold to the touch, as those hideous crutches all but fused to her arms.

她在 MS Foundation 找到一份打字的工作，凭的是「手在大部分时候还好用」；别的病人多是手废腿好，她偏偏相反——可当年她最想要的正是那双没用的腿。她半夜会哭会喊，但只在 Louisa 不在时；在女儿眼里她是个没有感觉的机器人。

**关键词**：current remission / still-okay legs / phantom sensation of walking / misted an eye

**为什么这样写**：锋利在 `still-okay legs` 与 `those slender strong uncherished legs of her youth` 的对照：同一双腿，在别人的评估表里是资产，在她的记忆里才是她自己。`She never so much as misted an eye when Louisa could see` 把这份克制写成应对策略；连「湿一下眼睛」都吝啬，说明体面是被训练出来的。

**读者视角提示**：紧随其后的独立成段那句是她整套策略的纲领：`Anne would rather be hated by her daughter than pitied`。此后她对 Walter 每一回刻薄，都是这条原则换了个对象。

> **原句 3:** “‘Special Cripple’s Edition’—that’s a good one,” he said, turning even more red, but it seemed with pleasure. Most people wilted when Anne said “cripple” and “crip,” so she said them as much as she could. Something dawned on her, without any lessening of her annoyance. “Is it because I don’t look Oriental? If that’s what you’re puzzling over, you’re right. Kang was my husband’s name.” Poor Walter’s face stayed red, but the chuckles drained out.

他笑她那句关于驾照的自嘲，而她发现自己总是先说出 cripple、crip，因为多数人一听就躲开。他猜她「不像东方人」，她于是报出丈夫的姓——Kang。

**关键词**：Special Cripple’s Edition / I don’t look Oriental / Kang was my husband’s name / the chuckles drained out

**为什么这样写**：这一段把名字和身体摆上同一张赌桌：迷住他的是信箱上那行她自己打上去的字，而她立刻用它换出一个答案——那个姓是丈夫的，人也不是那个外形。落差用体温写：`Poor Walter’s face stayed red, but the chuckles drained out`。她赢了这一轮，代价是丈夫刚被一句 `He’s dead` 结掉。

**读者视角提示**：他接下来的话是 `I would have liked to talk to him`：一个昨天才认识、连她的姓都念错的人，反而比丈夫本人更想认识丈夫。

> **原句 4:** Mr. Ogawa has arrived, in a jacket and tie. He and Serk, two of a kind, shake hands, take a step back, and bow to each other—while Anne stands to one side in her nice dress, pair of clip-ons, even lipstick you’d be forgiven for not noticing, since her jaw’s on the ground. But she’s on the outside of this, a spectator. … A volatile man and a languid man-boy; no one who actually looked would mistake them for brothers. Yet they talk and talk and talk, about what, Anne will never find out beyond a scant handful of facts that could not fill five minutes, let alone nearly three hours. … Yet she’s been enthralled, as if at an opera.

丈夫请来她的日语老师 Mr. Ogawa 吃饭。草坪上摆一张牌桌，两人握手、后退一步、互相鞠躬；Anne 穿着好久没穿的好裙子站在一侧，全程看着。菜下去、酒上来，她成了观众。

**关键词**：two of a kind / a spectator / A volatile man and a languid man-boy / as if at an opera

**为什么这样写**：两组对照把读者钉在她的位置上：`two of a kind` 是握手鞠躬时的相似，`A volatile man and a languid man-boy` 是随之而来的、连亲兄弟都不会认错的差异。她的位置由 `a spectator` 一句交代——不是被赶出去的，是自己站在那一侧；而 `they talk and talk and talk` 之后她知道，对方那三个小时里能被外人听懂的，还不足五分钟。

**读者视角提示**：她真正被打动的地方在闪回更早处：`This emergence of his hidden competence doesn’t anger but moves her`——那些家务本事原来一直藏着，是孤独攒出来的手艺。

> **原句 5:** But Louisa sure did love the sauce; Anne still made it so often, she suspected, in quest of that too-rare sensation of pleasing her child. Not far into her first year at college, Louisa had called to ask Anne to write down the recipe for her and Anne had discovered the recipe existed nowhere in her brain. She couldn’t write down the shopping list, let alone the instructions. … Anne only knew how the right onion felt in her hand, the same way she knew how the number of shakes from the saltshaker felt in her wrist. She couldn’t put these quantities into words any more than she could describe how brown the meat should get or how crushed the tomatoes should be. She had tried—she had gone to the trouble of finding her measuring spoons, she had stopped herself after each step to write down what she’d done, notepad balanced on her thigh where she sat in her chair, left arm reaching up to keep the pan’s contents moving around while she scribbled so the sauce wouldn’t burn. And it had come out completely, mystifyingly wrong, the wrongness just as impossible to put into words as the ingredients and actions had been. … Anne was afraid to find out that Louisa had tried it and found it bad, so that this one thing Louisa liked from her childhood was spoiled; and Anne was equally afraid to find out that, after all of her effort, Louisa hadn’t bothered to try it at all.

她站在 Walter 的灶台边，闻得出酱汁每一处不对，却写不出配方：洋葱该 medium 还是 large 她不知道，她只「知道对的洋葱在手里是什么感觉」。她硬写了一遍，写出来的东西完全不对，也说不清哪里不对。她既怕女儿尝了觉得难吃，也怕女儿根本没试。

**关键词**：too-rare sensation / felt in her wrist / mystifyingly wrong / hadn’t bothered to try it at all

**为什么这样写**：这段把爱写成一种无法翻译的知识：`Anne only knew how the right onion felt in her hand, the same way she knew how the number of shakes from the saltshaker felt in her wrist`。她掌握的是身体量，不是单位；而 `mystifyingly wrong` 连「错在哪」都无法命名——配方和它写坏的成品一起退到语言够不着的地方。

**读者视角提示**：收尾那两怕是本章母女关系的缩影：她怕的两种结果，一样是那件她给得出的东西落空。而这道酱正是 Louisa 吃惯了的那种：`smooth meat sauce spooned on the pasta`。

> **原句 6:** Anne knew that despite what the absence of a corpse proved to her, it also proved nothing. It rendered both the death, of which Anne was certain, and the survival, on which Louisa insisted, mere stories. Anne believing hers true only made it more false for Louisa.

猫消失之后，她认定它死在工地，Louisa 认定她还活着。这一段把分歧抽象掉：尸体的缺席对她算证据，对谁都不算证据，只让「死了」和「活着」各自变成一个故事；她信自己的，只会让 Louisa 更不信。

**关键词**：the absence of a corpse / mere stories / only made it more false for Louisa

**为什么这样写**：三句都是判决，节奏压到最短：`the absence of a corpse` 先把物证清空，再由 `mere stories` 把两个相反结论降成同一类东西。末句 `Anne believing hers true only made it more false for Louisa` 是全段最冷的一击：相信不需要证据，只需要对面那个人承受不了。

**读者视角提示**：同一种「不在场」结构在本章开头已经写过 Louisa 一次：她的笑和她的骄傲自日本那趟旅行之后 `had been absent from Louisa’s repertoire`。两处要连读，才看得出这一章反复在处理什么。

> **原句 7:** a box that had been there since Anne had moved in and that had been taped closed since Anne and Louisa had left Gerald and Dina’s, almost ten years ago. It was the kind of box that had long since ceased being a container and transformed instead into an enigmatic solid it would have caused Anne more effort to throw away than to ignore. … She remembered, as she hadn’t in years, that for all of their time in Japan she had believed that her symptoms were psychosomatic. That it was all a mental failing of hers, an unconscious willed weakness so powerful that no conscious imploring of hers could unseat it. Please, she remembered begging herself, on those long afternoons on the uncomfortable, too-short, rat-scratchy Japanese sofa. Please, just get your fucking feet on the floor, and get your fucking legs and your butt up above them, and walk, just get up! Just get up! She had thought she was crazy. So had everyone. The Japanese doctors. Her husband. Her child. … the neurological specialist at Cedars-Sinai had seated himself on the exam-room footstool and, taking Anne’s two useless feet in his hands, asked, “Can you feel this, Anne?” And when she’d said no, he nodded confirmation and said, “Because your nerves have stopped doing their job.”

她搬进来时就带着的那只箱子，胶带是从离开 Gerald 和 Dina 家那天封上的，快十年没打开。剪之前箱子已经先散出「过去的气味」。她想起在日本时自己一直以为这些症状是心理作用，连医生、丈夫、女儿都这么想；直到 Serk 死后，Cedars-Sinai 的神经科医生握着她的脚问她还能不能感觉。

**关键词**：taped closed / psychosomatic / Cedars-Sinai / stopped doing their job

**为什么这样写**：作者先让箱子散气味，再让她在这股气味里想起自己曾怎样辱骂自己的身体：`Please, just get your fucking feet on the floor`。判决因此必须极平——`Because your nerves have stopped doing their job`，没有修辞，只有因果。而叙述随即把她的身份改写掉：`She was not a hysteric but the victim of a disorder that could be explained in the simplest terms`。

**读者视角提示**：这里的时间坐标要按顺序排：Serk 死后她与 Louisa 住进 Gerald 家，在那里看的神经科；离开那家之后胶带才封上，快十年后的今天才拆开。搬家、租下带阳光的公寓是紧跟着的那一年。

> **原句 8:** What she remembers, sitting quietly with Walt on her sofa, as Walt with one of his giant yet dexterous hands depresses the eject button and takes the tape from the hinged-open mouth of the tape deck and slots it back into the clear plastic box, is that Serk watched for that tiny, bright light on the water, as it flashed out and vanished at irregular intervals, as if it were a code he was trying to crack. “That’s not a fishing boat, that’s something different.” Anne can’t remember how long they sat there, but she remembers the feeling she had, of curiosity more than unease, that Serk himself was made so uneasy, by this trivial thing that he could not explain.

她与 Walt 并坐在沙发上，他按下退带键、把磁带收回塑料盒，她想起当年 Serk 盯着海面上那个一点亮光反复明灭的样子，像在破译某个密码；她记得的与其说是不安，不如说是好奇。

**关键词**：depresses the eject button / a code he was trying to crack / Serk himself was made so uneasy

**为什么这样写**：结尾把「手边的声音」和「当年海上那个光」接到一起：一边是精确到手指的动作 `depresses the eject button`，一边是 `as if it were a code he was trying to crack`。而她修正记忆的方向也在这里：记住的不是自己的处境，是丈夫的困惑，收口在 `this trivial thing that he could not explain`——一个连他都解释不了的小东西。

**读者视角提示**：磁带里是什么、光是什么，本章都不解释：Walt 只说过那是给间谍听的 `a numbers station—a message for spies`，播的是 Radio Pyongyang。本章交付的就到此为止，一个动作、两个解释不清。

## 一句话总结

她把「宁可被恨也不要怜悯」当作活法，却在这一年里被一个不请自来的邻居闯进生活：被他把椅子推进桌边，被他替她做饭、找猫、被他认出一盘她录了却没听懂的磁带里藏着给间谍的数字电台。真正被她打开的不是磁带，是那只封了近十年的箱子——医生那句「你的神经已经不再工作了」把「一切都是自己想出来的」一次推翻。结尾她并排坐着，看这位邻居收好磁带，想起的是丈夫盯着海上那个亮点、像在破译一个他解释不清的密码的样子。
