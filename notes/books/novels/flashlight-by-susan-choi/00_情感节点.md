# 《Flashlight》情感节点

## 节点一：防波堤上的最后一段话，与床垫下的手电筒（ch01）

**叙事概括：** 十岁的 Louisa 在同一年里做了两件事：把防波堤上父亲最后那段关于游泳的话重新听一遍，以及被姑姑带去见一位自称 Jerry 的儿童心理医生。诊室里她坚持《第三类接触》里的一切都属正常、正因如此外星人才显得真实；她把父亲的失踪归成一种「偷」——不是水带走他，是死亡把人偷走，而死亡从不解释。全章的落点是那支手电筒：它落地的声音本该证明父亲还在附近，可它几乎无声地落进沙里，`an absence where a presence was expected`——于是她把这一段从记忆里抠出来，藏进床垫与床头板之间。

**关键引语：**

> ① "These are the last words he ever says to her."（ch01）
> ② "“When you told people your father was kidnapped, I think you meant he’d been taken away from you. Stolen. Death steals the people we love.”"（ch01）
> ③ "This fact—that the flashlight, in falling, landed almost noiselessly in sand—rippled over her like the pale cloud of light on the ceiling. It was not a memory, as Louisa understood memory: a fragmented, juddering filmstrip of image and sound. This wasn’t something but nothing, an absence where a presence was expected. There had been no clattering onto the rocks. There had been no splash in the water. The flashlight had landed almost noiselessly in sand."（ch01）

## 节点二：被没收的名字，与替别人签下的信（ch02–ch04）

**叙事概括：** 这条线从 1945 年六岁开始：母亲把他的名字换成一个日本名，说是因为「我们是你」；学校要他回叫那个名字，十四岁起在家里也得叫；他不识自己的母语文字，却替六岁的弟妹签下一封不属于自己的信。Anne 的那一侧补上另一半：她把那些人只当成「用来逃走」的对象——而 Serk 与她不同，他没有家。多年后在美国，这封信变成第八封，写的仍是拖延——一封信在他手里成了特务的证据，一份「教育交流」的教职成了回故乡的入口；他最后去讨的不是许可，是时间：向一无所知的妻女解释那个家所需的时间。

**关键引语：**

> ④ "“We are,” said his mother. “You are. That’s why your name isn’t really Hiroshi, it’s 석.”"（ch02）
> ⑤ "To Serk, though, she presented them as people purely to escape. How else to justify having escaped them? For, unlike her, Serk had no family at all."（ch03）
> ⑥ "In the coming weeks Tom did not reappear, and then Mrs. Lee also was gone. Whether she’d gone to meet Tom, his “disappearance” having been a misunderstanding, Serk didn’t learn. He was too consumed by the preparations for his own departure. The decision he’d avoided for so many months had erupted at last. His department chair announced he must make up his mind; Serk told Anne he’d been offered a working sabbatical; and suddenly what had been a most unlikely scenario appeared as a last chance, to be regretted if it wasn’t seized. To Soonja he wrote, “I ask that you afford me the time that I need to explain to my wife and my daughter, who know nothing whatsoever about our family, and who will have a very hard time understanding it, being Americans.”"（ch04）

## 节点三：一个假笑换来的姿势，与一家三口的维恩图（ch05–ch07）

**叙事概括：** 搬到日本后，Louisa 在观光合影上因为笑而被当众喝止，从此练出 Japanese Smile：抿住嘴唇、藏起牙齿、低头走路，母亲说这不像她，而她本来就不像 Louisa。练习有代价——她越像日本人，与母亲同走时就越愧疚，再由愧疚转成愤怒，末尾甚至希望母亲能 `in some painless way` 消失。同一年父亲那侧积出另一种亲密：女儿八岁那年成了他最理想的同伴，电影院、动物园、Mackinac Island、沙丘；而一家三口被画成一张维恩图，两个圆里是大人，重叠处是 Louisa，曾经属于 Anne 和 Serk 的那种冒险被推到了旁边。他还在为一场关于 `travel permits` 的旧争吵反复磨刀，女儿只听得见那句「你不懂」。Anne 那一侧初到日本时被抹掉的是旧债：儿子不叫她母亲、只叫她 Anne，这个称呼反倒像一次友谊与仁慈；而住下一个月后，一个十八岁的儿子找上门来。

**关键引语：**

> ⑦ "This was also the start of her Japanese Smile. Even when it was all right to smile, she could no longer smile like she used to. She kept her lips pressed together, concealing her teeth, despite her mother urging her to “smile naturally.” “You don’t look like Louisa when you smile that weird way,” said her mother. But Louisa didn’t feel like Louisa, so she didn’t see why she should look like her, either. She kept smiling her Japanese Smile. She also started walking with her head bowed, in the hopes she’d seem shorter."（ch05）
> ⑧ "She finished her omelet without tasting it, staring down at her plate. Her father had likely come up with this trip as a way to get her mother to leave the apartment. In that case, why couldn’t the two of them go by themselves? Every day that Louisa lived in Japan and felt more Japanese and more embarrassed to be seen with her mother, she also more and more felt another bad way—guilty—which in turn made her feel yet a third bad way—angry. She felt it to the point that she wished her mother might in some painless way disappear."（ch05）
> ⑨ "That he didn’t call her Mother, but Anne, touched her. It seemed a gesture of friendship and mercy, a sweeping erasure of debt by the one to whom so much was owed."（ch06）
> ⑩ "By this stage of Louisa’s childhood, their family life was represented by a simple Venn diagram, of the sort that he might draw for Louisa, though without using this example. Two circles overlap. In one circle, Anne. In the other one, Serk. In the zone of overlap, Louisa. Serk and Louisa saw movies and conducted explorations. When she was little they went to the zoo together, or ambled around the town park. Once she was slightly older they drove around in the car, sometimes all the way to Chicago. He took her to Mackinac Island, where they rented bikes and ate fudge. He took her to the Indiana Dunes, where they scaled the highest one, and sat at the top shaking sand from their socks, and afterward they bought salamis from a butcher whose store had sawdust on the floor. Once, in a past chapter of life, it was Anne and Serk who had such adventures, whose very togetherness was an adventure, the sort of electrification that illuminates mundane surroundings. But Louisa’s transformation had taken place in tandem with Anne’s, as if the same motion that drew Louisa into the foreground pushed Anne back."（ch07）

## 节点四：被当成账来算的许可，与照着后脑勺的那支手电筒（ch08–ch09）

**叙事概括：** 一天里三场成人的戏穿过一个小女孩：银幕上的飞船没吓住她，普通的美国日常吓住了她；厨房里一个自称「也是她儿子」的陌生年轻男人把手搭上母亲的眉骨，那双曾经连鸡蛋都打不破的手正朝她伸过来，像在求告；车里两位大人把去元山的许可当成可以算的账——北朝鲜不会想留着老人，于是许可成了各方都能记账的东西，而问她能不能别提这件事的人有责任。同年在海边，怕水的父亲与不能动、说不出、听不见回应的母亲一起被海抓走：女儿被海水吐回沙滩，父亲两天后宣告搜寻结束、海里始终没有尸体。两个镇民找到她时，一个把手电庄重地照在她后脑勺上，另一个嘶声喊警察——那支手电只用来确认死亡，不用来提问。

**关键引语：**

> ⑪ "“Japan will take the risk of sending old people because the DPRK won’t want to keep them. The Japanese can then say they’ve been kind to a family. You shouldn’t have asked after the permit. Don’t do it again.”"（ch08）
> ⑫ "The deepest depth of night doesn’t come all at once as the word nightfall wants you to think. … It is this amplification that at last detaches Anne’s helplessness from herself. … Anne understands all at once that Louisa and Serk have been plucked off the beach by the arms of the sea."（ch09）
> ⑬ "So obviously is she dead that the two townsmen who find her don’t immediately turn her over. Rather, one waits, his flashlight solemnly trained on the back of her head, while the other, shouting hoarsely, conveys the discovery to the police. … But there is no such investigative eye in any of the grave, pale faces surrounding Louisa, not even those that belong to the officers of the prefectural police. After all, there is nothing here that defies explanation."（ch09）

## 节点五：假身份、确诊，与海面上那一闪而逝的亮光（ch10–ch11）

**叙事概括：** 刚满二十岁的 Tobias 以一个假身份进入母亲的公寓，把「全部扔掉」执行成一次无从挑选的堆积：黄色手柄的伞、Louisa 的旧校鞋、Serk 的橡胶套鞋、一整个冬天的冬衣——他明知自己不会照办，因为把他自己的过去丢在身后的正是那股撞见活物的震动。掀开榻榻米底下那页关于海水的梦之后，他起飞离境；飞机还在爬升，邻座老人握住他的手，跟着重复一句用外国话说出来的「我们已经是勇敢的人」。Anne 那一侧，寄居兄嫂家的日子在女儿上小学四年级那年结束；医生的诊断把「一切都是自己想出来的」一次推翻——`your nerves have stopped doing their job`，她这才想起当年自己在日本沙发上哀求自己站起来。她拆开那只封了近十年的箱子，末尾与邻居并坐收好磁带，想起的却是丈夫盯着海面那个一闪而逝的亮光，像在破译一个他解释不清的密码。

**关键引语：**

> ⑭ "By the time Tobias’s flight lifted off it was October 11, 1978, one week into his twentieth year, and more than four weeks into the time he’d intended to spend helping Anne in Los Angeles. One thing after another had seemed to conspire to delay his departure, people and places he’d been unable to part from without visiting one last time; things, most often insignificant, to which he’d paid tender tribute. The weathered shrines by the roadside; the sample soups, grown congealed and buzzing with flies, in the noodle house windows; the tattered plastic tents within which wizened men tended wizened bonsai. He’d found himself suffering the sensation of leaving a homeland, he who’d felt no such thing for the actual homeland he’d left. … That harrowing day he’d seen Louisa inert on the cottony slab of her hospital bed, he’d thought of the wisdom-struck faces of the young Egyptian dead, painted, as if to serve as a wordless unspeakable label, on the face-ends of their mummies. He had stood in line the previous year with Adrian to see King Tut’s tomb contents at the Field Museum. Louisa in her hospital swaddling had not even been as expressive as an Egyptian memorial portrait. Louisa, who had defied death by drowning, had not seemed alive. … As the plane continued to struggle upward, like a senseless fish butting its head through one hostile current after another, the man peered thoughtfully in Tobias’s face, and then took his hand."（ch10）
> ⑮ "a box that had been there since Anne had moved in and that had been taped closed since Anne and Louisa had left Gerald and Dina’s, almost ten years ago. It was the kind of box that had long since ceased being a container and transformed instead into an enigmatic solid it would have caused Anne more effort to throw away than to ignore. … She remembered, as she hadn’t in years, that for all of their time in Japan she had believed that her symptoms were psychosomatic. That it was all a mental failing of hers, an unconscious willed weakness so powerful that no conscious imploring of hers could unseat it. Please, she remembered begging herself, on those long afternoons on the uncomfortable, too-short, rat-scratchy Japanese sofa. Please, just get your fucking feet on the floor, and get your fucking legs and your butt up above them, and walk, just get up! Just get up! She had thought she was crazy. So had everyone. The Japanese doctors. Her husband. Her child. … the neurological specialist at Cedars-Sinai had seated himself on the exam-room footstool and, taking Anne’s two useless feet in his hands, asked, “Can you feel this, Anne?” And when she’d said no, he nodded confirmation and said, “Because your nerves have stopped doing their job.”"（ch11）
> ⑯ "What she remembers, sitting quietly with Walt on her sofa, as Walt with one of his giant yet dexterous hands depresses the eject button and takes the tape from the hinged-open mouth of the tape deck and slots it back into the clear plastic box, is that Serk watched for that tiny, bright light on the water, as it flashed out and vanished at irregular intervals, as if it were a code he was trying to crack. “That’s not a fishing boat, that’s something different.” Anne can’t remember how long they sat there, but she remembers the feeling she had, of curiosity more than unease, that Serk himself was made so uneasy, by this trivial thing that he could not explain."（ch11）

## 节点六：一只空箱子、一道搜身，与自动亮起的手电筒（ch12）

**叙事概括：** 一个暑假、两只大陆，她带着一只没有床单被褥的空箱子上路，一路靠「不懂也能装懂」撑过去：在降神会上说那是一场戏，坐过没付钱的地铁，被海关勒令 `Arms out and legs spread` 搜身，而她真正抓住的只有一件 T 恤上 Roman 的气味。同一段夏夜还有一支手电筒：它亮了一下又灭掉，快到所有人还没来得及叫出声，黑暗就重新合拢，没有人的下巴、指头或发卷被那点光捉住。此前被人翻出来的日记也对不上号——页上的日期是 1978 年 1 月 18 日，正是他们刚搬到日本的时候，而那场梦里的水塔与她自己的溺水一模一样。三年后同一只箱子在别人嘴里已经变成「独立」的证据：当年饭桌上谈的是「她一个人拎着一只箱子来，多么了不起」。

**关键引语：**

> ⑰ "So quickly it was possible to feel, afterward, that it might not have happened, the flashlight blinked on and off, disclosing itself like a candle at the center of darkness, no Vanessa’s chin or finger or corkscrew curl caught by the glow—all this happened with such suddenness that by the time they had all gasped air into their lungs in preparation for screaming, the darkness was total again."（ch12）
> ⑱ "Alone in the room Louisa could hear the clock’s second hand twitching from one to the next with a sound like a very tiny door, in the very far distance, closing again and again. … While trapped inside she realized it held Roman’s smell. She pressed the fabric against her face, drinking his smell in and blotting her tears. … “Arms out and legs spread. Cooperate and we’ll get it done with.”"（ch12）
> ⑲ "“My parents were so impressed by your independence—they always talked about it, how you arrived with that single suitcase, all alone. So different from us, who were scared to death, and we’d all arrived there together! I’m just surprised you left school,” Amalia concluded abruptly, as if there had been more that she’d wanted to say, but then thought better of it."（ch12）

## 节点七：被记成审讯记录的善意，与互相不通的父女（ch13–ch15）

**叙事概括：** 醒来后他被问的第一个问题是国籍，用的是母亲的语言（不是她的声音）。此后每一份善意都被记在同一套账上：被打掉的牙记成他自己的错，女儿记成一个「进步」的证据，母亲的一句玩笑记成他自以为是的病根。他用童年的母语教一个人读韩文，那人认真记笔记，戒心被一点点磨掉——直到最后一段当面被宣称为从头到尾的诱饵：女儿不在这个国家，那些笑话是编的，而他一直被记成间谍。同一段时间里，两条亲情线各自在算旧账：Anne 承认自己清掉了女儿寄放的东西、把 Serk 的名字据为己有，Louisa 曾为「你为什么还留着他的名字」冲她喊；而 Louisa 自己说出口的是——记得好像和爸爸对她有一个秘密，也喜欢那种感觉，后来才发现那也是对她自己的秘密。再远处，Tobias 给自己划的位置是 marginal：贴海报、舔信封、送咖啡，不上台；当对方逼他相信一个解释时，他的答案是「我什么都不敢信」。

**关键引语：**

> ⑳ "Your daughter is not in this country. Those who have said otherwise have been lying to you. Please understand they did so with the purest of motives. In their sincere hope of redeeming your consciousness, your earliest handlers spun a fairy tale of your daughter for you. … in your small craft, very far from Japan’s territorial waters and very close to our shore, your intention was to infiltrate our country, … to spy for your adopted country of the US. … I still believe you capable of service to my Great Leader and Nation."（ch13）
> ㉑ "“I remember feeling as though Daddy and I had a secret from you, and I liked feeling that, and felt bad about liking it,” Louisa finally said. “But then I realized it was also a secret from me.”"（ch14）
> ㉒ "“When you’re forced to go without an explanation for so many years, you’ll believe anything—we go mad if we don’t believe something. But, knowing this, I disbelieve my own beliefs—I distrust all of them.” … It was hard to believe all alone."（ch15）

## 节点八：只去 City Hall 的复婚，与一潭不再流动的水（ch16–ch17）

**叙事概括：** 复婚只去 City Hall，谁也没请——两个人都出身于 small, strange families，回避集体庆祝是共享的本能；George 的可靠给她一种兴奋，凡事 risk-free、有计划、不必操心。飞机还没落地她就断定自己来过，可气味一旦熟悉就从某个具体的地方变成「哪儿都是」，失落感随之出现却说不清是什么；回家后那只发霉的圆筒袋里翻出几页旧纸，是她自己童年时的笔迹，她把判词从「我记错了年份」改回「小时候的我是对的」；Anne 在电话里补上另一块：那天海平得浪都不碎，怕水的是父亲。同一段时间，另一端的一个人把营里的时间想成一潭不再流动的水；领袖死后举国的恸哭让他一度把这个国家看成整体，随即它散成烧炭队和一辆没有座位的卡车。他跟着冻河走进中国，一部没有另一半的手机让他明白世界照常在走；替渔夫跑腿时，他用一段关于来路的自白换来搭档关系——父母来自济州岛，战时去日本谋生，战争结束后南边没有给他们国籍。结案之后两个男人一起哭，对方把世界的口径念了一遍：有人把父亲找回来了——可我没有。

**关键引语：**

> ㉓ "When Louisa remarried she and George went to City Hall, inviting no one; they both came from small, strange families and avoidance of group celebrations was an instinct they shared … it was this Louisa pored over in the weeks leading up to the trip … but this was life with George: the inexhaustible thrill of his reliability. … Whatever they did would be risk-free, enjoyable, and meticulously planned."（ch16）
> ㉔ "Time is not a river moving ceaselessly into the future but a stagnated pool. Breathing at its surface, drowning in its depths, are the same."（ch17）
> ㉕ "His curses were all directed at the Japanese Prime Minister. … Serk was not surprised to see the other man crying, but he was surprised to be crying, himself. … “The story is over—that’s what they’ve decided. The whole world can shrug and say, This was resolved. Ji-hoon got his dad back. But I didn’t!”"（ch17）

## 节点九：失踪者照片墙、the Crab，与钱包里的一张脸（ch18）

**叙事概括：** 一个人靠替南边找回被绑架者过活，他的「办公室」只有壁橱大小，四面钉满失踪者的照片，他父亲的照片就在正中央，另有一张「照片的照片」——照片里的父亲年轻到像是他的儿子；他管这本事叫 Fisherman，也管那位收留的朋友叫 the Crab：蟹有硬壳、会夹人，要紧的是极易受惊，一点声响就退走，这样看了很多年，直到最近。那位朋友只穿一身衣服就从北方走出来，出生在日本、从未有过日本国籍，手里只有一张早该过期、即便没被宣告死亡也已作废的绿卡；夜里他认不出门在哪里，反复说要上学，还会抓一件 Ji-hoon 的衣服当外套穿，念着「Anne 生气是因为饭凉了，Louisa 明天要上学」。而那句 `My daughter` 才让 Ji-hoon 终于知道那个神秘的 Louisa 是谁。交易本身荒唐得双方都清楚：要一个北朝鲜的消息，换一张被放大的脸和一个肯替他打听的人。真正被保存下来的，是他把那张粥脸美国年轻人的照片放进了自己的钱包。

**关键引语：**

> ㉖ "the Crab had been born in Japan to Korean parents left stateless after the end of the Japanese empire, he had never had Japanese citizenship; and he’d vanished from his life in the US while only holding a green card that would have expired years ago, even if he hadn’t been declared dead. … who would sooner throw him back to the DPRK, and from there to a prison-camp grave, was a forbidding challenge even for a professional fixer, as the Fisherman considered himself. … Still, Ji-hoon wept that the Crab hadn’t trusted him sooner, so that they could sooner have tried. … Ji-hoon himself had never trusted the Crab; he’d used him, as he used everyone from whom he extracted a service in exchange for the least payment they would accept. And the Crab had been using him, also. … “My daughter,” the Crab began, and at last Ji-hoon knew who the mysterious “Louisa” had been."（ch18）
> ㉗ "Ji-hoon took the picture of the porridge-faced young American man and put that into his wallet instead."（ch18）

## 节点十：room seventy-four，与看着起伏停下（ch19）

**叙事概括：** 她一个人过去。开篇先把记忆判了死刑：这些不是她回忆起来的事件，它们不活在记忆里的任何地方；她没有任何事件可以填满「踩上湿沙」与「在病床醒来」之间那段空——两个人都活过，而共同的过去是一片空白，连被改写的时间线都不是，更不是一条线。到了 room seventy-four，一个姓当场被认了出来：三个形状合起来是她的姓，母亲把这一辈子都说错了；紧接着是那个词，它的意思是「胜利」，当年被挑来听成「日本的胜利」。落水的真相也从对话里翻出来——他当年说，他们把他从船上推下来，他游了，一路游到岸；那一夜死亡不是想法也不是恐惧，只是像空气一样包住她的存在，而她并没有淹死。她随即做了一次私人的谎：他们确实早就相识，她答「是」，替已经不在的母亲补上一条命。九月他得了肺炎，她在一张复苏表单上逐项答「不」，只在吗啡那一项答「是」，因为他那只被她握了几小时的手开始抽动，像一簇将熄的火苗。最后她守着他的喉咙，看那层皮肤随无声的呼吸起伏，直到起伏停下。
