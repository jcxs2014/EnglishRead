# 7. Serk

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：只有一次出门的重量：来日本前夕，Serk 发现自己想和女儿去看电影，此后父女俩靠影院、动物园、`Mackinac Island`、沙丘攒起共同生活；同一副心思还在反复排练那场关于 `travel permits` 的旧争吵，女儿只听见他反复说的那句 `You don’t understand`。

- **情感弧线位置**：外部是一条轻松内疚的线（`a strangely impersonal pride`、`as if this were the best way to keep her in sight`），内部是一条反复加深的旧账（`honed to a superiorly devastating sharpness`）。两条线在章末合成一次轻快的落点（`A movie! A movie!`），没有展开成大悲。

- **人物弧线**：从一个人看电影（`a lone moviegoer`）到主动把女儿拉进自己的一天里，再到发现心里还压着一场没有对手的争吵；他对「陪伴」的处理方式和 Soonja 对他的指责是同一件事的两面——两人都觉得自己是被抛下的那一个。

- **叙事手法**：第三人称限制视角贴紧 Serk：先给一张 Venn diagram 把三人关系摆平（`In the zone of overlap, Louisa`），再用清单式的内心算式写他的恐惧，最后靠视角的意外泄密收尾——女儿一直在场。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** Just before they came to Japan, Serk and Louisa had become moviegoers. They had seen Star Wars, which wasn’t to Serk’s taste except for the music, tolerable poor man’s Beethoven he afterward bought for Louisa, the big black double album, and let her put on the turntable again and again. They had seen Smokey and the Bandit, again not to Serk’s taste, but his enjoyment of sharing this ritual with her, the purchasing of the tickets and popcorn, the selection of seats, the quickening of anticipation as the house lights faded down, was so keen he did not really mind what they saw. From his arrival in the States he’d been a lone moviegoer. He had never wanted to share moviegoing with anyone, not even Anne in their earlier days. But his daughter, at age eight, abruptly revealed herself as the ideal companion for movies. It was nothing that she did, no request that she made. One day he simply realized he wanted to go to the movies with her. Then came the search through the paper for something appropriate.

来日本前夕，父女俩成了电影常客。他自己并不喜欢那些片子——`Star Wars` 他只为音乐，还买成大黑胶唱片送女儿；`Smokey and the Bandit` 也不合意。可买票、买爆米花、挑座位、灯暗下去时那阵期待的加速，让他并不真在意看的是什么。而他到美国后一直独自看电影，从没想过与人分享，连早年和 Anne 在一起的那些年也没有。直到女儿八岁那年，她自己成了那个同伴。

**关键词**：moviegoers / lone moviegoer / ideal companion / something appropriate

**为什么这样写**：这一段把「喜欢电影」与「喜欢看电影这件事」拆开：他连片子的好坏都不评，只在括号里带一句 `not to Serk’s taste`，被肯定的却是仪式本身——`his enjoyment of sharing this ritual with her`。真正的转折是一个没有请求的瞬间：`One day he simply realized he wanted to go to the movies with her`；而前面那句 `He had never wanted to share moviegoing with anyone, not even Anne in their earlier days` 把例外提前说破，于是女儿不是被请进来的，是被发现一直在那儿。

**读者视角提示**：段落收在 `Then came the search through the paper for something appropriate`：想要的对象从「和她一起看」变成了「看什么」，他开始替她挑片。本章后面几次出行，都是这个动作的放大。

> **原句 2:** By this stage of Louisa’s childhood, their family life was represented by a simple Venn diagram, of the sort that he might draw for Louisa, though without using this example. Two circles overlap. In one circle, Anne. In the other one, Serk. In the zone of overlap, Louisa. Serk and Louisa saw movies and conducted explorations. When she was little they went to the zoo together, or ambled around the town park. Once she was slightly older they drove around in the car, sometimes all the way to Chicago. He took her to Mackinac Island, where they rented bikes and ate fudge. He took her to the Indiana Dunes, where they scaled the highest one, and sat at the top shaking sand from their socks, and afterward they bought salamis from a butcher whose store had sawdust on the floor. Once, in a past chapter of life, it was Anne and Serk who had such adventures, whose very togetherness was an adventure, the sort of electrification that illuminates mundane surroundings. But Louisa’s transformation had taken place in tandem with Anne’s, as if the same motion that drew Louisa into the foreground pushed Anne back.

这一段把三个人摆成一张维恩图：两个圆重叠，一个圆里是 Anne，另一个是 Serk，重叠区里是 Louisa。父女俩看电影、一起出门：动物园、城里公园、开车去 Chicago、Mackinac Island 骑车吃软糖、Indiana Dunes 爬最高的那座沙丘、坐在顶上从袜子里抖沙子。作者随即补一句：曾经有这类冒险的是 Anne 和 Serk；而 Louisa 的挪动与 Anne 的退场，是同一个动作的两面。

**关键词**：Venn diagram / In the zone of overlap / pushed Anne back

**为什么这样写**：图示本身就是论点：`In one circle, Anne. In the other one, Serk. In the zone of overlap, Louisa`——三个短句只做定位，不做解释，也没有一个形容词。句末 `as if the same motion that drew Louisa into the foreground pushed Anne back` 用一个 `pushed` 把母亲写成被动项：重叠区里的人越多，越说明原来那个双圆的中心已经空出来。

**读者视角提示**：这一段的旅行清单（`Mackinac Island`、`the Indiana Dunes`）是本章被写实的一处「公共时间」。它与后面那场争吵共用同一个 Serk——读者最好把两种陪伴分开记账。

> **原句 3:** From Louisa’s birth Serk had monitored her development as a series of cognitive landmarks: learning her numbers, her letters, first recognizing certain printed words, first using such flamboyant modifiers as actually. But other amassments—of composure, curiosity, insight—would escape him in their increments, then ambush him all of a sudden. One day, she’d turned into a person he could treat to a restaurant meal, who would sit still, with her napkin and hands in her lap, and tell him funny stories, ask him funny questions. One day, she’d turned into a person he wanted to take to the movies. Of course he felt pride, but it was a strangely impersonal pride, more akin to wonder. He didn’t feel he could take any credit for her. He felt if anything an admiring estrangement, across the growing distance of her greater maturity, and it was this that inspired him to treat her to special occasions, as if this were the best way to keep her in sight.

从女儿出生起，他就把她的成长记成一系列认知地标：数字、字母、认出印刷的字、开始用 `actually` 这类浮夸的限定词。但另一些积累——镇定、好奇、洞察——是以零头溜走的，然后突然伏击他。他当然感到骄傲，却是 `a strangely impersonal pride`；他不觉得其中有自己的功劳，只感到一种仰慕式的疏远，于是用一次次特殊场合把她留在视线里。

**关键词**：cognitive landmarks / escape him in their increments / ambush / strangely impersonal pride / admiring estrangement / keep her in sight

**为什么这样写**：作者把养育写成两种时间：能测量的（`learning her numbers, her letters`）与不能测量的（`escape him in their increments`）。动词的施事很关键——`escape`、`ambush` 的主语都是女儿，不是他；他从记录者变成被将的一方。`an admiring estrangement` 更是把 affection 与距离焊死：越欣赏她，越要用 `special occasions` 把人按在眼前，好让自己别被甩掉。

**读者视角提示**：`first using such flamboyant modifiers as actually` 是作者替读者标注的「可笑」：孩子开始学大人的口气说话。本章反复出现的 `You don’t understand` 与此同源——她在长大，他跟不上。

> **原句 4:** When Louisa breathlessly reported that posters had appeared everywhere along the shopping street advertising the opening of an American movie, Close Encounters of the Third Kind, he immediately agreed he would take her to a screening in Osaka. The movie, like the multicolored spaceship of its poster, seemed to have magically descended to distract Louisa from everything Serk feared she might notice and that it was intolerable to him she might notice: the shabbiness of their apartment and of his homeland in general, the lassitude of her mother, the bitter strain between himself and his “friend,” a main source of which was the so-called friend’s anger at being explained to Louisa this way. “I am her auntie,” Soonja had said to him. “Just because your American wife threw her own family away doesn’t mean you can throw me away.”

女儿气喘吁吁地报告商业街挂满海报、一部美国电影要在大阪上映，他立刻答应带她去；这部电影像海报上那艘彩色飞船，仿佛从天而降，专为把女儿从他怕她注意到的东西里拽走：公寓和他整个祖国的破败、母亲的倦怠、他与那位「friend」之间的苦斗。段落末尾补上那位「friend」的原话。

**关键词**：screening in Osaka / distract Louisa from everything / the shabbiness / the bitter strain / I am her auntie

**为什么这样写**：作者先写电影的功用，再写电影的负担：`seemed to have magically descended to distract Louisa from everything Serk feared she might notice`。紧接着那份清单把「怕她注意到」摊成了三样：母亲的疲惫被写成 `the lassitude of her mother`，一段争吵被写成 `the bitter strain`，而当事人只以 `his “friend,”` 出场。同段末了才把名字交出来：`I am her auntie`，配上那句`Just because your American wife threw her own family away doesn’t mean you can throw me away`，一次说清关系——她说自己是被丢掉的那一个。

**读者视角提示**：注意这一块的因果方向：他主动替女儿安排注意力（挑电影、挑日子）。下一块里方向反过来，女儿听见了他的旧账。

> **原句 5:** “You don’t understand—where are the travel permits, where are our parents? You said you had it all arranged, were you lying or are you just goddamn stupid? I don’t know which is worse!” And this was the too-cruel, because too-true, final barb that Soonja only ever could answer with silence or tears, because their parents no longer sent word of travel permits, or any word at all. Sometimes he relived the argument exactly as it had most recently unfolded, sometimes his own part was honed to a superiorly devastating sharpness, such that Soonja’s part would then include pleas for his forgiveness, that she’d made him false promises and all but forced him to upend his life. These were pleas the real Soonja never actually made. Their parents, perhaps even their brothers and sisters, were coming, the real Soonja insisted. He just had to be patient, this was not the US, where things apparently happened like clockwork for him, as if he’d turned into some kind of prince. “You forget yourself,” Soonja had said.

争吵的顶点：他指着 `where are the travel permits`，追问父母在哪里，并逼问对方是在骗他还是蠢；叙述称这句是 `the too-cruel, because too-true, final barb`。他们的父母早已不再来信，连 `or any word at all` 都没有。他有时在脑中重排这场争吵，把自己的话磨得更利，好让对方的部分变成求饶——而那些求饶是真实的那个人从未说出口的。

**关键词**：where are the travel permits / final barb / honed / the real Soonja never actually made / You forget yourself

**为什么这样写**：这段的叙述者两次拆掉自己：`honed to a superiorly devastating sharpness` 说明他在把自己磨成更锋利的一方，`These were pleas the real Soonja never actually made` 则承认对面那个版本是虚构的。于是这场争吵在文本里同时是真的又不是真的——真的是创伤，假的台词。收尾那句`You forget yourself` 确实是发生过的话，却被放在所有想象之后。

**读者视角提示**：请把它与上一块合看：女儿那边是海报和影院，他这边是一段没有对手的历史。作者故意不给过渡，让两条线共享同一个人。

> **原句 6:** To his surprise, she didn’t beg him to let her cut school, pointing out, as she could have, the number of times he’d recently pulled her from school himself. She only leaped gazelle-like from one end of the small cluttered room to the other while singing, “A movie! A movie!”

他原以为女儿会求他准她逃课——她本可以指出他最近几次亲自把她从学校接走过。她没有求；她只是像瞪羚一样在又小又乱的房间里从一头跳到另一头，唱着 `A movie! A movie!`。

**关键词**：didn’t beg him / as she could have / pulled her from school himself / leaped gazelle-like / A movie

**为什么这样写**：反预期全在 `She only leaped` 上：她手里握着一件可以当武器的东西（他接送她的旧账），却只用它换一句回答，然后立刻把空间交给身体动作。`pointing out, as she could have` 这个插入语把没发生的那句话留在纸面上，比写出来更重；`leaped gazelle-like` 又把这场雀跃压回孩子的身量，像她还没有别的事可担心。

**读者视角提示**：本章在此收束。她没有听懂争吵的内容，只撞见他重排争吵的样子（`while he obsessively rehearsed these arguments`）；她的回应绕过整套账目，只把日子换成一次出门。

## 一句话总结

女儿八岁那年自己变成了他的 `ideal companion`，他于是用一次次出门把那份仰慕式的疏远填成日常；但同一颗心还在替旧争吵磨刀（`honed`），直到女儿用 `Your next day off from school` 把这场只有他一个人在打的仗截断——他没有解释，她也没有追问，只是把下一格填上了。
