# 18. Ji-hoon

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：一个靠替南边找回被绑架者过活的人，在中国的午餐馆里约见一位自称已退休的国务院人士做交易；他要谈的不是公事，而是那个他收留、对方夜里常常认不出他的朋友——本章把「一个人如何被一个国家弄丢」写成了一个人如何被另一个人的记忆弄丢。

- **情感弧线位置**：起点是例行的焦躁（`as usual worrying`），中途被一句自问顶到最高恐惧（`Was he losing his mind, like his friend?`）；低点有两处：对着陌生人的脸承认 `not remembered what the kid looked like`，以及开口求人时的 `He was suddenly as afraid as if it was his own life he was asking this stranger to save.`；随后靠一个动作（把照片放进口袋）短暂回升，末句的 `so impossible` 把它按回地面。

- **人物弧线**：从 `a professional fixer, as the Fisherman considered himself`——用服务换最少报酬的那种人，到把一个陌生人的失踪装进自己钱包的那个人。他自称的名字来自一片海：`an ocean of tears`，那片海带走了他的 `appa`，也给了他 `Fisherman` 这个称呼；本章里他为一件事先付出、事后才盘算回报：拍下朋友的那张照片，以及把它交出去。

- **叙事手法**：第三人称限制视角贴紧 Ji-hoon，全章靠两段时间并置推进：餐馆里与 Roger 的问答是现在时，the Crab 的呓语是回不去的过去；fog 是二者之间的换气阀——它先遮住现在（`obscuring the present like fog`），后化成雨慢慢消掉。疯话按 `school` / `home` / Anne / Louisa 四类轮流回来，每一类都逼 Ji-hoon 多说一句话，于是照护被写成逐层加码的审讯。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** through an American reporter who’d done a human-interest story about Ji-hoon’s lifelong quest to recover South Korean abductees and embarrass the South Korean government for its inaction. … in his closet-size “office,” backgrounded by the dozens of photos of lost loved ones in the center of which was his appa. … A photo of a photo, in which his appa is so young that at this point he could be Ji-hoon’s son. … “Check us out, Appa!” he said, holding the newspaper photo up to his appa’s portrait’s eye level. “You and me, in a photo together!”

他约在一家自己敢当办公室用的午餐馆里见人——认识这位美国人的路径，是一位替他写过 `human-interest story` 的记者。那篇稿子写的是他 `lifelong quest`：替南边找回被绑架者，并让南边政府为自己的不作为难堪。他的“办公室”是 `closet-size` 的，墙上 `backgrounded by the dozens of photos of lost loved ones`，正中央是父亲（`appa`）的照片。他先为拍照骂自己虚荣（怕中国政府拿到他发胖的最新照片），随即自己拆穿这份虚荣：真话是 `because he wanted his appa’s face in the paper`。剪下报纸上那两个人的合影贴上墙之前，他先把报纸举到照片里那位老人的眼前：`Check us out, Appa!`

**关键词**：human-interest story / photos of lost loved ones / A photo of a photo / Check us out, Appa!

**为什么这样写**：整段靠一次位置对调完成身份说明：满墙失踪者，最中间那一张是他父亲；他要的从来不是自己的脸，而是 `he wanted his appa’s face in the paper`。照片里的老人年轻到 `he could be Ji-hoon’s son`——年纪的方向被作者反着摆了一次。虚荣与真话被写成同一件事的两句（`Later he’d scolded himself for this vanity` / `But in truth`），所以他不是在为自己开脱，而是在交代交换。这段还给出他的活动半径：记者是他通往美国外交圈的接口，而接口是有代价的——`Did you notice I answered my phone?` 的下一句是 `That’s only possible if I stay out of prison.`

**读者视角提示**：本块末尾那半句自问 `Was he losing his mind, like his friend?` 是本章的暗线：他担心的是那位朋友，而不是自己。作者把它放在讲完 appa 之后、正式命名 the Crab 之前，于是全章的恐惧从一开始就是「照顾会不会比遗忘更伤人」。

> **原句 2:** The reporter was laughing because he and Ji-hoon had good rapport. “Why not say what you actually think?” And so the meeting was set, and now Ji-hoon was waiting and as usual worrying—not about the meeting, but about the friend for whose sake he was having the meeting, whom in Fisherman-ese he had nicknamed the Crab. … Crabs have hard shells, as his friend did, and snap threateningly, as his friend did, but mostly, in Ji-hoon’s opinion, crabs despite their armor and weaponry are excessively wary and easily startled. … At the least sound or movement, they scuttle away, and so he’d been watching his friend do for years—until recently.

记者与他 `good rapport`，笑他不肯把心里话直说；约见就这样定下。他等着，照旧 `worrying`，但 `not about the meeting`——担心的是那位 `friend for whose sake he was having the meeting`。他用 `Fisherman-ese` 给这位朋友起了外号 the Crab：蟹有硬壳（`Crabs have hard shells`）、会夹人（`snap threateningly`），可要紧的是 `excessively wary and easily startled`；一点声响就退走。这样看了朋友很多年，`until recently` 才收尾。

**关键词**：not about the meeting / nicknamed the Crab / excessively wary and easily startled / until recently

**为什么这样写**：比喻的三个特征逐一对上人，作者还刻意重复了两次 `as his friend did`——把比喻写成对照表。但承重的是最后一个特征：前两条讲的是难相处，最后一条讲的是会消失，而 `until recently` 把这个「观察了很多年」一次推翻——变的不是他的眼力，是被看的人。外号用隐语 `Fisherman-ese`，说明这套称呼是他自己那套身份语言的一部分：给危险人物起代号，才敢把人带回家。

**读者视角提示**：本章往后每一次失控都从这只「会缩回壳里的动物」继续长出来：被房东赶出去（`thrown out by his landlord for not paying rent`）、凌晨三点撞开门、抓走看护人的裤子当外套。拟人化写到这里就不再是修辞，而是人物关系的说明。

> **原句 3:** the Crab had been born in Japan to Korean parents left stateless after the end of the Japanese empire, he had never had Japanese citizenship; and he’d vanished from his life in the US while only holding a green card that would have expired years ago, even if he hadn’t been declared dead. … who would sooner throw him back to the DPRK, and from there to a prison-camp grave, was a forbidding challenge even for a professional fixer, as the Fisherman considered himself. … Still, Ji-hoon wept that the Crab hadn’t trusted him sooner, so that they could sooner have tried. … Ji-hoon himself had never trusted the Crab; he’d used him, as he used everyone from whom he extracted a service in exchange for the least payment they would accept. And the Crab had been using him, also. … “My daughter,” the Crab began, and at last Ji-hoon knew who the mysterious “Louisa” had been.

他听到的新身世：the Crab 生在日本，父亲母亲是 `left stateless after the end of the Japanese empire` 的朝鲜人，所以他从来没有日本国籍；而他是在只握着一张绿卡的情况下，从 Ji-hoon 在美国的生活里消失的（`he’d vanished from his life in the US`）。要核实这套说法，又不能把这人在中国的存在抖出去（中国人会 `throw him back to the DPRK`，再从那里到 `prison-camp grave`），连自称 `professional fixer` 的 Ji-hoon 也承认这是 `a forbidding challenge`。他哭的是对方没有更早信任他；紧接着他把自己那一份也交代清：他从未信任过对方，用他，如他用所有 `from whom he extracted a service in exchange for the least payment they would accept`；末句 `And the Crab had been using him, also.`。

**关键词**：left stateless after the end of the Japanese empire / prison-camp grave / professional fixer / And the Crab had been using him, also.

**为什么这样写**：这段把「核实」写成一串物理风险：核实要文件，要文件就要暴露，暴露就回 DPRK，回 DPRK 就是坟。链条的每一环都由别人的机构替他决定，他真正能动的只有查不查——于是作者让自称 fixer 的人写下 `a forbidding challenge`，这是他全章少见的认输。互相利用写成两句对称句（`he’d used him` / `And the Crab had been using him, also.`），那个 `also` 把指控降级成承认：他不比对方干净，只是更会算账。

**读者视角提示**：收尾的 `“My daughter,”` 是本章的转折点：在此之前 Louisa 只是夜里被顺口提起的名字，这里成了亲缘关系。紧接着 `Unable to finish his sentence, the Crab put his face in his hands.`——句子没能说完，于是他失去的不是一个抽象的“女儿”，而是一句没讲完的话。

> **原句 4:** The lost life drifted in more and more, obscuring the present like fog. Two words recurred: school and home. Both words provoked great agitation, as if to reprimand the Crab with duties undone. … “I need to get to school,” he would tell Ji-hoon, pacing here and there in the tiny apartment as if he could no longer locate the door. … “You don’t go to school—you’re old! You’re all done! It’s okay!,” imagining that perhaps the Crab suffered from that universal nightmare, of some final exam in some class he hadn’t known he was taking. Even Ji-hoon still dreamed this sometimes. But one day, instead of greeting Ji-hoon’s nice reassurance with disregard, the Crab wheeled on him and shouted, “You think you’re funny, dumbshit? You won’t be laughing when you fail!”

承认身世之后 `the floodgates opened`：走失的旧日越漂越近，`obscuring the present like fog`。反复回来的只有两个词——`Two words recurred: school and home`，两个词都 `provoked great agitation`，像在 `reprimand` 他还有 `with duties undone` 的 duty 没做。他半夜说 `I need to get to school`，在小公寓里来回走、连门都找不着；Ji-hoon 照着他陪演：`You don’t go to school—you’re old!`换来的是一声吼：`You won’t be laughing when you fail!`

**关键词**：The lost life drifted in more and more / Two words recurred: school and home / as if to reprimand the Crab with duties undone / You won’t be laughing when you fail

**为什么这样写**：fog 是本章的主控意象，这里先以「漂进来遮住现在」的形态出现；同一个比喻后面还会长出另一半——`fog transformed into rain slowly melting away`，那才是 Ji-hoon 愿意陪他走完的形态。两个词被写成`reprimand` 与 `with duties undone` 这一对，即不是想家，而是被判了「有义务没完成」的罪；本章末尾他把朋友的事托给 Roger，用的是同一种语法：把未完成的责任转手。

**读者视角提示**：那句怒吼里的 `fail` 出卖的是 Ji-hoon 自己——他安慰对方时先说漏了：`Even Ji-hoon still dreamed this sometimes.`，梦的内容是 `some final exam in some class he hadn’t known he was taking`。照护者和被照护者在这一段里共享同一个噩梦。

> **原句 5:** More often, it was the women: the wife Anne and the daughter Louisa. … “I must go home,” the Crab might beg urgently when he burst through the door in the night, … The Crab would snatch up any item of clothing of Ji-hoon’s, even pants, and yank it over his arms in the role of a coat. … “Anne is angry when dinner gets cold! Louisa has school tomorrow!”

更常回来的不是课堂而是女人：`More often, it was the women: the wife Anne and the daughter Louisa.` 他半夜 `I must go home`，抓过 Ji-hoon 的任何一件衣服（连裤子）`in the role of a coat`，然后报出两条理由：`Anne is angry when dinner gets cold!` 和 `Louisa has school tomorrow!`

**关键词**：the wife Anne and the daughter Louisa / I must go home / in the role of a coat / Louisa has school tomorrow

**为什么这样写**：本章对 Anne 与 Louisa 的全部了解都出自这两句台词，作者不给旁白、不给补充，只让疯话承担人口普查：菜凉要生气、女儿明天要上学，一个家庭的分工被压成待办清单。裤子那一笔是理解本段的钥匙——他拿走的是看护人的衣服，`in the role of a coat` 点明这不是取暖而是按剧本扮；同时它也把 Ji-hoon 固定成这场演出里那个永远迟到的人。

**读者视角提示**：细节会一层层自己长出来，直到一句多余的话把整个家庭钉在墙上：`She hates cooking because she isn’t good at it, and this makes her get offended easily`，而 Ji-hoon 只敢接一个字：`“Women,”`。

> **原句 6:** the version who actually believes he won’t be made for an agent because he has a slight paunch and is dressed like a tourist, complete with fanny pack and pricey hiking shoes. … he was investigating the disappearance of an American student who’d last been seen hiking in the mountains of southwestern China, a mere three thousand miles from where they were sitting. … Ji-hoon saw an unlined Western male face, porridge-cheeked and generic. Ji-hoon could have stared at the picture for decades and not remembered what the kid looked like. … “He will have aged since this was taken,” CIA added, ridiculously. If Ji-hoon couldn’t remember the face while staring at it, it wasn’t likely he could envision its future. But, playing along, he asked:

记者带来的人 `looked exactly the way` 一个老练现实主义者预期中的 CIA 样子：不是电影里那身黑西装加墨镜，而是 `a slight paunch`、像游客一样带 `fanny pack` 和贵价登山鞋的那一版。他要查的是一名美国学生，最后一次被看见是在 `the mountains of southwestern China` 徒步，离他们坐着的地方 `three thousand miles`。照片推上桌：一张 `unlined Western male face, porridge-cheeked and generic`——`Ji-hoon could have stared at the picture for decades and not remembered what the kid looked like.` 对方补一句 `He will have aged since this was taken`。

**关键词**：porridge-cheeked and generic / not remembered what the kid looked like / three thousand miles from where they were sitting / He will have aged since this was taken

**为什么这样写**：这一段先把 CIA 从组织降格成穿着判断，再让看照片的人当场失败：记不住脸，就无法想象这张脸之后的样子——`it wasn’t likely he could envision its future`。所以那句补充既荒唐又必要，它是为数不多能把时间挂到这张脸上的东西。`porridge-cheeked and generic` 是作者给「普通到无法辨认」定的说法，它与后面证人改口后的 `some other young white male American tourist` 出自同一套词汇：失踪者先被拍平成一个类型。

**读者视角提示**：照片的年份由对方当场坐实：`Six years. This photo was taken shortly before the last time he was seen.`；而 Ji-hoon 的第一反应是劝他放弃（`Maybe it’s time to give up.`）——这句劝降与本章结尾他求人打听时的姿态，正好是同一个人在同一天里的两端。

> **原句 7:** Ji-hoon took the picture of the porridge-faced young American man and put that into his wallet instead.

饭吃完了，盘子被收走、直接扔进餐馆的水槽。Roger 把照片塞回它来的那只信封（`the kid’s face disappearing instantly from Ji-hoon’s mind as he’d known that it would`），又从同一只信封里取出一张钱包尺寸的小照片给他。这一段的全部动作落在最后半句：`Ji-hoon took the picture of the porridge-faced young American man and put that into his wallet instead.`

**关键词**：the porridge-faced young American man / into his wallet instead

**为什么这样写**：全章最短的一块，作者也把这一次的决定写成纯动作：动词只有 took 与 put，`instead` 把被替换的东西留在暗处——他钱包里最后放进去的不是刚才那张两家孩子并肩的照片，而是这个陌生失踪青年的脸。作者先让他把一个正常人生整段预演一遍（回韩国、娶一个不介意他 `middle-aged and fat and drank and swore too much` 的女人、生两个被亲戚笑着说 He’s just like his father 的孩子），再让他把这些统统放进 as if；结果是：一个想象出来的家进不了钱包，一个陌生人的失踪进了。

**读者视角提示**：他交给 Roger 的那张照片是几天前用新手机拍的，拍之前他先看到朋友 `staring up at the TV as if staring not outward, but inward`，一口哽咽被他 `forcefully swallowed and later suffered as indigestion`。同一章里被保存进钱包的是两张照片：一张没有察觉，一张没有面孔。

> **原句 8:** “I know a man here,” he finally said. “He walked out of North Korea with only the clothes on his back. He didn’t even have shoes. He says he’s an ethnic Korean who was born in Japan. … And he says he had a US green card and a wife and a daughter. And he says the North Koreans kidnapped him and his daughter, I’m not even sure how long ago but my guess is the late seventies.”

他终于开口：`“I know a man here,”`——这个人 `walked out of North Korea with only the clothes on his back`，连鞋都没有；自述是 `an ethnic Korean who was born in Japan`，有 US green card、妻子和女儿；至于被绑架的时间，他自己先说 `I’m not even sure how long ago`，再退一步给出一个猜测：`my guess is the late seventies`。

**关键词**：He walked out of North Korea with only the clothes on his back / an ethnic Korean who was born in Japan / he had a US green card and a wife and a daughter / my guess is the late seventies

**为什么这样写**：全章他都是拿别人的故事开口（先是记者的稿子，再是对方的失踪学生），这次轮到自己，句子却变成转述：每一句都以 he says 起头，连时间都从断言退成 `I’m not even sure how long ago but my guess is`。一个自称 fixer 的人，最后能交出的只有一份二手证词——他要救的那个人已经无法作证，而对方正在 senile 这件事，他自己在这一场对话里已经说过：`Because he’s going senile.`作者用请求内容的贫乏，反过来量出请求本身的重量。

**读者视角提示**：对方的回话是 `“Just don’t get your hopes up.`，理由很硬：`My people are poor and they’re criminals, to put it bluntly.`；而这一场 Roger 端详他交的照片，花的时间 `studied the photo far longer, Ji-hoon felt guiltily, than he had studied the porridge-faced kid`。两笔交换都不成立，作者仍旧让他们交换了号码、握了手。

## 一句话总结

这一天是笔交换：Roger 要一个 North Korea 的消息，Ji-hoon 要一张被放大了的脸和一个肯替他打听的人——两人都知道这不成立（`Each man’s errand for the other was so impossible`）。他交出去的朋友只剩一份转述，连年份都只能说 `my guess is the late seventies`；对方给的回话是 `“Just don’t get your hopes up.`。全章最后被真正保存下来的，是他把那张 `porridge-faced young American man` 的照片放进了自己的钱包——一张陌生人的脸，替一张他想不出来、也不配有全家福的脸腾了地方。
