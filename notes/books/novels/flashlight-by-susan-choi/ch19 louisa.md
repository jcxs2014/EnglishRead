# 19. Louisa

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：一个女儿去首尔找一个被告知已溺死的父亲。她独自飞抵西村，在 room seventy-four 认出一个被母亲说错的姓、一个写着 `Seung` 的音节，以及一件旧事——他与 Anne 早就相识；九月他得了肺炎，她逐一拒绝复苏抢救，末句停在 `she saw when the rippling ceased.`

- **情感弧线位置**：不是一条上扬的团圆弧，而是一次清算后缓慢下沉：高点是他认出她时的 `There is no question he knows who she is`，低点是对着喉咙看它停止起伏。中途的松弛点落在 `She hadn’t drowned.`——她把童年救回来一句，代价是承认当年被夺走的事实。

- **人物弧线**：从那个 `never pushed her mother’s chair, wouldn’t even touch the handles` 的女儿，到本章开篇自己发问 `Did she really never once push Anne’s wheelchair?`、并真的动手推他的人；中间她被当作 `the discreet, efficient, interchangeable nurses` 那样忽略过，最后的选择却是一次私人的谎言——`“Yes,” she lies.`——替死去的母亲补上一条命。

- **叙事手法**：第三人称限制视角贴紧 Louisa。开头把童年那一夜写成一份没有证词的记录，通篇用 `may still be sitting` / `possibly` 挂上不确定，再用一句 `Do the witnesses make the event?` 把证据一笔勾销；全章的对话只靠一本翻过来递过去的记事本推进——`CAN WE SPEAK ENGLISH` 被划掉，换成 `CAN YOU WRITE MY NAME?`，笔最后交到了他手里。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** These are not the events Louisa recalls because she has never recalled them, they live nowhere in memory. … Do the witnesses make the event? … She did not possess any events to fill the space between stepping with her father onto the damp sand and waking up in a hospital bed. … Even when she tries to pursue memories that seem certain, they morph. … She lived and he lived, and their shared history is a blank. It’s not even a rewritten timeline or even a line.

（前两段写的是她十岁那年跟父亲在海边被人带走。）这一段是全章的地基：这件事「她从未回想起过」——不是忘了，是从没有过记忆。从踩上湿沙到在医院醒来，中间那段不是空白，而是被别的东西填满了：一个从外面递进来的、连续的「坠落并溺毙」的故事。

**关键词**：they live nowhere in memory / Do the witnesses make the event? / their shared history is a blank

**为什么这样写**：作者把「失踪」写成一个认识论问题，而不是一段剧情。她先排除证据：`Even when she tries to pursue memories that seem certain, they morph.`——追下去的记忆会变形；接着连她最笃定的那一点也开始松动：`Even the stepping onto damp sand with her father may not be an admissible point on the timeline.` 于是那句 `Do the witnesses make the event?` 才问得出来，而它的落点是 `their shared history is a blank`：一个被取消的坐标系，连「线」都不剩。通篇最狠的判断在 `The story of her father’s death by drowning is one she somehow both authored and received passively.`——她既是自己写下这段故事的人，也是被动接收它的人；再往后 `It emerged in response to a logic and it equally dictated logic.` 把因果两端调了个位置。

**读者视角提示**：被带走那几段在前面（P6–P7）：先是 `a confusing nebula of heads and limbs`、父亲被用一种她没听过的语言问话，接着 `a damp pad clapped over her mouth`、`She’s stuffed in a sack`，而她喊的是 `she screams for her father and even her mother`。这一段之后作者连问一串问题——`Do the witnesses make the event?`、`She did not possess any events to fill the space`——随即把话头收住——没有人证，也没有物证，只有两个当事人，而其中一位当事人什么都没记住。

> **原句 2:** Charse Kim is a colleague of some kind of Roger’s in the world of not-quite-extragovernmental diplomatic fixing. … Louisa is aware, remotely, that she is in shock; that perhaps she has been in shock for four-fifths of her life. … Louisa’s husband, her grown sons, her brother and his wife, even her aunt Soonja, all have assented to her request that she do this alone; for some reason, her greatest fear is not seeing her father, but being seen while seeing him. … once Roger has seen Louisa through the hospital’s various bureaucracies as he has seen her through every other kind of bureaucracy, he will vanish to some nearby bar; he will leave her alone.

为这趟远行她把身边的人都问过一遍：老公、成年的儿子、哥哥和他太太，连姨妈 Soonja 都同意她一个人去；她自己知道这种状态叫 `in shock`，而且可能已经持续了她大半辈子。此行最怕的不是见到父亲，而是被人看见正在见父亲。

**关键词**：not-quite-extragovernmental diplomatic fixing / four-fifths of her life / being seen while seeing him

**为什么这样写**：作者先用一串转手关系把「她的住处」写出来：公寓属于 Charse Kim，借给 Roger，Roger 再借给 Louisa（`Charse Kim resides in the US but owns this apartment, which he has loaned Roger, and Roger has loaned Louisa`）——她和父亲之间隔着一整套半官方的手续，`not-quite-extragovernmental diplomatic fixing` 这个自造词把这种身份的暧昧直接写进了名词。最关键的一句是把两个「看见」对调：`her greatest fear is not seeing her father, but being seen while seeing him.` 整章她真正争的就是这件事——她要私下完成，不许它成为一场公开的重逢；Roger's 台词随即把它挑明：`the host government’s eagerness for their role in the reunion to remain unknown`，而护士也被蒙在鼓里：`Even the nurses on the ward are not aware of anything unusual`。

**读者视角提示**：本章也交代了她来这里做的功课：`a Korean tutor`，两个月学会字母表，并且知道自己会被几个发音难住的元音卡住（`Heejin crows over her ability to pronounce the different ooos and uhs and ohs`）。这套字母表会在第四块成为父子之间仅存的沟通媒介。

> **原句 3:** Seventy, seventy-two, seventy-four. … the three shapes—half square, half cross, and circle—that thus combined form an unfamiliar sound that is apparently her surname. Louisa pauses with her hand on the door, feeling astonishment that she has just recognized her own surname, without forethought or effort. … Did you know that you’ve said our name wrong all these years? Did you know you’ve never once said it right? … It’s true! She experiences her bereavement for her mother as annoyance, that her mother isn’t there for her to scold with the things her mother couldn’t do right or gobsmack with the things her mother couldn’t have imagined. … In those years after coming back home she never pushed her mother’s chair, wouldn’t even touch the handles.

她数着门牌号走到自己父亲的房间门口。白板上有人用绿笔写了三个形状，拼出来是她「自己的姓」——她当场认出来了，而这意味着母亲这些年把这个姓一直说错了。对母亲的丧失，她感受到的不是悲伤，是「有气无处发」的烦躁；她至今不敢碰轮椅的把手。

**关键词**：an unfamiliar sound / never once said it right / her bereavement for her mother as annoyance

**为什么这样写**：这一段的机关全在 `Seventy, seventy-two, seventy-four.`——她用数门牌代替情绪。紧接着作者把「认出姓」写成一次没有任何前兆的识别：`feeling astonishment that she has just recognized her own surname, without forethought or effort`，而她真正想问母亲的话是一句用错字的中文口气的英文质问：`Did you know that you’ve said our name wrong all these years?`——这个姓是母亲说错的，也是母亲不肯承认自己说错的。于是丧失被改写成 `annoyance`：她的母亲还在那里，就只是为了让她有地方发火，而母亲不在。轮椅那段是同一逻辑的身体版：`Their hard plastic grips made her nauseous.`，她怕的不是椅子，是自己的手；所以这一场里 `the wheelchair is the only thing she’s brave enough to look at.`

**读者视角提示**：轮椅这条线本章还剩一处回收：她母亲那句初次见到 Julian 时说的 `I’ve seen those feet before.`，多年后她才听懂那其实是 `I’ve seen those feet before on your father`，而说话的人就是 Anne（`is what Louisa now understands Anne to have meant`）；而她在这一场里盯着那双 `thick-soled orthopedic black vinyl shoes` 时，已经想到的是 `feet like her sons’ might be hiding`。

> **原句 4:** “You don’t remember your own name, I guess you don’t remember why they chose it. Because it means ‘victory.’ So if a Japanese criticized them for your Korean name, they could say they meant ‘Victory for Japan.’ Goddamn stupid shit.” He still curses with relish, the shhhhh protracted like a blade slicing paper. Louisa’s heart is rioting dangerously, she’s afraid she’ll dispel her own transfiguration into this Seung—a brother? And “they” who named Seung: his parents. Delicate as a soap bubble, this memory chamber quivering in his mind, to make sense of her presence. What can she say to expand it, and not make it pop?

（前一两段里他抓起笔写下一个音节，她照着发音念了出来：Seung。）本块从解释开始：这个名字的意思是「胜利」；当初要是有人批评他们的韩语名字，他们可以答说——意思是「为日本的胜利」。他把这套逻辑骂成蠢话。而她最怕的是：认下这个名字就等于取消掉她自己。

**关键词**：it means ‘victory.’ / they could say they meant ‘Victory for Japan.’ / a brother?

**为什么这样写**：这个名字是本章的题眼答案，而且答案本身是一场跨国改名术的反转：韩语名字 `Seung`＝`victory`，当年被挑中是为了让批评者听成 `‘Victory for Japan.’`——同一个音，两种解释，取哪一种取决于谁在说话。写的人与读的人站在不同一边，这就是全章的机制：父亲用韩语对她说，她用英语对他写。作者把这个揭晓交给最不像揭晓的方式：他不是郑重地报出旧名字，而是把它骂一顿（`Goddamn stupid shit.`），并且还在同一句里抱怨发音——`taking care with the vowel as Heejin has taught her` 变成她的动作，而抱怨者是那个本来就一直在抱怨的人。收尾那个 `a brother?` 是她怕自己认错身份的落点：`she’s afraid she’ll dispel her own transfiguration into this Seung`，而这个名字的来源随即被她记成一句制度性的话：`And “they” who named Seung: his parents.`

**读者视角提示**：他名字里的血脉在这一块之后才被彻底翻过来（下一段）：`her father was born to Korean migrants to Japan`、1952 年 `made an alien registrant in the nation of his birth`、`his US green card expired after he was declared dead`，而结论是一句 `Nothing aligns.`——归属在这一段被逐条注销。

> **原句 5:** Did she really never once push Anne’s wheelchair? … At the closed door to room seventy-four she’s stopped by the problem of how to hold the door open while pushing the wheelchair inside, and then is visited by the muscle memory of pushing a child in a stroller. … As her children did when they were babies, in a gesture she’d found so eloquent in them, her father points at his bed. … he shockingly launches himself from the chair, grabs the bedrail with one hand, and, flailing with the other, hurls himself in, landing short of the pillow … she kneels before him again, pries off the shoes and uncovers the frail, thin feet, sheathed in socks, unimaginable as the base for a vertical body. … Standing and bracing herself, she takes him under the armpits, feeling his meagerness, the worn upholstery barely clothing his bones, and, closing her mind to this, hauls him up the length of the bed … “Let me help you,” she says again. “I’m going to get you on your pillow.”

她推着他走到门口才想起：她从来没有推过母亲的轮椅。可这一次她推了——得靠一段婴儿车式的肌肉记忆才能同时推人和顶门。他抬手指自己的床，像孩子们婴儿时做过的那样；随即他几乎是从轮椅上弹起来扑进床里。她跪下来给他脱鞋，再托住他的腋下把那样轻的身体拽上整张床，反复说的只有一句：`Let me help you.`

**关键词**：never once push Anne’s wheelchair / the muscle memory of pushing a child in a stroller / in a gesture she’d found so eloquent in them

**为什么这样写**：作者把这一场写成一次身体的技术演练，每一步都是机械问题：怎么在推的同时按住门（`and then is visited by the muscle memory of pushing a child in a stroller`，她真的用屁股顶开了门，还 `to soften the bump of the threshold`）；轮子太重；刹车的机关是 `lift each bar by the obvious handle`。这些细节不是背景，它们把「推轮椅」从一件象征物变回一件要用蛮力做的事。而最重要的对照在第一句——`Did she really never once push Anne’s wheelchair?`——这一问把上一块里那份不敢碰把手的厌恶倒着说了一遍：同一双手，这次推的不是母亲的。段落收在他自己扑进床里：`he shockingly launches himself from the chair`，接着 `How did I get here? his face asks her. Who are you? Why?` 用三个问句写在叙述里、不加引号，是这张脸上一句不加修饰的坦白；而她能做的仍然只有 `“Let me help you,”`。

**读者视角提示**：接下来他连说两声 `“Thank you,”`，用的是 `a strange, small, docile voice`——这个「乖」在后面被她自己当作识破冒认的理由（`Like a wind-up toy.`）。再往下一段他喊出她的名字 `“Louisa?”`，她这才被他认出：`There is no question he knows who she is`。

> **原句 6:** When he closes his eyes again, she feels the presence of that outer limit—death—as she felt it that night. It had not been a thought, or a fear. Only a presence, empty and total as the air she’d lived in all her life, and enclosing and filling her as the air did. … the fact that her energy outlasted that presence was not a matter of will but more a matter of mathematics or physics, of near-balanced forces in steady contention until one slightly outweighed the other. … death had enveloped her in the water, not the same as the water or limited by it, simply borrowing it, and that its nearness had been unremarkable, an invisible transfiguration of the molecules around her that had left her untouched. … The simplest physical act feels impossible.

他闭上眼，她立刻感觉到那夜海里的东西又回来了——但作者这一次的判词与常识相反：那不是恐惧，也不是思想，只是「一个像空气一样的存在」把它包住了。她之所以浮起来，是因为力量差一点，而不是因为她意志坚定。

**关键词**：It had not been a thought, or a fear / not a matter of will but more a matter of mathematics or physics / simply borrowing it

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章最重要的一次改写：先取消恐惧——`It was only later that fear came, after understanding departed, which had been the worst thing, that fear with no conception of its source.`；再把活下来的原因从「意志」改成「算术」：`a matter of mathematics or physics, of near-balanced forces in steady contention until one slightly outweighed the other.` 三个短语一路递降，把一场生死写成天平倾斜。而最漂亮的是那个借用的比喻：死亡 `not the same as the water or limited by it, simply borrowing it`，于是一场溺毙在物理上从来不曾发生——`an invisible transfiguration of the molecules around her that had left her untouched`。这段的知识论锋口与第一块那三个问句是同一件事的两端。

**读者视角提示**：触发这一切的是他的一句话：`“They threw me out of the boat, and I swam.”`随后他反问 `“All the way?”`，她答 `“All the way to the shore,”`，并在叙述里真正记起当年的身体：`collapsing in the frill of the surf as she crawled up the incline of shore, unable to go any farther`。落点是她自己的补全：`She hadn’t drowned.`——「没有淹死」这句话，是把第一块那个被判为无效的童年救回来一点。

> **原句 7:** So it’s true, they really did know each other: she and he, and she and Anne, and he and Anne. “Yes,” she lies.

（前面几段里他先叫了她的名字，随后问起她的母亲；她还追问了当年是谁在照料她。）本块给出结论：她与 Anne、与他、他与 Anne，本来就是彼此认识的——而她答的是谎话。

**关键词**：they really did know each other / “Yes,” she lies

**为什么这样写**：这一段的推进方式是把一个本来应该属于童年的事实推到桌面上：`he and Anne` 早就在认识，而且他记得她洗完澡的样子（`She didn’t know I was there. She was so thin, I could see all her ribs.`）——他比她更早看见母亲的瘦。整段最重的一句评价只有五个词：`“Yes,” she lies.` 前面那句 `So it’s true, they really did know each other: she and he, and she and Anne, and he and Anne.` 用三个 `and` 把关系摆成一组对称的三角。而他对她这套谎言的态度是：`“Oh thank God,” he says hoarsely, as if the effort of saving Anne’s life has been completed by him just that moment.`——他以为是自己救活的。回答问题的方式也说明他病得多深：`“Anne,” he says, with something almost querulous in his voice, as if Anne herself has appeared.`

**读者视角提示**：这个谎后面会被追责。当晚她给 Tobias 打电话，他先给了一句判词 `“You’ve gone to the land of the dead,”`，随后补一句 `“Like Odysseus.”`；她转述父亲主动问起母亲（Tobias 反问 `“Of course you did.”`），而他的裁决是：`“I don’t think it counts as a lie. She thought he was dead all those years and it gave her peace. Thinking she’s alive will give him peace.”`——这句话同时也是她的辩护词。

> **原句 8:** In early September, her father contracted pneumonia; the nurses fetched an attending who spoke beautiful English, who asked Louisa what her father’s directive was. … Louisa asked for the forms of resuscitation to be listed for her. … When she laid her palm on the cotton blanket covering his chest, the body beneath felt like a delicate box made of wood. The slightest pressure could splinter it into kindling. … She said, “No,” one by one to each form of resuscitation. … She said, “Yes,” to morphine when she noticed that his hand, which she now held for hours at a time, had begun to twitch, a movement that made her think of a guttering flame. … The skin of his throat also now seemed exquisitely fine; it rippled like water with each inaudible breath. She kept her gaze on his throat and she saw when the rippling ceased.

九月初他得了肺炎，护士叫来一位英语说得很好的主治医生，那位医生问 Louisa 她父亲的「指示」是什么。她一项一项答「不」；看见他的手开始抖，她才答「是」——那一项是吗啡。最后她盯着他的喉咙，看着那片起伏停下。全章停在这里。

**关键词**：a delicate box made of wood / a guttering flame / she saw when the rippling ceased

**为什么这样写**：这段的写法是把死亡写成一张表单：先是 `Louisa asked for the forms of resuscitation to be listed for her.`，然后是一句 `She said, “No,” one by one`——她对每一项复苏措施逐一说「不」，重复本身就是把一件痛苦的事拆成可执行步骤的动作。例外落在吗啡那一项：`She said, “Yes,” to morphine`，理由写得极其克制，只说手在抖、`a movement that made her think of a guttering flame`——她甚至不把它当成对话（`She didn’t interpret this movement as an effort to communicate with her`）。身体被写成一件不能碰的东西：`a delicate box made of wood`，`The slightest pressure could splinter it into kindling`，握住它像托着一只睡着的蝙蝠；喉咙 `rippled like water with each inaudible breath`。而全章最后六个词只写了一个观察动作：`she saw when the rippling ceased.`

**读者视角提示**：这一段之前最后一次谈他的去处，答案已经说完了：`“The question” was, to what nation would her father immigrate?` 而她的判断是 `I mean, I think he’s going to solve this problem—of where and how he should move—by not moving.` 同一个餐厅窗外的树是四百多年前的：`From the perspective of the tree almost everything else here was alien and new, which perhaps meant alien and new things were all equally at home.`

## 一句话总结

她把三样东西对上了：一个音（`half square, half cross, and circle` 拼出来的、母亲说错了一辈子的姓）、一个名字（`Because it means ‘victory.’`，一个当年被挑来听成 `‘Victory for Japan.’` 的名字）、一段被删掉的历史（`Nothing aligns.`，以及 `he and Anne` 早就相识）。对上的同一刻她选择撒谎，并把这场重衔定义成一次不许公开的私事；而关于他最后该去哪个国家，答案早就写好了：`by not moving.`九月他得了肺炎，她在一张复苏表单上逐项答“不”，只在吗啡那一项答“是”，最后看着他的喉咙 `she saw when the rippling ceased.`
