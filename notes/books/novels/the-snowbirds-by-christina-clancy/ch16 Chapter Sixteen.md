## Quote Blocks

### 1

> "The fourth time Grant left I really thought he might be dead."

Kim reflects on Grant's pattern of disappearing, this time coinciding with her mother's recent death. The sentence conveys the genuine fear underlying what has become a repeated pattern in their marriage.

### 2

> "Polly had her stroke in mid-December 2008 and died after a short stay in the ICU. Her sudden passing left me utterly gutted."

Kim explains the context: her mother's death in December 2008, just a month before this confrontation with Grant. Grief colors every interaction, making even small tensions feel insurmountable.

### 3

> "Anyone up for an ice cream at the Chocolate Shoppe?"

Grant tries to reconnect with his family after returning from teaching a JanTerm class. The simple offer—ice cream at a local shop—represents his modest hope for family warmth upon coming home.

### 4

> "Dad, no. It's freezing."

Dort's curt rejection exemplifies the difficult teenage years the girls are navigating. Grant's attempts at connection are met with dismissal, leaving him isolated in his own home.

### 5

> "There's a talk about human rights in Southeast Asia at the Union tonight. Anyone?"

Another of Grant's failed overtures. He retreats to intellectual pursuits as a substitute for emotional connection, offering lectures as a way to spend time together.

### 6

> "Then don't come home. We're all busy, Grant. My mother just died. You want us to drop everything the minute you walk through the door."

Kim reaches her breaking point and articulates the fundamental mismatch: she is deep in grief and parenting teenagers, while Grant expects the same enthusiastic reunion that marked earlier years of their marriage.

### 7

> "No, Kimmer. I'm sorry I snapped."

Grant responds with unexpected gentleness, taking responsibility for his own frustration rather than escalating the conflict further.

### 8

> "And then you walked away. You walked right out of the door, and when I heard the door shut behind you, I felt like you'd cut me in half."

Grant describes the emotional devastation of being rejected, revealing that his dramatic exits are triggered by feeling abandoned rather than calculated decisions to punish Kim.

### 9

> "You either stole the vehicle, or you bought it hot."

Officer Brady delivers the unsettling conclusion: the Jeep Grant purchased in Barstow was likely stolen property, adding another layer of complication to the mystery of his disappearance.

### 10

> "It's been over two days now, Mrs. Duffy. We've closed the canyon and activated tremendous resources."

The search has reached a critical juncture. Brady's matter-of-fact statement that resources are committed underscores how dangerous Grant's situation has become.

## One-Sentence Summary

Kim recounts a pivotal January 2009 confrontation with Grant after her mother Polly's death—Grant returns home seeking connection but finds only rejection, walks out, and is later in a car accident—then flashes forward to the present, where Officer Brady reveals the Jeep may have been stolen, deepening the mystery of Grant's disappearance.
