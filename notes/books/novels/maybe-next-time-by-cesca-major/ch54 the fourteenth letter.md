**为什么这样写**：What a year = Dan's opening—2020 was extraordinary. "We're almost there, maybe. Or not." = Dan's uncertainty—he doesn't know if the pandemic is ending. "It's really hard to tell what the next few months will bring" = Dan's observation—uncertainty. "I think it's official that global pandemics suck balls." = Dan's humor—he's blunt. "And we should all definitely be awarded some kind of medal." = Dan's humor—everyone deserves a medal. "We're still together. I mean, we sort of have to be because as I write this we are in the second lockdown." = Dan's humor—the lockdown forces them together. "Neither of us can leave the building—but would you even have the energy to leave me?" = Dan's humor—Emma wouldn't leave. "I am definitely running on about 15 percent these days." = Dan's exhaustion—he's tired. "Leaving sounds like Very Hard Work." = Dan's humor—leaving would be hard work. This is the letter's opening: Dan's humor about the pandemic, setting a light tone before the serious content.

**中文理解**："And I've convinced myself it's the lockdown, it's COVID, it's all the added stresses on you but . . . it still hasn't really forced you to slow down."

This is the letter's core insight: even during a pandemic, Emma hasn't slowed down. Her busyness is a pattern, not a circumstance.
