> **原句 1:** “'Emma, EMMA,' she called over me scrabbling around for more examples. 'What's going on? Why are you having a nervous breakdown? You're freaking me out.'”
**中文理解**：Jas 叫 Emma："Emma, EMMA"，over her scrabbling around for more examples。"What's going on? Why are you having a nervous breakdown? You're freaking me out."
**关键词**：nervous breakdown / freaking me out
**为什么这样写**：nervous breakdown = Jas's interpretation of Emma's behavior—she thinks Emma is having a breakdown, not experiencing a loop. "freaking me out" = Jas's fear—Emma's knowledge is unsettling. This is the loop's first sharing: Emma tries to explain, Jas resists. The comedy (Emma knowing about the apple turnover, witches) becomes the evidence.
**读者视角提示**：nervous breakdown = Jas's interpretation; "freaking me out" = her fear; Emma's knowledge as evidence.

**读者视角提示**：I lived it before = Emma's claim; "Literally before" = her emphasis; the specific details (apple turnover, loo, witches) are her evidence.

## 一句话总结

Emma tells Jas about the loop—she knows about the apple turnover, witches submission, audiobook contracts; Jas believes her ("That's pretty freaky shit"); Emma reveals "Dan died. He gets run over"; Jas: "You have to save him. You've been given a second chance."——the loop's purpose revealed.

## 读者视角提示

- **Jas as believer**：Jas is the first person who believes Emma. Hattie (ch22) rationalized ("Maybe that's what your dream was about?"). Jas doesn't rationalize—she believes. "That's pretty freaky shit" = her acceptance. This is Emma's first successful sharing of the loop.
- **"You have to save him"**：Jas's instruction is the loop's thesis—the loop exists to save Dan. "You've been given a second chance" = the loop as gift. "That's pretty amazing" = Jas's awe. This is the novel's central revelation: the loop is not a curse, it's a chance.
- **"I'd been given a chance to stop it"**：Emma's realization—the loop's purpose is to prevent Dan's death. This is Emma's mission: she must save Dan. The loop's tension: can she? The loop allows small changes but punishes big ones (ch21). Can she make the one change that matters?
- **Groundhog Day comparison**：Jas compares the loop to a book she read (not the film). Emma says "it was based on the film Groundhog Day." Jas: "I've never seen that. Is it good?" This is the novel's meta-commentary on its own premise—it's aware of the Groundhog Day comparison but distinguishes itself.
- **"Dan died. He gets run over."**：Emma's confession—she tells Jas about Dan's death. This is the first time Emma speaks Dan's death aloud to another person (she couldn't tell Hattie). Jas's "I love Dan" = their shared connection. The loop's central secret is now shared.
- **loop structure**：ch20 (loop #3, Emma tries to change) → ch21 (loop #3 continues, Emma's big change backfires) → ch22 (loop #3 continues, Emma tries to share with Hattie) → ch24 (loop #3 continues, Emma shares with Jas, Jas believes). The loop is giving Emma more agency and more allies each iteration. But the outcome (Dan's death) remains fixed. The loop's central tension: can Emma change the outcome? Or is Dan's death inevitable? Jas's "You have to save him" = the loop's mission. Emma's "I'd been given a chance to stop it" = her acceptance of the mission. But can she? The loop suggests: small changes are allowed, big changes are punished. Preventing Dan's death is the biggest change of all. Can Emma do it? The novel's answer is in the remaining chapters.
