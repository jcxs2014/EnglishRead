**中文理解**：Seeing Hayley in the lobby did something to Emma；the urge to tell someone, a friend, overwhelmed her。
**关键词**：urge to tell someone, a friend, overwhelmed me
**为什么这样写**：urge to tell someone = Emma's need to share her loop-awareness—she can't carry it alone. "a friend" = Hayley is her friend (from ch06: "Hayley, a friend who'd acquired one of my first authors"). "overwhelmed me" = the need is physical, not chosen. This is Emma's first attempt to break the loop's isolation: she wants to tell someone. But Hayley won't listen (later: "I don't have time to be a friend"). The loop isolates Emma.
**读者视角提示**：urge to tell someone = Emma's need to share; "a friend" = Hayley; overwhelmed = physical need; the loop isolates Emma.

**读者视角提示**：Ow = Emma's pain check (reality verification); "Just . . . checking" = her euphemism; the ellipsis shows her hesitation; everyone swiveled = the meeting's disruption.

**读者视角提示**：come off Twitter altogether = Emma's suggestion (backfires); "voice bright" = her performance; the suggestion makes things worse—Arthur storms out, Linda is angry.

**读者视角提示**：I need to talk to my client alone = Linda excluding Emma; "in a hiss" = her anger; "I will talk to you later today" = her threat; Emma's correction backfires.

**关键词**：I don't have time to be a friend
**为什么这样写**：Hayley's rejection is the chapter's emotional climax—Emma reached out for help, and Hayley refused. **读者视角提示**：I don't have time to be a friend = Hayley's rejection; Emma's need vs Hayley's availability; the loop isolates Emma—she can't tell anyone.

> **原句 8:** “'No,' I said carefully. 'No, not dying. It can wait . . .'”

**关键词**：not dying / It can wait
**为什么这样写**：not dying = Emma's literal answer (she's not dying—Dan is, but Emma doesn't know how to explain). "It can wait" = the loop's cruel irony—Emma thinks she has time, but the loop will reset and she'll forget.
**读者视角提示**：not dying = Emma's literal answer; "It can wait" = her deferral; the loop's cruel irony—Emma thinks she has time, but the loop will reset.

## 读者视角提示

- **Emma's correction backfires**：Emma tries to change the meeting's outcome (suggesting Arthur come off Twitter instead of the bursary lie). But this makes things worse—Arthur storms out, Linda is furious, Melissa threatens to pause the contract. The loop allows small changes (drink, text) but punishes big ones. This is the loop's determinism: Emma can change small things, but the big events (Arthur's storm-out, Linda's anger) are fixed.
- **"Ow" (pen jab)**：Emma jabs her pen into her arm to check if this is real. "Just . . . checking." = her euphemism. This is the loop's physical verification: pain = reality. Emma is checking if she's in a dream. The pain confirms she's real (or in a very real loop).
- **Hayley's rejection**："I don't have time to be a friend" = the loop's isolation. Emma can't tell anyone about the loop, and the people she reaches for can't help. Hayley is professional, not personal. Emma's "I really need to talk to you" vs Hayley's "I don't have time to be a friend" = the gap between Emma's need and Hayley's availability.
- **"It can wait"**：Emma's deferral—she thinks she has time to tell Hayley later. But the loop will reset and she'll forget. This is the loop's cruel irony: Emma thinks she has time, but she doesn't. The loop will reset, and she'll lose this information.
- **"No, not dying"**：Emma's literal answer. She's not dying—Dan is (but she doesn't know how to explain). This is the loop's central asymmetry: Emma knows, Dan doesn't. Emma can't share what she knows.
- **loop structure**：ch20 (loop #3, Emma tries to change small things) → ch21 (loop #3 continues, Emma's big change backfires). The loop is giving Emma more agency each iteration, but big changes backfire. The loop's central tension: can Emma change the outcome? Or is Dan's death inevitable? The loop suggests: small changes are allowed, big changes are punished. Emma's tragedy: she can't make the one change that matters (preventing Dan's death) without making things worse.
