# 《The Librarian of Burned Books》概述

## 全书梗概

**一个死讯开始，落在两扇终于被敲开的门之间。** 1944 年 5 月的纽约，Vivian Childs 收到一封电报：
- The telegram regretfully informing Vivian Childs that her husband had died in battle arrived before his last letter.（ch01）
- 而桌上摊着丈夫 Edward 的最后一封信——他生前最卖力地鼓吹的那件事，眼下正要被人一条修正案连根拔起。The army has launched an ingenious initiative wherein they ship us poor, bored boys small, portable books to keep us entertained and distracted from all the bombs that are landing inches from our heads.（ch01）

**1932 年 12 月的柏林，先给一个相信书的女孩。** Althea James 在冬日市集的灯串下走进一场噩梦般的狂欢：
- They sang in German, cried and laughed and danced, and Althea cried and laughed and danced alongside them, patriotism for her ancestors’ country thrumming in her blood, heady and hot and irresistible despite the fact that the pride was so new to her.（ch07）
- 那时候的柏林是这样的：Berlin was magical and Althea was charmed, wooed, nearly under a spell.（ch03）
她相信书是能被救下来的，人也是。

**1933 年 2 月，禁令下来了。** 第一条规矩简单得可怕：
- “The first rule of the Reich, darling, is don’t question the Reich.”（ch11）
- 排队交出自己的藏书那天，她遇见了那个后来毁掉她的人。She was twenty-five, and this was her very first kiss.（ch07）

**1936 年 10 月的巴黎，火焰烧过了国境。** Hannah Brecht 在被烧书图书馆当值，做的第一件事是把每本禁书都读一遍：
- She had found she liked them, a tangible marker of the work she was doing to fight the fascists. Even if she hadn’t turned to bullets and bombs like the young radicals wanted to, her efforts were no less important in this battle.（ch09）
- 她在这里第一次看见 Althea——一个在铁丝网另一边、同样不肯放手的人。That had always suited Hannah, who had never been able to completely reconcile who she was—who she loved—with any religion that condemned her lifestyle.（ch09）

**1944 年 5 月，Viv 推开布鲁克林那扇玻璃门。** 房间出奇地小，却塞满了顶到天花板的书架：
- It was surprisingly small but packed with towering shelves and tables crowded with sloppy stacks of books.（ch06）
- 管理员说出了全书题眼：“Books are a way we leave a mark on the world, aren’t they? They say we were here, we loved and we grieved and we laughed and we made mistakes and we existed. They can be burned halfway across the world, but the words cannot be unread, the stories cannot be untold. They do live on in this library, but more importantly they are immortalized in anyone who has read them.”（ch06）
- It was surprisingly small but packed with towering shelves and tables crowded with sloppy stacks of books.（ch06）
- 而 Viv 带走的另一样东西是一个词：Vendetta. Viv liked that word, the picture it painted. Again, she thought about the way to tell a story, the way to get people to care. People cared about vendettas. They found them interesting. One had to look no further than Romeo and Juliet’s lasting popularity to know that.（ch06）
- A guardian. It was a fanciful notion, perhaps, picturing this woman as a protector of books, but Viv liked the idea.（ch06）

**她要的不是道理，是一个让人抬头看的理由。** 走进 Emmett Hale 办公室之前她这样想：
- “But you know better than anyone people love a good story. I just have to find the right one to tell.”（ch15）
- The most dangerous thing about knowing someone well was that you knew how to hurt them.（ch15）
- 她先说的是这件事：“Strange Fruit was approved for one of next year’s series. As long as you win against Taft,” Edith said. “Which you will.”（ch21）
- “Strange Fruit was approved for one of next year’s series. As long as you win against Taft,” Edith said. “Which you will.”（ch21）
没人会质疑一套被参议员点过名的丛书了。

**代价是她没算到的那一句。** 她以为自己要的是盟友：
- “They destroyed the place, which was conducting groundbreaking research on women, homosexuals, and sexual intermediaries,” the librarian said, without a single tremor in her voice. Viv wanted to be as blasé, wanted to be sophisticated, but in the circles she moved in she had not once heard two of those words spoken out loud.（ch17）
- 而那位管理员在同一年说过另一句话：“But as I said, words cannot be unwritten simply because you burn them. Ideas cannot simply be erased. People cannot be erased.” The librarian touched the spine of the book gently, reverently, before moving on. “Burning books about things you do not like or understand does not mean those things no longer exist.”（ch17）
- Viv sidled closer, trying to get a glimpse of the cover, but the librarian pulled it to her chest. A protective gesture.（ch17）
这两句是同一枚硬币。

**1944 年 6 月，出版社先开了刀。** Taft 绕开正面，直接拿禁书项目开刀。Viv 读了那一整摞书评：
- Viv did pick up on a slight condescending tone throughout all of the reviews. They made sure to mention in the same tone that Althea had no formal education, as if she were some special dog who could ride a bicycle, and there were several velvet-covered barbs about her ability despite the fact that she was a woman. Her editor, a man who had discovered her through a quirk of fate, also featured heavily, something Viv doubted would have happened had Althea also been a man.（ch25）
- The New York Post’s book editor mused that the change might have been down to the fact that Althea had spent six months in 1932 and 1933 as a guest of Joseph Goebbels and his initiative to bring authors to Germany for residencies to show off the successes of the master race.（ch25）
她喊了整本书的那句口号，喊的是出版商。

**1944 年 7 月，Maine 的海边，Althea 被捡了回来。** 老朋友 Joe 开卡车把她带回酒吧楼上：
- Althea’s mouth opened once, shut, pressed into a thin line. And then she whirled, leaving Viv standing on the cliffs, a metaphorical door slamming.（ch43）
- 而在同一个月，Viv 正忙着把两个人同时请进同一个房间：Viv worried that Althea James might faint on her. The woman was far too pale, her mouth set, her hands clutched so that her knuckles were white.（ch49）

**真正的和解在纽约发生，但没有人先开口。** Hannah 给了 Althea 全书最狠的一句诊断：
- Hope seemed worse than foolish, it seemed dangerous.（ch50）
- Hannah Brecht alive and not buried in some mass grave.（ch50）
- Althea 在街上站了整整一章才上楼：Althea waved off Vivian Childs’s offer to escort her around the city to see all the sights.（ch52）
- She had truly thought that if she did the right thing, if she fought the valiant fight, she could be redeemed. But redemption had never lived in one single moment. It lived in a thousand of them.（ch52）
- 门开了。Then Hannah opened the door.（ch53）
- The land of freedom and equality had done little to roll out the welcome mat for her.（ch53）
- Hannah 第一次同意自己当一次懦夫。She didn’t often consider herself a coward, but for once she let herself be one.（ch53）

**尾声落在 1995 年的柏林。** 焚书纪念碑前面坐着两个人，看着陌生人往长明灯里添书。Few people noticed the two women sitting on the bench watching from a distance as the Book Burning Memorial in Bebelplatz was unveiled.（ch54）

## 三个主题

**一、书是唯一烧不毁的证据。** 管理员说字不能被读回：
- “Books are a way we leave a mark on the world, aren’t they? They say we were here, we loved and we grieved and we laughed and we made mistakes and we existed. They can be burned halfway across the world, but the words cannot be unread, the stories cannot be untold. They do live on in this library, but more importantly they are immortalized in anyone who has read them.”（ch06）
- Hannah 在同一本书里说过同一件事的另一半：The men who sought violence didn’t understand that while swords could destroy bodies, a pen could destroy a nation.（ch09）
- 全书对「谁在保护谁」的最终回答是：**最先保护别人的人，救的是自己。** She never again would wish herself a hero, but maybe she proved with each action she took that she wasn’t a villain, either.（ch52）

**二、爱不是解药，是被反复碾过的东西。** Dev 的警告从未落空：
- “I write them, too, though no one remembers that part,” Dev said, sounding both bitter and resigned. “When you’re the face in front of the camera, people tend to forget anything else.”（ch11）
- Hannah 花十年做审判者，Althea 花十年做被审判的人：For years, Hannah had been the judge, jury, and executioner over each move Althea had made, and she had no one to blame for that but herself.（ch52）
- 这本书拒绝廉价和解——门是被一句一句敲开的：And then she crossed the street, climbed the stairs, and knocked on the door.（ch52）
- For years, Hannah had been the judge, jury, and executioner over each move Althea had made, and she had no one to blame for that but herself.（ch52）
- 而门那头的人先承认了自己害怕。She didn’t often consider herself a coward, but for once she let herself be one.（ch53）

**三、叙事本身就是斗争的一部分。** Viv 的全部本事都在这里：
- Vendetta. Viv liked that word, the picture it painted. Again, she thought about the way to tell a story, the way to get people to care. People cared about vendettas. They found them interesting. One had to look no further than Romeo and Juliet’s lasting popularity to know that.（ch06）
- 全书的反讽是——她喊了整本书的口号，喊的是出版商：Viv did pick up on a slight condescending tone throughout all of the reviews. They made sure to mention in the same tone that Althea had no formal education, as if she were some special dog who could ride a bicycle, and there were several velvet-covered barbs about her ability despite the fact that she was a woman. Her editor, a man who had discovered her through a quirk of fate, also featured heavily, something Viv doubted would have happened had Althea also been a man.（ch25）

- 转折：So maybe she could be brave. Just this once.（ch31）

## 可迁移表达

- `the words cannot be unread`（字不能被读回）——不可逆的动词否定，描述阅读留下的痕迹
- `Vendetta`（血仇）——一个人人被理解、但一个人会受益的词
- `go to slaughter`（走进屠场）——同义替换里的立场差
- `Once upon a time.`（从前啊。）——把一个时代的全部重量压进三个词
- `A guardian.`（一个守护者。）——用一句独段给自己安一个身份
- `Home might not be a place, but rather a person.`（家也许不是一个地方，而是一个人。）——全书的落点
