# 《The Lonely Hearts Book Club》概述

## 全书梗概

**起点是一个图书管理员和一句找碴的话。** 一个再普通不过的日子，科达伦公共图书馆的虚构区走道里，一位读者挡住了她的路：
- The day I met Arthur McLachlan was perfectly ordinary.（ch01）
- 她是被未婚夫的母亲形容成一个圆的那种人——而她自己也同意这个形容。她和布雷特的婚约从一开始就不是因为爱他：Believe it or not, I was engaged to be married to a doctor. Technically, he was a chiropractor, but I’d learned early on in our relationship that to introduce him as anything but Dr. Marcowitz would end in a battle royal.（ch02）
而这本书的第一句话就把这件事说了出来——她嫁给的是一份工作、一个屋顶、一套不用挣就有的家庭。

**然后她遇见了书。** 严格说，是遇见了一本书和它的主人。她被那位读者气得回敬了一句《杀死一只知更鸟遇上波莉安娜》，然后在同一个下午被塞了一本《雨中的赛车》：
- “If you’re looking for a book recommendation, I think you should pick up The Art of Racing in the Rain. It’s what I suggest to all our regular patrons. I know it has a reputation for being sad, but—”（ch01）
- 这场争执最后传到了图书馆的另一头，梅赛带着她自己的理由认下了一个读者；而真正被改变的是斯隆——那是她姐姐去世之后，第一个让她感到活过来的东西：And then just Sloane.（ch03）

**读书会是一个越界的解决方案。** 斯隆原本只想留住这个老人，好让自己有个理由继续往那栋房子跑。可他不但不肯读，还抢走了唯一一本自己的书：“I don’t want all five copies. I only want this one.” He held it to his chest, his grip so tight that the bulbous blue veins stood out on his hands. “She can have the rest, but this one is mine.”（ch08） 于是她搞出了一个五人小组，条件是必须选他接受、而他不会同意的东西。这个决定把四个陌生人塞进了一栋本该只属于一个人的房子里。

**四个人各有各的伤。** 梅赛的女儿在十五岁那年离家，而她把这件事的诊断权交给了那位最不该做诊断的老人：“My grandmother died young. Too young, a few weeks after my mom turned twelve.” From breast cancer, but I didn’t want to say that part out loud. Too many women in my family had fallen on that particular sword. “It would’ve been tragic for any little girl, but my mom was left alone in that house with Arthur. For six whole years, it was just the two of them facing their grief alone.”（ch23） 马特奥是一个由单亲母亲一手捏大的夜店歌手，他一辈子在躲她，也一辈子在唱她教他的歌；格雷格的外祖母在他母亲去世后独自撑了六年，撑到自己也走了；林肯什么都没说，只是每次都在场。四个人的故事线在书会里第一次交叉的地方，是马特奥那一声说不出口的「Ye-es」。

**他们用读书会逼出了一个坦白。** 有人在餐桌上说出了自己最不堪的那一面：“I stand by all of it, you hear? Every single word.（ch27） 那句话是关于爱自己母亲的——他恨她，也爱她，而这两件事同时成立。书会没有解决他的问题，但它让他知道这张桌子上至少有一个人的问题和他一样重。

**转折点是一张旧年鉴照片。** 一个陌生人奈杰尔·迦太基用两万块买通了读书会，又在一个月后从那栋房子的门口消失。格雷格一路查到北爱达荷学院的档案室，只用五分钟就找到了答案：1973 年的一张合影上，两个男人各伸出一只胳膊，搭在同一个穿围裙的女人肩上——而她是他祖母：“My grandmother.”（ch25） 这条线把那个老人最深的羞耻翻了出来。

**爆发与反击。** 老人在客厅里当着所有人的面，逐个清点了他们的账本——每一句都准确：
- “Both of you run around and yammer on about books like they mean something, but it’s obvious you’ve never learned a thing from them. If you had, you wouldn’t waste your time following dreams that belong to someone else. Sloane, marrying a man who will bore her to tears within six months. Mateo, shuffling through careers that mean nothing to him.” He opened his mouth in a soundless laugh. “And why? Because you’re too chickenshit to do something that really matters? So afraid of failure that you’re both willing to lie down before you even try to stand? I thought we were finally starting to get somewhere, but I see I was wrong.”（ch26）
- 三个被骂的人第一次组成了同一道防线。读书会从这一章起不再是一个附带活动，而是一支军队：“It’s high time the Racing in the Rain Book Club made the formal introduction of our newest and most illustrious member.” Mateo rubbed his hands together with a greedy—and welcome—crackle. “Arthur McLachlan isn’t the only one around here who knows where to hit so it really hurts.”（ch26）

**代价是病历上的两行字。** 奈杰尔是来告别的，而那个来告别的动作本身把老人的心脏送进了急救室。这一段是全书最重的一次反转，因为它同时证明了两件事：那本禁止提起的书从来没有被埋葬过；Mateo shook his head, his expression so somber it was like looking in a mirror. “I don’t think he came all this way to chat about books and rile you up about the past, Arthur. I think he’s here to say goodbye.”（ch32） 而他们用来留住斯隆的方法，从头到尾都是奈杰尔教给老人的那一招。

**落点在医院的一间病房。** 他们用一场读书会干预把斯隆骗回这张桌子上，然后在书的第 37 章画了三行橙线——那是她姐姐当年想让她记住、而她二十年来没读懂的话：
- There, in bright orange, stood the words I’d never before taken the time to appreciate—the ones promising that life would go on, that joy was still to be had in the world. They were the words my sister had tried so hard to instill in me all those years ago, the words I’d been too young and too heartbroken to understand. It had taken twenty years and a stranger with an orange highlighter to finally break through.（ch35）
- 全书最后一句话不是任何一个人的结局，是一次回话：It was time for me to answer.（ch35）

## 三个主题

**一、用书代替说话，以及它的代价。** 这本书里几乎所有人都不会直接说心里话，而办法都一样：
- 借一本书、一张借书卡、一枚绣着首字母的手帕。奈杰尔四十年前用一本过夜快递挽回了他的人生：“I sent it by overnight mail.（ch31）
- 老人到八十岁还在用同一招：She was in every book I’d ever read, every tale that had ever touched my heart. Fiction and nonfiction, memoir and short story—no matter what I read, I always found her. It was why I resorted so often to thrillers and German philosophy in my declining years, even though Sloane did her best to steer me away from them.（ch31）
- 而这本书对这套办法的最终判决，是斯隆在第 418 号房里当着所有人的面把话说破：“I’ve seen the books around your house, Arthur. All of them. I know what they mean. I know what they’re trying to say.”（ch35）
说话需要先有话说——而这是全书最难学的一门课。

**二、「照顾」和「拯救」的差别。** 书会里每个人都在照顾另一个人：梅赛照顾女儿的记忆，马特奥照顾母亲，格雷格照顾祖父，斯隆照顾一个死了女儿的图书管理员。而这部小说反复证明的是：照顾一旦单向，就会变成另一种形式的伤害。老人对梅赛的诊断（她不懂自己女儿）是对的、也是错的：“I did.” Maisey fell to one of the dining room chairs without waiting to be invited. “It was the hardest thing I’ve ever done in my life, but I’ve had a lot of practice. I just had to pretend she was one of my clients. I’m always fighting with myself not to tell them what I really think—and believe me, some of them desperately need to hear it. But they don’t call because they want the truth. They call because they need someone to listen.”（ch32） 而他对自己女儿的那份判断，到全书结束也没有被修正。

**三、把「活着」当成一种需要练习的技能。** 这本书对死亡的写法很特别——它从不预言，只报事实：
- “I suspected it from the start. The yellowing in his eyes, how thin he looks, the fact that he’s throwing massive amounts of cash around—it’s end-stage liver disease. He only has a few months left.（ch35）
- 而它对活着的写法是具体的、感官化的：It was the same thing I loved about working at the library. On the outside, it was a place of whispers and stillness. But inside—oh, what marvelous depths there were to explore.（ch34）
- 斯隆全书最后想明白的，其实是她姐姐十二岁时就懂的那件事，而她用了二十年和一支橙色荧光笔才补上：I clutched the book to my chest in sudden, heart-wrenching sadness. Not because of the people we’d lost and would continue to lose, but because even with that loss on every horizon, life still called to me.（ch35）

## 人物弧光

**Sloane Parker**——

- 起点：Once upon a time had always been good enough for me.（ch02）
- 中段：Not once had he asked if I was doing okay.（ch24）
- 最低点：“But you don’t love me.”（ch34）
- 落点：“I know,” I said as I flashed my empty left hand. “That’s why I broke it off with Brett before I came up here.”（ch35）
- 她的转折不是任何一次胜利，而是第一次为自己说话（而不是为别人）：“I wasn’t asking for your permission, Brett. I was informing you of my intentions.” I was pretty sure I was yelling again, but I couldn’t seem to stop myself. I wondered if this was what had happened to my parents, a sloughing off of all restraint, a determination to say what needed to be said no matter what the cost. Maybe, after a while, you just got used to it.（ch33）

**Arthur McLachlan**——

- 起点：It was pathetic, really. I’d lived alone in this house for almost thirty years, and I’d done fine with each and every one of them. I had a routine to keep me from getting bored, my books to keep my mind active. I’d gone for days at a time without having to speak a word to anybody, and I regretted none of them.（ch28）
- 转向：It was still the only thing approaching a home I had.（ch26）
- 最低点：I, Arthur McLachlan, didn’t have the right words.（ch30）
- 落点：“There she is,” he murmured. “I knew you had a big and bright life burning somewhere inside you.”（ch35）
- 他一生只做过一件难事：向一个人承认自己需要别人：“And I am desperate, Nigel. She’s going to leave.（ch31）

**Maisey Sharpe**——

- 起点：Relentless kindness tinged around the edges with condescension.（ch04）
- 转向：“Yes, please,” Maisey said. “I don’t know what I’d do if the book club wasn’t here to keep me going. You guys are all I have.”（ch22）
- 落点：“You’re a good mom to that child,” I said. “Better than she deserves, that’s for sure.”（ch32）
- 她是全书唯一一个从头到尾都没有变成别人的人——而她做的事是听：“She’s going to join a few clubs to see if that helps. I suggested volleyball and drama. She’ll give them a two-week trial and call back to update me on her progress.” She tapped the side of her nose and winked at Greg. “See? I can learn. Keep her on the hook. Give her an opening so she has no choice but to come back for more.”（ch32）

**Mateo Sharpe**——

- 起点：Save me, that look said. Protect me.（ch18）
- 最低点：“What kind of kid does that?” I asked. “What kind of person? I spent three days down there, Lincoln. I could have died. And all I cared about was how nice it was to look up and see the sky.”（ch21）
- 落点：“I don’t need it in writing.” He hesitated. “I just needed to hear that you respect my medical opinion before I say this next part.”（ch32）
- 他的整条线是关于一句话的：一整章他没有说出口，而全书最后他只要求了一句收据：“I have no confirmation of this, and I wouldn’t say it if you hadn’t…” He took a deep breath, his eyes growing watery again. “If you hadn’t said those nice things about letting myself be loved. But you’re right. I do need to stop hiding from the things that matter. And I’m not the only one.”（ch32）

**Greg Kowalski**——

- 起点：I knew, because I was enduring that same pain. I’d been enduring it for five long months.（ch23）
- 转向：We were being friendly. We were being friends.（ch25）
- 落点：“What did her voice sound like?” I asked, since it seemed like the only way I could repay her.（ch27）
- 他做的事从头到尾只有一件：把一个老人的遗嘱问成了一封家书：“What would you say to her?” I asked, my next line just as clear as all the rest. “If she was with us right now?”（ch27）
