# 41. IX

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：12 月 8 日星期三凌晨两点，Brightwell Road 那栋房子的前门被「a tremendous hammering」惊醒上尉 Ambrose——这一次不是前夜那声「single, almost sardonic tap」，而是「a ferocious banging and clattering」，像是有人拼命要叫醒屋里沉睡的住客、而对对方不应越来越不耐烦。上尉发现床边的蜡烛居然还亮着（「how tired and distracted must he have been」），提烛上平台，发现 Fell 太太也提着烛站在那儿，两人都穿睡衣、眼惺忪不安；她说「I was beginning to wonder if it was all in my head.」再是一阵猛敲，上尉答「It's most certainly not, ma'am… You stay here now」，她仍跟了下来。走到门口时又是三下更轻的敲击——「as though whoever it was somehow knew that the captain and the lady were awake now」；上尉隔门喊「Who's there? Who knocks at our door at such an hour as this?」，Fell 太太提醒「The rules.」，上尉答「Forget the rules for a moment」，门外却传来「a voice… sounding faint and frail but highly recognisable」：「Open up, Captain Ambrose! I've been waiting out here for far too long.」那「high, rather querulous tone」不容错认——是 Ebenezer。上尉「Without thinking」伸手去取钩上的钥匙，Fell 太太抓住他手臂低声说「The first rule!」，上尉答「I would know that old man's voice anywhere… The poor man must be catching his death.」她话说了一半被打断：「Captain, I don't think you should.」但上尉已「put the key into the lock and turned it. ‘It will all be better,’ he said, ‘now that Ebenezer's here.’」门开处站着的确是 Ebenezer Scrooge，在冷风里发抖、恼怒于久候；上尉心中「no doubt at all」他是自己的老友与导师——「At least at first.」Fell 太太也暂时放下疑虑；然而门外的「东西」一声不吭，只是盯着他们，像「a figure in a painting come to life」，随后开始笑——不是善意的笑，也不是重逢的笑，而是「an awful sneering kind of grin, a peeling back of his lips to show the withered, yellowing teeth」。在两支闪烁的蜡烛照亮的黑暗里，Fell 太太挪到上尉身边；那「figure」开始前倾下蹲，「his posture becoming similar to that of a monkey」，作者随即改口：it（因为已经很难再称它为「他」）似乎正蓄势待扑；它蹲得很低，像动物蹲在后腿上——「a pose that would have been quite impossible for that elderly gentleman whose form this entity had adopted」。Fell 太太尖叫，那东西作势扑来；「Close the door!」已来不及，它「moving through the air with a speed that was quite impossible」「passed straight through them, like it was made of so much mist」，伴着疾风般的声响，两人跌倒在地，蜡烛熄灭。起身后再无踪迹，只有一股难闻气味停留片刻后散去。Fell 太太颤声说「I feel as if I've been the victim of some horrible joke…」，上尉答「if it is a joke, then it's not a very funny one」，他出门四顾无人，回来说「We can beat this… Whatever this thing is in here, we can beat it.」她问「Are you sure of that?」——话一出口才发现自己「realising too late that she had formed a question」。上方随即传来「a single, laconic knock」。上尉提议「let's see if we can stoke up the fire a bit」，两人到起居室守着一炉「slumbering but not extinct」的火，坐得「closer than convention would allow」，彼此无言，「an unspoken agreement that this was not the place or the time to discuss what they had seen」。尽管两人都强烈感到「the house seemed somehow to be watching them with mocking eyes」，他们最终在火前一起睡着，女子的头「resting delicately upon the gentleman's shoulder」——「Fear brings with it its own kind of exhaustion.」

**情感弧线位置**：本章弧线为「惊醒—互相确认（不是幻觉）—下楼的紧张—门口的犹豫—认出是 Ebenezer（安全感）—异变（笑容）—兽化（惊骇）—穿透（失控）—气味余烬（不安）—硬撑（We can beat this）—又一声敲击（回压）—守火无言—相拥而眠（耗尽）」。全章的张力曲线是**两次骤升**：认出旧友是一次急升，笑容与兽化是第二次急升并直接坠落到底。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现了「半夜敲门」与「假扮亲友的鬼怪」两个经典 trope。反转有三处：其一，Scrooge 确实是「要来的那个人」——他真的在门外，却不是读者以为的那个 Scrooge；其二，认出熟人（Ambrose 说「I would know that old man's voice anywhere」）反而是最危险的一步，因为声音的忠告正是诱惑的核心；其三，**Fell 太太试图守规矩，反而是上尉破坏规矩救下了门**——守规矩者差点被规则害死。

**人物弧线**：Fell 太太在本章完成从「笃信规矩」到「怀疑规矩」的第一步：她在门口仍喊「The rules」，却在事后说出「Are you sure of that?」并意识到自己「had formed a question」——她第一次触到第三条禁令的边。上尉则从守夜人变为行动者：他「Forget the rules for a moment」，他开门，他事后说「We can beat this」，他的保护欲在开场那句「You stay here now」就已成形。两人之间的情感也从客套转为身体接触：她靠上他的肩。

**叙事手法**：一是**时间与天气双压**：凌晨两点、十二月的冷风、「catching his death」——生理危险是开门的第一诱因。二是**称谓的偷换**：作者从「the old man」滑到「the thing」，再到明写「for one can hardly continue to refer to it as 'he' a moment longer」，最后用 it／this entity／the being——语言本身在被迫承认这不是人。三是**感官分层与感官缺失**：这一夜几乎没有视觉（只有两支蜡烛的黑暗），却充满听觉（hammering、knock、rushing wind、silence）与嗅觉（the smell, most unpleasant）——「Still no sound of any kind came from it, a fact which somehow made the whole performance still more awful」——安静的兽比吼叫的兽更可怕。四是**结尾的交叠**：「Fear brings with it its own kind of exhaustion」把恐惧写成体力消耗，从而合理化两人在鬼屋客厅里睡着。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** At two o’clock in the morning on Wednesday, eighth of December, Captain Ambrose was woken by the sound of a tremendous hammering upon the front door of the house on Brightwell Road. This was not the single, almost sardonic tap that he had heard the previous evening when he had stood alone in the hallway, but rather a ferocious banging and clattering. It was the kind of noise that one would make if you were trying your utmost to wake a pair of slumbering residents and becoming impatient with their failure to appear.

**中文理解**：12 月 8 日星期三凌晨两点，Ambrose 上尉被一阵砸在 Brightwell Road 那栋房子前门上的巨响震醒。这不是前一晚他独自站在门厅时听到的那一下「single, almost sardonic tap」，而是一阵凶猛的砸门与碰撞声。那声音像是有人拼尽全力想叫醒一对熟睡的住客、而对他们迟迟不应越来越不耐烦。

**关键词**：At two o’clock in the morning on Wednesday, eighth of December / a tremendous hammering upon the front door / not the single, almost sardonic tap / a ferocious banging and clattering / becoming impatient with their failure to appear

**为什么这样写**：作者用精确日期与时刻开篇，把这一夜钉进日历。而「single, almost sardonic tap」（几乎带着讥诮的一下轻敲）与现在的「ferocious banging and clattering」形成对照：前夜的敲是试探，今夜的敲是索取。sardonic 一词还提前泄露了来者的态度——带着嘲弄。

**读者视角提示**：读者已在前章见过那一声轻敲，现在它的回声变了。

> **原句 2:** Somewhat to his surprise, Ambrose saw that the candle beside his bed was still lit (how tired and distracted must he have been, he thought, to have neglected to extinguish it upon rolling over for sleep) and so he rose, took the candle and went out to the landing. Here, to his surprise but also to his considerable relief, he found Mrs Fell, who also held her candle out before her. Both were dressed in their nightgowns. Both were bleary-eyed and unsettled. “So you heard it too?” said the lady.

**中文理解**：令 Ambrose 有些意外的是，他看见床边的蜡烛居然还亮着（他心想，自己当时该是多疲倦、多心不在焉，才会在翻身睡去时忘了吹灭它），于是他起身，拿起蜡烛，走到楼梯平台。在那里，令他意外但也大大松一口气的是，他发现 Mrs Fell 也在——她同样把蜡烛举在身前。两人都穿着睡衣，两人都睡眼惺忪、神情不安。「你也听见了？」那位女士说。

**关键词**：the candle beside his bed was still lit / how tired and distracted must he have been / to neglect to extinguish it / to his considerable relief / Mrs Fell, who also held her candle out before her / Both were bleary-eyed and unsettled / So you heard it too?

**为什么这样写**：作者让两人的「对称」精确到细节：都提烛、都穿睡衣、都被吵醒——甚至连「蜡烛忘了吹」这种小纰漏都只有上尉一人有。作者先写上尉的疏忽（tired and distracted），再写他的如释重负（considerable relief）——因为有人作证，他不必怀疑自己的耳朵。「So you heard it too?」一句确立了这夜的性质：不是幻觉，是共同的遭遇。

**读者视角提示**：同病相怜的确认，是这一夜两个人第一次并肩。

> **原句 3:** “I was beginning to wonder if it was all in my head.” As she spoke, another ferocious knocking came at the front door, one floor beneath them. “It’s most certainly not, ma’am,” said Ambrose. “Someone is out there. And I wish to see who it is. You stay here now.” He walked away, candle aloft. He had reached only the top of the staircase when he heard the lady padding indignantly after him. “Certainly not,” she said. “I won’t be staying anywhere.” Down the stairs they nervously trod.

**中文理解**：「我正开始怀疑是不是我自己的幻觉。」她说话时，前门又传来一阵猛敲，就在他们楼下。「那绝对不是，夫人，」Ambrose 说，「外面有人。我倒要看看是谁。你待在这儿别动。」他举着烛走开。才走到楼梯顶，就听见那位女士气鼓鼓地跟了上来。「才不，」她说，「我哪儿也不待。」两人紧张地下了楼。

**关键词**：I was beginning to wonder if it was all in my head / another ferocious knocking came at the front door / Someone is out there / I wish to see who it is / You stay here now / the lady padding indignantly after him / I won’t be staying anywhere

**为什么这样写**：上尉的保护欲在这一句里成型：「You stay here now」是命令。而她的回应用「indignantly」（气恼地）跟随——作者用一个情绪副词把她从「被保护者」写成「不服从者」。这句俏皮的对抗让恐怖不至于压死整章，也预示了稍后她抓住他手臂阻止开门时的那份不肯退让。

**读者视角提示**：她不会听他的话，这一点马上要用上。

> **原句 4:** There were no further sounds, save for their own ragged breathing. Once they had reached the ground, and were stepping towards the door, there came again three further knocks, but quieter this time, as though whoever it was somehow knew that the captain and the lady were awake now and standing close by. Or perhaps as if they were growing tired. The captain stopped beside the door. “Who’s there?” he called out. “Who knocks at our door at such an hour as this?” “Captain!” said the lady. “The rules.”

**中文理解**：再没有别的声音，除了他们自己粗重的呼吸。两人走到楼下、正要走向门口时，又传来三下敲击，只是这次更轻了，仿佛敲门的人知道上尉与那位女士此刻已经醒着、就在近旁。又或者，仿佛对方已经累了。上尉在门边站住，喊道：「谁在那里？在这个钟点敲我们门的，是谁？」「上尉！」那位女士说，「规矩。」

**关键词**：no further sounds, save for their own ragged breathing / three further knocks, but quieter this time / as though whoever it was somehow knew / Or perhaps as if they were growing tired / Who knocks at our door at such an hour as this? / The rules.

**为什么这样写**：作者在紧张里塞进两次留白：「as though… knew」与「Or perhaps… growing tired」——读者必须自己判断对方是人还是别的东西。而那句「The rules.」只有两个词，是全章最短也最重的一句提醒；Fell 太太把「规矩」当作护身符递出去。

**读者视角提示**：Bethlem 的第一条规则在此第一次被执行。

> **原句 5:** “Forget the rules for a moment,” said Ambrose. “I want to see exactly who this is.” A voice came from the other side of the door then, sounding faint and frail but highly recognisable all the same. “Open up, Captain Ambrose! I’ve been waiting out here for far too long.” That high, rather querulous tone was unmistakeable. “Ebenezer?” said the captain. The voice came back. “Of course it’s Ebenezer! Now, in the name of mercy, please open this door.” Without thinking, the captain reached for the key on its hook beside the door.

**中文理解**：「先 forget 一会儿那些规矩，」Ambrose 说，「我要看清这到底是谁。」这时门那边传来一个声音，听起来虚弱而苍老，却 unmistakably 的熟悉。「开门，Ambrose 上尉！我在这儿等得太久了。」那尖高的、略带躁急的语调不可能认错。「Ebenezer？」上尉说。声音回来了。「当然是 Ebenezer！看在怜悯的份上，求你开门。」上尉没多想，伸手去取门边钩子上的钥匙。

**关键词**：Forget the rules for a moment / I want to see exactly who this is / a voice… sounding faint and frail but highly recognisable / Open up, Captain Ambrose / That high, rather querulous tone was unmistakeable / Without thinking, the captain reached for the key on its hook

**为什么这样写**：作者先给声音两个标签（faint and frail、high and querulous），再宣布它「highly recognisable」——辨认的依据全是声音，没有视觉。这正是陷阱所在：能骗过耳朵的东西骗不过眼睛，所以它只给声音。作者还用「Without thinking」给上尉的动作定性——那不是勇敢，是本能。

**读者视角提示**：全章的转折就在这只伸向钥匙的手上。

> **原句 6:** The lady grabbed at his arm. “Captain,” she hissed. “The first rule!” “I would know that old man’s voice anywhere,” he said. “Now, we really cannot leave him standing out there in the cold a moment longer. The poor man must be catching his death.” “I don’t…” the lady began, then broke off as a further double knock came at the door. “Captain, I don’t think you should.” Ambrose did not reply but put the key into the lock and turned it. “It will all be better,” he said, “now that Ebenezer’s here.”

**中文理解**：那位女士抓住了他的手臂。「上尉，」她压低声音说，「第一条规矩！」「那个老人的声音，我到哪儿都认得出来，」他说，「我们真不能再让他在外面冷风里多站一分钟。那可怜的人会冻出病来的。」「我不……」她刚开口，又一阵双响的敲门打断了她。「上尉，我觉得你不该开。」Ambrose 没有回答，把钥匙插进锁里转开了。「现在 Ebenezer 到了，一切都会好起来，」他说。

**关键词**：The lady grabbed at his arm / The first rule! / I would know that old man’s voice anywhere / The poor man must be catching his death / I don’t think you should / put the key into the lock and turned it / It will all be better, now that Ebenezer’s here

**为什么这样写**：作者把三样东西焊在一处：冷（真实的生理危险）、同情（poor man must be catching his death）、规则（she grabbed at his arm）。上尉的每一步都有正当理由，而正因如此才危险——这让开门不是疏忽，而是**被说服**。她的「我不……」被敲门声截断，作者用物理打断代替说服，因为她的理由（那不是他）无法说出口。

**读者视角提示**：这一段是全章的道德支点：规矩被一个善意的人打破了。

> **原句 7:** He unlocked the door. The lady shrank back. Ambrose pulled the door back to reveal, standing upon the threshold, none other than Ebenezer Scrooge. He was shivering in the cold and he looked profoundly irritated to have been kept waiting for so long. There was no doubt at all in the mind of Captain Ambrose that it was his old friend and mentor, no doubt at all. At least at first. “Mr Scrooge!” This was Mrs Fell now, her doubts temporarily cast aside. “It’s really you! Oh, thank goodness!” “Ebenezer!” Ambrose said.

**中文理解**：他开了锁。女士往后退。上尉拉开门，出现在门槛上的不是别人，正是 Ebenezer Scrooge。他在寒冷中瑟瑟发抖，脸上带着因久等而生的深深恼怒。Ambrose 上尉心里毫无疑问那是自己的老友与导师，毫无疑问。「至少一开始是这样。」「Scrooge 先生！」这时是 Fell 太太，她的疑虑暂时抛开了。「真是你！哦，谢天谢地！」「Ebenezer！」上尉说。

**关键词**：He unlocked the door / The lady shrank back / none other than Ebenezer Scrooge / shivering in the cold / profoundly irritated to have been kept waiting / no doubt at all… his old friend and mentor / At least at first

**为什么这样写**：作者用「none other than」制造确认感，又用「At least at first.」四字立刻撤销它——这是全章最漂亮的一次预告式背叛。而「profoundly irritated to have been kept waiting」把 Scrooge 的表情解释成「等久了不高兴」，是唯一合理的误读；读者此刻也这样误读。

**读者视角提示**：「At least at first」是作者给读者的预告。

> **原句 8:** “Whatever has taken you so long?” The warmth in both their voices soon ebbed away, however, as the thing on the doorstep said nothing at all in reply. The old man simply stood there, staring at them both, like a figure in a painting come to life. “Ebenezer?” said Ambrose again, more carefully this time. The old man did not speak but only began to smile. Not a kind smile nor one of happy reunion but an awful sneering kind of grin, a peeling back of his lips to show the withered, yellowing teeth beyond.

**中文理解**：「什么事耽搁了你这么久？」可是两人声音里的暖意很快就消退了——因为门口那东西一言不发。老者只是站在那里盯着他们俩，像一幅画里复活的人像。「Ebenezer？」上尉又问了一次，这一次更谨慎。老者没有说话，只是开始笑起来。不是善意的笑，也不是重逢的笑，而是一种可怕的、带着讥诮的咧嘴——把嘴唇向后扯开，露出后面那口枯萎发黄的牙。

**关键词**：the warmth in both their voices soon ebbed away / the thing on the doorstep said nothing at all in reply / like a figure in a painting come to life / did not speak but only began to smile / an awful sneering kind of grin / the withered, yellowing teeth

**为什么这样写**：作者先用「ebbed away」（退去）写温度的消失——热情像潮水一样退掉。然后用「like a figure in a painting come to life」把场景推向超自然：画像里的人物活了过来。笑容的三个否定（不是善意、不是重逢、而是讥诮的咧嘴）层层剥掉最后一点人味，而「peeling back of his lips」把笑写成一种解剖动作。

**读者视角提示**：此前它一直不说话；笑是第一个动作。

> **原句 9:** In a darkness that was broken only by the two flickering candles, Mrs Fell stole close to the captain’s side. “Ebenezer, please,” Ambrose said, though he had by then, of course, come to understand that whatever stood before him, it was no friend of his. With that hideous grin growing still wider and more horrible, the figure of Ebenezer Scrooge began to slump forward and to crouch, his posture becoming similar to that of a monkey. There was something mocking and dreadful in the sight.

**中文理解**：在只有两支闪烁蜡烛照亮的黑暗里，Fell 太太悄悄挪到上尉身边。「Ebenezer，求你了，」Ambrose 说——尽管到这时他当然已经明白，站在他面前的那个东西，绝不是他的朋友。随着那可怕的咧嘴越咧越大、越发可怖，Ebenezer Scrooge 的身形开始向前塌陷、蹲伏下来，姿态变得像一只猴子。那景象里有某种嘲弄而可怖的东西。

**关键词**：In a darkness that was broken only by the two flickering candles / Mrs Fell stole close to the captain’s side / whatever stood before him, it was no friend of his / began to slump forward and to crouch / his posture becoming similar to that of a monkey / something mocking and dreadful in the sight

**为什么这样写**：作者在此完成关键动作：先由上尉**理性地**承认「no friend of his」，再由形体**直观地**证明它——塌陷、蹲伏、猴相。视觉的怪诞与判断的清晰同步推进。而「steal close to the captain's side」用一个 steal（悄悄）写出她的移动是本能的、不愿被看见的。

**读者视角提示**：作者先说「它不是朋友」，再让你亲眼看见为什么。

> **原句 10:** It (for one can hardly continue to refer to it as ‘he’ a moment longer) seemed to be making ready to spring. Still no sound of any kind came from it, a fact which somehow made the whole performance still more awful and dream-like. It crouched low upon the ground, like an animal squatting upon its hind legs, a pose that would have been quite impossible for that elderly gentleman whose form this entity had adopted. At last, Mrs Fell screamed. The thing looked as though it was getting ready to spring towards them.

**中文理解**：它（因为此刻已经很难再继续称它为「他」）似乎正蓄势待扑。它没有发出任何一点声响——这个事实不知怎地使整场演出变得更加可怖、更加像一场梦。它低低地蹲在地上，像一只以后腿蹲坐的动物——那种姿势对那个被它借用了形体的老先生来说，是完全不可能的。终于，Mrs Fell 尖叫起来。那东西看上去已经准备好要扑向他们。

**关键词**：for one can hardly continue to refer to it as ‘he’ a moment longer / seemed to be making ready to spring / Still no sound of any kind came from it / more awful and dream-like / crouched low upon the ground, like an animal squatting upon its hind legs / quite impossible for that elderly gentleman / At last, Mrs Fell screamed

**为什么这样写**：作者公然宣告自己在改换称谓（it／this entity），读者被迫跟着换。而「Still no sound of any kind came from it」是关键的反恐怖写法：真正的兽会吼，沉默的兽才可怕——作者明说这一点。随后一句「that elderly gentleman whose form this entity had adopted」把这个身体正式降格为一套被穿上的衣服。

**读者视角提示**：叙述者在此放弃了「他」这个称呼。

> **原句 11:** “Close the door! Close the door!” the lady shouted. But it was too late. The thing that wore the form of Ebenezer Scrooge sprang towards them both, moving through the air with a speed that was quite impossible. Both Ambrose and Mrs Fell stumbled backwards, acting on pure instinct, but the thing somehow passed straight through them, like it was made of so much mist. As this occurred, there came a sound as of rushing wind, and the lady and the gentlemen fell together upon the ground. Their candles were extinguished.

**中文理解**：「关门！关门！」那位女士大喊。但已经太迟。那个顶着 Ebenezer Scrooge 外形的东西朝他们两人扑来，以一种完全不可能的速度穿行在空气中。Ambrose 与 Mrs Fell 都纯凭本能踉跄后退，可那东西竟径直穿过了他们俩，仿佛它只是由一片雾气构成。就在这一瞬，传来一阵如疾风掠过般的声响，两人都倒在地上。他们的蜡烛熄灭了。

**关键词**：Close the door! Close the door! / it was too late / sprang towards them both / with a speed that was quite impossible / stumbled backwards, acting on pure instinct / passed straight through them, like it was made of so much mist / a sound as of rushing wind / Their candles were extinguished

**为什么这样写**：作者用三连的「Close the door!」写出她的失控，又立刻以「it was too late」掐断希望。而「passed straight through them, like it was made of so much mist」是全章唯一说明这物本质的句子：它不是实体，所以抓不住、挡不住。疾风般的声响与蜡烛熄灭构成一个完整的自然现象：一阵风穿过房间，风灭了两支烛。

**读者视角提示**：它穿过了他们——这是本夜最关键的事实。

> **原句 12:** They rose uncertainly to their feet. Of the being – the gaseous being? – there was now no sign at all. Though there was a smell, most unpleasant, which lingered for a few moments before dissipating. Mrs Fell looked at the captain, and he looked at her. “I feel,” she began, speaking as steadily as she was able, “I feel as if I’ve been the victim of some horrible joke…” The captain did not reply but only held her tightly. She permitted this for a moment, then stepped away from him.

**中文理解**：他们uncertainly地站起身来。那个东西——那个气态的东西？——已经全无踪影。只留下一股极其难闻的气味，停留了片刻才渐渐散去。Mrs Fell 看着上尉，上尉也看着她。「我觉得，」她开口，努力把声音说得平稳，「我觉得自己像是某个可怕玩笑的受害者……」上尉没有回答，只是紧紧抱住她。她允许他抱了一会儿，然后从他身边退开。

**关键词**：They rose uncertainly to their feet / the gaseous being? / there was now no sign at all / a smell, most unpleasant, which lingered for a few moments / speaking as steadily as she was able / the victim of some horrible joke / only held her tightly

**为什么这样写**：作者的「gaseous being?」带一个问号，是罕见的犹疑——连叙述者也不确定该如何分类这个东西。而「a horrible joke」这个比喻轻得可怕，把一场鬼物袭击说成玩笑；读者随即意识到这个比喻有多不合适。而她的「speaking as steadily as she was able」写出镇定是硬撑出来的。

**读者视角提示**：唯一留下的是气味——它比形状更持久。

> **原句 13:** “If it is a joke,” he said, “then it’s not a very funny one.” He stepped outside, looked up and down the darkened street. Seeing no one, he came back in and closed the door again. The smell was dissipating. “We can beat this,” he said firmly. “Whatever this thing is in here, we can beat it.” “Are you sure of that?” asked the lady, realising too late that she had formed a question. From somewhere up above them, there came a single, laconic knock. She looked again at the captain and he at her.

**中文理解**：「如果这是个玩笑，」他说，「那可不是什么好笑的玩笑。」他走到外面，向上下打量漆黑的街道。看不到人，他便回来又关上门。气味正在散去。「我们能赢过它，」他坚决地说，「不管这屋里那东西是什么，我们能赢过它。」「你确定吗？」那位女士问道——话一出口才意识到自己问了问题。从他们上方某处，传来一下简短而冷淡的敲击。她又看向上尉，他也看向她。

**关键词**：if it is a joke, then it’s not a very funny one / looked up and down the darkened street / We can beat this / Whatever this thing is in here, we can beat it / Are you sure of that? / realising too late that she had formed a question / a single, laconic knock

**为什么这样写**：上尉用「We can beat this」硬撑——但他用的还是「beat」，像在对付一个对手，而不是驱散一团雾。而作者让 Fell 太太在**说出问题的同一刻**才意识到自己犯规：第三条规矩（不许提问）在此完成它的最终一击。而「a single, laconic knock」（一下简短冷淡的敲击）从上方传来，与第 39 章前夜那一声遥相呼应——这栋房子仍在按自己的节奏敲门。

**读者视角提示**：那声敲击来自楼上——顶层。

> **原句 14:** “Come,” he said, “let’s see if we can stoke up the fire a bit for a while.” And so they went to the drawing room and sat by the fire, which was slumbering but not extinct, and they found a little warmth there. They sat together, closer than convention would allow, and each said nothing at all to the other. There was between them a kind of unspoken agreement that this was not the place or the time to discuss what they had seen or to speak aloud the worst of their suspicions.

**中文理解**：「来，」他说，「我们看看能不能把火再拨旺些。」于是两人去了起居室，坐在那炉沉睡着却尚未熄灭的火边，在那里找到一点微暖。他们坐在一起，比礼节所允许的更近，彼此一句话也没说。他们之间有一种不言自明的默契：这里不是讨论所见所闻的场合，也不是把最坏的猜疑说出口的时候。

**关键词**：let’s see if we can stoke up the fire a bit for a while / sat by the fire, which was slumbering but not extinct / they found a little warmth there / closer than convention would allow / each said nothing at all to the other / an unspoken agreement / not the place or the time to discuss what they had seen

**为什么这样写**：作者让「拨旺火」成为一个具体动作——火是这屋里唯一能对抗那东西的东西（白天有暖意，夜里守火），而「slumbering but not extinct」（沉睡却未灭）是全章最像 Scrooge 的一个比喻：读者想起第 38 章那本被打烂的圣经、想起他说「Without this he would surely be dead」。火未灭＝命未绝。

**读者视角提示**：火未熄，是这一夜唯一的安慰。

> **原句 15:** In spite of what they had witnessed, and in spite of the strong sensation (they both felt it) that the house seemed somehow to be watching them with mocking eyes, in the end they fell asleep together before the fire, the lady’s head resting delicately upon the gentleman’s shoulder. Fear brings with it its own kind of exhaustion. And so, in this manner, was that long night passed.

**中文理解**：尽管他们目睹了这一切，尽管两人都有一种强烈的感觉——这栋房子似乎正以嘲弄的目光监视着他们——最终他们还是在火前一起睡着了，女子的头「delicately」地搁在绅士的肩上。恐惧自带一种属于它自己的疲惫。于是，那个漫长的夜晚，就以这样的方式过去了。

**关键词**：the house seemed somehow to be watching them with mocking eyes / they fell asleep together before the fire / the lady’s head resting delicately upon the gentleman’s shoulder / Fear brings with it its own kind of exhaustion / that long night passed

**为什么这样写**：作者用一句插入语把最坏的猜测按住不说——「mocking eyes」承接着第 39 章「the house… was curious about him」，房子从好奇升级为嘲弄。而「Fear brings with it its own kind of exhaustion」是全章的收束语，也是作者给出的最冷的人情观察：恐惧会累死人，所以两个被吓的人可以在鬼屋里睡着。末句「that long night passed」平淡得可怕。

**读者视角提示**：这一夜的结束毫无戏剧性，只有疲惫。

## 一句话总结

12 月 8 日星期三凌晨两点，Brightwell Road 那栋房子的前门被「a tremendous hammering」惊醒上尉——不是前夜那声「single, almost sardonic tap」，而是「a ferocious banging and clattering」；上尉发现「the candle beside his bed was still lit」（自己该是多 tired and distracted），提烛上平台，见 Fell 太太也提着烛「Both were bleary-eyed and unsettled」，她问「So you heard it too?… I was beginning to wonder if it was all in my head.」；上尉答「Someone is out there… You stay here now」，她仍「padding indignantly after him」跟了下来；到门口又有三下更轻的敲击，「as though whoever it was somehow knew」，上尉喊「Who knocks at our door at such an hour as this?」，她提醒「The rules.」，他答「Forget the rules for a moment」；门外传来「a voice… faint and frail but highly recognisable」：「Open up, Captain Ambrose! I’ve been waiting out here for far too long.」那「high, rather querulous tone was unmistakeable」——是 Ebenezer；上尉「Without thinking」伸手取钥匙，她「grabbed at his arm」低声说「The first rule!」，他答「I would know that old man’s voice anywhere… The poor man must be catching his death」，她话说一半被敲门打断：「Captain, I don’t think you should.」他仍「put the key into the lock and turned it」「It will all be better」「now that Ebenezer’s here」；门开处确是 Ebenezer Scrooge，在冷风里发抖、恼怒久候，上尉心中「no doubt at all」他是老友与导师——「At least at first.」；然而那东西一声不吭，「like a figure in a painting come to life」，继而「began to smile」——不是善意的笑、不是重逢的笑，而是「an awful sneering kind of grin, a peeling back of his lips to show the withered, yellowing teeth」；它「began to slump forward and to crouch, his posture becoming similar to that of a monkey」，作者改口称 it：「for one can hardly continue to refer to it as 'he' a moment longer」，它「seemed to be making ready to spring」「Still no sound of any kind came from it」；Fell 太太尖叫，「Close the door!」已迟，它「sprang towards them both, moving through the air with a speed that was quite impossible」「passed straight through them, like it was made of so much mist」，伴「a sound as of rushing wind」，两人倒地、蜡烛熄灭；再无踪迹，只留「a smell, most unpleasant」片刻散去；她颤声说「I feel as if I’ve been the victim of some horrible joke…」，他答「If it is a joke」「then it’s not a very funny one」「We can beat this」，她问「Are you sure of that?」——「realising too late that she had formed a question」；上方随即传来「a single, laconic knock」；两人到起居室守着「the fire, which was slumbering but not extinct」，坐得「closer than convention would allow」，默默守着「an unspoken agreement」不谈所见；尽管「the house seemed somehow to be watching them with mocking eyes」，他们仍在火前一起睡着，「the lady’s head resting delicately upon the gentleman’s shoulder」——「Fear brings with it its own kind of exhaustion.」
