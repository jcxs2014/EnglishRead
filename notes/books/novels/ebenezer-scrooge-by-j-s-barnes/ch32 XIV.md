# 32. XIV

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：While, in London, onlookers of every kind clustered around the failing form of Mr Scrooge, and while, upon the train, Captain Ambrose and Mrs Fell found themselves mired once again in anxious silence, in the little town of Starningham, certain preparations were being made. At the end of Brightwell Road, close by the sea, stood the house. There were no lights within it now, nor was there any sign of activity or life. Until, that is, the front door opened and a stout, neatly dressed woman in her middle years stepped out. Oh but she was all bustle and importance, this lady! Her every movement was suggestive of urgent responsibility. She had so much to do and so many demands upon her time and she seemed to wish that any who were sufficiently fortunate as to cross her path should know these things on first acquaintance. She slammed the door behind her, brought out a ring of keys and – noisily, busily – found the correct one, placed it in the keyhole and locked the door. She stepped back then. First one step, then two. She stopped short and looked up at the house. A wise observer might have noticed how theatrical was this performance, as though it were intended to distract herself from some other, deeper emotion that had beset her within the house. The woman looked up at the uppermost floor of the place and at its dark and narrow windows. Was there a kind of fluttering to be glimpsed behind the glass? The barest indication of movement? The woman looked away and told herself she was mistaken in what she thought she had seen. “Be good, won’t you?” she said softly, presumably to the house itself since the street was otherwise deserted. “You’ll be kind this time? Please do.” She looked up again at the windows, dark and baleful. If she was expecting a reply of any kind, none came. Then she turned and hurried off, back up the street and away from the house. For she had an appointment to keep and a lady to meet, and a terrible duty to perform. After she had left and the street was empty again, a single black shape moved very quickly behind the left-hand window of the top floor. Then, after that, there was nothing but the sound of the sea. The house on Brightwell Road was once again still and stern and waiting.

## 一句话总结

第三十二章（Stave Three XIV）：While, in London, onlookers of every kind clustered around the failing form of Mr Scrooge, and while, upon the train, Captain Ambrose and Mrs Fell found themselves mired once again in anxious silence, in the little town of Starningham, certain preparations were being made. At the end of Brightwell Road, close by the sea, stood the house. There were no lights within it now, nor was there any sign of activity or life. Until, that is, the front door opened and a stout, neatly dressed woman in her middle years stepped out. Oh but she was all bustle and importance, this lady! Her every movement was suggestive of urgent responsibility. She had so much to do and so many demands upon her time and she seemed to wish that any who were sufficiently fortunate as to cross her path should know these things on first acquaintance. She slammed the door behind her, brought out a ring of keys and – noisily, busily – found the correct one, placed it in the keyhole and locked the door. She stepped back then. First one step, then two. She stopped short and looked up at the house. A wise observer might have noticed how theatrical was this performance, as though it were intended to distract herself from some other, deeper emotion that had beset her within the house. The woman looked up at the uppermost floor of the place and at its dark and narrow windows. Was there a kind of fluttering to be glimpsed behind the glass? The barest indication of movement? The woman looked away and told herself she was mistaken in what she thought she had seen. “Be good, won’t you?” she said softly, presumably to the house itself since the street was otherwise deserted. “You’ll be kind this time? Please do.” She looked up again at the windows, dark and baleful. If she was expecting a reply of any kind, none came. Then she turned and hurried off, back up the street and away from the house. For she had an appointment to keep and a lady to meet, and a terrible duty to perform. After she had left and the street was empty again, a single black shape moved very quickly behind the left-hand window of the top floor. Then, after that, there was nothing but the sound of the sea.
