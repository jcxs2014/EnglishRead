# 09. IX

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：Fell 太太开始讲述 1832 年那次探访：八岁的她随父赴 Norfolk 的 Starningham，住的正是死胡同尽头那栋 25 号；第一夜床铺上凭空出现秋叶，第二夜父亲与叔叔在楼下争吵时有个陌生女声连唤她两声名字，第三夜她踢到被褥下一个温热而活、像一团流动黑墨的东西；天亮她逃离，Scrooge 随即摆出三条查证的路——今晚见 Beck、查律师 Cadge、亲自去 Starningham；她拒绝留在俱乐部，坚持同赴 séance。

**情感弧线位置**：本章是全书最长、也是唯一一次大幅回溯的一章，曲线呈「日常—渐暗—坠入深渊—急刹—理性收拾」。前段是童年的暖色（与父亲单独相处的两天旅程）；中段随着她走近 Starningham 而降温，父亲"似乎陷入秘密与沉默"；进屋后是纯粹的感官恐怖（utter… wrongness、不受欢迎、被不友善的智慧审视）；第三夜达到全书最惊悚的顶点；结尾急转冷峻——Scrooge 说她别无选择，除非去领救济院那"靠不住的怜悯"，然后三人像布置一场战役似的分头行动。末句"两位先生终究都不敢对 Louisa Fell 说不"是全章唯一一处胜利感的出口。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现前几章铺垫的"被访客"与"常驻者"之争——本章证明 1832 年那个孩子已被这栋房子盯上，而非单纯路过。三个反转：其一，童年异象都有物理解释的余地（叶子、声响、风），Ambrose 明确提出理性主义解释却被她礼貌驳回；其二，最恐怖的其实是父亲与叔叔的争吵和那个陌生女声——真正的鬼不是黑墨，而是"家里有人在隐瞒什么"；其三，Scrooge 听完最骇人的证词后不慌不忙地谈遗产条款，把灵异案件变成一桩可执行的商业侦查。

**人物弧线**：Fell 太太在本章完成从"求助的寡妇"到"证词的来源与主导者"的转变：她先说"我那时太小"，随即用"I shall omit no detail"把叙述权抓回自己手里，被追问时仍能给出精确到地址与门牌的回答。她的勇敢有代价——她明说不愿回那栋房子、尤其不愿带着新生儿住进去，却仍选择同行。Scrooge 显出罕见的急迫与进攻性：手指敲击扶手、宣布三条路线、立刻派人出击；Ambrose 扮演全书固定角色——唱反调、提合理化解释、承担跑腿。

**叙事手法**：回溯体（framed narrative）套在质询体（interrogation）里：整章是 Scrooge 用"Go on"和精确追问一步步逼出的证词，作者不断用舞台提示打断——"She took a careful sip of her wine. Outside, the rain grew louder."、"The rain drummed harder at the windowpane, but otherwise the library lay in absolute silence."雨声与静默交替充当节拍器。文体上大量使用短促的否定式问答（"No, sir. There were none."）、孩子视角的具象比喻（"a moving mound of black ink – or a swarm of insects"），并在两处用未加解释的诡异细节（银镜、门厅的 wrongness）替代解释。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** The year,” said the lady, “was 1832. The month was September. I was but eight years old. And it was decided by my father that we should visit my mother’s brother in the town of Starningham in the county of Norfolk. For reasons that I never understood, my mother did not accompany us upon this considerable journey, nor did any of my siblings. Instead, it was only my father and I who made the trip, which is in excess of a hundred miles. It took us two full days to reach the city of Norwich, travelling by stagecoach, and staying in inns along the way. We were weary and bewildered by the trip, though I remember also enjoying being by my father’s side for so long, to be his sole company and to have his whole attention. No doubt you will think that selfish of me.” At this, there came assurances from both men that nothing could be further from the truth, that the lady should not say such things of her younger self and that they were both most pleased that there was at least one happy memory to have been gleaned from that weird visit. “Thank you, gentlemen,” the lady went on. “But I should say that any incidental joy I might have felt on that adventure of ours melted away as soon as we drew near to our destination. At Norwich we had engaged a gig to take us on to Starningham, a distance of some twenty further miles. The day had already been a long one. It was growing dark. And my father, who had spent much of our trip being cheerful and in good spirits, seemed to sink into secrecy and silence. Those small games and jokes which had lightened our hours of travelling now raised between us nothing more than the most fleeting and temporary of smiles.” Mr Scrooge broke into the narrative. “Had he not given you any indication of why you were making this journey? Was there, would you say, a sense of urgency and of mission?” “There was most certainly that,” said Mrs Fell, “but no true motive for the trip was ever offered to me, at least nothing beyond vague talk of it being in some fashion both long overdue and the right and proper thing for some representative of the family to do.”

**中文理解**：女士说：那是 1832 年，9 月，她才八岁；父亲决定带她去看母亲在 Norfolk 郡 Starningham 镇的兄弟。母亲没有同行，兄弟姐妹也没有，只有父女二人，行程一百多英里，驿马车走了整整两天，沿途住客栈。他们疲惫而茫然，她也记得能这样长时间独占父亲的陪伴而高兴，还自嘲说"你们大概会觉得我很自私"。两位先生连连保证绝无此心，说她不该这样说从前的自己，并都为至少还留下一点快乐记忆而高兴。她说，那点小小的快乐在接近目的地时就融化了：在 Norwich 雇了马车再走二十英里，天色已晚，一路说笑的父亲却陷入沉默与隐秘，旅途中的游戏如今只换来最短暂的笑。Scrooge 打断她，问父亲是否给过此行的理由、是否有某种紧迫感与使命感。女士答：确实有那种感觉，但从未有人告诉她真正的动机，只说是"早就该办、也是家里该有人办"的含糊说法。

**关键词**：in excess of a hundred miles（超出一百英里）/ stagecoach（驿马车）/ sole company（唯一的伴）/ a sense of urgency（某种紧迫感）/ no true motive（真正的动机）/ long overdue（早该办的）

**为什么这样写**：作者用倒叙里的第一句就把时间坐标钉死："The year… was 1832. The month was September. I was but eight years old."三个短句像三记敲击，把读者从雨夜的书房一下推到六十余年前的童年。紧接着"由父亲决定"让整趟行程带上被迫感——不是她想去，是被安排；"母亲没有同行……兄弟姐妹也没有"这一串排比让"只有父女二人"显得既是恩赐也是异样：一个八岁女孩被单独带离家，去看一个几乎从未谋面的舅舅。随后作者补一笔自私的自觉："No doubt you will think that selfish of me."——她先替读者定罪，两位先生随即出面否认，读者就被拉进了辩护的阵营，这个小小的攻防让后面恐怖的开场变得合情合理。最见功力的是气氛的转折："任何偶然的快乐……在接近目的地时就融化了"，紧跟着"父亲……似乎陷入秘密与沉默"——孩子不懂发生了什么，只知道父亲的玩笑停了；这一笔让"父亲知道什么"成为悬念，比直接写家里有鬼更有效。Scrooge 的插话也做得漂亮：他抓的是"紧迫感与使命感"这个细节，并立刻得到一个耐人寻味的回答——"确实有那种感觉，但真正的动机从未给过我"。理由有，动机没有；作者用这句区分，把一个家庭秘密的形状精确地描了出来。

**读者视角提示**：请记住这支旅程的规格：1832 年 9 月、她八岁、一百多英里、两天的驿马车、只与父亲同行。母亲与兄弟姐妹不同行这一设定，后面会显出深意。

> **原句 2:** Scrooge nodded his thanks. “Go on,” he said. “Tell us of the house.” “Starningham is not a large place, gentlemen. It is somewhere between a large village and a small town. Its church is quite considerable, even by the standards of the county.” Mr Scrooge seemed intrigued by this detail. “Indeed?” he murmured. “Brightwell Road itself is an odd little cul-de-sac, all but in the shadow of the church itself. It is a tapering alleyway which can only be reached by way of the street beside the church. The sea lies beyond it. It’s a row of terraces, cramped at first, becoming grander as one processes along them. Yet the last house – number twenty-five – is set apart. It is the last house on the street, and it is of quite different character from the rest. Three storeys high, built of pale stone and wearing, if a house can be said to do so, a doleful expression. A great wall lies beyond it, and few venture down so far. Our gig would not take us there, so we had no choice but to walk the full length of Brightwell Road with our luggage in our hands. As we approached, my uncle stepped out to greet us. He was a tall, lean man who, upon seeing us, smiled like a well-fed cat. I can see him now, in my mind’s eye, the scene just as precise as a painting.” “Curious, is it not,” said Ebenezer Scrooge, with the soothing, coaxing manner of a man who is accustomed to drawing from his guests the very strangest and most intimate of stories, “what the mind recalls with clarity and what it is happy to disregard? There are for all of us, I think, moments – just as you have described – which linger in the imagination for years after the event, as crisp and clear as a portrait.” “Quite so,” agreed the lady, “and that image of my uncle on the threshold will persist in my mind for many years to come, I have no doubt – as will… certain other moments from that visit.”

**中文理解**：Scrooge 点头道谢，让她讲那栋房子。她说 Starningham 不大，介于大村与小镇之间，教堂即便按本郡标准也算相当可观；Scrooge 对此似乎颇感兴趣。接着她描述那条街：Brightwell Road 是一条奇怪的小死胡同，几乎笼罩在教堂阴影里；一条渐窄的小巷，只能从教堂旁的街进入；海就在它背后；路旁是一排排联排住宅，起初逼仄，越走越阔；可最后一栋——25 号——单独隔开，是全街尽头，与其余房子性格迥异：三层楼，浅色石头砌成，仿佛房子也能戴上哀容；它后面是一道高墙，很少有人走到那么远。马车进不去，他们只得提着行李走完整条街。走近时叔叔出来迎接：瘦高的一个男人，见到他们便"像一只喂饱的猫"笑起来。她说这幅画面她会记一辈子。Scrooge 问道：为何人的心记得住某些时刻、忽略另一些时刻，倒是有趣；这些时刻会在想象里留存多年，清晰如肖像。女士同意，并说记忆中还会留下"某些别的时刻"。

**关键词**：an odd little cul-de-sac（奇怪的小死胡同）/ a tapering alleyway（渐窄的小巷）/ a row of terraces（联排住宅）/ set apart（单独隔开）/ doleful expression（哀容）/ smiled like a well-fed cat（像喂饱的猫般笑）/ as crisp and clear as a portrait（清晰如肖像）

**为什么这样写**：作者先用地名的大小落地，再让 Scrooge 对教堂"似乎很感兴趣"一句把读者的注意力提前支到教堂上——教堂、街道、海、25 号，这几样地理要素在后面都会回收。此段的描写手法是"由窄到阔再突变"：一条渐窄的小巷只容一条路进入，两侧是一排排联排住宅，起初逼仄，越走越阔——像一条逐渐张开的漏斗，把人往尽头引；然后 25 号突然被单独隔开、"与其余房子性格迥异"，读者的期待被硬生生折断。那个拟人化的细节是全段最锋利的一笔："仿佛房子也能戴上哀容"——作者自己加了条件从句，像在提醒读者别当真，可正是这半句让步让整句更可信；一座"悲相"的房子，紧接着后面就是墙外少有人去。然后是叔叔出场："像一只喂饱的猫笑起来"——这个比喻同时含有餍足与危险，猫是捕食者，且"喂饱"暗示他并不饿。Scrooge 的插话是全段的题眼："心记得清楚什么、又是乐于忽略什么"；他用"清晰如肖像"把这幅画面说成一幅肖像，画得越清晰，读者越觉得叔叔在那一刻已经知道将要发生什么。

**读者视角提示**：街道的地理要记牢：Brightwell Road 是死胡同、只能从教堂旁的街进入、海在尽头、25 号单独隔开、后面是高墙；教堂虽只有一句提及，却是 Scrooge 唯一"感兴趣"的细节，请留意它在后文的作用。

> **原句 3:** The lady: “I remember my uncle helping us up the steps and taking our cases, and I can remember him leading us inside, apparently as agreeable and as welcoming a host as one could wish for. He was all smiles and gaiety and was all for asking us about our journey and thanking us for making so onerous a trip. There were undercurrents, I have no doubt, in the conversation between the two men, though I was too young to discern them. I can remember no specific words that were exchanged. Instead, I remember… something else… A thing that cannot easily be described.” “And what was that?” Scrooge asked gently. “It was a sensation, Mr Scrooge. A feeling which afflicted me from the moment I set foot into the house on Brightwell Road. The building, as I say, was unremarkable enough, if a little forlorn in aspect. Though nothing in the appearance of its exterior could have prepared me for what I felt when I stepped inside for the first time, into the long, narrow hallway which awaited us.” “And what feeling was that?” “Mr Scrooge, it was a feeling of utter… wrongness.” The old man peered at her with a great seriousness of purpose in his gaze. “Can you tell us just a little more?” “No doubt you will think that what I am describing are the workings of a young girl’s imagination – and that I have permitted what I experienced subsequently in that house to colour my first impressions. But I am certain that I felt in that hallway a strong sense of being profoundly unwelcome. A feeling of being scrutinised also, and by no friendly intelligence. It was so powerful and so intense that I gasped aloud at the force of it. My father noticed and patted my head absently, no doubt thinking that I was merely tired from the miles we had travelled.” “I see,” said Mr Scrooge and there was nothing in his tone to indicate whether he believed or disbelieved. “Go on.” “Well, we went in after that and my uncle showed us something of the house. That feeling of being unwanted did not dissipate entirely, but it did decrease a little as Uncle showed us around the rooms of the place, each of which was stark and dim and almost empty.” “How many years had your uncle lived there?” asked Captain Ambrose. “By then, I believe, for almost twenty.” “Yet the house was almost empty?” The lady gave the officer a weary smile. “Even as a child it struck me as odd and unusual.” The captain, unable to entirely hold back from providing the rationalist’s view (this having long been his role in his work with Mr Scrooge), remarked that empty (or almost-empty) houses created many odd noises and echoes, and that old houses too, especially those that were likely to be damp and cold, were bound to have an inhospitable air. Courteously, the lady agreed that this was assuredly so, but that such facts by themselves could scarcely account for all that befell her in Starningham.

**中文理解**：她记得叔叔帮他们上台阶、提箱子，领他们进门，像最和气、最欢迎主人的主人那样：满脸笑容、兴致极佳，不停问他们旅途如何、感谢他们跑这么远的路。她那时太小，辨不出两位大人对话里的暗流；记不住任何具体的字句，只记得"别的东西"——一件难以描述的事。Scrooge 温和地问是什么。她说那是一种感觉：从踏进那栋房子的瞬间就被它折磨；房子外观平平无奇，只是略显凄清，可外观再怎样也准备不了她第一次踏进那条狭窄长门厅时的感受。那感受是"全然的……不对劲"。老人以极大的认真注视着她，请她多说一点。她先做了预防：你们大概会以为这是少女想象力的产物、是我后来的经历影响了最初的印象；但她确信在门厅里就有强烈的"深深不被欢迎"之感，还有被审视之感，而且不是被什么友善的智慧所审视；那力量强大到让她大声喘气。父亲只当她走累了，心不在焉地拍拍她的头。Scrooge 听不出信还是不信，只说继续。她说那种"不被需要"的感觉没有完全消散，但在叔叔带他们看各房间时淡了一些；每个房间都空旷、昏暗、几乎空无一物。上尉问叔叔在那儿住了多久，她说那时大概已近二十年；又问房子几乎是空的？她笑着说即使作为孩子也觉得古怪异常。上尉提出理性主义解释：空房子会制造怪声与回响，老房子潮湿阴冷，自然带着不宜居的气息。她礼貌地承认这当然没错，但仅凭这些事实，绝不足以解释她在 Starningham 经历的一切。

**关键词**：undercurrents（暗流）/ wrongness（全然的不对劲）/ profoundly unwelcome（深深不被欢迎）/ by no friendly intelligence（不是友善的智慧）/ rationalist（理性主义者）/ inhospitable air（不宜居的气息）/ could scarcely account for（不足以解释）

**为什么这样写**：这一段把恐怖全部交给身体感受，不交给任何鬼影。作者先写叔叔的表面："满脸笑容、兴致极佳"、不停问旅途、感谢他们跑这么远的路——一个无可挑剔的主人；紧接"有暗流……但我太小辨不出"，孩子视角的好处正在这里：她只能报告"气氛不对"，说不出内容，而读者会自己补上可怕的内容。最妙的是"Instead, I remember… something else… A thing that cannot easily be described."——"something else" 后面的省略号把语言本身写成了失效，记忆绕过词语直接保存感觉。紧接着"It was a sensation"与"a feeling of utter… wrongness"两级递进：先说这是"感觉"，再说它的名字；那个"utter… wrongness"的省略号是关键，它表示连这个词都不够用。门厅细节被作者安排得极为克制：不是"有东西在看我"，而是"被审视的感觉，而且不是被任何友善的智慧所审视"——把"智识"与"友善"拆开否定，是全书最冷的一句恐怖描写。父亲"心不在焉地拍拍她的头"是这段最痛的一击：成人的迟钝与孩子的恐惧被一句动作写尽。末尾她礼貌地驳回理性解释——"仅凭这些事实绝不足以解释"，"单凭它们"是留给后文"另有原因"的接口。

**读者视角提示**：叔叔的形象关键词是"像喂饱的猫"与"哀容的房子"。八岁女孩的门厅感受（utter… wrongness、profoundly unwelcome、by no friendly intelligence）是全书最早的一次"被注视"，与第八章"常驻的鬼"形成对照。

> **原句 4:** Honour thereby being satisfied in a story of this kind, the lady continued. “I should now be precise about what I saw there. There were three incidents, gentlemen. The first of them took place on our first night there. I recall my uncle preparing for us a rough meal. There never appeared to be domestic staff of any sort. I was given the room upon the uppermost floor of the house. It was a large space, but it was not filled with much in the way of furniture. A narrow bed had been placed there. The room was empty except for a large, ornate silver mirror which had been set above the fireplace on the wall. In spite of the oddness of that place – which felt not in the least like a bedroom of any kind – I fell almost at once into a deep sleep, lulled, I expect, by the sound of the sea.” She took a careful sip of her wine. Outside, the rain grew louder. “And then I was woken in the reaches of the night by a most curious phenomenon.” The two gentlemen in the room did not prompt the lady any further but only listened to the rain and waited. “I felt,” she said, “something touching my face, brushing, very gently against the skin. Something neither hard nor soft but rather, somehow… brittle. It happened once, twice, three times. I expect I stirred in my sleep and assumed that I was dreaming. Yet what was happening to me did not stop but somehow only grew in intensity, until I had no choice but to wake and to open my eyes and to see that the bed and all around me was covered with…” She stopped speaking, then sighed and looked at the two men. “Covered with leaves. Autumn leaves, of dark yellow and brown.” Scrooge did not seem surprised. “I have heard of such things before. Objects appearing without warning in a place they have no business being. It is a trick beloved of the poltergeist class.” He glanced towards his companion. “Captain?” “Were there any trees in the vicinity?” he asked, in a kindly tone. “No, sir. There were none. Nor – before you ask – were there any gaps or holes in the structure of the building which might have allowed the leaves into my room.” “What did you do when you saw them?” Ambrose asked. “I… Well, Captain, I went back to sleep. You must understand that I was very young and very tired. And also that there was nothing menacing in what happened, not then at least. **中文理解**：她喝干了杯中剩下的酒。她说，两位先生现在大概明白她为什么最不想再回到那栋房子，更不用说带着刚出生的孩子住进 Brightwell Road 的尽头。上尉以虚张的同情口吻说：这对任何女士来说都实在令人不快。Scrooge 仔细看着她，脸上有计算的神色：可事情开始显得，她除了接受遗嘱的条件、把 Starningham 当作自己的家之外，已经没有别的选择了。她说：正是这样，先生，是的。老人说：除非你打算把自己扔到救济院那靠不住的怜悯上去。她说：我也是这么说的。他说：你的故事里有许多地方实在耐人寻味，尤其是你叔叔那份遗嘱的事实在我看来尤其古怪……不过我真诚地希望，夫人，即使考虑到你告诉我们的一切之后，我们仍能帮上忙。他左手枯瘦的手指在椅子扶手上敲着，说从这边他看出在不得不做出那个可怕选择之前有三条进攻路线：他今晚稍后要去拜访 Beck 先生，看看他知道那地方的什么；另外，Fell 太太，你还记得把这份奇怪遗嘱交给你的那位律师的名字吗？如果不记得，很容易查到。她说当然记得：Walter Cadge，Cleeford, Crawlin and Cadge 事务所，在 Chancery Lane 109 号。他说：你记性真好。她说只是点头致谢。他转向上尉：可以去见见那位先生吗？看看他知道的是不是比嘴上说的多。上尉答：我这辈子没见过哪个法律行当的人不是这样。交给我，我去探探他的底。老人说：好极了。他的手指敲得嗒嗒响，仿佛他的形体几乎容纳不住那股精力：那么我们明早再见，看看查到了什么；之后，作为我方案的第三部分，我们恐怕得考虑自己跑一趟 Starningham。上尉说他倒很乐意去海边待几天。Fell 太太问：你们两位去查我的事时我该做什么？上尉说您尽管留在这里。Scrooge 说：俱乐部的一切设施都听候您使用。女士说：不。我已经休息够了；对你们的帮助我感激不尽，但我也必须自己帮自己。为此，Scrooge 先生，我今晚打算和您一起去参加那场降神会。两位先生交换了一个吃惊而担忧的眼神。上尉刚开口"夫人"，她便用手势打断他，斩钉截铁地说："My mind is quite made up."此后，正如各位此刻不难想象的，两位先生始终不敢对 Louisa Fell 太太说一个"不"字。

**关键词**：dubious mercy of the workhouse（救济院靠不住的怜悯）/ means of attack（进攻手段）/ ferret out（探出底细）/ a quality of calculation（计算的神色）/ Jolly good（好极了）/ facilities of the Club（俱乐部的一切设施）/ quite dared to say（敢说一个不字）

**为什么这样写**：本章收尾是全书最漂亮的段落之一：作者先让最不可能的一方先开口，用 Scrooge 的冷静把读者从恐惧中拽出来。Ambrose 那句"这对任何女士来说都实在令人不快"是虚张的同情，作者称之为"虚张声势的同情"，一个词就把角色的职业性写尽。Scrooge 的转折是全段的机关："他脸上有计算的神色"——Scrooge 关心的从来不是鬼，而是遗产、房子和可操作的线索；紧接着"除了接受遗嘱的条件你别无选择"与那句冷得像刀的话"除非你打算把自己扔到救济院那靠不住的怜悯上去"——"dubious mercy"（靠不住的怜悯）四个字把救济院写成了比鬼更可怕的东西，也是本书的阶级底色。真正的转折在他数出"三条进攻路线"时：作者用军事隐喻"三种进攻手段"，把灵异侦查写成战役部署；他随即"嗒嗒嗒"地敲手指，这个动作从第八章的轻敲升级成"容不下的精力"，暗示他已经嗅到了机会。Ambrose 那句"我这辈子没见过哪个法律行当的人不如此"是全书最妙的人物台词之一：既是玩笑，也是对律师职业的判决。他还负责喜剧性的支线——"好极了，我倒很想去海边待几天"——把一场调查说成度假，反差中全是时代的轻佻。最后她拒绝留下，坚持同赴 séance，"不。我已经休息够了"与"我也必须自己帮自己"——女主的主动性在此完成；而"My mind is quite made up."（我主意已定）之后那句"两位先生终究都不敢对她说不"是全章最好的收束：既是幽默，也是一句关于权力的判词。

**读者视角提示**：三条查证路线请记牢：今晚见 Beck（Archibald Beck）、查律师 Walter Cadge（Cleeford, Crawlin and Cadge，Chancery Lane 109 号）、亲自去 Starningham。她拒绝留在俱乐部，坚持同赴 séance。

## 一句话总结

1832 年 9 月，八岁的 Louisa Fell 随父赴 Norfolk 的 Starningham 探访母亲兄弟，住在死胡同尽头的 25 号：第一夜床上凭空出现秋叶，第二夜楼下父亲与叔叔激烈争吵时近处有陌生女声连唤她两声名字，第三夜她踢到被褥下一个温热而活、像流动黑墨并"似乎有智慧"的东西；Scrooge 听完三件怪事后并不惊慌，反而摆出三条查证的路——今晚见灵媒 Beck、查律师 Walter Cadge、亲自去 Starningham——而她拒绝留在俱乐部，斩钉截铁地说"My mind is quite made up."，两位先生终究不敢说不。
