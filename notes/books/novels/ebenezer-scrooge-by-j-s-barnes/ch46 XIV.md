# 46. XIV

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：午后，Mrs Fell 在自己的卧房里向 Susan Naylor 宣布她已做了决定——午餐后她带这姑娘看过楼下的房间。Susan 声音里带着一丝促狭，因为那句"那四面墙内不得问任何问题"的禁令，她当成一场好玩的游戏来对待：「you must tell me what you've decided.」Fell 太太的判断是审慎的：如果接受这屋子里确有什么存在，那么它除了吓人之外似乎做不了别的——它能敲墙、能制造幻觉，也许在她还是孩子时还能把干枯的叶子放到她床上的被褥里；但超出这些，就「seems to be beyond it」。Susan 笑说「Right and brave too」，Fell 太太有点害羞地否认，坚持说那份勇气不属于自己，而属于「the little one」（腹中的孩子）；Susan 立即接上「They're to be lucky to be given you as a mother.」当被问到自己的做法是否正确时，楼上传来一声敲击，两位女子对看一眼。Susan 说她认为对——「For as you say, all it can do is frighten. At least…」，然后停住，像是要说超出本意的话；Fell 太太催她继续，Susan 才说出真正的忧虑：除非它找到办法做得更多、说得更多，除非它「somehow… finds a voice…」——「How would it do that?」楼上又是一声敲击，比前一次响些，「a sound like frustration」。Susan 说她也不知道，那只是她出生前的一个老故事：一个声音「leapt… leapt from body to body, like a fly going from breadcrumb to breadcrumb」。Fell 太太眼里浮起不安。Susan 随即收手，说那全是瞎编、Fell 太太当然是对的，然后借口有很多事要忙，转身就走了；Fell 太太站在原地听她 downstairs 忙活的声响，从这份家常气里得到一点安慰，困意袭来，倒在床上；正要睡去时，眼皮被近在手边的敲门声惊开——是上尉，有些紧张地站在门槛上：「Forgive me, ma'am. I didn't want to wake you. It sounds as if Susan is making herself quite at home.」Fell 太太心里一动，几乎要提起姑娘那句"声音在人身间跳跃"的怪话，终究没说；上尉想出门转转 town，试试旅店开门时的情况，打听还有没有什么该知道的，也看看有没有 Scrooge 的消息。太太看出这位先生不擅长等待，急于到外面去、想实实在在做点什么，立刻同意，并保证 Susan 在时自己在屋里很安全。「I’ll come back」「before it gets dark」太太请他不必担心，并说自己的 distinct feeling 是这屋子已经把最坏的都朝他们扔出来了，等 Scrooge 来了多半也会这么想。她说这番话时，将近下午两点。叙述者收尾：到午夜，他们将证明这想法「horribly misguided」。

**情感弧线位置**：本章弧线为「宣布决定—互相试探—楼上的敲击— Susan 说出真正的恐惧—突兀告辞—家常声响带来的安心—困意—上尉来访—被压下的话—上尉外出—太太的错误自信—叙述者冷讽」。基调在"理性评估"与"渐次渗入的不安"之间摆动：全章的正戏是两位女人关于"这屋子能做什么"的推演，而读者会在最后一句被告知：她们推演得太乐观了。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现了「本地少女知道不该说的事」这一 trope——Susan 掌握一条关于"声音在人身之间跳跃"的老传说，那正是本书的题眼（Marley 的链与声音）。反转有三处：其一，Fell 太太的推论"它只能吓人"被 Susan 一句话顶住——「Unless it… somehow… finds a voice…」；其二，Susan 用"全是瞎编"收尾并几乎是逃开，但作者写她走时「turned upon her heels」；其三，最重的一处是**叙述者的全知预告**：「By midnight, they would prove to be horribly misguided.」——把读者推到全知位置，看着两个人乐观地锁上自己的门。

**人物弧线**：Fell 太太在本章完成一次转变：从第 36 章的"进屋就怕"到"我做了决定"；但她的转变基础是错的——她把恐惧缩减成一份清单（敲门、幻觉、落叶），正如作者在第 9 章已交代：落叶、还有那团"活的墨"与" intelligent"。Susan Naylor 本章从第 44 章的笑声里露出真正的分量：她懂得"这屋子的规矩"的严重性，也知道一条老传说；她的退场是战术性的。Captain Ambrose 仍是全书的功能性支点：他不擅长等待，要去镇上做实事——而正是他离开、屋里只剩两人，构成了接下来那些事的舞台条件。

**叙事手法**：一是**敲击的节拍**（一声、又一声更响）穿插在对话中间，把对话切成两半，并让读者听见"它在听"；二是**Susan 的语言层层后退**：她先玩笑、再纠正、再沉默（"At least…"）、再吐出恐惧、再用"全是瞎编"封口；三是**结尾的全知冷讽**，一句话把全章推翻。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** I do believe I’ve come to a decision,” said Mrs Fell to Susan Naylor, in the lady’s bedchamber, after they had eaten luncheon and after she had shown the girl the lower floors of the house

**中文理解**：「我确实想清楚了一个决定。」Fell 太太在女士的卧房里对 Susan Naylor 说道——此时她们已经吃过午饭，而她也已经带这姑娘看过楼下的房间了。

**关键词**：I do believe I’ve come to a decision / said Mrs Fell to Susan Naylor / in the lady’s bedchamber / after they had eaten luncheon / after she had shown the girl the lower floors of the house

**为什么这样写**：作者用「I do believe」这种带保留的语气起手：一个"下了决心"的人这么说，往往是因为决心还不牢。而交代地点与前置动作（看过楼下房间）是为了让读者明白，Susan 此刻已有资格被听取——她已经在事实上成了这屋子的半个主人。

**读者视角提示**：她的"决定"建立在只看过楼下房间的基础上。

> **原句 2:** Please, madam,” said Susan, with something of a twinkle in her voice, for she was treating the insistence that no question be asked within those four walls as a pleasant game, “you must tell me what you’ve decided

**中文理解**：「求您了，夫人，」Susan 说，声音里带着一丝促狭——因为那句"那四面墙内不得发问"的坚持，她一直当成一场好玩的游戏在遵守——「您一定得告诉我您决定了什么。」

**关键词**：Please, madam / with something of a twinkle in her voice / she was treating the insistence that no question be asked within those four walls as a pleasant game / you must tell me what you’ve decided

**为什么这样写**：作者用「with something of a twinkle in her voice」给出 Susan 的语调底色——玩笑。但括号里那句解释更重要：Bethlem 的禁令（第 37 章第三条：别提问）被她当成游戏，这正说明镇上的规矩对本地人是仪式而非恐惧。而"twinkle"（眼里的闪光）一词把她的机敏写在脸上。

**读者视角提示**：Susan 把禁令当游戏，说明规矩对本地人只是仪式。

> **原句 3:** “Now,” Mrs Fell began, “I have been thinking, and I am not certain – if one accepts that there is some presence here in this house – that it can do anything other than frighten. Yes, it can knock against the walls. It can surely create illusion. Perhaps, when I was a child, it could even place dry leaves upon the blankets of my bed. But anything more than that… seems to be beyond it.”

**中文理解**：「那么，」Fell 太太开口，「我一直在想。而且我并不确定——如果承认这屋子里确实有什么存在的话——它除了吓人之外还能做别的。它能敲墙。它当然能制造幻觉。也许在我还是孩子的时候，它甚至能把干枯的叶子放到我床上的被褥里。但超出这些……似乎就超出它的能力了。」

**关键词**：if one accepts that there is some presence here in this house / that it can do anything other than frighten / It can surely create illusion / place dry leaves upon the blankets of my bed / anything more than that… seems to be beyond it

**为什么这样写**：这是本章的推理核心，作者让 Fell 太太用"如果承认 A，则 B 不成立"的形式把超自然现象缩减成一份清单。而清单的第三项"把干叶子放到床上"直接呼应第 9 章——她记得童年那件事，却把它当作无害的怪癖。作者让读者在这里发现：她的推理漏掉了最可怕的一项。

**读者视角提示**：她漏掉了第 9 章那团"活的墨"。

> **原句 4:** Susan smiled. “I think you might be right, miss. Right and brave too.” Mrs Fell turned a little bashful at this remark and insisted that she was nothing of the sort, before adding that “If I find any measure of courage then it is not for me but for the little one

**中文理解**：Susan 笑了。「我想您可能是对的，小姐。对，而且勇敢。」Fell 太太被这句话弄得有点害羞，坚持说自己完全不是那样的人，接着补充道：「如果我真找到了一点勇气，那也不是为了我自己，而是为了那个小的。」

**关键词**：Susan smiled / I think you might be right, miss / Right and brave too / turned a little bashful at this remark / insisted that she was nothing of the sort / If I find any measure of courage then it is not for me but for the little one

**为什么这样写**：作者写人物性格不靠形容词，靠**转手**：Fell 太太一被夸就「turned a little bashful」，又「insisted that she was nothing of the sort」——谦虚得近乎推拒。而「it is not for me but for the little one」一句让她把自己从"需要被保护的人"改写成"为了孩子而战的人"，这是她本章性格的落点。

**读者视角提示**：她的勇气不属于自己，属于孩子。

> **原句 5:** They’re going to be a lucky child, miss,” Susan said. “They’re to be lucky to be given you as a mother.” “You’re very kind,” said the older woman. “Not kind,” the girl said, “but merely saying what I see before me

**中文理解**：「他们会是个幸运的孩子，小姐。」Susan 说，「能有您做母亲，是他们的幸运。」「您太好心了，」年长的女人说。「不是好心，」姑娘说，「只是说出我眼前所见。」

**关键词**：They’re going to be a lucky child, miss / They’re to be lucky to be given you as a mother / You’re very kind / Not kind / but merely saying what I see before me

**为什么这样写**：Susan 的两句短句刻意用"直陈"取代赞美：先说"不是好心"，再说"只是说出我眼前所见"。作者借此把她与 Fell 太太区分开——一个靠情感判断，一个靠观察判断。而这观察力正是她后面能说出那则老传说、也能在结尾"看出"事情不对劲的原因。

**读者视角提示**：Susan 靠观察，不靠同情。

> **原句 6:** “Yes,” murmured Susan. “I do. For as you say, all it can do is frighten. At least…” She stopped talking, the very picture of someone who was about to say more than they intended. “Go on,” said the widow. “Unless, of course, it finds a way to do more. Or say more. Unless it… somehow… finds a voice…” “How would it do that?”

**中文理解**：「那么你认为我的做法是对的，是吗？」就在这个问题问出口时，楼上传来一声敲击。两位女子交换了一个眼神。「是的，」Susan 低声说，「我确实这么认为。毕竟如您所说，它能做的也只是吓人。至少……」她停了下来，那模样活像一个人正要说出超出本意的话。

**关键词**：For as you say, all it can do is frighten / At least… / the very picture of someone who was about to say more than they intended

**为什么这样写**：作者把敲门精确地放在"提问—回答"的那一瞬，让房子像在偷听。更妙的是作者对 Susan 停顿的描写：「the very picture of someone who was about to say more than they intended」——她本来打算说的那句被敲门吓了回去。

**读者视角提示**：敲门正好打断在关键的话头上。

> **原句 7:** Unless, of course, it finds a way to do more. Or say more. Unless it… somehow… finds a voice…” “How would it do that?” Again, from upstairs, another knock, a little louder than before, a sound like frustration. “I don’t know, miss, I’m sure. Just an old story from before I was born. About a voice that leapt… leapt from body to body, like a fly going from breadcrumb to breadcrumb.” Mrs Fell looked at her with disquiet in her eyes

**中文理解**：「说吧。」寡妇说。「除非——当然，除非它找到办法做更多的事。或者说更多的话。除非它……以某种方式……找到了一个声音……」「它要怎么做得到？」楼上又传来一声敲击，比刚才响些，那声音里带着一丝受挫的意味。「我不知道，小姐，我确定。」Susan 说，「只是我出生前的一个老故事。说的是有一个声音跳……跳着从一个人身上跳到另一个人身上，像一只苍蝇从一粒面包屑跳到另一粒。」Fell 太太看着她，眼里浮起不安。

**关键词**：it finds a way to do more / Or say more / Unless it… somehow… finds a voice / How would it do that? / another knock, a little louder than before / a sound like frustration / a voice that leapt… leapt from body to body / like a fly going from breadcrumb to breadcrumb / Mrs Fell looked at her with disquiet in her eyes

**为什么这样写**：这是全书最重要的一次"预言式对话"：Susan 用比喻描述的正是 Marley 的行为方式——声音在身体之间传递。而第二声更响的敲门带着「a sound like frustration」，作者暗示那不是随机噪声，而是被拒之门外的不满。「like a fly going from breadcrumb to breadcrumb」这个比喻选得极妙：跳、短暂、一次次落在新的落点。

**读者视角提示**：Susan 描述的正是 Marley 附身传声的方式。

> **原句 8:** I’m sure it’s all just make believe, and I’m sure you must be right in what you want to do. Now, you’ve been most welcoming, but I’ve plenty to do before I leave you for the night. I’ll be about my duties, miss, if that’s all the same to you.” And before Louisa Fell could say anything further, the girl had turned upon her heels and left the room. From downstairs, she heard the sounds of her busying herself in the kitchen

**中文理解**：「我敢肯定那全是瞎编的，也敢肯定您想要的做的事是对的。好了，您一直非常客气，不过我还有很多事要忙，得先去做您的活儿了，小姐，就这样吧。」没等 Louisa Fell 再说什么，姑娘已经转身出了房间。从楼下，她听见厨房里传来她忙活的声响。

**关键词**：I’m sure it’s all just make believe / I’m sure you must be right in what you want to do / I’ve plenty to do before I leave you for the night / I’ll be about my duties, miss / the girl had turned upon her heels and left the room / From downstairs, she heard the sounds of her busying herself in the kitchen

**为什么这样写**：作者让 Susan 用最快的方式撤退——她把刚才的恐惧归零为"瞎编"，然后用"我得干活"把自己从超自然的谈话里拔出来。而「turned upon her heels」是仓促的动作，与她先前的从容形成反差；「she heard the sounds of her busying herself in the kitchen」则把"她其实害怕"这一判断留给读者去补。

**读者视角提示**：她说"全是瞎编"，但逃得很快。

> **原句 9:** The lady stood and listened a while, finding a little comfort in the sounds of domesticity, for all the oddness of the girl’s departure. Then, feeling a great wave of tiredness wash over her, she sank, with gratitude, onto her bed. She even thought she might drift into sleep, for recent days had been amongst the strangest and most difficult of her life. Then her eyelids snapped open at the sound of a tapping, very close at hand now, on the door to her room.

**中文理解**：那位女士站着听了一会儿，从这些家常声响里找到一点安慰，尽管姑娘的离去透着古怪。随后，一阵巨大的倦意席卷而来，她心怀感激地倒在了床上。她甚至想自己或许会就这样睡过去，因为近来这几天都是她一生中最古怪、最艰难的日子。然后她的眼皮猛地睁开——因为就在近旁，有人在敲她的房门。

**关键词**：The lady stood and listened a while / finding a little comfort in the sounds of domesticity / for all the oddness of the girl’s departure / a great wave of tiredness wash over her / she sank, with gratitude, onto her bed / the strangest and most difficult of her life / her eyelids snapped open / at the sound of a tapping, very close at hand now / on the door to her room

**为什么这样写**：作者用「sounds of domesticity」五个字对抗前面整章的超自然：锅碗的声音成了安慰本身。而「she sank, with gratitude」的"感激"用得反常——感激困意，是疲惫到极点的标志。最后「her eyelids snapped open」与第 43 章 Scrooge 的「eyelids snapped open」同构，是作者埋下的形式呼应：两人都在假睡的边缘被拉回。

**读者视角提示**：两人用同一个"猛地睁眼"的动作被唤醒。

> **原句 10:** It was the captain, who stood, with some nervousness, upon the threshold. “Forgive me, ma’am. I didn’t want to wake you. It sounds as if Susan is making herself quite at home.” “Yes,” said the lady. “I think she’ll be quite invaluable.” Briefly, she thought then of mentioning the girl’s peculiar remark about a voice that leapt from one person to another

**中文理解**：是上尉，他带着几分紧张站在门槛上。「请原谅我，夫人。我本不想吵醒您。听声音 Susan 好像已经把这儿当成自己家了。」「是啊，」那位女士说，「我想她会非常不可替代。」她心里短暂地闪过一个念头：要不要提起姑娘那句关于声音在人与人之间跳跃的怪话……

**关键词**：It was the captain, who stood, with some nervousness, upon the threshold / Forgive me, ma’am / I didn’t want to wake you / It sounds as if Susan is making herself quite at home / I think she’ll be quite invaluable / Briefly, she thought then of mentioning the girl’s peculiar remark / about a voice that leapt from one person to another

**为什么这样写**：作者让 Fell 太太**想说而没说**——这是本章最经济的悬念处理：她知道一条关键信息（声音能跳跃），却把它咽了回去，只用「Briefly, she thought」六个字交代。而这一吞，就是后来午夜那场事的伏笔。

**读者视角提示**：她把关键的话咽了回去——这是后患所在。

> **原句 11:** I thought I might take a turn around the town,” said the captain. “Perhaps try the inn when the landlord opens it up. See if I can’t find out if there’s anything else we need to know about our predicament. And see if there’s any sign of Mr Scrooge also

**中文理解**：「我想我该到镇上转转，」上尉说，「或许等旅店开门时去试试。看看我们还能打听到什么关于我们处境的事。也看看有没有 Scrooge 先生的踪迹。」

**关键词**：I thought I might take a turn around the town / Perhaps try the inn when the landlord opens it up / See if I can’t find out if there’s anything else we need to know / about our predicament / And see if there’s any sign of Mr Scrooge also

**为什么这样写**：作者让上尉的出门带三条任务，其中第三条（找 Scrooge 的踪迹）把本章与主线重新挂钩。而「our predicament」（我们的处境）用的是复数——他默认自己与这屋子的命运已经捆在一起，尽管他今晚就要离开。

**读者视角提示**：他要去打听"我们的处境"，却马上要离开她。

> **原句 12:** The lady – sensing that the gentleman was not adept at waiting, that he was eager to be out in the world and contributing in some material way – agreed at once that he should go, and assured him that she would be quite safe in the house while Susan was with her.

**中文理解**：那位女士——她察觉到这位先生不擅长等待，急于到外面去、想实实在在地做点什么——立刻同意他该去，并保证只要 Susan 陪着她，她在屋里十分安全。

**关键词**：sensing that the gentleman was not adept at waiting / that he was eager to be out in the world / and contributing in some material way / agreed at once that he should go / while Susan was with her

**为什么这样写**：作者用一个长长的插入语把太太的心理写足：她看出上尉的不安不是虚弱而是**憋不住要行动**——这既是体贴也是纵容。她的"十分安全"的保证此刻有 Susan 陪着，所以这句话在两小时后回想起来格外沉。

**读者视角提示**：他是因为闲不住才走的，她由着他。

> **原句 13:** I’ll come back,” said the captain, “before it gets dark

**中文理解**：「我会回来的，」上尉说，「天黑之前。」

**关键词**：I’ll come back / before it gets dark

**为什么这样写**：一句六词的承诺，作者单独成段。作者让上尉先给出精确的时间界限（天黑前），而叙述者随后立刻把这个界限变成反讽的标尺：全章末尾说「By midnight, they would prove to be horribly misguided」——天黑与午夜之间，就是他承诺与灾难之间的距离。

**读者视角提示**："天黑之前"——这句承诺后面就跟着全章的反讽。

> **原句 14:** don’t fret. I’ll have Susan with me until nightfall. Besides, I have the distinct feeling that this house has already thrown at us the worst it has. I dare say when Mr Scrooge gets here that he will most likely think the same.” When the lady spoke these words it was almost two o’clock

**中文理解**：「请吧，」那位女士说，「别担心。到天黑之前我都让 Susan 陪着我。再说，我有一种明确的感觉：这栋房子已经把最坏的都朝我们扔出来了。我敢说等 Mr Scrooge 来了，他多半也会这么想。」她说这番话时，将近下午两点。

**关键词**：don’t fret / I’ll have Susan with me until nightfall / I have the distinct feeling that this house has already thrown at us the worst it has / I dare say when Mr Scrooge gets here that he will most likely think the same / When the lady spoke these words it was almost two o’clock

**为什么这样写**：全章的反讽核心在这句自信里：她把"最坏的已经过去"当作既成事实，而她用的词是「already thrown at us」——仿佛那东西是**扔**过来的、投完了就算了。而作者给她配了一个精确的时刻（将近两点），这样最后一句「By midnight」的时间跨度（十小时）才显得刺目。

**读者视角提示**：她确信最坏的已经过去——这正是错得最离谱的地方。

> **原句 15:** By midnight, they would prove to be horribly misguided

**中文理解**：到午夜，他们将证明这想法「horribly misguided」——错得极其离谱。

**关键词**：By midnight / they would prove to be horribly misguided

**为什么这样写**：全章最后一句只有十个词，却把整章的从容全部作废。作者用将来时的"将证明"把读者推到未来视角，回望此刻这两个人正在说的豪言。「horribly misguided」——不只是判断错，而是**方向错**，错得离谱。

**读者视角提示**：作者提前宣布这十个小时的乐观是徒劳的。

## 一句话总结

午后，Mrs Fell 在卧房向 Susan Naylor 宣布「I do believe I’ve come to a decision」；Susan 声音里「with something of a twinkle in her voice」，把"四面墙内不得发问"的禁令当「a pleasant game」；Fell 太太的判断是：若承认屋里有某种存在，它除了「frighten」似做不了别的——「It can knock against the walls」「It can surely create illusion」「place dry leaves upon the blankets of my bed」，而「anything more than that… seems to be beyond it」；Susan 笑说「Right and brave too」，她害羞地否认，坚持勇气属于「the little one」，Susan 答「They’re to be lucky to be given you as a mother」「Not kind」「but merely saying what I see before me」；当被问「You think my course of action is right, then?」时，楼上「came a single knock」，两人「exchanged a glance」；Susan 说「For as you say, all it can do is frighten. At least…」却停住，「the very picture of someone who was about to say more than they intended」，终于说出「Unless it… somehow… finds a voice…」；楼上又一声敲击「a little louder than before, a sound like frustration」，她答那只是老故事：「a voice that leapt… leapt from body to body, like a fly going from breadcrumb to breadcrumb」，Fell 太太「with disquiet in her eyes」；Susan 随即收口「I’m sure it’s all just make believe」并借口有活要干「turned upon her heels and left the room」，楼下的「sounds of her busying herself in the kitchen」反倒让太太得了些安慰；她因倦意「sank, with gratitude, onto her bed」，正要睡去时被近旁的敲门惊醒——是上尉「with some nervousness, upon the threshold」，她说「I think she’ll be quite invaluable」，而「Briefly, she thought then of mentioning the girl’s peculiar remark about a voice that leapt from one person to another」却没说出口；上尉要「take a turn around the town」「try the inn when the landlord opens it up」并打听 Scrooge 的踪迹，她看出他「not adept at waiting」、急于「contributing in some material way」，当即放行并保证安全，「I’ll come back」「before it gets dark.」；太太答「don’t fret」「I have the distinct feeling that this house has already thrown at us the worst it has」「when Mr Scrooge gets here that he will most likely think the same」，说这话时「it was almost two o’clock」——而叙述者收尾：「By midnight, they would prove to be horribly misguided.」
