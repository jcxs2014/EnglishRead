# 65. II

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：本章是全书「幽灵猎手 Scrooge」履历的自陈与 Brightwell Road 之夜的开场。作者先给履历：与 Ambrose 上尉结伴之前有威尔士海边那座「still cannot be named in print」的闹鬼修道院与 Cirencester 的「spectral swine」；结伴之后有 Blackpool 酒馆楼上「a white lady was said to walk」的房间、苏格兰高地一间鬼屋、伯明翰近郊线路上那节腐烂的闹鬼车厢——其内容「had caused even the captain to blanch」；而他直到 1853 年那些事之前总说，Brightwell Road 上那栋房子「had the worst and most profoundly upsetting atmosphere of them all」；他形容那气氛「as though one were entering a space which had been filled with thick, choking smoke which was somehow quite invisible to the naked eye」，进门时「gasping for air」，被「the possessed, hollow-eyed Captain Ambrose」无声扶住手臂；楼梯脚下现身的是「the small, demure figure of Mrs Fell」，她说「He is waiting for you. … You must go alone.」；Scrooge 像问路的行人那样平静道谢，随即转身喝问「Fight it! Both of you! … I know that you can!」——那对「motionless as dolls」没有回答，但「some subtle shift in their bearing and posture」让他确信自己被听见了；他独自登楼，听见身后「the rustle of invisible robes」，在顶楼门上轻叩，一个年轻女子在里面唤「Come in, Ebenezer!」；门缝下「a surge of pale, eldritch light」，他推门而入。

**情感弧线位置**：弧线为「履历铺垫—旧日战绩—旧屋最恶—踏入窒息—两位战友已被占据—孤身登楼—应召入门」。本章是全书情感最低点之前的最后一段静默：Scrooge 已在门外，门内是全书最核心的相遇。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现「老练驱鬼人面对终极凶宅」这一母题，但反转在两点：其一，他不是新手，他的战绩单亮得刺眼，恐惧恰恰来自专业判断——Brightwell Road 是他见过最糟的；其二，他向两位战友喊「Fight it!」却得不到回应，这句此前在别处是对活人说的，此处是对已被占据者的无效呼告。

**人物弧线**：本章是 Scrooge 从「猎手」到「孤身赴约者」的身份收缩。他对 Mrs Fell 与 Ambrose 的怜悯（「a shudder of pity」）与鼓励，说明他仍把二人当活人；而他「put his right hand upon the balustrade」又「thinking better of it」转身，说明他尚未忘记人间礼仪。

**叙事手法**：一是**履历式开场**——用一串地名与鬼物把此前的故事一笔带过，让本章获得重量；二是**感官化描写**——不可见的浓烟、气短、无形衣袍的窸窣；三是**静默的应答**——Dolls 与无声的姿势变化，恐怖不靠尖叫，靠没人回应；四是以一句年轻女子的呼唤把门推开。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** By this time in his career as a ghost hunter, which had been extensive (in spite of certain erroneous reports having once been made that he had had no further intercourse with spirits since that fateful Christmas Eve), Ebenezer Scrooge had set foot in numerous haunted places.

**中文理解**：到那时，Scrooge 作为幽灵猎手的生涯已经相当漫长——尽管曾经有过一些错误的报道，说他自那个命中注定的圣诞夜之后便再未与亡灵有过任何往来。

**关键词**：career as a ghost hunter / which had been extensive / erroneous reports having once been made / no further intercourse with spirits since that fateful Christmas Eve

**为什么这样写**：作者一开口就以括号插叙一段「错误报道」，看似玩笑，实为全书的反讽框架：读者已经知道他此后与亡灵往来频繁。职业的「extensive」也先立起分量——后面那栋房子之所以可怕，是因为见过世面的人仍然怕。

**读者视角提示**：他自称是资深猎手，先立功绩。

> **原句 2:** In the time before his association with Captain Ambrose, for example, there had been the haunted abbey in a Welsh coastal town, which still cannot be named in print, and the spectral swine in Cirencester.

**中文理解**：在与 Ambrose 上尉结伴之前，例如，曾有威尔士一处海边小镇上的闹鬼修道院——那地方至今仍不便在印刷品上指名——以及 Cirencester 的幽灵猪群。

**关键词**：In the time before his association with Captain Ambrose / the haunted abbey in a Welsh coastal town / which still cannot be named in print / the spectral swine in Cirencester

**为什么这样写**：作者用「不便指名」这一说法制造真实感与禁忌感，好像这桩案例仍被行业回避；「spectral swine」把农庄传说直接接到驱鬼题材上，说明猎人的门类不限。

**读者视角提示**：连地名都讳莫如深。

> **原句 3:** With the captain at his side there had been the room above a Blackpool tavern where a white lady was said to walk, an abandoned gamekeeper’s cottage in the Highlands of Scotland where something uncanny scampered and scuttled upon the rooftops, and even that haunted railway carriage which had been left to rot in a siding just outside Birmingham, the contents of which had caused even the captain to blanch.

**中文理解**：与上尉同行时，则有 Blackpool 一家酒馆楼上那个据说有白衣女子走动的房间、苏格兰高地一间被废弃的看林人小屋——有什么诡异之物在屋顶上窸窸窣窣地跑窜——以及甚至那节被丢在伯明翰近郊侧线上任其腐烂的闹鬼车厢，车里的东西连上尉都吓得脸色发白。

**关键词**：the room above a Blackpool tavern where a white lady was said to walk / an abandoned gamekeeper’s cottage in the Highlands of Scotland / something uncanny scampered and scuttled upon the rooftops / that haunted railway carriage / had caused even the captain to blanch

**为什么这样写**：这一段用三个地点串起战绩，而收尾处特意落在「连上尉都变脸」——上尉此前一直被写成沉稳的向导，让他的失态反衬出车厢的可怕，也提示读者：这一行的对手能压过专家。

**读者视角提示**：连同伴都曾被吓到。

> **原句 4:** Yet he would always say – at least until those particular events of 1853 – that the house on Brightwell Road had the worst and most profoundly upsetting atmosphere of them all.

**中文理解**：然而他总是说——至少在 1853 年那些特定事件发生之前——Brightwell Road 上那栋房子拥有所有这些地方中最坏、最令人深深不安的气氛。

**关键词**：Yet he would always say / until those particular events of 1853 / the house on Brightwell Road / the worst and most profoundly upsetting atmosphere of them all

**为什么这样写**：作者用一句「直到 1853 年那些事之前」给未来的章节留了后门：读者此刻尚不知 1853 年发生了什么，只知道那句话后来会失效。评价用「最坏」而非「最可怕」，也留了被更高一级超越的余地。

**读者视角提示**：这房子的坏，还在后头。

> **原句 5:** It was, he said, as though one were entering a space which had been filled with thick, choking smoke which was somehow quite invisible to the naked eye. It rocked him back upon his heels as he was beckoned into the hallway of that place and he found himself gasping for air. Seeing this, the possessed, hollow-eyed Captain Ambrose took him wordlessly by the arm and held him steady until the worst of it had passed.

**中文理解**：他形容说，那感觉就像走进一个空间——里面充满浓稠、呛人的烟，可这烟却又完全看不见。他被引进那地方的走廊时，身子向后仰了一下，忍不住大口喘气。见此情形，被附体的、双目空洞的 Ambrose 上尉一言不发地抓住他的手臂，扶住他，直到最难熬的一阵过去。

**关键词**：as though one were entering a space which had been filled with thick, choking smoke / quite invisible to the naked eye / It rocked him back upon his heels / gasping for air / the possessed, hollow-eyed Captain Ambrose / held him steady until the worst of it had passed

**为什么这样写**：作者用「看不见的浓烟」这一悖论把压迫感实体化，又不惊动感官；随后让全书的可靠向导以「被附体、空洞双眼」的模样出场——他一直护着 Scrooge，此刻自己已是待救者。

**读者视角提示**：向导已被占据，还在扶他。

> **原句 6:** As they walked on and reached the foot of the staircase, the small, demure figure of Mrs Fell emerged. “Ebenezer,” she said. Scrooge took the merest glance at the woman whose plaintive story had led him to Starningham in the first place. With a shudder of pity, he saw precisely what he expected then to see: that her movements were as leaden as those of the captain and that her eyes were milky-white. “He is waiting for you,” she said. “On the uppermost floor. You must go alone.”

**中文理解**：他们继续往前走，来到楼梯脚下，Fell 太太那娇小而娴静的身影出现了。「Ebenezer，」她说。Scrooge 只朝那位把他引到 Starningham 来的哀诉者瞥了一眼。他心中一阵怜悯的颤动，看见的正是他早已预料到的：她的动作像上尉一样沉重迟缓，而她的眼睛是乳白色的。「他在等你，」她说，「在最高的一层。你必须一个人去。」

**关键词**：the small, demure figure of Mrs Fell emerged / a shudder of pity / her movements were as leaden as those of the captain / her eyes were milky-white / He is waiting for you / You must go alone

**为什么这样写**：作者把「娇小娴静」与「乳白眼睛、动作沉重」并置，一眼可怖，却不靠血腥。Fell 太太一句「你必须一个人去」是全章的调度令，把 Scrooge 从战友身边剥离。

**读者视角提示**：她也失去了神智，还要他独自上去。

> **原句 7:** Scrooge nodded and thanked them both, just as calmly and kindly as though he were a pedestrian who had stopped to ask directions. He put his right hand upon the balustrade and one foot upon the first step. Then, thinking better of it, he turned swiftly to face the lady and the captain. They stood, motionless as dolls. Their white eyes gazed sightlessly at him. An awful moment of silent waiting. Then Scrooge hissed at them: “Fight it! Both of you! Fight it with all of your heart and soul! I know that you can!”

**中文理解**：Scrooge 点了点头，平静而客气地谢过他们两位——就像一个停下来问路的行人那样。他右手扶上楼梯扶手，一只脚踏上了第一级台阶。随后他转念一想，又猛地转身面向那位太太与上尉。他们伫立不动，如同两具人偶。那一双双白眼茫然地望着他。一段可怕的沉默等待之后，Scrooge 冲他们低声喝道：「抵抗它！你们两个！用你们全部的身心去抵抗！我知道你们做得到！」

**关键词**：just as calmly and kindly as though he were a pedestrian who had stopped to ask directions / put his right hand upon the balustrade / thinking better of it, he turned swiftly / They stood, motionless as dolls / Their white eyes gazed sightlessly at him / Fight it! Both of you! / I know that you can!

**为什么这样写**：作者先写他半只脚已踏上门阶，再写他「转念一想」折返——这一退让是他人性的最后证据。喊话内容与前文对活人说话的口吻一致，却对着两个不再回应的人喊，慈悲因此成了无效的。

**读者视角提示**：他还在对他们喊「抵抗」。

> **原句 8:** The pair gave no response, yet some subtle shift in their bearing and posture was sufficient to persuade the old man that, on some subterranean level, he had been heard.

**中文理解**：那两人没有回答，可是他们姿态与举止中某种细微的转变，却足以说服老人：在某个隐秘的层面上，他的话被听见了。

**关键词**：The pair gave no response / some subtle shift in their bearing and posture / sufficient to persuade the old man / on some subterranean level, he had been heard

**为什么这样写**：作者不肯把希望写实，只给「某种细微的转变」与「某个隐秘的层面」——两次都用模糊限定词，恐惧与希望同时保持悬置。整段的张力全靠这一句不确认的确认。

**读者视角提示**：也许他们还听得见。

> **原句 9:** He said no more to them, nor they to him. Scrooge simply ascended, rather effortlessly, up to the very top floor. As he walked, he heard from behind him the rustle of invisible robes. At the top of the house, the door was closed. He tapped gently upon it with a gnarled, wrinkled fist.

**中文理解**：他没再说什么，他们也没再说什么。Scrooge 只是相当轻松地登上顶楼。一路上，他听见身后传来无形衣袍的窸窣声。屋子的最顶层，那扇门是关着的。他用一只嶙峋多皱的拳头轻轻叩了叩门。

**关键词**：He said no more to them, nor they to him / ascended, rather effortlessly, up to the very top floor / the rustle of invisible robes / the door was closed / He tapped gently upon it with a gnarled, wrinkled fist

**为什么这样写**：作者用「轻松地爬楼」反衬楼梯的沉重——他不是走不动，是强撑着轻巧。身后的衣袍窸窣是全书最精准的一处听觉恐怖：追来的不是脚步声，是衣料声。

**读者视角提示**：身后有东西跟着上来了。

> **原句 10:** From somewhere within, a young woman called out: “Come in, Ebenezer! Come in and make yourself welcome.” The old ghost hunter noticed then a surge of pale, eldritch light fleetingly visible beneath the door. Yet he did not hesitate but turned the handle, pushed the door open and walked inside to his fate.

**中文理解**：从门内某处，一个年轻女子唤道：「进来吧，Ebenezer！进来吧，让自己感到受欢迎。」老幽灵猎手这才注意到门缝下有一道苍白的、诡异的光一闪而过。然而他没有迟疑，转动把手，推开门，走了进去，走向他的命运。

**关键词**：a young woman called out / Come in, Ebenezer! Come in and make yourself welcome / a surge of pale, eldritch light fleetingly visible beneath the door / turned the handle, pushed the door open, pushed the door open / walked inside to his fate

**为什么这样写**：作者让邀请语与「走向命运」同段落下，最后一句用「走向他的命运」把门内发生的一切提前悬置——读者此刻什么也不知道，只知道进去就回不来了。门缝光是一闪而过，作者不肯多给。

**读者视角提示**：门内有人唤他进去。
