# 71. VIII

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：顶楼真相由女仆苏珊之口补全：Scrooge 绝口不提「what took place in that awful little room」，只肯给上尉「the sketchiest account of the facts」；苏珊「could recall next to nothing」，只记得「a few snatches of speech」——埃比尼泽在「goading the spirit which had taken up unwanted residence within her mind, daring it to leave her body and to reveal its true self」，那东西终于现形：先如「a swarm of summer insects」凝成尸相，Scrooge 斥它「barely even a spectre … Nothing but a revenant! … Nothing but a ghoul!」；镜中另有「something gowned and shrouded」在动，苏珊认出「this thing was no reflection but something moving – somehow, impossibly – within the world of the mirror itself」；Gowing 渐成实体「A dead man, a wretched evil dead man, congealing and decaying」，Scrooge 断言「this world is most certainly done with you」、且「I have never truly been alone … I have friends. And… guardians also.」，遂被镜中之物拖入；Gowing 挣扎时踢翻油灯，房子「a veritable tinderbox」烧起；上尉抱走昏迷的苏珊，Scrooge 独自留下，握着那本被子弹打坏的圣经，「He closed his eyes and waited.」——五分钟后「the whole house was ablaze」。

**情感弧线位置**：全书最高点后的收束。前面是悬疑的层层剥开，本章是驱魔的最后一击与最沉重的代价——胜利以 Scrooge 独困火场、放弃逃生告终。情绪从揭底的紧张转为同归于尽式的平静。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现「镜中世界」「旧友的鬼魂」「以信仰之器克邪」三母题。反转有三处：其一，「old friend」竟是被拖走的 Gowing；其二，Scrooge 自己不走，把生路让给苏珊；其三，他握着的那本圣经正是当年挡下子弹的那一本——同一样东西两次救他，一次挡枪，一次陪伴。

**人物弧线**：Scrooge 在本章说出「I have never truly been alone … I have friends. And… guardians also.」，这是全书对他孤独一生的最终结算：他仍是独自站在火里的人，但不再是孤身。

**叙事手法**：一是**视角转手**——从 Scrooge 转到苏珊，让受害者视角补足主角不肯讲的细节；二是**降格反讽**（spectre→revenant→ghoul 的三连降级）；三是**静默收尾**（闭眼等待 → 五分钟后全屋烧尽），用两句极短的话把整场火的结局交代掉。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** As for what took place in that awful little room on the top floor of the house, Mr Scrooge would never be drawn in detail, and would provide (and even then only to Captain Ambrose) the sketchiest account of the facts. For a fuller explanation, we must now turn to the testimony of the maidservant, Susan.

**中文理解**：至于那间可怕的小屋——那栋房子顶层的那间——究竟发生了什么，Scrooge 先生绝不会被细细描摹；即便只对上尉安布罗斯，他也只肯给出关于事实的「最潦草的一份交代」。要想得到更完整的解释，我们现在必须转而听女仆苏珊的证词。

**关键词**：would never be drawn in detail / even then only to Captain Ambrose / the sketchiest account of the facts / we must now turn to the testimony of the maidservant, Susan

**为什么这样写**：作者先声明主角的缄默，再宣布改用他人证词——这是标准的「不可靠叙述者交接」：读者知道 Scrooge 在隐瞒什么，却要从一个记不清的姑娘嘴里拼出真相。而「sketchiest account」这个最高级形容词，把他的「交代」贬到近乎抵赖。

**读者视角提示**：他不肯细讲，改由苏珊作证。

> **原句 2:** It was a great mercy to her that she could recall next to nothing of her ordeal at the hands of the spirit of Mr Gowing, and could recollect hardly anything at all of what had taken place after she had arrived at the house on the previous evening. She was to suffer from bad dreams for the next year or so, but she believed a few nightmares to be a small price to pay for her own survival, a most sensible view with which I would be inclined to concur.

**中文理解**：对她来说，能几乎完全不记得自己落到 Gowing 先生之灵手中的那场折磨，是莫大的幸运；对前一晚来到这栋房子之后所发生的一切，她更是几乎毫无记忆。接下来一年左右，她都会做噩梦；但她认为几场噩梦为自己活命付出的小小代价，这一判断极为明智，我也倾向于赞同。

**关键词**：It was a great mercy to her / she could recall next to nothing of her ordeal / hardly anything at all of what had taken place / a few nightmares to be a small price to pay for her own survival / a most sensible view with which I would be inclined to concur

**为什么这样写**：作者让「遗忘」本身成为情节：苏珊记不得，正说明那东西有多可怕。末句以叙述者的「I would be inclined to concur」把这套说法背书——这是本书罕见的口吻：叙述者会站出来替角色说一句公道话。

**读者视角提示**：她几乎什么都不记得；叙述者附议她的取舍。

> **原句 3:** Yet as Gowing’s power faded over the other occupants of the house as he concentrated his power and will upon Mr Scrooge, Susan became increasingly aware of her condition. She can recall a few snatches of speech, and it was her impression that Ebenezer was goading the spirit which had taken up unwanted residence within her mind, daring it to leave her body and to reveal its true self. It was at the moment when this finally occurred that – like Mrs Fell and the captain downstairs – all of her faculties were restored to her.

**中文理解**：然而，随着 Gowing 把力量与意志集中到 Scrooge 身上、他在别的住客身上的力量随之消退，苏珊越来越能意识到自己的处境。她能记起几句零星的话语；在她看来，埃比尼泽是在激那个已在她心里安家落户的灵，逼它离开她的身体、现出真面目。就在这一刻终于发生——就像楼下的 Fell 太太与上尉一样——她全部的神智都回到了自己身上。

**关键词**：as Gowing’s power faded over the other occupants / he concentrated his power and will upon Mr Scrooge / Susan became increasingly aware of her condition / a few snatches of speech / goading the spirit which had taken up unwanted residence within her mind / daring it to leave her body / all of her faculties were restored to her

**为什么这样写**：作者用一句话把上一章「注意力转移」与本章「挣脱」接上：同一个机制，两处生效。而「daring it to leave her body」把 Scrooge 的战术说得极准——他不是赶它走，是激它现形，让它不得不把自己最丑的一面亮出来。

**读者视角提示**：埃比尼泽在激它离开她的身体，现出真身。

> **原句 4:** She found herself sprawled upon the floor in the uppermost room, beside the fireplace, beneath the mirror and with the lamp to her left. Groggy and nauseous, she looked up just in time to see what seemed to her at first to be a dark cloud, something like a swarm of summer insects which coalesced into the face and form of a cadaverous man – that of Mr Gowing. “Ah!” This was the visitor, Mr Scrooge speaking. “So that is your true self? You’re barely even a spectre, sir. Nothing but a revenant! Worse than that. Nothing but a ghoul!”

**中文理解**：她发现自己摊倒在最顶层的地板上，挨着壁炉，在那面镜子底下，油灯在她左手边。她昏昏沉沉、恶心欲呕，就在这时抬头，恰好看见一团像是黑云的东西——起初像一群夏日昆虫——凝聚成一张死人的脸、一个人的形貌：Gowing 先生的脸。「啊！」来客 Scrooge 说道。「原来这就是你的真面目？先生，你甚至算不上一个幽灵。只是一具游魂！比那更糟。你不过是个食尸鬼！」

**关键词**：sprawled upon the floor in the uppermost room / beside the fireplace, beneath the mirror / a dark cloud, something like a swarm of summer insects / coalesced into the face and form of a cadaverous man / You’re barely even a spectre / Nothing but a revenant / Nothing but a ghoul

**为什么这样写**：作者先给位置（壁炉旁、镜下、灯在左手），再给形态：黑云、虫群、尸脸——三个比喻一路升级。Scrooge 的三连降级更是全书的修辞招牌：你甚至算不上幽灵，只是一具游魂，比那更糟，你不过是个食尸鬼；一句话把对手从灵异谱系里一路贬到最低一档。

**读者视角提示**：它现出尸相；Scrooge 三连降级：幽灵、游魂、食尸鬼。

> **原句 5:** The amorphous being above her snarled at this and she realised that he was allowing himself to be riled. She looked up and caught old Scrooge’s eye. Very carefully he moved his head towards the door, indicating that she ought to do her level best to get out of this death-trap. As carefully as she could, and without attracting the attention of the dead thing that floated in the air above her, she began to crawl towards the door. “You shall never have that which you seek!” Scrooge said to the remnants of Mr Gowing.

**中文理解**：她头顶上那个无形的东西咆哮起来，她这才明白它是在故意被激怒。她抬眼，正撞上老 Scrooge 的目光。他极小心地把头朝门的方向偏了偏，示意她应当拼命逃出这个死亡陷阱。于是她尽可能小心、又不惊动那个浮在她上方的死物，开始朝门口爬去。「你永远也得不到你想要的东西！」Scrooge 对 Gowing 先生的残骸说道。

**关键词**：The amorphous being above her snarled / he was allowing himself to be riled / Very carefully he moved his head towards the door / get out of this death-trap / without attracting the attention of the dead thing / she began to crawl towards the door

**为什么这样写**：作者用「无声的配合」推进险情：一个眼神、一个偏头，读者全懂；而「allowing himself to be riled」这半句提前点破 Scrooge 的激将法正在生效。爬行这一细节把她的虚弱写到极致，也为下章她「crumpled down again」的再次跌倒埋了因。

**读者视角提示**：他以眼神示意她爬向门口。

> **原句 6:** “You made the most appalling choices all of your life, sir. You are dead now and so you must remain. Face your punishment, Mr Gowing! The world of the living is not for you!” “Fine words from a hypocrite!” said Gowing. “You are envied more than you know, Ebenezer, for your glorious second chance. My master – with whom I once made a deal – he knows of you, sir. He hungers for you!” “Why should I care about that?” Scrooge said pugnaciously. “Why should I listen to what is barely anything more than a talkative swarm of flies?”

**中文理解**：「你一生做过最可怕的选择，先生。你现在死了，就得死着。接受你的惩罚吧，Gowing 先生！活人的世界不属于你！」「一个伪君子说得好听的话！」Gowing 说道。「你比自己知道的更被人艳羡，埃比尼泽，为你那份光荣的第二次机会。我的主人——我曾与他做过一笔交易——他知道你，先生。他渴望你！」「我为什么要在乎那个？」Scrooge 好斗地说，「我为什么要听一个不过是群聒噪的苍蝇似的东西说话？」

**关键词**：You made the most appalling choices all of your life / Face your punishment, Mr Gowing! / Fine words from a hypocrite! / You are envied more than you know / for your glorious second chance / My master – with whom I once made a deal / He hungers for you! / a talkative swarm of flies

**为什么这样写**：作者让 Gowing 抛出「my master」这个全书最重的悬念——Scrooge 的第二次机会是全书主题，而对方竟说「他渴望你」。Scrooge 的回应却轻描淡写：把对手贬成「一群聒噪的苍蝇」——他不愿接这个话题，也不肯让恐惧主导对话。

**读者视角提示**：Gowing 提到「我的主人」渴望他；Scrooge 把它贬作一群苍蝇。

> **原句 7:** Susan, who had by now reached the door, looked up and saw two remarkable things. The first was that the Gowing-creature was becoming ever more solid and real, more and more of a living human being, a feat which she imagined must cost that wicked entity dear in terms of willpower and effort. The second was that, behind him, in the great silver mirror which hung upon the wall, something was moving, something gowned and shrouded.

**中文理解**：此时已走到门口的苏珊抬头，看见了两件不寻常的事。第一件是，那个 Gowing 造物变得越来越实在、越来越真实，越来越像一个活生生的人——她想，这项壮举对那个邪恶实体而言，必定要在意志与努力上付出昂贵的代价。第二件是，在他身后那面挂在墙上的大银镜里，有东西在动——一个裹着长袍、被遮蔽起来的东西。

**关键词**：reached the door / the Gowing-creature was becoming ever more solid and real / a feat which she imagined must cost that wicked entity dear in terms of willpower and effort / in the great silver mirror which hung upon the wall / something was moving, something gowned and shrouded

**为什么这样写**：作者让苏珊以「两件不寻常的事」分列观察，说明她此时已能冷静观察——恢复神智的标志。而「越来越实在」与镜中「有什么在动」并置：正面是渐强的敌人，背景里是真正的援手，两个方向同时逼近。

**读者视角提示**：Gowing 越来越实在；镜中另有东西在动。

> **原句 8:** Though, she realised almost at once, this thing was no reflection but something moving – somehow, impossibly – within the world of the mirror itself. “You’re too late,” said the Gowing-creature. “My work in this house has already begun. I have already begun to exert my will upon the vessel.” “Oh, but you rather overstate your own capacities, I think, Mr Gowing.” Still Mr Scrooge stood firm, though Susan noticed that both of his hands were trembling. “You’re nothing more than an echo! Nothing more than a bad smell, sir!” Now Gowing came fully into his corporeal form.

**中文理解**：不过她几乎立刻就意识到，那东西不是倒影，而是某个东西——不知怎么、不可思议地——正在镜子自己的世界里移动。「你太迟了，」Gowing 造物说道。「我在这栋房子里的工作已经开始了。我已经开始对这具容器施加我的意志了。」「可你实在高估了自己的能力吧，Gowing 先生。」Scrooge 仍稳稳站着，尽管苏珊注意到他的双手都在发抖。「你不过是个回声！先生，不过是一股臭气！」此时 Gowing 完全显出了他的实体形态。

**关键词**：this thing was no reflection but something moving / within the world of the mirror itself / My work in this house has already begun / I have already begun to exert my will upon the vessel / you rather overstate your own capacities / both of his hands were trembling / You’re nothing more than an echo / Nothing more than a bad smell

**为什么这样写**：作者用「不是倒影」四字把恐怖从光学推到本体论层面：镜子里有另一个世界，那东西住在里面。而 Scrooge 双手在抖这一笔，是全书少见的「身体泄底」——嘴上说着「回声」「臭气」，身体却先招了。

**读者视角提示**：镜中不是倒影；Scrooge 双手在抖。

> **原句 9:** Susan saw it and then had to cast her eyes away for it was an awful sight. A dead man, a wretched evil dead man, congealing and decaying. A monstrous sight. An abomination. He stood now upon the floorboards and leered at Scrooge. “Is this an echo, Scrooge? No! I am here. I am real. And I am not yet done with this world.” Behind him, something was gathering in the mirror. “Oh,” said Scrooge, allowing himself a smile. “But this world is most certainly done with you.”

**中文理解**：苏珊看见了，只得以移开眼睛——那景象太可怕：一具尸体，一具龌龊邪恶的尸体，正在凝结、正在腐坏。一幅怪物般的景象。一场亵渎。他此刻站在地板上，对 Scrooge 龇牙。「这是回声吗，Scrooge？不！我在这里。我是真的。我还没玩完这个世界。」「哦，」Scrooge 允许自己露出一个笑，「可这个世界已经彻底玩完你了。」

**关键词**：had to cast her eyes away / A dead man, a wretched evil dead man, congealing and decaying / A monstrous sight. An abomination / he leered at Scrooge / I am here. I am real / this world is most certainly done with you

**为什么这样写**：作者用三连短句把这具尸体写成一场视觉灾难：凝结、腐坏、怪物、亵渎，节奏急促到近乎喘不过气。Scrooge 的回应则用一个「世界已经玩完你了」把对方的宣言整个掉头——对方说「我还没玩完这个世界」，他答「这个世界已经玩完你了」。

**读者视角提示**：Gowing 完全显形；Scrooge 笑说这个世界已经玩完他。

> **原句 10:** “You can’t do anything. You are one man and all you have are words!” “On the contrary,” said Scrooge. “I am not just one man. For if there is one thing you have to understand about me, it is this: I have never truly been alone, not once, since that Christmas Eve long ago. I have friends. And… guardians also.” Too late now did old Gowing realise that he had been drawn into a trap. He began to ask for an explanation, but before he could do so, thin grey arms emerged from the mirror and reached for him.

**中文理解**：「你什么也做不了。你只有一个人，能用的只有嘴话！」「恰恰相反，」Scrooge 说道，「我不只是一个人。若说你必须了解我的一件事，那就是：自从很久以前那个圣诞夜以来，我从来没有真正孤单过。我有朋友。还有……守护者。」老 Gowing 这才too late地意识到自己被引进了一个圈套。他刚开口要一个解释，镜子里就伸出几条灰白的胳膊，朝他抓去。

**关键词**：You can’t do anything. You are one man and all you have are words! / I am not just one man / I have never truly been alone, not once, since that Christmas Eve long ago / I have friends. And… guardians also / he had been drawn into a trap / thin grey arms emerged from the mirror

**为什么这样写**：这是全书人物弧线的结算句，也是全书对「圣诞夜」这一母题的最终解释：他的第二次机会不是神迹，是「朋友」与「守护者」。而紧随其后的「thin grey arms」把理念立刻兑现为动作——读者不必等到下一章就知道守护者是什么。

**读者视角提示**：他说自己有朋友与守护者；镜中伸出灰白的胳膊。

> **原句 11:** Gowing turned around, flailing at the air, but the being from the mirror had its arms around him and held him fast by the shoulders. He kicked and struggled and, in the midst of doing so, he kicked over the oil lamp. It caught fire almost at once for the house was old and dry, a veritable tinderbox. The flames – as we have already heard – spread very quickly. Gowing cried out but he could form no words.

**中文理解**：Gowing 转过身，在空中胡乱扑打，可是镜中那东西的两条胳膊已经箍住他的双肩，把他牢牢抓住。他踢着、挣扎着——也就在挣扎之中，他踢翻了那盏油灯。房子又老又干，是十足的干柴火匣子，灯几乎立刻就烧了起来。火焰——正如我们已经听说过的那样——蔓延得非常快。Gowing 叫出了声，却一个字也说不出来。

**关键词**：held him fast by the shoulders / He kicked and struggled / in the midst of doing so, he kicked over the oil lamp / the house was old and dry, a veritable tinderbox / The flames – as we have already heard – spread very quickly / he could form no words

**为什么这样写**：作者把火灾写成意外而非天罚：灯是被挣扎踢翻的，房子本就是干柴火匣子。可笑的是Gowing 越挣扎，毁灭得越快——他最后连「一个字也说不出来」，与前几章的滔滔雄辩形成反差。

**读者视角提示**：他挣扎时踢翻油灯，房子立刻烧起来。

> **原句 12:** The thing in the mirror had him now and began pulling him into it, like a predator from a lake dragging in its prey. Upon the far side of the room, the fire was growing in intensity. As Gowing disappeared into the mirror, dragged there by the being, the flames grew higher still. Scrooge turned to her and smiled. “That was an old friend of mine,” he said, before adding: “Well, perhaps not quite a friend…” Susan tried to clamber to her feet then, but found herself unsteady. She crumpled down again, her long possession taking its toll.

**中文理解**：镜中那东西此刻擒住了他，开始把他往里拖，像一个从湖里拖拽猎物的捕食者。房间另一边，火势越来越旺。随着 Gowing 被那东西拖进镜子，火焰烧得更高了。Scrooge 转向她，笑了笑。「那是我一位老朋友，」他说，随即又补上一句：「嗯，也许算不上朋友……」苏珊这时想挣扎着站起来，却发现自己站立不稳，又瘫坐下去——被占据这么久，代价终于显现。

**关键词**：began pulling him into it / like a predator from a lake dragging in its prey / the fire was growing in intensity / the flames grew higher still / That was an old friend of mine / Well, perhaps not quite a friend / her long possession taking its toll

**为什么这样写**：作者用「像湖里拖猎物的捕食者」写镜中之物，把一个抽象的「世界」写成有口腹的活物。而 Scrooge 那句「老朋友……也许算不上朋友」是全书最冷的一记反讽：他与死者确有旧交，那旧交正是被他亲手送进去的。

**读者视角提示**：他被拖进镜中；Scrooge 说那是他「老朋友」。

> **原句 13:** The door was pushed open and Captain Ambrose ran in. “Here!” he said and held out his hand to the girl. Gratefully, she fell into his arms. “Save her!” said Ebenezer. “Get her away from here.” The flames by now had come closer to him, blocking his exit from the room. Ambrose looked at him helplessly. In his arms, the girl had fainted. “Take her downstairs!” said Scrooge. “Get her out. I shall make my own way.” Fire was all around him now and he was choking on the smoke. The wound in his side ached badly.

**中文理解**：门被推开，安布罗斯上尉冲了进来。「来！」他说着向那姑娘伸出手。她感激地倒进他的怀里。「救她！」埃比尼泽说道。「带她离开这里。」此时火焰已经逼近他，堵住了他离开房间的路。安布罗斯无助地看着他。怀里的姑娘已经昏了过去。「带她下楼！」Scrooge 说道。「带她出去。我自有办法出去。」火此刻已包围了他，他被烟呛得直咳。侧腹的伤口痛得厉害。

**关键词**：The door was pushed open and Captain Ambrose ran in / held out his hand to the girl / Gratefully, she fell into his arms / Save her! / Get her away from here / blocking his exit from the room / the girl had fainted / I shall make my own way / choking on the smoke / The wound in his side ached badly

**为什么这样写**：作者让 Scrooge 在生死关头仍先发号施令救人，句子短促如口令；随后三句身体实况——火挡路、烟呛、伤口作痛——把他从「胜利者」打回「垂死的老人」。全章的代价，最终由他一个人承担。

**读者视角提示**：上尉抱走昏迷的苏珊；他被火与烟困住。

> **原句 14:** “I will come back for you!” said the captain, though such a thing already seemed impossible given the speed at which the house was going up. He wasted no more words but ran from the room. Left alone there, Scrooge found himself unable to move, trapped. He ached all over. He felt very old and tired indeed. And as the flames grew higher, all around him, he reached for the little Bible in his pocket which had been damaged by a bullet that was meant for him. He stood and held it close to his chest.

**中文理解**：「我会回来救你！」上尉喊道，尽管以房子烧起来的速度看，这事似乎已不可能。他不再多说一个字，冲出了房间。被独自留在那里的 Scrooge 发现自己动弹不得，被困住了。他浑身作痛。他觉得自己真是又老又累。就在火焰四周越烧越高时，他伸手去摸口袋里那本小圣经——它曾被一颗本是冲他而来的子弹打坏。他站着，把它紧紧贴在胸口。

**关键词**：I will come back for you! / given the speed at which the house was going up / he ran from the room / found himself unable to move, trapped / He ached all over / He felt very old and tired indeed / the little Bible in his pocket which had been damaged by a bullet that was meant for him / held it close to his chest

**为什么这样写**：作者在最后一刻把两件旧事缝在一起：那本圣经正是几十章前医生从血泊里取出、宣告「Without this he would surely be dead.」的那一本。同一件东西，先挡住子弹，再陪伴等死——读者会在这两句话之间自动完成一次全书回顾。

**读者视角提示**：他握着那本挡下过子弹的圣经，贴在胸口。

> **原句 15:** He closed his eyes and waited. Five minutes later and the whole house was ablaze, a fire so ferocious that not a single splinter of it would survive.

**中文理解**：他闭上眼睛，等待。五分钟后，整栋房子燃成一片——一场如此凶猛的火，不会有哪怕一根木片存活下来。

**关键词**：He closed his eyes and waited / Five minutes later / the whole house was ablaze / not a single splinter of it would survive

**为什么这样写**：全章以两句极短的话收束：一个动作（闭眼等待），一个时限（五分钟），一个结果（灰飞烟灭）。作者不写他是怎么出来的，也不写他有没有出来——把悬念留给下一卷。而「不会有哪怕一根木片存活」既是火势的量化，也顺手宣告了旧宅、遗嘱、镜子的彻底终结。

**读者视角提示**：他闭眼等待；五分钟后整栋房子化为灰烬。

## 一句话总结

顶楼真相由女仆苏珊之口补全：Scrooge 绝口不提「what took place in that awful little room」，只肯给上尉「the sketchiest account of the facts」；苏珊「could recall next to nothing」，只记得「a few snatches of speech」——埃比尼泽在「goading the spirit which had taken up unwanted residence within her mind, daring it to leave her body and to reveal its true self」，那东西终于现形：先如「a swarm of summer insects」凝成尸相，Scrooge 斥它「barely even a spectre … Nothing but a revenant! … Nothing but a ghoul!」；镜中另有「something gowned and shrouded」在动，苏珊认出「this thing was no reflection but something moving – somehow, impossibly – within the world of the mirror itself」；Gowing 渐成实体「A dead man, a wretched evil dead man, congealing and decaying」，Scrooge 断言「this world is most certainly done with you」、且「I have never truly been alone … I have friends. And… guardians also.」，遂被镜中之物拖入；Gowing 挣扎时踢翻油灯，房子「a veritable tinderbox」烧起；上尉抱走昏迷的苏珊，Scrooge 独自留下，握着那本被子弹打坏的圣经，「He closed his eyes and waited.」——五分钟后「the whole house was ablaze」。
