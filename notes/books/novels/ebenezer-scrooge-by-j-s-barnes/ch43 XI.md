# 43. XI

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：开篇一句话先报喜：「Mr Ebenezer Scrooge – you shall be very pleased to hear – had survived that long night.」医生 Tarkin 的缝合「so expertly applied… (and without the slightest question of a fee)」没有崩开，他也没有在痛苦中醒来；他在 Bentinck 办公室的小床上躺了几个小时，秘书、过路人 Penberry、几位俱乐部成员守夜，最要紧的是 Charlotte——她照料他、替他擦洗、在床边长夜守候。天刚破晓（而此刻 Mr Beck 仍在 The Jolly Boaters 门槛上与 Miss Hare 说着支离破碎的话），Charlotte 独自陪着老人，开始往最坏处想。她坐在床边椅子上，自己已困得眼皮下垂、下巴前倾，正要睡过去时，一个声音把她立刻推回清醒：老人的呼吸变了——从弱而规则变成「something far less rhythmic」，吸与呼之间「waited too long」，「labour and gasp」。这声音她听过，也知道它常常预示着什么；她把椅子拉近，握住他枯瘦的手，低声说「Fight it, Mr Scrooge」「Fight it with all your strength. The world’s not yet ready for you to leave it.」老人当然没有回答，只是费力地呼吸着。Charlotte 起身要去叫医生，却听见床边传来一个又细又高的声音：「Spirit?… What would you have me do?」他眼睛仍闭着，像在说梦话；随后只像在听，仿佛有什么看不见的存在在同他说话。接着他轻轻说：「You forget, I think, how very old I have become. I may be too weak for what you ask of me.」又停了一下，唇边闪过一丝笑。Charlotte 轻唤「Mr Scrooge?」，老人毫无所觉，只是呼吸似乎回复了正常，她大感宽慰——「He lived and struggled on. He still had fight in him」，想到这一点，她心中涌起一股强烈的感情，「not, perhaps, so very far from love for him, like that of a granddaughter for her grandfather」。她认定他睡着了，便轻轻溜出房间去叫医生——她确信他会想知道这个小小变故。然而就在她带上门的一瞬，老人眼皮骤然睁开，沙哑地低语一声，至于那是对自己说还是对某个看不见的对话者，「we shall never know」：「Starningham,」他说。

**情感弧线位置**：本章弧线为「报平安—守夜—困极—呼吸变调—临终关口—呼唤 Spirit—示弱—笑意—与孙女的爱—以为他睡去—睁眼—一个地名」。情绪自「读者与叙述者共同的长夜之后的松弛」，经Charlotte 的困与惧滑入「死亡可能就在眼前」的紧张，被老人的梦中之语短暂托住（不是解脱，是心疼），最后以一个孤零零的地名收束——昏迷中的 Scrooge 心里只有 Starningham。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现了「临终幻觉与召唤灵媒」的 trope，但反转了它的通俗形态：老人没有看见 Marley，没有被链条拖走，也没有忏悔；他喊的是「Spirit?」——一个泛称，一个把自己当使唤者的口吻，且他说「I may be too weak for what you ask of me」，把这场召唤变成一次**讨价还价**。第二个反转在末尾：Charlotte 以为他睡着了，作者却让他睁眼说出「Starningham」——她整个判断（「Contenting herself that he slept」）被一句地名推翻。

**人物弧线**：Charlotte 是本章真正的行动者。她从第 38 章那个只会哭喊的少女，变成守了一整夜、能辨呼吸节奏、能握住枯手说「Fight it」的人；而作者借她之口说出对 Scrooge 最接近爱的一层感情——「not, perhaps, so very far from love for him, like that of a granddaughter for her grandfather」。Scrooge 本章几乎不出现，只以声音与一个地名出现：他的身体在退场，心却在 Starningham。作者用一句「whether to himself or to some unseen interlocutor we shall never know」明确拒绝给出解释。

**叙事手法**：一是**首尾的告知式短句**：「you shall be very pleased to hear」用第二人称把读者拉进来，末尾「we shall never know」又把读者推开；二是**括号插入**：（and Mr Beck was still in ragged conversation with Miss Hare on the threshold of The Jolly Boaters）——一句跨章并置，把伦敦线与主线并排放；医疗细节（缝合线、收费、呼吸形态）写得极细，让超自然时刻有可触摸的生理底子；三是**声音的三个层级**：Charlotte 的低语、老人又细又高的一声「Spirit?」、结尾沙哑的耳语。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** Mr Ebenezer Scrooge – you shall be very pleased to hear – had survived that long night

**中文理解**：埃比尼泽·斯克鲁吉——你们会很高兴听到——熬过了那个漫长的夜晚。

**关键词**：Mr Ebenezer Scrooge / you shall be very pleased to hear / had survived that long night

**为什么这样写**：作者一开口就破题，而且是带着笑意的破题：「you shall be very pleased to hear」是报喜的口吻，把读者当同席的朋友。破题句只交代结果、不交代过程，把「怎么熬过来的」全部留给下文——这一句既是悬念，也是本书体裁里罕见的「先给结果」的节奏。

**读者视角提示**：全书最黑的一夜，结论先给。

> **原句 2:** His condition had not markedly improved, but neither had he woken in agony, nor had the stitching – so expertly applied by Dr Tarkin (and without the slightest question of a fee) – come undone. Instead, he had lain for hours in the little bed in Bentinck’s office, watched over by the secretary himself, by the passer-by, Mr Penberry, by several other, distinguished members of the Club and, above all, by Charlotte, who had tended to him and who had kept him clean and who had sat long vigil by his bedside

**中文理解**：他的状况没有明显好转，但他也没有在痛苦中醒来，Tarkin 医生那缝合得极为精当、并且分文不取的伤口也没有崩开。他就这样在 Bentinck 办公室的那张小床上躺了几个小时，秘书本人守着他，那位路人 Penberry 守着他，俱乐部里几位有声望的会员守着他，尤其是 Charlotte——她照料他、替他保持干净、在他的床边长久守夜。

**关键词**：His condition had not markedly improved / neither had he woken in agony / the stitching – so expertly applied by Dr Tarkin / without the slightest question of a fee / the little bed in Bentinck’s office / watched over by the secretary himself / by the passer-by, Mr Penberry / by several other, distinguished members of the Club / above all, by Charlotte

**为什么这样写**：作者用一串否定式（没有更糟、没有醒来痛、缝合没崩）来写"还活着"，语气近乎俏皮。守夜名单里唯一被抬到 "above all" 的是 Charlotte——一个女仆，排在秘书、Penberry 和诸位名流之上，这一置换是本章人物关系的核心。而「without the slightest question of a fee」把 Tarkin 的医治写成一种早已不计回报的旧情。

**读者视角提示**：名单里最重的是女仆，不是董事。

> **原句 3:** Dawn had just arrived (and Mr Beck was still in ragged conversation with Miss Hare on the threshold of The Jolly Boaters) when Charlotte, who was by now alone with the old man, began to fear the worst

**中文理解**：天刚亮（而这时贝克先生还在快乐水手酒馆的门槛上与海尔小姐说着支离破碎的话），查洛特——此时只剩她与老人独处——开始往最坏处想。

**关键词**：Dawn had just arrived / Mr Beck was still in ragged conversation with Miss Hare / on the threshold of The Jolly Boaters / Charlotte, who was by now alone with the old man / began to fear the worst

**为什么这样写**：作者用括号把另一个场景（呼应第 42 章那场未完成的救援）按在同一时间轴上，让读者知道伦敦那边正在发生的事仍未收束。而「began to fear the worst」的转折极为克制：她不是慌，是"开始往最坏处想"——这是陪床人特有的冷静恐惧。

**读者视角提示**：括号是作者的跨线并置手法。

> **原句 4:** She had herself, sitting upright in the chair beside the patient, begun at last to grow drowsy, exhausted by the emotional exertions of the night. Her eyelids drooped and her chin sank forward and she was on the cusp of slipping into outright slumber when she heard a sound which pushed her instantly into waking once again

**中文理解**：她本来坐在病人旁边的椅子上，已经困了——一夜的情绪消耗把她磨尽。眼皮垂下来，下巴往前沉，正要滑进真正的熟睡时，她听见一个声音，立刻被推回清醒。

**关键词**：sitting upright in the chair beside the patient / begun at last to grow drowsy / exhausted by the emotional exertions of the night / Her eyelids drooped and her chin sank forward / on the cusp of slipping into outright slumber / a sound which pushed her instantly into waking once again

**为什么这样写**：作者把困倦写成身体的三个细节（眼皮、下巴、临界点），让读者跟着她一起滑向睡意；然后用一个声音把这一切拦腰截断。「on the cusp」这个词尤其准——她不是睡着，是悬在睡着的那条边上，所以下一个瞬间的醒来才显得惊险。

**读者视角提示**：她在睡与醒的临界点上被拉回。

> **原句 5:** The quality of the old man’s breathing had changed, moving from a weak but regular sound of inhalation and exhalation into something far less rhythmic, something which seemed to wait too long between out-breath and in-breath, something which seemed to labour and gasp

**中文理解**：老人的呼吸声变了：从原本虚弱却规律的吸与呼，变成一种远不那么有节奏的东西——吸气和呼气之间似乎等得太久，似乎费劲，似乎在喘息。

**关键词**：The quality of the old man’s breathing had changed / a weak but regular sound of inhalation and exhalation / something far less rhythmic / seemed to wait too long between out-breath and in-breath / seemed to labour and gasp

**为什么这样写**：这是全书最见功力的一处生理描写：作者不写"他快不行了"，只写节奏的变化——「wait too long between out-breath and in-breath」把死亡写成了一次呼吸之间的空档。而三个分句（rhythmatic / wait / labour and gasp）从听觉写到体感，一层层加压。

**读者视角提示**：作者用呼吸节奏替代了"病危"二字。

> **原句 6:** It was a sound she had heard before and she knew what it oftentimes portended. She drew her chair closer to him and took his withered hand in hers. “Fight it, Mr Scrooge,” she murmured. “Fight it with all your strength. The world’s not yet ready for you to leave it.” The elderly gentleman gave, of course, no response to this. He breathed effortfully on. The young woman got to her feet and made towards the door, meaning to summon again the doctor, at once

**中文理解**：这声音她以前听过，也知道它常常预示着什么。她把椅子拉近，握住了他那只枯瘦的手。「Scrooge 先生，跟它斗。」她低声说，「用尽你全部的力气跟它斗。这世界还没准备好让你离开。」老人当然毫无回应，只是费力地继续呼吸。年轻女子站起身，朝门口走去，打算立刻去把医生再叫来。

**关键词**：It was a sound she had heard before / she knew what it oftentimes portended / took his withered hand in hers / Fight it, Mr Scrooge / with all your strength / The world’s not yet ready for you to leave it / gave, of course, no response / meaning to summon again the doctor

**为什么这样写**：Charlotte 的这句话是全章最有人味的一句：「Fight it」既是劝人，也暗合全书的核心意象——与鬼、与旧账、与自己的怯懦搏斗。她用第二人称说「The world’s not yet ready for you to leave it」，把死亡说成世界的失约。而老人「of course」的无回应带着叙述者的凉意：他听不见。

**读者视角提示**："跟它斗"呼应全书主题。

> **原句 7:** As she did so, she heard from the direction of the bed a thin, high voice. “Spirit?” he said. His eyes were still closed and he was speaking (or so she thought) in some manner of dream. “What would you have me do?” He said nothing after that but only seemed to listen, as though some other, invisible presence were speaking to him.

**中文理解**：就在这时，她听见从床那边传来一个又细又高的声音。「Spirit?」他说。他眼睛仍然闭着，说话的样子（至少她这么觉得）像是在说梦话。「你要我怎么做？」说完这句他不再出声，只是像在听，仿佛有另一个看不见的存在正在跟他说话。

**关键词**：from the direction of the bed a thin, high voice / Spirit? / speaking (or so she thought) in some manner of dream / What would you have me do? / only seemed to listen / as though some other, invisible presence were speaking to him

**为什么这样写**：作者用「thin, high voice」把老人的声音写得陌生，与他平日的威严判若两人。关键在「What would you have me do?」——这不是被拖拽的惨叫，是**交涉**：谁要你做什么，你办吧。而括号里的「or so she thought」是叙述者唯一一次介入：Charlotte 以为是梦话，其实是对话。

**读者视角提示**：那不是呓语，是交涉。

> **原句 8:** You forget, I think, how very old I have become. I may be too weak for what you ask of me.” Another pause then, and something like a smile flickered about his lips

**中文理解**：「你忘了吧，我想，我变得多么老了。我也许太虚弱，做不动你要我做的事。」又停了一下，唇边闪过一丝近乎微笑的东西。

**关键词**：You forget, I think, how very old I have become / I may be too weak for what you ask of me / Another pause then / something like a smile flickered about his lips

**为什么这样写**：这句话是 Scrooge 全书最动人的一次自陈：他不是拒绝，是抬价。而「something like a smile flickered」——是"近乎笑"的一瞬，作者不肯让它变成笑，说明他并不真觉得好笑；这一丝笑是疲惫里的认命，也是谈判里的一点弹性。

**读者视角提示**：他在跟对方讲条件。

> **原句 9:** Mr Scrooge?” said Charlotte, very gently. But the old man showed no signs of having heard her. His eyelids still were closed, but his breathing seemed, to her great relief, to have returned to something like normality. He lived and struggled on

**中文理解**：「Scrooge 先生？」Charlotte 极轻地问。但老人没有任何听见的样子。他的眼皮仍然闭着，可让她大为宽慰的是，他的呼吸似乎已回到接近正常的状态。他活了下来，还在挣扎。

**关键词**：Mr Scrooge? / said Charlotte, very gently / showed no signs of having heard her / his breathing seemed, to her great relief, to have returned to something like normality / He lived and struggled on

**为什么这样写**：Charlotte 的问话没有回应——这既说明他仍在梦中，也说明她已经被排除在这场对话之外，她只是个守在床边的人。而「He lived and struggled on」这个短句是叙述者从旁给的判语：既是医学结论，也是人物定义的改写——第 1 章那个把命当成算盘的 Scrooge，如今被写成"挣扎着活"的人。

**读者视角提示**：他的挣扎与第 1 章已完全不同。

> **原句 10:** He still had fight in him and, at that thought, she felt a powerful swell of something which was not, perhaps, so very far from love for him, like that of a granddaughter for her grandfather. Contenting herself that he slept, she slipped out of the chamber to summon the doctor, for she felt sure that he would want to know of this small change in circumstances. She closed the door softly behind her

**中文理解**：他身上还有斗劲；想到这里，她心中涌起一股强烈的感情，那感情「大概离爱也不太远」——像孙女对祖父的那种爱。她认定他睡着了，便溜出房间去叫医生，因为她确信他会想知道这个小小的变故。她在身后轻轻带上门。

**关键词**：He still had fight in him / a powerful swell of something / not, perhaps, so very far from love for him / like that of a granddaughter for her grandfather / Contenting herself that he slept / slipped out of the chamber to summon the doctor / he would want to know of this small change in circumstances / closed the door softly behind her

**为什么这样写**：作者借 Charlotte 之口第一次（也是全书少有几次）承认对 Scrooge 的爱，而且立刻用「like that of a granddaughter for her grandfather」限定它——是亲情式的、不追究的、愿意守夜的爱。而 "Contenting herself that he slept" 里的 contented 一词意味深长：她是在**自我说服**，读者却已经感到不安。

**读者视角提示**：她认定他睡了，但读者会不安。

> **原句 11:** As she did so, just for a moment, the old man’s eyelids snapped open and he gave a hoarse whisper, whether to himself or to some unseen interlocutor we shall never know. “Starningham,”

**中文理解**：就在她这样做的当口——就那么一瞬间——老人的眼皮猛地睁开，发出一声沙哑的低语，至于那是对自己说的还是对一个看不见的对话者说的，我们永远不会知道。「Starningham，」他说。

**关键词**：just for a moment / the old man’s eyelids snapped open / a hoarse whisper / whether to himself or to some unseen interlocutor / we shall never know / Starningham

**为什么这样写**：全章最后一击：就在 Charlotte 带上门的那一瞬，一个地名被扔出来。作者不解释、不给上下文，只给 "we shall never know"——叙述者拒绝做语义分析，把"他要去 Starningham"这一行动指令的来源保持在悬空。而这个地名正是第 35 章以来全书最重的那个词：它同时是亡妻的旧宅、闹鬼的房子、以及他此行要面对的一切。

**读者视角提示**：昏迷中他心里只有那个地名。

## 一句话总结

作者开篇即报平安——「Mr Ebenezer Scrooge – you shall be very pleased to hear – had survived that long night.」；他状况未明显好转但「neither had he woken in agony」，Tarkin 医生「so expertly applied… and without the slightest question of a fee」的缝合也未崩开，在 Bentinck 办公室小床上由秘书、Penberry、俱乐部会员与「above all, by Charlotte」守夜；天刚亮时（括号里作者并置「Mr Beck was still in ragged conversation with Miss Hare on the threshold of The Jolly Boaters」），独陪老人的 Charlotte「began to fear the worst」；她困到「on the cusp of slipping into outright slumber」时被一个声音唤醒，随即听出老人的呼吸由规则转为「something far less rhythmic」「seemed to wait too long between out-breath and in-breath」「seemed to labour and gasp」；她握住他的 withered hand 说「Fight it, Mr Scrooge」「Fight it with all your strength. The world’s not yet ready for you to leave it.」，他「gave, of course, no response」；她正要去叫医生，却听见「a thin, high voice」：「Spirit?… What would you have me do?」——他闭着眼「in some manner of dream」，只像在听「as though some other, invisible presence were speaking to him」；随后「You forget, I think, how very old I have become. I may be too weak for what you ask of me.」，唇边「something like a smile flickered」；Charlotte 轻唤无人应答，呼吸却「returned to something like normality」，作者判语「He lived and struggled on. He still had fight in him」，她心中涌起「not, perhaps, so very far from love for him, like that of a granddaughter for her grandfather」；她「Contenting herself that he slept」出门叫医生，却在带上门的一瞬见他「eyelids snapped open」，沙声低语一句「whether to himself or to some unseen interlocutor we shall never know」：「Starningham,」他说。
