# 34. II

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：Mr Penberry, the young legal clerk, hurled himself through the West End streets, scattering slower pedestrians in his wake and causing a considerable commotion. Not, he thought as he ran, and as he called out hurried apologies to those whose progress he had interrupted, that many of them would mind in the slightest if they knew that his urgent journey was to help Mr Scrooge in his hour of need. The old man was popular indeed in these parts and there were doubtless several whose path he had already crossed that afternoon whom he had assisted in the past, either in purely philanthropic terms or through his other, more mysterious work. Dodging traffic and horses and people on foot, ducking through all the bustle of the city streets, he came at last to Sargasso Street and sprinted along the pavement down to number 157. It was a grand building, with a green door, and a flight of steps which led up to that door. He ran up these and all but barrelled into the door himself, narrowly stopping in time before hammering hard with the knocker. At this ruckus, a window was opened on the first floor and a silver head, belonging to a man of distinction, peered out. "What is it? What do you want?" He sounded as though this interruption was by no means the first of the day. "You Dr Tarkin?" Penberry called up. "Of course I am! Who are you?" "My name's not important, sir!" shouted the legal clerk, with commendable modesty under the circumstances. "I'm here on behalf of Mr Scrooge!" The old fellow peered at him. "Mr Ebenezer Scrooge?" he asked. A cry went up. "The same, sir!" "Been taken ill, has he?" Mr Penberry passed a hand across his forehead. Caught up in the emergency, he had scarcely noticed how heavily he was perspiring. "Worse, sir!" he said. "Ebenezer Scrooge has been shot!" No answer came to this except the sudden slamming down of the window above. Penberry looked about him in some alarm, and was all set to hammer once again upon the front door, only for it to be thrown open and for old Dr Tarkin to emerge at a trot, still in the midst of putting on his frock coat. He did not wait for Penberry to acknowledge the situation but started off at a run, back in the direction in which the legal clerk had come. "He's at the Club, I presume?" he called back. Mr Penberry, catching up with the physician, said that yes he was, that Scrooge was still bleeding and was even now entirely insensible. "Then we should hurry!" shouted the doctor, altogether unnecessarily since they both were now running as fast as they could back towards St James's Square, to save the life of one whom they considered to be amongst the finest men in England.

## 一句话总结

第三十四章（Stave Three II）：Mr Penberry, the young legal clerk, hurled himself through the West End streets, scattering slower pedestrians in his wake and causing a considerable commotion. Not, he thought as he ran, and as he called out hurried apologies to those whose progress he had interrupted, that many of them would mind in the slightest if they knew that his urgent journey was to help Mr Scrooge in his hour of need. The old man was popular indeed in these parts and there were doubtless several whose path he had already crossed that afternoon whom he had assisted in the past, either in purely philanthropic terms or through his other, more mysterious work. Dodging traffic and horses and people on foot, ducking through all the bustle of the city streets, he came at last to Sargasso Street and sprinted along the pavement down to number 157. It was a grand building, with a green door, and a flight of steps which led up to that door. He ran up these and all but barrelled into the door himself, narrowly stopping in time before hammering hard with the knocker. At this ruckus, a window was opened on the first floor and a silver head, belonging to a man of distinction, peered out. "What is it? What do you want?" He sounded as though this interruption was by no means the first of the day. "You Dr Tarkin?" Penberry called up. "Of course I am! Who are you?" "My name's not important, sir!" shouted the legal clerk, with commendable modesty under the circumstances. "I'm here on behalf of Mr Scrooge!" The old fellow peered at him. "Mr Ebenezer Scrooge?" he asked. A cry went up. "The same, sir!" "Been taken ill, has he?" Mr Penberry passed a hand across his forehead. Caught up in the emergency, he had scarcely noticed how heavily he was perspiring. "Worse, sir!" he said. "Ebenezer Scrooge has been shot!" No answer came to this except the sudden slamming down of the window above. Penberry looked about him in some alarm, and was all set to hammer once again upon the front door, only for it to be thrown open and for old Dr Tarkin to emerge at a trot, still in the midst of putting on his frock coat. He did not wait for Penberry to acknowledge the situation but started off at a run, back in the direction in which the legal clerk had come. "He's at the Club, I presume?" he called back. Mr Penberry, catching up with the physician, said that yes he was, that Scrooge was still bleeding and was even now entirely insensible. "Then we should hurry!" shouted the doctor, altogether unnecessarily since they both were now running as fast as they could back towards St James's Square, to save the life of one whom they considered
