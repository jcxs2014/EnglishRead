# 16. XVI

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：Scrooge and Mrs Fell in cab rattling back towards club; after drama of evening, lady and gentleman fell into silence; "Do you know what troubles me the most?" old man asked; "It's the look in that young man's eyes... He did not seem accustomed to the hearing of it"; "You think him a fraud? Or, perhaps… a fraud until tonight?"; "I am not sure that matters are quite so plain-cut as that... I am far from certain that we are done with Mr Beck"; Mrs Fell about Talbot seeking full repayment; Scrooge chuckles, "I do wish we could have spoken directly to him... You didn't recognise him, I suppose?"; "No... family estrangement"; "Giles is never usually wrong... We must learn as soon as possible how the captain has fared tonight"; Mrs Fell asked "How about you, sir? That voice which spoke through Beck. It said it knew you. It gave its name as… Marley. Is that right?"; Scrooge nodded; "And was it? Him, I mean? Was it Mr Marley who spoke to you tonight?"; "I don't know... I think it improbable… but I'm just not certain. And I'm not even sure what's worse"; "Then what would that thing be?"; "I've a terrible feeling... this case is more than simply the haunting of a location. It seems to me that some element of possession is in play."

**情感弧线位置**：chapter 从 séance（possession）过渡到 cab conversation（Scrooge and Mrs Fell's debrief），把 narrative 从 supernatural event 转向 investigation and reflection; Scrooge's uncertainty about Marley sets up ongoing tension。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：cab conversation trope 兑现（rattling back, silence, debrief）；uncertainty trope 兑现（Scrooge "I don't know"）；possession trope 兑现（"some element of possession is in play"）。

**人物弧线**：Scrooge——从 composed to uncertain, 显示 his hidden fear of Marley; Mrs Fell——从 questioning to fixed gaze, 显示 her growing resolve to go to Brightwell Road; Beck——absent but discussed, 显示 his ambiguous status (fraud or genuine)。

**叙事手法**：third-person close on Scrooge and Mrs Fell; cab setting; dialogue-driven debrief; chapter ends with Scrooge's "possession is in play" (cliffhanger)。
