# 39. VII

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：本章把镜头移回 Starningham：Bethlem 太太「thorough but decidedly brisk」地带着 Fell 太太与 Ambrose 上尉参观 Brightwell Road 尽头那栋临海的老宅。房子状态出奇地好——房间整齐、家具齐全，作者说它有「the air of a kind of museum, preserved but unchanging」，但这「surely only a tribute to the industry and honesty of Mrs Bethlem and her late husband」。一楼三间卧室，上尉与 Fell 太太分住对门、中隔一个小楼梯平台；Bethlem 若觉得不妥也未说出口。二人获准上顶层：Bethlem 点起蜡烛交给上尉，上尉举在身前，Fell 太太立在他身侧——房间并不算太可怕，只是一间大而无家具的空屋，铺着三块磨旧的大地毯，墙上挂着那面「ornate and framed with silver」的旧镜子，比楼下冷得多。上尉一句「must be where you slept when you last visited」点破了顶层正是当年关押小女孩的房间，Fell 太太摇头不答、转身要走。两人下楼时 Bethlem 眼里闪着「something like vindication」，且明显急于离开；在门厅里她道别时留下三条提醒：「you will be perfectly happy here. You and your little one. So long as you remember what I told you」「As to anything else… you'll just have to discover it on your own. For this is a house of some surprises. Even your uncle found that to be true.」Fell 太太想开口问，却被她抬手止住：「I was quite in earnest earlier」「If you wish to ask a question, then please God let us do it outside these walls」「please, please let us do so outside. After that, I must be away」；又说「I shall tell you my address in case you should need me again.」两个女人退到街上冷风里；上尉独自留在屋内，本是孤僻的他却「difficult not to throw open the door again and go out into the night」——因为「the house in which he stood was curious about him, and that the brand of curiosity was not a friendly or an innocent one」，他「rather bristled at the notion」。他带着虚张与恼怒大声问：「So why can't we ask any questions in here, then? What's so wrong about asking b—y questions?」话音未落，屋内深处传来一声敲击——不像老屋吱呀，而「quite distinctly like the sound of knuckles upon wood」；等了一等，再无声。上尉赌气喊：「Oh, hurry up, Ebenezer… In God's name, hurry up and get to Starningham.」门再度打开，Fell 太太独自回来，说 Bethlem 已回家，并提议吃饭、也提议遵守 Bethlem 的规矩；上尉「on both counts too」地应了。两人进厨房看晚饭，在「succeeded in asking not a single question」的探索中收场。末段：楼上顶层，趁他们摆出那些派、麦酒与乡野吃食时，「something moved noiselessly in the darkness and listened.」

**情感弧线位置**：本章弧线为「参观的轻快—顶层旧镜的寒意— Bethlem 的告诫—街上的释然—独处的警觉—挑衅—敲击声—归来的稳定—厨房的沉默—黑暗中的聆听」。全章的张力不在事件而在**声响**：先是门外的对话，再是屋内的一声敲击，最后是黑暗中「listening」的东西——声音从「人声」变为「屋声」再变为「它声」。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现了「女巫/房客留下三条禁忌」的老 trope，但作者反转了重点：Bethlem 的三条规则不是用来防鬼，而是用来**防提问**——「I mean exactly what I said」；她真正要保护的是这栋房子会「answer」。而结尾的「something moved noiselessly in the darkness and listened」把「聆听者」的位置反转：之前是人听屋，最后是屋听人。

**人物弧线**：Fell 太太在本章完成从「访客」到「住户」的转变——她接受了顶层那间房（虽摇头不答），接受了「you will be perfectly happy here」的安抚，也接受了「don't ask」的沉默；她甚至主动提议遵守规矩、并且一句问题也没问。上尉则从陪同者变成独自承受者：他的转变在「he found it difficult not to throw open the door again」这句里——一个本该享受安静的人，此刻只想逃走。Bethlem 太太是本章的规则制定者兼谜面：她一切都知道，因此「eager to be away」，也因而被漏掉了最后的提问机会。

**叙事手法**：一是**感官分层**：蜡烛光（视觉）→冷（触觉）→敲击声（听觉），作者按此顺序把读者推进顶层。二是**虚词提示**：「It was, perhaps, a mere coincidence」——作者替读者标出这是巧合，随后用「No, thought the captain」把巧合否掉。三是**破折号遮挡**：「b—y questions」用遮字法表现上尉的粗暴与危险的暗示，也让「baby」一词被掐断。四是**末句倒置镜头**：全章以两个主人视角的轻松收束，突然切到顶层黑暗中的第三方。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** In Starningham, the tour of the house on Brightwell Road did not take long. As a guide, Mrs Bethlem was thorough but decidedly brisk. It soon became apparent that the house was really in excellent condition, given its circumstances. In many ways, it was a brighter (and surely a cleaner) place now than it had been when Gowing himself had lived here. The rooms were neatly kept and well furnished.

**中文理解**：在 Starningham，参观 Brightwell Road 上那栋房子并没有花多少时间。作为向导，Bethlem 太太十分周全，但相当干脆。很快就明显看出，鉴于这房子的处境，它保养得实在极好。在许多方面，它现在比 Gowing 本人住在这里时更明亮（也肯定更干净）。各个房间收拾得整齐，家具齐全。

**关键词**：the tour of the house on Brightwell Road did not take long / Mrs Bethlem was thorough but decidedly brisk / really in excellent condition / neatly kept and well furnished

**为什么这样写**：作者用「did not take long」和「decidedly brisk」在开头就把参观的节奏定死——Bethlem 太太要赶时间，这是全章的推动力。而「brighter (and surely a cleaner) place now than it had been when Gowing himself had lived here」暗示前住户留下的是脏乱，而 Gowing 正是第 1 卷里 Scrooge 追问的失踪者：他究竟是受害者还是共犯？

**读者视角提示**：Gowing 的名字在此只被提起一次，不解释。

> **原句 2:** If it had something of the air of a kind of museum, preserved but unchanging, then that was surely only a tribute to the industry and honesty of Mrs Bethlem and her late husband. The first floor had three bedrooms, and it was quickly arranged between the captain and the lady as to who should have each chamber. Theirs would be opposite one another, separated by a small landing.

**中文理解**：如果这房子有几分「博物馆般的气息——保存完好却一成不变」，那也肯定只是对 Bethlem 太太及其亡夫勤勉与诚实的敬意。一楼有三间卧室；上尉与那位女士很快便商定了各住哪一间。他们的房间彼此相对，中间隔着一小段楼梯平台。

**关键词**：the air of a kind of museum, preserved but unchanging / a tribute to the industry and honesty of Mrs Bethlem and her late husband / The first floor had three bedrooms / Theirs would be opposite one another, separated by a small landing

**为什么这样写**：博物馆这个比喻很准：一切都好，却没有任何变化——被照料得没有生气。而「opposite one another, separated by a small landing」这一安排被作者写成「quickly arranged」，轻描淡写，实则把两个人放在同一条走廊的两端，等于把一栋鬼屋的经典布景安排妥当了。

**读者视角提示**：「a small landing」是本章最关键的空间细节。

> **原句 3:** If Mrs Bethlem perceived anything indecorous or inappropriate in such an arrangement, then she did not say so. They were even permitted (for, they both later agreed, that is exactly how it felt) to see the uppermost floor. A candle was lit by Mrs Bethlem and given to the captain. He held it out before him while Mrs Fell stood by his side.

**中文理解**：如果 Bethlem 太太察觉到这样的安排有什么不得体或不合适，她也没有说。他们甚至获准（后来两人都说，那感觉确实就是这样）参观最顶层。Bethlem 太太点起一支蜡烛，交给上尉；他举在身前，Mrs Fell 站在他身旁。

**关键词**：If Mrs Bethlem perceived anything indecorous or inappropriate / she did not say so / They were even permitted / that is exactly how it felt / to see the uppermost floor / A candle was lit by Mrs Bethlem and given to the captain

**为什么这样写**：作者先说贝特勒姆太太「没有说」——她的沉默是有判断的沉默，而非没看见。随后用括号里的一句附语把叙述从当下拉回事后，让读者知道这两个人后来回想起来也觉得那是一段被许可的越界。「甚至获准」三字里的「甚至」暗示：获准看顶层，是额外开恩。

**读者视角提示**：叙述者在这里用了「后来两人都同意」的时间跳跃。

> **原句 4:** The space was not so very alarming, even seen by flickering candlelight alone. It was nothing like so clean and tidy as the two storeys which lay below it, but merely a large, unfurnished room. The only attempts at comfort of any kind were three big, frayed rugs which had been laid out upon the wooden floor.

**中文理解**：即便只靠闪烁的烛光来看，这空间也不算太吓人。它完全不像下面两层那样干净整洁，只不过是一间宽敞、没有家具的屋子。唯一称得上舒适的东西，是铺在木地板上的三块又大又磨旧的地毯。

**关键词**：not so very alarming, even seen by flickering candlelight alone / nothing like so clean and tidy as the two storeys which lay below / merely a large, unfurnished room / three big, frayed rugs / laid out upon the wooden floor

**为什么这样写**：作者先解除恐怖预期（算不上多么吓人），再补一刀（远不如下面两层干净整洁）——下面越整洁，上面越显凄凉。而「三块大而磨旧的地毯」是全章唯一被允许的舒适物：破旧、磨秃，说明这屋子的舒适是临时凑的。

**读者视角提示**：三层楼房里只有顶层有地毯，即今晚他们住的地方。

> **原句 5:** A mirror, ornate and framed with silver, hung upon the wall, as it had all those years ago. The place was quite considerably colder than the rest of the house as well. “So this,” said the captain, “must be where you slept when you last visited.” “It was.” “What a very strange choice… To put a child in here.” The lady did not reply but only shook her head and turned to leave.

**中文理解**：墙上挂着一面镜子，华丽，镶着银框——正如当年那样。这地方也比屋里别处冷得多。「这么说，」上尉说，「上次你来时就是睡在这里的。」「是的。」「真是奇怪的选择……把一个孩子安置在这种地方。」那位女士没有回答，只是摇了摇头，转身要走。

**关键词**：A mirror, ornate and framed with silver / as it had all those years ago / quite considerably colder than the rest of the house / must be where you slept when you last visited / What a very strange choice / To put a child in here / shook her head and turned to leave

**为什么这样写**：镜子与「as it had all those years ago」把第 9 章那个八岁女孩房里的大银镜重新带回读者眼前——同一面镜子，同一个房间。而「quite considerably colder」是物理上的异样，也是气氛上的异样。上尉的两句问话是全章唯一触及童年创伤的时刻；Fell 太太的摇头与转身，是她保护自己也保护孩子的方式。

**读者视角提示**：这面镜子的旧主人，正是当年被关在这里的小女孩。

> **原句 6:** With some relief, the pair descended the staircase to the first floor, where Mrs Bethlem was waiting for them with something like vindication glinting in her eyes. After that, there was little enough left for them to do. Besides, the woman seemed eager to be away. Back on the ground floor, in the hallway, Mrs Fell thanked her again for her labours. “You’re very welcome,” Mrs Bethlem said.

**中文理解**：两人如释重负地下了楼，回到一楼，Bethlem 太太正等在那里，眼里闪着「近乎 vindication（证明自己没说错）」的光。之后就没什么可做的事了。何况那女人显然急着要走。回到底层门厅，Mrs Fell 再次为她的辛劳道谢。「不客气，」Bethlem 太太说。

**关键词**：With some relief, the pair descended the staircase / something like vindication glinting in her eyes / there was little enough left for them to do / the woman seemed eager to be away / in the hallway, Mrs Fell thanked her again for her labours

**为什么这样写**：「vindication」（自证清白）在法律与宗教语境里指「证明指控成立」，用在 Bethlem 太太眼里很微妙：她像是在证明自己对这栋房子的判断（危险而不可言说）是对的。而「eager to be away」是作者给出的关键情报——她急于离开，正是为了避免被提问。

**读者视角提示**：她眼里那点得意，是本章唯一一次承认她知道些什么。

> **原句 7:** “And I know you will be perfectly happy here. You and your little one. So long as you remember what I told you. As to anything else, I dare say you’ll just have to discover it on your own. For this is a house of some surprises. Even your uncle found that to be true.”

**中文理解**：「我知道你会在这里过得 perfectly happy。你和你那个小的。只要你记得我告诉你的话。至于别的，我敢说你就得自己慢慢去发现了。因为这是一栋充满意外的房子。连你舅舅也发现过这一点。」

**关键词**：you will be perfectly happy here / You and your little one / So long as you remember what I told you / you'll just have to discover it on your own / For this is a house of some surprises / Even your uncle found that to be true

**为什么这样写**：这段话把 Bethlem 从「女房东」写成「引路人」：先给保证，再设条件，最后抛出真正的钩子。「Even your uncle found that to be true」——她提到「你舅舅」，而读者在第 9 章已知那位舅舅正是把外甥女送进这屋子的 Gowing。这句话既是安慰，也是警告：连他都吃了亏。

**读者视角提示**：Bethlem 太太口中「你舅舅」＝第 9 章的 Gowing。

> **原句 8:** “Mrs Bethlem, if I may,” began Mrs Fell, only for the other woman to hold up her hand to stop her speech. “I was quite in earnest earlier,” she said.

**中文理解**：「Mrs Bethlem，我可以问一句吗，」Mrs Fell 刚开口，对方便抬手拦住了她的话头。「我先前是认真的，」她说。

**关键词**：Mrs Bethlem, if I may / only for the other woman to hold up her hand to stop her speech / I was quite in earnest earlier

**为什么这样写**：作者用「hold up her hand」把话拦在半空——这个手势是命令，也是边界。Bethlem 太太抢在问题出口之前重新界定「认真」的含义，从而掌握谈话规则。这是全章的转折点：规则不是被告知的，是被强行执行的。

**读者视角提示**：她拦话时没说「不许问」，只说「换个地方问」。

> **原句 9:** “If you wish to ask a question, then please God let us do it outside these walls. I have a few minutes more. And if I can answer them I shall. But, please, please let us do so outside. After that, I must be away. Don’t fret. I shall tell you my address in case you should need me again.”

**中文理解**：「你若想问问题，那就求上帝让我们在这墙外面问。我还有几分钟。如果我能回答，我就回答。但求你们，求你们让我们到外面去问。之后我就得走了。别担心。我会把我的地址告诉你，以防你还需要找我。」

**关键词**：If you wish to ask a question / please God let us do it outside these walls / I have a few minutes more / If I can answer them I shall / please, please let us do so outside / After that, I must be away / I shall tell you my address in case you should need me again

**为什么这样写**：这段里有两个「please」叠用（please, please let us do so outside），是恳求，也是威胁的软化。而真正的条件藏在第一句里：她并不拒绝回答，她拒绝的是**在墙内**回答。「I have a few minutes more」——她给自己设了时限；「After that, I must be away」——时间一到就断。最后那句留下地址，是全章唯一的善意，也是唯一的钩子。

**读者视角提示**：她把「什么问题」与「在哪里问」切开了。

> **原句 10:** Another glance was exchanged between the captain and the lady at this, but, of course, Mrs Fell ended by saying that she quite understood. And so the door was opened and the two women retired to the cold of the street outside. For the first time all day, the captain was left alone. He had for many years been by nature a solitary man and he really ought to have enjoyed a few moments of quiet.

**中文理解**：上尉与那位女士就此事交换了一个眼神；当然，Mrs Fell 最后只说她完全理解。于是门开了，两个女人退到外面街上寒冷之中。一整天以来，上尉第一次独自一人。他多年来天性孤僻，本该享受几分钟安静；可恰恰相反，他发现很难不去再次推开门走进夜色里。

**关键词**：Another glance was exchanged between the captain and the lady / the door was opened / retired to the cold of the street outside / For the first time all day, the captain was left alone / He had for many years been by nature a solitary man / he really ought to have enjoyed a few moments of quiet

**为什么这样写**：作者先用一个眼神写两人的默契，再写上尉独处。作者特意强调他素来是「a solitary man」——正因为孤独惯了，享受安静对他毫不困难，读者才会感到他此刻的难受有多反常。开放式「难以不去推门走进夜色」是恐惧的直白写法。

**读者视角提示**：作者用「本该享受安静」反衬，反而是警报。

> **原句 11:** Yet, on the contrary, he found it difficult not to throw open the door again and go out into the night. For there was – absurd though it might sound – a strong sense that the house in which he stood was curious about him, and that the brand of curiosity was not a friendly or an innocent one. He found that he rather bristled at the notion.

**中文理解**：因为——尽管听来荒谬——他有一种强烈的感觉：他此刻站着的这栋房子对他感到好奇，而这种好奇的品性既不友好，也不无辜。他觉得自己对这个念头有些不耐烦，甚至有点怒意。

**关键词**：a strong sense that the house in which he stood was curious about him / the brand of curiosity was not a friendly or an innocent one / He found that he rather bristled at the notion

**为什么这样写**：作者用了被动的拟人（the house… was curious about him），把施动者从人换成屋。而「the brand of curiosity」这个略显笨拙的名词化表达反而更阴森——好奇在这里是一种「品类」，有善恶之分。上尉的「bristled」（像豪猪竖毛）把情绪写成动物性的本能反应。

**读者视角提示**：这是全章最核心的一句：好奇的是房子，而且不友好。

> **原句 12:** Then, speaking in part from bravado and in part from true irritation after a long and often vexing day, he called out: “So why can’t we ask any questions in here, then? What’s so wrong about asking b—y questions?” It was, perhaps, a mere coincidence, but it sounded very much at that moment as if a single knock was heard, not from outside the house, at door or window, but from somewhere within it.

**中文理解**：于是，他半是逞强、半是因漫长而恼人的一天而生的真气，喊道：「那么，为什么我们不能在这儿问问题？问 b—y 问题有什么不对？」也许只是巧合，但就在那一刻，声音听起来极像是有一记敲击——不是从屋外门或窗传来，而是从屋内某处。

**关键词**：in part from bravado and in part from true irritation / So why can’t we ask any questions in here / What’s so wrong about asking b—y questions / It was, perhaps, a mere coincidence / a single knock was heard / not from outside the house / but from somewhere within it

**为什么这样写**：作者先把上尉的动机说成半虚张半真怒，并让读者知道他自己也知道这是虚张（in part from bravado）。而他的问句用遮字法「b—y」（baby）——把无辜的词掐断，反而把「问孩子的问题」这层意思露出来。紧接着「It was, perhaps, a mere coincidence」是作者递给读者的一根拐杖，随即被下一段推翻。

**读者视角提示**：这声敲击在屋里，不在屋外。

> **原句 13:** It did not sound like a creak or like a noise which an old house might make when, having been left empty for so long, it suddenly acquires new residents. No, thought the captain, it sounded quite distinctly like the sound of knuckles upon wood. He waited but no further sound could be heard. “Oh, hurry up, Ebenezer,” he said defiantly. “In God’s name, hurry up and get to Starningham.”

**中文理解**：那声音不像吱呀，也不像一所被遗弃多年、忽然有了新住客的老屋会发出的响动。不，上尉心想，那声音听上去非常分明，就像指节敲在木头上的声音。他等了一阵，但再没有别的声音。「快点吧，Ebenezer，」他赌气地喊道，「看在上帝份上，快点赶到 Starningham 来。」

**关键词**：It did not sound like a creak / like a noise which an old house might make / the sound of knuckles upon wood / He waited but no further sound could be heard / In God’s name, hurry up and get to Starningham

**为什么这样写**：作者让上尉用排除法替这声音定性：不是吱呀，不是老屋自然的响动，而是「knuckles upon wood」——有人或有东西在敲门。这一次没有「perhaps a mere coincidence」的缓冲，语气笃定。而他喊的那句「快点吧，Ebenezer」是对不在场的 Scrooge 喊的，语带怨气：你就快点来把这事收了吧。

**读者视角提示**：「knuckles upon wood」把「有人」与「无物」的区别推给读者。

> **原句 14:** Then the door opened again and Mrs Fell returned to the house. She was alone. “Mrs Bethlem’s gone home,” she said by way of explanation. “And I don’t know about you, Captain, but I am now very hungry indeed. I think we should eat. And I think also that we should follow Mrs Bethlem’s rules.” “I think the same,” was all that the captain said to this, “and on both counts too.”

**中文理解**：随后门又开了，Mrs Fell 回到屋里，独自一人。「Mrs Bethlem 回家了，」她解释道，「还有，我不管上尉您怎么想，我现在确实饿坏了。我想我们该吃点东西。我还想，我们该遵守 Mrs Bethlem 的规矩。」「我同意，」上尉对她这句话只说了这一句，「而且两方面都同意。」

**关键词**：Then the door opened again and Mrs Fell returned to the house / She was alone / Mrs Bethlem’s gone home / I am now very hungry indeed / we should follow Mrs Bethlem’s rules / on both counts too

**为什么这样写**：Fell 太太进门后的第一句是解释「她一个人回来了」——但作者单独加重了「She was alone」一句，把读者的注意力钉在这件事上：Bethlem 没有一起回来，也没有人送她。上尉那句「on both counts too」呼应她的两句话（吃、守规矩），用一句轻描淡写把这笔带过——而这一句正是两人整晚唯一“一致”的时刻。

**读者视角提示**：两人达成了今晚第一个也是唯一一个共识。

> **原句 15:** They retired together to the kitchen then, to see what Mrs Bethlem had left them for their supper, in the course of which exploration they succeeded in asking not a single question. Upstairs, on the top floor, while the gentleman and lady brought forth those pies and ale (and sundry other pieces of rustic fare) which had been left for them, something moved noiselessly in the darkness and listened.

**中文理解**：于是两人一同退到厨房，去看 Bethlem 太太给他们留下的晚饭；在这次探看的过程中，他们成功地一个问题也没有问。而在楼上的顶层，趁那位绅士与那位女士摆出那些派、麦酒（以及其他各式乡野吃食）时，某个东西在黑暗中无声地移动着，听着。

**关键词**：retired together to the kitchen / succeeded in asking not a single question / Upstairs, on the top floor / those pies and ale / something moved noiselessly in the darkness and listened

**为什么这样写**：作者先用一句冷幽默收束：遵守规矩的结果就是「一个问题也没问」——这是 Bethlem 的规则被成功执行的证据（读者甚至不必追问「是什么问题」）。而最后一句是全书的镜头魔术：视点从厨房移到楼上，从人移到物，从看变成被看。「moved noiselessly… and listened」两个动词一静一动，把「它」写成一个耐心的听者。

**读者视角提示**：整本书里第一次，是这栋房子在听人说话。

## 一句话总结

本章回到 Starningham：Bethlem 太太「thorough but decidedly brisk」地领着 Fell 太太与 Ambrose 上尉参观 Brightwell Road 尽头的老宅，房子状态好得惊人（「neatly kept and well furnished」），有「the air of a kind of museum, preserved but unchanging」；一楼三间卧室，两人分住对门、中隔「a small landing」；获准上顶层时 Bethlem 点烛交给上尉，房间只是一间「large, unfurnished room」，地上铺着三块「big, frayed rugs」，墙上仍是那面「ornate and framed with silver」的旧镜子，「quite considerably colder than the rest of the house」——上尉一句「must be where you slept when you last visited」「What a very strange choice… To put a child in here」让 Fell 太太「shook her head and turned to leave」；下楼时 Bethlem 眼里闪着「something like vindication」且「eager to be away」，在门厅留下告诫：「you will be perfectly happy here… So long as you remember what I told you」「you'll just have to discover it on your own. For this is a house of some surprises. Even your uncle found that to be true.」；Fell 太太刚要开口问，她抬手止住：「I was quite in earnest earlier」「please, please let us do so outside. After that, I must be away.」「I shall tell you my address in case you should need me again.」两个女人退到街上，上尉独自留下；素来孤僻的他本该享受安静，却「difficult not to throw open the door again and go out into the night」，因为他强烈感到「the house in which he stood was curious about him, and that the brand of curiosity was not a friendly or an innocent one」，「he rather bristled at the notion」；他半是逞强半是真怒地喊「What's so wrong about asking b—y questions?」，屋内随即传出一声敲击——「not from outside the house, at door or window, but from somewhere within it」，「quite distinctly like the sound of knuckles upon wood」；他赌气喊「In God's name, hurry up and get to Starningham」；Fell 太太独自回来，说 Bethlem 已回家，提议吃饭并「we should follow Mrs Bethlem's rules」，上尉「on both counts too」地应允；两人进厨房看晚饭，在「succeeded in asking not a single question」中收场，而顶层黑暗中「something moved noiselessly in the darkness and listened」。
