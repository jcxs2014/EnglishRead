# 24. VI

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：墓园里，一条“lean and black”的黑狗龇牙低吼，Scrooge“stayed stock still, knowing better than to back away”，在“on the edge of flight”之际被人一声“Hi! Hi! Away with you!”救下；来者手执“一根粗棍”，自称 Bel 的丈夫 Jack Trelawney——他与 Scrooge 站在“the same gravestone”两侧，一个问“don't I know you?”，另一个以“You were, Mr Trelawney. You were.”把自己也判为“曾是”；Bel 生前“mentioned you from time to time”且“with great kindness”，他怀疑那不过是“a benign piece of fiction”；他自称“Transformed entirely”，也说“I find that business rather takes care of itself nowadays”；他明言未退休，因“For I have a mighty debt to work off”、“Much to sacrifice and many yet to help and protect”，并已许诺“travel to Norfolk by nightfall”；临别 Trelawney 送出 Bel 的圣经（“It was her Bible”），称“she would have wanted you to have this”，Scrooge 推辞后收进“the right-hand pocket of his jacket”；末段 Trelawney 独自祈祷，却仿佛又见那黑狗“padding behind Ebenezer as if in pursuit of him”，眨眼之后“both man and hound (if it had even been there at all) had vanished”。

**情感弧线位置**：全书情感密度最高的一章，弧线为「对峙—解围—认出—揭伤—被原谅—立誓—赠物—诡异收尾」。前二块是身体性的恐惧；三、四块身份揭开；五、六块是两位幸存者之间关于 Bel 的对谈，情绪最软；七、八块 Scrooge 说出“a mighty debt to work off”，是全章暖心高点；末块再把刚建立的暖意翻成不安——那条狗不见了。

**Tropes 兑现/反转**：兑现“Bel 之死与 Scrooge 的关系”这一悬念的第一层。反转有四：其一，救场者是 Bel 的丈夫，而 Scrooge 是他从未交谈却“not entirely a stranger”的人；其二，Bel 临终仍说他的好话，Scrooge 却认定那是“a benign piece of fiction”；其三，他说“Transformed entirely”，Trelawney 只回一句“Well, that's very good, I expect”——旁人并不关心他变成了什么，只关心 Bel 怎么看他；其四，最重要的反转在末句：狗不是恶犬，而是跟着他、护送甚至追他去的某物，作者用“if it had even been there at all”把整场遭遇悬置为幻觉。

**人物弧线**：Scrooge 在本章第一次被陌生人指着说“you look… different somehow”并以 Bel 的名义善待；随即把这份善意转成行动纲领“a mighty debt to work off”。他仍不敢接那本圣经（“I can't possibly accept this”），仍要问“Why?”——他依旧不习惯被爱，这与上一章“this may be goodbye”连成一线：他在离开前把该还的都还了。Jack Trelawney 则以鳏夫的克制完成了全书最关键的一次推动：他不追问 Scrooge 的过去，只给一句“Providence works at a pace that is all its own”，并把 Bel 的圣经交出去。

**叙事手法**：全章是“动作—对话—赠礼”三段式，夹在两块强悬念括号之间。开头用动物视角制造威胁，结尾用旁观者视角把威胁改写为守护（“padding behind Ebenezer as if in pursuit of him”——追，其实是护送），同一只狗因此被重定义。作者大量使用英国式轻描淡写来缓冲重击：“Age, I fear to relate, does not make encounters such as these any easier to bear”，以及一句反讽的“a thing which was not entirely true but nonetheless the kind of remark which convention dictates is made by the graveside”——用体面的客套反衬两人心里真正的东西。

## 精读

> **原句 1:** In the cemetery, Ebenezer Scrooge turned to face the animal that had so rudely interrupted his reverie. … The beast was lean and black. … Its teeth were bared, and as the old man turned to face it, the creature snarled once again. … You may take it from me that even the most ardent admirer of canine kind – the greatest enthusiast alive for the companionship that may be provided to Man by the best of dogs – would have flinched at the sight of this dark brute. … Another snarl.

**中文理解**：在墓园里，Ebenezer Scrooge 转身面对那只粗鲁打断他默想的动物。那畜生又瘦又黑，龇着牙；老人转身面向它的当口，它又嘶吼了一声。您尽可相信：即便是最爱犬类之徒——世上最爱犬类、最享受人与最好的狗之间那份陪伴的狂热分子——看到这头黑家伙也会退缩。它又吼了一声。

**关键词**：so rudely interrupted his reverie / lean and black / Its teeth were bared / the most ardent admirer of canine kind / would have flinched / this dark brute / Another snarl

**为什么这样写**：开头一句就用拟人化的“so rudely interrupted his reverie”——“reverie”（默想、遐思）把 Scrooge 的悼念抬成一个带文学色彩的静场，“rudely”则立刻把那个静场撕开；语序上还让狗先“打断”再现身，读者先听见动作、后看见形象。随后两句是速写：“lean and black”六个词给出全部外形，“Its teeth were bared”七个词给出全部威胁。作者接着用夸张到滑稽的插入语——“the greatest enthusiast alive for the companionship that may be provided to Man by the best of dogs”——把读者从恐惧里拽出来：连最爱狗的人都会退缩，说明这不是普通野狗。末句“Another snarl”单独成句，把恐怖悬在半空不落地。

**读者视角提示**：注意“by the best of dogs”这个对照——作者已经在暗示：黑狗未必是“最差”的。

> **原句 2:** Scrooge stayed stock still, knowing better than to back away. … At that time, wild dogs were still by no means unknown in the viler quarters of the city, and it was of these dangerous aberrations that the old man first took it to be. … Then the animal snapped its jaws and made as if to spring. … The old man was on the edge of flight and trusting to providence that he could outpace the creature (a feat which seemed most unlikely) when he heard a voice call out: “Hi! Hi! Away with you!”

**中文理解**：Scrooge 站着一动不动，清楚地知道最好别后退。那年头，野狗在城里下等街区并不算稀罕，老人起初就把这只畜生看成这类危险的变种。随后那动物咔哒一声咬合牙关，作势要扑。老人已到了要拔腿就跑的地步，只能把身家性命托付给天意——指望自己能跑得比这畜生快（这看起来极不靠谱）——就在此时他听见有人喊了一嗓子：“Hi! Hi! Away with you!”

**关键词**：stayed stock still / knowing better than to back away / wild dogs were still by no means unknown / the viler quarters of the city / dangerous aberrations / snapped its jaws / on the edge of flight / Hi! Hi! Away with you!

**为什么这样写**：这一段把动作节奏推到最高点，作者用短促动词串起恐惧：“stayed stock still”——他知道后退会招致追击，这是经验之谈；“snapped its jaws and made as if to spring”——“as if”（好像）是关键，作者写的是姿态而非事实，紧张感来自不确定。“on the edge of flight”把逃跑写成一个临界状态而不是已经发生；结尾以“when he heard a voice call out”让救援恰好卡在最后一刻，而那声呼喝本身粗野、扁平，像街上赶狗人的口气。

**读者视角提示**：野狗的设定让读者以为这只是街头遭遇；记住这条狗是“lean and black”。

> **原句 3:** Behind the dog appeared another man, some few years younger than Scrooge, though looking in rather ruder health. … He held in his right hand a stout stick which he now held upright as if he meant to strike the dog in self-defence. … At this approach, and with a final snarl (which sounded to Scrooge altogether too much like frustration), the dog loped away. … The stranger approached, and as he did so, Scrooge realised that he was not entirely a stranger to him for all that they had never previously spoken.

**中文理解**：狗后面现出另一个男人，比 Scrooge 年轻几岁，但看上去健康得多。他右手握着一根粗棍，此刻直竖着，仿佛要朝狗打过去自卫。随着他走近，那狗发出最后一声嘶吼（在 Scrooge 听来，那声音实在太像 frustration——不甘、憋闷），随后便踱步跑开了。那陌生人走近；随着他的靠近，Scrooge 意识到，尽管他们从未交谈过，他对自己却并不算完全陌生。

**关键词**：a stout stick / as if he meant to strike the dog in self-defence / a final snarl / too much like frustration / the dog loped away / not entirely a stranger

**为什么这样写**：三句之内完成解围，但作者不肯让它干脆：狗是“loped away”——轻快的小跑，不是仓皇逃窜；它离开前还有“a final snarl”，而且这声嘶吼在 Scrooge 听来“too much like frustration”，这是全书第一次暗示狗的情绪不纯：它像是不甘心被打断。最后一句“not entirely a stranger… for all that they had never previously spoken”是全章第一个反常：解释只有两个——他认得他，或者他知道他。

**读者视角提示**：注意那根手杖是“as if he meant to strike the dog in self-defence”——他是防狗，不是打狗。

> **原句 4:** The man raised his hand in greeting. … “I owe you my thanks,” said Scrooge, perhaps a little more frostily than he might have wished. … “Oh, I doubt he would have done you any serious mischief,” said the newcomer. “Just hungry, I expect.” … “I am not so sure about that,” said Scrooge. “And so, once again: thank you.” … The other man nodded. … He stood beside the same gravestone as that which Ebenezer had come to visit.

**中文理解**：那人抬手致意。“我该谢你，”Scrooge 说道，语气也许比他自己希望的更冷硬些。“哦，我信那畜生不会对你造成什么严重伤害，”新来者说，“我想它不过是饿了。”“这一点我也说不准，”Scrooge 说，“那么，再说一次：谢谢。”对方点了点头。他站在 Scrooge 这次要拜的那同一块墓碑旁。

**关键词**：raised his hand in greeting / perhaps a little more frostily than he might have wished / serious mischief / Just hungry, I expect / once again: thank you / the same gravestone

**为什么这样写**：这里的对白节奏极快，作者用三个“谢谢”和一连串否认把礼貌写成一场互相防范的谈判。Scrooge 那句“perhaps a little more frostily than he might have wished”是全书式的人物注脚：作者不写他冷漠，只写他自己也觉得冷了一点——自责藏在一个副词里。而句末“the same gravestone”把两个人的注意力第一次放到同一块石头上，读者这才意识到：他不是随便路过。

**读者视角提示**：两人站在同一块碑前——这个巧合就是全章的引擎。

> **原句 5:** “Forgive me, sir,” said the man, “but don’t I know you? It is Mr Scrooge, isn’t it?” … “It is, sir. Yes.” … “Jack Trelawney.” The man extended his hand and Scrooge shook it. … “Ah,” said Scrooge, thinking of the last time that he had seen the gentleman in question, in a kind of vision that had been presented to him by a spirit on the night before Christmas some years before. “You were her husband,” he said, as though he did not already know the answer.

**中文理解**：“请原谅，先生，”那人说道，“我是不是认得你？你是 Scrooge 先生吧？”“是我，先生。是的。”“Jack Trelawney。”那人伸出手，Scrooge 握住了。“啊，”Scrooge 说道，他想起上一次见到这位先生，是在某个圣诞夜被一个幽灵以幻象的形式呈现在他面前的时候。“你曾是她丈夫，”他说，仿佛他并不知道答案。

**关键词**：don’t I know you / It is Mr Scrooge, isn’t it? / extended his hand and Scrooge shook it / a vision presented to him by a spirit / You were her husband

**为什么这样写**：身份揭晓被拆成四步，每一步都留了缝隙：先伸手致意 → 报出名字 → 握手 → Scrooge 那句翻转时间的“Ah”。最妙的是那句回忆——上一次见到这位先生，竟是“一个幽灵以幻象的形式”在某个圣诞夜呈现给他的：全书第一次把 Scrooge 的圣诞奇遇与 Bel 的丈夫接上头。而“You were her husband”他明知故问，“as though he did not already know the answer”，把两人各自的试探写成了同一种怯懦。

**读者视角提示**：Scrooge 记得 Bel 丈夫的脸——这是他从没忘过的证据。

> **原句 6:** “I was, sir, yes, and most fortunate I was indeed to have that honour.” … “You were, Mr Trelawney. You were.” … They looked at one another for a long minute after that. … Age, I fear to relate, does not make encounters such as these any easier to bear. … “She mentioned you from time to time,” he said. “Always with great kindness when she did so.” … Mr Scrooge – who suspected this of being a benign piece of fiction – murmured that such remarks demonstrated the excellence of the lady’s character.

**中文理解**：“是的，先生，我确是，而且能拥有那份荣幸，我实在幸运之至。”“你曾是，Trelawney 先生。你曾是。”此后两人长久地对望了一分钟。我恐怕得说：年纪并不会让这样的相遇更容易承受。“她偶尔会提起你，”他说，“每次提起都带着极大的善意。”Scrooge——他怀疑这不过是一段无伤大雅的虚构——含糊地表示，这类话足以证明那位女士的品性高洁。

**关键词**：most fortunate I was indeed to have that honour / You were, Mr Trelawney. You were. / does not make encounters such as these any easier to bear / mentioned you from time to time / with great kindness / a benign piece of fiction

**为什么这样写**：这是全书关于 Scrooge 过去最锋利的一处对照。“you were her husband”那句用的是过去时的时态歧义：“were”与“was”同形，Scrooge 把它读成“你曾是（不再是）”，Trelawney 却答“I was, sir, yes”——一个时态差把两个人的位置差摆明了。“You were, Mr Trelawney. You were.”同一句话说了两遍，Scrooge 像在说服自己：他只是个“曾是”的人。而“Age, I fear to relate”这种十九世纪小说家的口头禅，既示弱又保持体面，还把读者的同情调动起来。

**读者视角提示**：“You were… You were.”是 Scrooge 对自己婚姻的判决——他把自己也算进了“曾是”里。

> **原句 7:** Mr Trelawney agreed that this was so. … “I hope you’ll forgive me for saying so, sir,” said the widower, “but you look… different somehow than when I glimpsed you last.” … “Oh, I have been changed,” Scrooge said, “yes. Transformed entirely.” … “Well, that’s very good, I expect.” … “Yes, Mr Trelawney. Yes, it is.” … “And how do you spend your days now, sir? No doubt you’re knee-deep in matters of business, just as much as ever you were?”

**中文理解**：Trelawney 先生说确是这样。“请原谅我这么说，先生，”那位鳏夫说道，“不过您看起来……和上次我瞥见您时不太一样了。”“哦，我是变了，”Scrooge 说，“是的。全然变了。”“那很好，我猜。”“是的，Trelawney 先生。是啊，是的。”“那么您现在怎么打发日子呢？您想必仍像从前那样，深深扎在事务堆里吧？”

**关键词**：different somehow / Transformed entirely / that’s very good, I expect / knee-deep in matters of business

**为什么这样写**：这一段把“人世已改”摆上台面，而 Trelawney 的回应冷淡得让人心凉：“Well, that's very good, I expect”——他关心的不是 Scrooge 变成了什么，而是 Bel 怎么看他，所以对“全然变了”只给一句客套的“那很好”。“somehow”被保留下来，鳏夫自己也说不清变化在哪，于是“变”这件事只落在读者眼里。而问句里的“knee-deep in matters of business”仍是商人的刻度，Scrooge 恰恰要在这句话上作答。

**读者视角提示**：Scrooge 说“事务会自己照料自己”——这句话把他一生追求的东西一笔勾销。

> **原句 8:** Mr Scrooge smiled then, for the first time that morning. “Oh, I find that business rather takes care of itself nowadays.” … “Then you are doubtless enjoying your retirement?” … “I’m not yet retired, sir. Nor do I have any expectation of becoming so. For I have a mighty debt to work off. Much to sacrifice and many yet to help and protect. Speaking of which, I must away. I have made a promise to travel to Norfolk by nightfall.”

**中文理解**：那天早上，Scrooge 第一次笑了。“哦，我发现如今那些事务倒会自己照料自己。”“那么您想必是在安享退休生活了？”“我还没退休，先生，也不指望退休。因为我有一笔大债要还。有许多东西要牺牲，还有许多人要帮助、要保护。说到这个，我得走了。我已经许诺要在天黑之前赶到 Norfolk。”

**关键词**：business rather takes care of itself / enjoying your retirement / a mighty debt to work off / Much to sacrifice / travel to Norfolk by nightfall

**为什么这样写**：Scrooge 那句“business rather takes care of itself nowadays”是全书最漂亮的一次反转：那个曾把利润算到骨髓的人，现在让生意自己管自己——用商人的语法说出放弃的话。而他随即否认退休，用的仍是账目语言：“a mighty debt to work off”——把“补偿”说成还债，是把愧疚折算成资产负债；“Much to sacrifice”用复数名词把代价写成清单。最后“a promise to travel to Norfolk by nightfall”把动机落在具体的时限与地点上——这是全书第一次给出他此行的硬约束，也是读者能核对的承诺。

**读者视角提示**：记住这个承诺——天黑前到 Norfolk；后面所有时间压力都挂在这句话上。

> **原句 9:** Trelawney wished Scrooge luck with all of this and said how nice it was to have met him properly at last (a thing which was not entirely true but nonetheless the kind of remark which convention dictates is made by the graveside). … “Can I say something, sir?” said Scrooge, just before they parted. … “Of course.”

**中文理解**：Trelawney 祝他这一切顺利，并说到底见到他本人真是太好了（这并不全真，但却是墓前礼节规定要说的话）。“我能说句话吗，先生？”临别前 Scrooge 问道。“当然。”

**关键词**：not entirely true / a kind of remark / Can I say something / Of course

**为什么这样写**：作者把客套话标成了客套话：那句话虽然是“并不全真”，却仍是“礼节规定在墓前要说的那一类客套”——括号一插，社交的虚伪与真实同时在场：Trelawney 说这话不假，因为 Bel 的墓就在两人之间；而作者点破它，只为了让接下来那句真心话更有分量。而 Scrooge 那声“Can I say something, sir?”配一个 Of course，礼貌得近乎正式，像在请求一次发言许可。

**读者视角提示**：作者点破客套，是因为下一句才是真话。

> **原句 10:** “You’re very fortunate, sir. You really were. And are. I only wish that I had seen the truth much earlier in life, as, evidently, did you.” … Trelawney smiled a deep, kind smile. “We all of us realise what we are meant to realise only when we are supposed to realise it. Dear me, what a tangled sentence. But you comprehend my meaning?” … “I do, sir. Yes. Indeed, I do. Providence works at a pace that is all its own.” … Trelawney agreed that this was so before something seemed to occur to him.

**中文理解**：“您真幸运，先生。您过去真幸运。现在也幸运。我只恨自己没能更早看清真相——显然您是看清了的。”Trelawney 露出一个深沉而善意的微笑。“我们所有人，都只在该明白的时候才明白自己该明白什么。哎呀，这句子真绕。但你懂我的意思吧？”“我懂，先生。懂。确实懂。天意自有它的步速。”Trelawney 认同这一点，接着像是忽然想起什么。

**关键词**：You’re very fortunate / seen the truth much earlier in life / a deep, kind smile / realise what we are meant to realise / Providence works at a pace

**为什么这样写**：这是全书作者最明显的一次“现身说法”：Trelawney 说完那句绕口的哲理，自己先打趣“Dear me, what a tangled sentence”——作者借人物之口承认自己写得拗口，而读者在此刻与 Scrooge 同步了理解。而 Scrooge 那句三段递进的敬语——“You really were. And are.”——把祝福从亡者挪到当下：这是他第一次开始注意时态。“Providence works at a pace that is all its own”既是全书关于巧合的总纲，也是 Trelawney 解释自己为何在此的台词。

**读者视角提示**：注意“You were… And are.”——Scrooge 开始用时态了。

> **原句 11:** As if acting on a whim, he reached into his pocket and took out a small, leather-bound book. “Here,” he said. “I would like you to have this. Or, rather, I have the sudden sensation that she would have wanted you to have this. Belle, I mean.” … Almost gingerly, Scrooge reached out to take it. “What is it?” he asked. … “It was her Bible. She always had it with her.” … Scrooge touched the cover of the book. “I can’t…” he said. “I can’t possibly accept this. It must be very precious to you.”

**中文理解**：他像是随心所欲一般伸手进口袋，取出一本小小的皮面装订的书。“给，”他说，“我想把它送给你。或者更确切地说，我忽然觉得她一定希望你收下它。Bel，我是说。”Scrooge 几乎是小心翼翼地伸手去接。“这是什么？”他问。“是她的圣经。她总随身带着。”Scrooge 抚摸着书的封面。“我不能……”他说，“我绝不能收下这个。这对你必定极为珍贵。”

**关键词**：a small, leather-bound book / the sudden sensation that she would have wanted you to have this / Belle, I mean / Almost gingerly / It was her Bible / very precious to you

**为什么这样写**：这是全书最动人的一段，力量全部来自“她”。Trelawney 三次把赠予的主语从自己换成 Bel，最后那句“Belle, I mean”这个自我更正，把死人搬进了当下。而 Scrooge 的反应是一个完整的推拒链：先“Almost gingerly”伸手——身体已经接受；再“It must be very precious to you”——他第一反应仍是替对方着想，这是他一生最真实的性格。而 Trelawney 把一件沉重的赠礼说得极轻：“As if acting on a whim”（像随心所欲一般）。

**读者视角提示**：那本书是 Bel 的圣经；送它的是 Bel 的意思，不是 Trelawney 自己的意思。

> **原句 12:** “I think,” began Trelawney, “that it really ought to be yours now.” … “Why?” asked Ebenezer. … “Do you know, I’m not entirely certain? Seeing you here today after all these years… The dog… And, well, providence, I suppose.” … And after that, all Scrooge could do was to say thank you and to accept this gift and stow it away in the right-hand pocket of his jacket, and to shake Trelawney’s hand. “Thank you,” he said once again upon his departure.

**中文理解**：“我想，”Trelawney 开口，“它实在该归你了。”“为什么？”Ebenezer 问道。“这个嘛，我也不完全确定。多年之后今天在这里见到你……那条狗……还有，呃，我想是天意吧。”此后 Scrooge 能做的只有道谢、收下这份礼物，把它塞进外套的右侧口袋，再与 Trelawney 握手。“谢谢，”临别时他又说了一遍。

**关键词**：it really ought to be yours now / Why? / I’m not entirely certain / The dog / stow it away in the right-hand pocket of his jacket

**为什么这样写**：全章最重的信息落在“The dog”三个字上——Trelawney 把它解释为天意，读者会另有想法。而 Scrooge 的推拒链在此收口：他先摸书、再说“very precious to you”，第一反应仍是替对方着想。作者用一个极省的句式收束这场交割：“all Scrooge could do was to say thank you and to accept this gift and stow it away in the right-hand pocket of his jacket”——三个动作一气呵成，说明他心里其实早有接受的理由，嘴上那两句推辞只是礼数；“stow it away”用的正是商人的动词。

**读者视角提示**：Bel 的圣经从此在 Scrooge 口袋里——这是全书为数不多的实体信物。

> **原句 13:** Mr Trelawney lingered there a while longer and murmured a prayer over the grave. … This done, he looked up again and watched the figure of the old man disappear back into the city. … As he did so, Trelawney witnessed a moment of oddity. … For an instant, he thought that he saw the black dog again, padding behind Ebenezer as if in pursuit of him. … Then he blinked, rubbed his eyes and looked again only to see that both man and hound (if it had even been there at all) had vanished.

**中文理解**：Trelawney 先生在墓边又站了一会儿，低声为这座坟做了祷告。做完这些，他抬头再看，只见老人的身影消失在城中。就在这时，Trelawney 目睹了一件怪事。有那么一瞬，他以为又看见了那条黑狗，正小跑着跟在 Ebenezer 身后，仿佛在追赶他。随后他眨了眨眼，揉了揉眼睛，再看时却只见人和狗（如果它当真存在过的话）都已不见踪影。

**关键词**：murmured a prayer over the grave / disappear back into the city / witnessed a moment of oddity / padding behind Ebenezer as if in pursuit / if it had even been there at all

**为什么这样写**：结尾用一句陈述句和一串精确到近乎临床的动作收束全章：“witnessed a moment of oddity”——作者不用“supernatural”，只用“oddity”（怪事），把判断权留给读者。“padding behind Ebenezer as if in pursuit of him”是全书最锋利的措辞：追，这个动词把威胁改写成了护送；“as if”又给读者留了退路。最后“if it had even been there at all”用一整个条件从句把整场遭遇降格为幻觉可能，而“had vanished”里的完成时让两个“消失”一先一后。

**读者视角提示**：那条狗究竟是恶犬、幻象，还是别的什么——作者在这里拒绝给答案。

## 一句话总结

本章在墓园里把 Scrooge 与 Bel 的丈夫 Jack Trelawney 摆到同一块碑前：先是一条“lean and black”的黑狗龇牙扑来，他“stayed stock still”，靠一声“Hi! Hi! Away with you!”解围，狗“loped away”时那声“final snarl”在他听来“too much like frustration”；来者正是 Trelawney，他“stood beside the same gravestone”，认出他“don't I know you? It is Mr Scrooge, isn't it?”，Scrooge 则用 “You were, Mr Trelawney. You were.”（你曾是）两次把自己也判为“曾是”；Bel 临终仍“mentioned you from time to time”且“with great kindness”，他怀疑那不过是“a benign piece of fiction”；他承认“Transformed entirely”，却也说“I find that business rather takes care of itself nowadays”；他明言未退休，因为“For I have a mighty debt to work off”、“Much to sacrifice and many yet to help and protect”，并已许诺“travel to Norfolk by nightfall”；临别 Trelawney 送出 Bel 的圣经（“It was her Bible”），理由是“she would have wanted you to have this”，Scrooge 推辞再三后收进“the right-hand pocket of his jacket”；末段 Trelawney 独自祈祷，却仿佛又看见那条黑狗“padding behind Ebenezer as if in pursuit of him”，眨眼再看，“both man and hound (if it had even been there at all) had vanished”。
