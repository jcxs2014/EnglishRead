# The Leaving Season

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：Burt（ abusive husband）wakes to find wife Hilda gone；Hilda（cancer, "exit"）leaves with antler crown (deer head antlers) into the night lights; Burt drives to Royal (pub), sees Hilda in headlights, she charges with antlers; story ends with her charge. "Leaving Season" = women leave abusive husbands (like Burt's mother, who walked into lights).

- **时间**：当代 Australian Outback

- **地点**：farmhouse (Outback); Royal (pub in town)

- **人物**：Burt (abusive husband, farmer)；Hilda (wife, cancer, "exit")；Grace & Evelyn (daughters, estranged)；Burt's mother (left via "lights" when Burt was 10)；Margery (Hilda's mother, warned her)；Barb (widow publican)

- **叙事手法**：Third-person (Burt) + Hilda's POV (intercut); Australian Outback setting; "Leaving Season" as euphemism for women's escape; lights as psychopomp; antler crown as transformation; "crown of horns; crown of thorns"

- **与后文的钩子**：Strahan Introduction 提到 "Angela Slatter's story of a woman who leaves her husband, and the season when women walk into the light." 本篇是选集的 feminist horror 代表——death 作为 escape from abuse; "Leaving Season" = women's collective departure; Hilda's cancer as "exit" (choice). Theme: death as liberation; abusive marriage as death-in-life; women's collective escape.

## 精读

> **原句 1:** "The start of the season."

- **中文理解**：「The start of the season。」 （本块引语分析见上）

- **关键词**：start of the season

- **为什么这样写**：Slatter's "season" = euphemism for women's collective escape from abuse. "Start of the season" = predictable, annual, like harvest. Burt has seen it (father, brother, friends) but ignored it. "Season" naturalizes women's escape—like weather, unavoidable. This is the story's premise: women leave.

- **读者视角提示**：Slatter's "season" = women's escape euphemism. "Start of the season" = annual, predictable. Burt's denial = his tragedy.

> **原句 2:** "crown of horns; crown of thorns."

- **中文理解**：「crown of horns; crown of thorns。」 （本块引语分析见上）

- **关键词**：crown of horns / crown of thorns / Queenly

- **为什么这样写**：Slatter's "crown of horns; crown of thorns" = dual transformation—animal power (horns) + Christian suffering (thorns). "Queenly" = Hilda claims queenly authority (after 50 years of subjugation). Antlers = deer's power (male deer, but Hilda claims it). "Crown of thorns" = Christ's suffering, but Hilda's suffering is at Burt's hands. This is the story's feminist thesis: abused wife claims power through transformation.

- **读者视角提示**：Slatter's "crown of horns; crown of thorns" = dual symbolism. Horns = animal power; thorns = suffering. "Queenly" = authority claimed.

> **原句 3:** "Hilda opens the front door, floats onto the verandah."

- **中文理解**：「Hilda opens the front door, floats onto the verandah。」 （本块引语分析见上）

- **关键词**：opens the front door / floats onto the verandah

- **为什么这样写**：Slatter's "floats" = Hilda's transformation—no longer earthbound, no longer Burt's. "Floats" = weightless departure. This is the story's psychopomp imagery—lights guide women away. Hilda follows "other footprints" (women who left before). "Floats" = death's lightness.

- **读者视角提示**：Slatter's "floats" = transformation. Hilda no longer earthbound. Lights = psychopomp.

> **原句 4:** "Queenly, she thinks, nods. Feels the antlers bow and sway with the motion of her head."

- **中文理解**：「Queenly, she thinks, nods。Feels the antlers bow and sway with the motion of her head。」 （本块引语分析见上）

- **关键词**：Queenly / antlers bow and sway

- **为什么这样写**：Slatter's "Queenly" = Hilda's self-assertion—after 50 years of subjugation, she claims queenly authority. "Antlers bow and sway" = regal, animal power. This is the story's feminist thesis: transformation through self-assertion. Hilda's antler crown = her reclaimed power.

- **读者视角提示**：Slatter's "Queenly" = self-assertion. Antlers = reclaimed power. Transformation complete.

> **原句 5:** "And even if he could find that little address book—Did it have a pink cover? Did he remember a small pink book in her hands?—even if he could find those numbers, make his thick fingers dial, what would he say?"

- **中文理解**：「And even if he could find that little address book—Did it have a pink cover? Did he remember a small pink book in her hands?—even if he could find those numbers, make his thick fingers dial, what would he say?」 （本块引语分析见上）

- **关键词**：little address book / thick fingers dial / what would he say

- **为什么这样写**：Slatter's "little address book" = Hilda's invisible labor—managing all connections. Burt's "thick fingers" = physicality without finesse. "What would he say?" = Burt has no emotional vocabulary. This is the story's critique: abusive men depend on wives they abuse; when wives leave, men are helpless.

- **读者视角提示**：Slatter's "little address book" = Hilda's invisible labor. Burt's helplessness = consequence of abuse.

> **原句 6:** "His wife squares her shoulders, settles her feet into the dirt for purchase, drops her head, antlers pointed in the direction of the oncoming vehicle, and begins her charge . . ."

- **中文理解**：「His wife squares her shoulders, settles her feet into the dirt for purchase, drops her head, antlers pointed in the direction of the oncoming vehicle, and begins her charge . . .」 （本块引语分析见上）

- **关键词**：squares her shoulders / settles her feet / antlers pointed / begins her charge

- **为什么这样写**：Slatter's ending—Hilda charges Burt's ute with antlers. "Squares her shoulders" = warrior; "settles her feet" = grounded; "antlers pointed" = weaponized transformation. "Begins her charge" = active resistance, not victimhood. This is the story's climax: abused wife becomes charging deer. Burt's headlights = civilization; Hilda's charge = nature's revenge. The story ends with her charge—ambiguous (hit by car? or charges through?).

- **读者视角提示**：Slatter's ending = Hilda charges Burt. "Antlers pointed" = weaponized transformation. Ambiguous ending: hit or charge through?

## 一句话总结

Slatter 的 "The Leaving Season" 是选集中最 feminist horror 的 story：Burt（abusive husband）wakes to find wife Hilda gone；Hilda（cancer, "exit"）leaves with antler crown (deer head antlers) into the night lights; Burt drives to Royal (pub), sees Hilda in headlights, she charges with antlers; story ends with her charge. Slatter 的 thesis："death as escape from abuse"—"Leaving Season" = women's collective departure from abusive husbands; Hilda's cancer as "exit" (choice, not just disease); "crown of horns; crown of thorns" = dual transformation (animal power + Christian suffering); "Queenly" = abused wife claims authority. "Floats onto the verandah" = weightless departure (transformation). "Grief is all the love that remains" (not this story, but theme). "The start of the season" = women's escape as annual, predictable (like weather). Burt's helplessness (can't find address book, can't call daughters) = consequence of abuse. "She squares her shoulders... begins her charge" = abused wife becomes charging deer (active resistance). "Antlers pointed" = weaponized transformation. This is the anthology's "death as liberation" counterpoint. Theme: death as escape from abuse; "Leaving Season" = women's collective departure; Hilda's cancer as "exit" (choice); transformation through self-assertion; "crown of horns; crown of thorns" = power + suffering. The story's horror: Burt's realization that he drove Hilda away; his helplessness; Hilda's charge as final act. Death as liberation from abuse. "And it wasn't anything like he'd hoped it would be" = Burt's loneliness (consequence of abuse). Death as consequence and liberation.
