# The Heart in Service to Hunger

## 本章导航

- **一句话概括**：Narrator (Vietnamese maid "Peony"/"Rose") 与 Xiulan (Chinese maid, ill, hungry) 服侍 Page family；Xiulan 病重， narrator 偷 food for her；Xiulan becomes monster (eats Mrs. Page's tongue/jaw), narrator helps her escape; on train, Xiulan says "sister, I'm hungry"—narrator realizes Xiulan will eat people (including narrator?). Death as hunger; "I'm hungry" refrain; transformation.

- **时间**：19th century? (American South, Hollycross, Pages, servants)

- **地点**：Hollycross (Page house); Chicago Union Station

- **人物**：Narrator (Vietnamese maid, "Peony"/"Rose", orphan of Saigon port)；Xiulan (Chinese maid, Guangzhou, ill, becomes monster)；Mrs. Page (mistress, killed by Xiulan)；Mr. Page (killed by Xiulan)

- **叙事手法**：First-person (narrator); Southern Gothic; death as hunger; "I'm hungry" refrain; Xiulan's transformation from ill maid to monster; narrator's complicity (helps Xiulan escape); "kind of living death" as maid work

- **与后文的钩子**：Strahan Introduction 提到 "Nghi Vo's story of two maids in a household, and the hunger that consumes them." 本篇是选集的 Southern Gothic 代表——death 作为 hunger; "I'm hungry" = death's refrain; Xiulan's transformation from ill to monster; narrator's complicity. Theme: death as hunger; "kind of living death" = maid work; "I'm hungry" = death's voice; narrator's love/complicity.

## 精读

> **原句 1:** "I killed the cockerels in the morning and at eight o’clock they hit the table for Mr. Page’s birthday, gleaming with butter, gizzards simmered and strained into gravy, and garnished with rosemary and thyme."

- **中文理解**：「I killed the cockerels in the morning and at eight o'clock they hit the table for Mr. Page's birthday, gleaming with butter, gizzards simmered and strained into gravy, and garnished with rosemary and thyme。」Narrator's morning—killing cockerels, preparing feast for Page's birthday. "Gleaming with butter" = food's abundance (for Pages) vs. narrator's hunger. "I killed" = narrator as killer (of animals), later Xiulan kills humans.

- **关键词**：killed the cockerels / gleaming with butter / gizzards simmered

- **为什么这样写**：Vo's opening establishes food/abundance (for Pages) vs. hunger (for maids). "I killed the cockerels" = narrator as killer (of animals), foreshadowing Xiulan's killing (of humans). "Gleaming with butter" = food's abundance vs. narrator's hunger. This is the story's premise: maids serve food they can't eat; hunger drives transformation.

- **读者视角提示**：Vo's "killed the cockerels" = foreshadowing. Food abundance (Pages) vs. hunger (maids). Death as hunger begins here.

> **原句 2:** "It’s a kind of living death, that kind of work, a blur of aching muscles and pressure-bruised fingers from the endless cleaning, smelling like sweat and vinegar while pretending you don’t."

- **中文理解**：「It's a kind of living death, that kind of work, a blur of aching muscles and pressure-bruised fingers from the endless cleaning, smelling like sweat and vinegar while pretending you don't。」Narrator's description of maid work—"living death." "Aching muscles, pressure-bruised fingers" = physical toll; "smelling like sweat and vinegar" = bodily reality hidden; "pretending you don't" = performed invisibility. Maid work = living death.

- **关键词**：living death / aching muscles / pressure-bruised fingers / smelling like sweat and vinegar

- **为什么这样写**：Vo's "living death" = maid work as death-in-life. "Aching muscles, pressure-bruised fingers" = physical toll. "Smelling like sweat and vinegar" = bodily reality; "pretending you don't" = performed invisibility. This is the story's thesis: maid work = living death (invisible, exhausting, dehumanizing). "Living death" = the condition that produces Xiulan's transformation.

- **读者视角提示**：Vo's "living death" = maid work. "Pretending you don't" = performed invisibility. Death-in-life precedes actual death/transformation.

> **原句 3:** "She wouldn’t be dead."

- **中文理解**：「She wouldn't be dead。」Narrator's belief—Xiulan wouldn't die (from illness). "Wouldn't be dead" = hope/denial. But Xiulan does die (or transforms). This is the story's turning point—narrator's denial of Xiulan's death.

- **关键词**：She wouldn't be dead

- **为什么这样写**：Vo's "wouldn't be dead" = narrator's hope/denial. "Wouldn't" = conditional, uncertain. Narrator refuses to accept Xiulan's death. But Xiulan does die/transform. This is the story's tension: narrator's hope vs. reality. "Wouldn't be dead" = denial of death's inevitability.

- **读者视角提示**：Vo's "wouldn't be dead" = denial. Narrator refuses Xiulan's death. But death/transform happens.

> **原句 4:** "I’m hungry, so hungry, sis."

- **中文理解**：「I'm hungry, so hungry, sis。」Xiulan's refrain—hunger as death's voice. "So hungry" = intensification; "sis" = sister (narrator). This is Xiulan's transformation—hunger becomes her identity. "I'm hungry" = death's call.

- **关键词**：I'm hungry, so hungry, sis

- **为什么这样写**：Vo's "I'm hungry, so hungry, sis" = death's refrain. "So hungry" = intensification; "sis" = sister (narrator). This is Xiulan's transformation—hunger becomes her identity, her voice. "I'm hungry" = death's call (not request, demand). This is the story's thesis: death = hunger; hunger = death.

- **读者视角提示**：Vo's "I'm hungry" = death's refrain. Xiulan's hunger = death's voice. Transformation complete.

> **原句 5:** "sister, I'm hungry."

- **中文理解**：「sister, I'm hungry。」Final line—Xiulan's final words on train. "Sister" = narrator (now "sister"); "I'm hungry" = death's demand. "Her eyes flickered to the other people on the platform" = Xiulan eyeing prey. This is the story's ending—Xiulan's hunger is now predatory.

- **关键词**：sister, I'm hungry

- **为什么这样写**：Vo's final line—"sister, I'm hungry" with Xiulan's eyes on other people = predatory hunger. "Sister" = narrator's new role (Xiulan's sister, not just maid). "I'm hungry" = death's demand, now directed at others. "Her eyes flickered to the other people" = Xiulan eyeing prey. This is the story's horror: Xiulan is now predator, narrator is complicit.

- **读者视角提示**：Vo's final "sister, I'm hungry" = predatory hunger. Xiulan eyes others. Narrator complicit. Death as predation.

> **原句 6:** "I thought of Mrs. Page's tongue. I thought of Mr. Page, hollowed out so that he seemed like an empty basket, the liver, lungs, heart, and intestines all gone."

- **中文理解**：「I thought of Mrs. Page's tongue。I thought of Mr. Page, hollowed out so that he seemed like an empty basket, the liver, lungs, heart, and intestines all gone。」Narrator's memory—Mrs. Page's tongue (Xiulan ate it), Mr. Page hollowed out (Xiulan ate his organs). "Empty basket" = body without organs. This is the story's horror: Xiulan ate the Pages (tongue, organs). Narrator's complicity (helped Xiulan escape).

- **关键词**：Mrs. Page's tongue / hollowed out / empty basket / liver, lungs, heart, intestines all gone

- **为什么这样写**：Vo's "Mrs. Page's tongue" and "Mr. Page, hollowed out" = Xiulan's predation (ate Pages). "Empty basket" = body without organs. Narrator's memory = complicity (she knew Xiulan ate them, helped escape). This is the story's horror: Xiulan is monster, narrator is accomplice. "I thought of Mrs. Page's tongue" = narrator's guilt/memory.

- **读者视角提示**：Vo's "Mrs. Page's tongue" + "hollowed out" = Xiulan's predation. Narrator's complicity. Death as consumption.

## 一句话总结

Vo 的 "The Heart in Service to Hunger" 是选集中最 Southern Gothic 的 story：Narrator (Vietnamese maid "Peony"/"Rose") 与 Xiulan (Chinese maid, ill, hungry) 服侍 Page family；Xiulan 病重， narrator 偷 food for her；Xiulan becomes monster (eats Mrs. Page's tongue/jaw), narrator helps her escape; on train, Xiulan says "sister, I'm hungry"—narrator realizes Xiulan will eat people (including narrator?). Vo 的 thesis："death as hunger"—"I'm hungry, so hungry, sis" = death's refrain; "kind of living death" = maid work (invisible, exhausting, dehumanizing); "Mrs. Page's tongue" + "hollowed out" = Xiulan's predation; "sister, I'm hungry" = death's demand, now predatory. "I thought of Mrs. Page's tongue. I thought of Mr. Page, hollowed out" = narrator's complicity (knew Xiulan ate them, helped escape). "She wouldn't be dead" = narrator's denial. "Xiulan wants it, I'd tell them" = narrator's rationalization. "I'm hungry, so hungry, sis" = death's voice. This is the anthology's "death as hunger" counterpoint. Theme: death as hunger; "living death" = maid work; Xiulan's transformation from ill maid to monster; narrator's complicity; "I'm hungry" = death's refrain; violence for survival; love as complicity. The story's horror: Xiulan eats the Pages (tongue, organs), narrator helps her escape, Xiulan's final "sister, I'm hungry" with eyes on others = predatory hunger continues. Death as consumption, not ending. "sister, I'm hungry" = death's voice, now directed at others (including narrator). The narrator's final acceptance: "Of course you'll find me something good to eat." = narrator's resignation to Xiulan's predation. Death as hunger, complicity, transformation. "I'm hungry" = the story's final word—death's demand, unending.
