### ⑤

> **原句 5:** How do you make art when the art that's all around you keeps telling you to shut up and do the dishes? ### ⑥

> **原句 6:** I did like some things about Kerouac's prose style, just not the gender politics of the three men who were most often meant when people talked about the Beats. Those politics had contaminated Kerouac's On the Road for me when I was a teenager. I got as far as the protagonist's encounter with Terry—"the cutest little Mexican girl" who he later calls "a dumb little Mexican wench" with "a simple and funny little mind." And then the protagonist—a lightly fictionalized Kerouac—takes off and leaves her. As in the film, a woman is a stationary object, a man is a pilgrim and a heroic wanderer. He's Odysseus; she's Penelope, but Homer took an interest in the gallant struggle of the woman who stayed home. It seemed to me that I would never be the footloose protagonist, that I was closer to the young Latina on the California farm who gets left behind, and halfway through I put the novel down. The book was going to go on without people like me, and I was going to go on without it.

### ⑦

### ⑧

> **原句 8:** I became silently furious, back in the day when I had no clear feminist ideas, just swirling inchoate feelings of indignation and insubordination. A great urge to disrupt the event overtook me; I wanted to shout and to shout that I was not disrupting it because a woman is no one, to shout that since I did not exist my shouting did not exist either and could not be objectionable. I was, in that room, that time, clear and angry about my nonexistence that was otherwise mostly just a brooding anxiety somewhere below the surface. But I remained silent; contributing to the sense of women as burdensome, crazy, angry, intrusive, unfit was not going to help.

**中文理解**：我变得沉默地愤怒，在那个我没有清晰的女权主义思想，只有愤怒和反抗的混乱涌动的时代。一种打断活动的巨大冲动占据了我；我想大喊，大喊我不是在打断它，因为女人什么都不是，大喊既然我不存在，我的大喊也不存在，也不会令人反感。我在那个房间，那个时刻，对我的不存在感到清晰和愤怒，否则这种不存在大多只是表面下某个地方的一种沉思的焦虑。但我保持沉默；助长女性是负担、疯狂、愤怒、侵入、不适宜的感觉是没有帮助的。Solnit 用"I became silently furious"的并列，将"愤怒"定义为一种"沉默"：不是"表达"，而是"沉默"；不是"可见"，而是"不可见"。

**句子结构**：长复合句，"I became silently furious, back in the day when I had no clear feminist ideas, just swirling inchoate feelings of indignation and insubordination"。

**关键词**：**silently**（沉默地）、**inchoate**（混乱的）、**indignation**（愤怒）、**insubordination**（反抗）、**overtook**（占据）、**objectionable**（令人反感的）、**brooding**（沉思的）。"silently"是核心词——它暗示一种"沉默"：不是"表达"，而是"沉默"；不是"可见"，而是"不可见"。

**表达方式**：Solnit 用"since I did not exist my shouting did not exist either"的悖论，将"不存在"定义为一种"自我否定"：不是"被否定"，而是"自我否定"；不是"被抹除"，而是"自我抹除"。

**为什么这样写**：这段话是全书"愤怒即沉默"主题的缩影。Solnit 后来多次回到这个主题——"愤怒"不仅是"表达"，也是"沉默"；不仅是"可见"，也是"不可见"。

---

### ⑨

> **原句 9:** Often a phenomenon that appears revolutionary because of some new feature can be seen as drearily conventional because of others that stood out less at the time. The men considered the principal Beats were opening up space to be queer or bisexual, to experiment with drugs and consciousness and non-Western spiritual practices and philosophies, to try to find white literary equivalents to the great experiments of black jazz musicians then, to make of improvisation and the American vernacular and pop culture something truly of this time and place, not a dressed-up deference to Europe.

**中文理解**：通常，一个因为某些新特征而显得革命的现象，会因为其他在当时不那么突出的特征而被视为沉闷的惯例。被认为是主要垮掉一代的男人正在开辟空间成为酷儿或双性恋，试验药物和意识和非西方精神和哲学，试图找到与当时黑人爵士乐伟大实验相当的白人文学，使即兴和美国方言和流行文化成为真正属于此时此地的东西，而不是对欧洲的精心打扮的尊重。Solnit 用"appears revolutionary... can be seen as drearily conventional"的对比，将"革命"定义为一种"双重性"：不是"纯粹"，而是"混合"；不是"完全"，而是"部分"。

**句子结构**：长复合句，"Often a phenomenon that appears revolutionary because of some new feature can be seen as drearily conventional because of others that stood out less at the time"。

**关键词**：**revolutionary**（革命的）、**drearily**（沉闷地）、**conventional**（常规的）、**queer**（酷儿）、**bisexual**（双性恋）、**improvisation**（即兴）、**vernacular**（方言）。"revolutionary"是核心词——它暗示一种"革命"：不是"完全"，而是"部分"；不是"纯粹"，而是"混合"。

**表达方式**：Solnit 用"not a dressed-up deference to Europe"的表述，将"美国文化"定义为一种"独立"：不是"欧洲"，而是"美国"；不是"模仿"，而是"原创"。

**为什么这样写**：这段话是全书"革命即双重性"主题的缩影。Solnit 后来多次回到这个主题——"革命"不仅是"新"，也是"旧"；不仅是"进步"，也是"局限"。

### ⑩

> **原句 10:** And later on he notes of the women in these books, "Only in death can they be joined in an embrace as pure as that of males. The only good woman is a dead woman!" I didn't shout that day, but I did have my revenge on another one of the holy trinity of the Beats one February evening in my early twenties.

**中文理解**：后来他在这些书中关于女性写道："只有在死亡中，她们才能像男性一样被拥抱在纯洁的拥抱中。唯一的好女人是死女人！"那天我没有大喊，但我确实在二十多岁的一个二月晚上对垮掉一代神圣三位一体中的另一个进行了报复。Solnit 用"The only good woman is a dead woman!"的引用，将"性别暴力"定义为一种"死亡"：不是"生命"，而是"死亡"；不是"存在"，而是"不存在"。

**句子结构**：复合句，"And later on he notes of the women in these books, 'Only in death can they be joined in an embrace as pure as that of males. The only good woman is a dead woman!'"。

**关键词**：**embrace**（拥抱）、**holy trinity**（神圣三位一体）、**revenge**（报复）。"dead woman"是核心词——它暗示一种"死亡"：不是"生命"，而是"死亡"；不是"存在"，而是"不存在"。

**表达方式**：Solnit 用"I didn't shout that day, but I did have my revenge"的对比，将"报复"定义为一种"沉默"：不是"大喊"，而是"沉默"；不是"表达"，而是"行动"。

**为什么这样写**：这段话是全书"女性即死亡"主题的缩影。Solnit 后来多次回到这个主题——"女性"不仅是"生命"，也是"死亡"；不仅是"存在"，也是"不存在"。

## 一句话总结
Solnit 通过第一本书写作过程中的性别政治——采访中的性骚扰、垮掉一代文学中女性的"消失"、以及金斯堡摄影展上的"抹除"——探讨了"女性"如何被定义为"无名"、"静止"、"死亡"，而"愤怒"如何被"沉默"。
