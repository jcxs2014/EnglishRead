# 12. 11 Fading Away（渐渐淡出）

## 概览

- **出处**：*The Captain's Dinner*，Adam Cohen 著
- **作者**：Adam Cohen
- **章节定位**：全书第 11 章。叙述判决之后的余波：全球新闻、舆论分裂（精英对底层）、Harcourt 的妥协（六个月不配苦役）、女王的赦免、Dudley 的狱中上诉、Lever 向女王的请愿、Dudley 于 1900 年在悉尼死于腺鼠疫
- **字符数**：约 31,500（提取件 `text/ch12_11_fading_away.txt`）
- **一句话主旨**：判决之后，Dudley 与 Stephens 的“淡出”是双重的：淡出公众视野，也淡出法律记忆——Harcourt 的六个月妥协与女王的赦免结束了此案，Dudley 于 1900 年在悉尼死于腺鼠疫，几乎没有一篇新闻提到他的过去

## 论证结构

- **核心论点**：判决之后，Dudley 与 Stephens 的“淡出”是双重的：淡出公众视野，也淡出法律记忆——Harcourt 的六个月妥协与女王的赦免结束了此案，Dudley 于 1900 年在悉尼死于腺鼠疫，几乎没有一篇新闻提到他的过去。

- **证据链**：

  | 证据 | 类型 | 支撑什么 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 全球新闻（《悉尼先驱晨报》、《纽约时报》、《Wheeling Daily Intelligencer》） | 事实 | 显示此案的全球影响 |
  | Northern Echo 预测“free pardon” | 媒体 | 显示来自底层的同情 |
  | The Times：“mawkish, ill-directed sympathy” | 媒体 | 显示精英阶层的反对 |
  | Reynolds's Newspaper：“sickly, pernicious sentiment” | 媒体 | 显示激进报的改革立场 |
  | The Daily Telegraph：“shock to the conscience” | 媒体 | 显示同情的媒体 |
  | Harcourt 的妥协：六个月不配苦役 | 法律与政策 | 显示维多利亚改革式的妥协 |
  | 维多利亚女王：“没有证据表明她对此有任何兴趣” | 事实 | 显示女王的漠然 |
  | Parker 的纪念碑：“主啊，请勿将此罪记在他们名下” | 事实 | 显示家属立场暧昧 |
  | Dudley 的狱中上诉：“这让我血都凉了” | 人物 | 显示悔意 |
  | Lever 向女王的请愿与 Philippa 的信 | 人物与事实 | 显示支持者的奔走 |
  | “Captain Dudley, a sailmaker” | 事实 | 显示往事的抹去 |

- **论证脉络**：判决消息 → 舆论分裂（精英对底层）→ Harcourt 的妥协 → 女王赦免 → 狱中条件 → Dudley 的上诉 → Lever 的请愿 → Parker 的纪念碑 → Dudley 死于鼠疫 → “渐渐淡出”

- **可质疑处**：
  1. **“公众舆论”的两极化**：作者把公众写成“精英对底层”，可能过度简化。
  2. **Harcourt 的“妥协”**：作者把六个月写成“维多利亚改革的妥协”，但 Harcourt 的决定也可能是出于务实（避免民怨），而非改革理想。
  3. **Dudley 的“悔意”**：那句“这让我血都凉了”也可能是一种策略（为求赦免），未必是真正的悔恨。

## 选择性精读

> **原句 1:** "The news of Dudley's and Stephens's death sentences quickly spread around the globe. In Australia, The Sydney Morning Herald, a paper the men of the Mignonette might have been reading if their voyage had been a success, reported that the prisoners had been sentenced to death in a story about what it called "The Mignonette Affair." The New York Times put the sentencing of "The Mignonette Cannibals" on its front page. And in Wheeling, West Virginia, The Wheeling Daily Intelligencer reported in a subheadline that the defendants had been "Sentenced to Be Hanged" amid "an Impressive Scene in the Court." At home in London, The Times reported that Dudley and Stephens's "crime is judicially declared to be in strictness nothing less than murder.""

- **中文理解**：Dudley 与 Stephens 被判处死刑的消息很快传遍全球。在澳大利亚，《悉尼先驱晨报》——如果 Mignonette 号的航程成功，这几个人或许正在读它——报道说囚犯已被判处死刑，报道的标题写作它所谓的“Mignonette 事件”。《纽约时报》把“Mignonette 号食人者”的判决放上头版。在西弗吉尼亚州惠灵，《Wheeling Daily Intelligencer》在小标题里报道被告“被判处绞刑”，说那是一场“法庭上令人难忘的场面”。在伦敦老家，《泰晤士报》报道说，Dudley 与 Stephens 的“罪行经司法认定，严格来说无异于谋杀”。
- **句子结构**：五句递进：消息传遍全球 → 悉尼先驱晨报 → 纽约时报头版 → 惠灵小标题 → 泰晤士报定性。
- **关键词**：`"The Mignonette Affair"`、`"The Mignonette Cannibals"`、`"Sentenced to Be Hanged"`、`"an Impressive Scene in the Court"`
- **表达方式**：用各大洲的报纸把此案写成“全球性的”——这份“全球”正是后文“渐渐淡出”的反讽所在：全球喧哗之后，是无人问津的死。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把此案写成“全球事件”——正是这份全球声量，为后文的“淡出”备下反讽：当年满世界都在谈，最后却死在悉尼的无名墓穴里。

> **原句 2:** "It was certainly possible to comb through the details of the case and conclude that some form of mercy might come. The Exeter jury had recommended it, and Baron Huddleston had as well. The lord chief justice had publicly said that he and the other justices all supported bestowing the royal prerogative of mercy. Noting that Lord Coleridge had not worn the black cap, news stories took it as a sign that Dudley and Stephens would not be executed. But all this was just guesswork and supposition. In predicting a pardon, newspapers may simply have been telling their readers what they wanted to hear. "The men were virtually pardoned by public opinion before they were put on trial," The Daily Telegraph observed. Now there was "universal agreement," The Daily News reported, that the death sentence should not be carried out."

- **中文理解**：确实可以梳理案情的细节，得出某种形式的宽赦将会到来的结论。Exeter 的陪审团曾建议宽赦，Baron Huddleston 也是。大法官还公开说过，他与其他法官都支持行使王室赦免权。由于 Lord Coleridge 没有戴黑帽，各家新闻把这当作 Dudley 与 Stephens 不会被处死的迹象。然而这一切都只是猜测与臆测。在预告赦免这件事上，报纸也许只是在告诉读者他们想听的话。《每日电讯报》评论道：“这些人在受审之前，实际上就已经被公众意见赦免了。”如今《每日新闻》报道说，各方已达“普遍一致”：死刑不应执行。
- **句子结构**：八句递进：可能有宽赦 → 陪审团建议 → Huddleston 建议 → 大法官表态 → 黑帽的暗示 → 只是猜测 → “早已被公众赦免” → “普遍一致”。
- **关键词**：`"virtually pardoned by public opinion before they were put on trial"`、`"universal agreement"`
- **表达方式**：用“guesswork and supposition”把媒体的预告写成“凭空揣测”——这份揣测正是后文“Harcourt 的决定”所处的不确定背景。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把媒体的预告写成“猜测”——正是这份猜测，为后文“Harcourt 的决定”留下不确定：报纸都在报赦免，而 Harcourt 偏偏不这么办。

> **原句 3:** "Taken as a whole, though, the newspaper opinion pages were a reminder that much of the nation's elite still felt strongly that killing Parker was wrong. These were people who traveled in the same intellectual and social circles as the home secretary, whose recommendation would almost certainly decide their fate, and many of them were hoping for a punishment with some teeth in it."

- **中文理解**：然而总的来看，报纸的评论版提醒人们：国家精英中仍有相当一部分人强烈认为杀死 Parker 是错误的。这些人与内政大臣出没于同样的知识与社会圈层，而内政大臣的建议几乎肯定将决定他们的命运；他们当中许多人盼望得到的，是一场带点牙齿的惩罚。
- **句子结构**：三句递进：精英仍认为杀人有错 → 与内政大臣同圈层 → 盼望一场有分量的惩罚。
- **关键词**：`"punishment with some teeth in it"`、`"same intellectual and social circles as the home secretary"`
- **表达方式**：用“punishment with some teeth in it”把精英舆论写成“严厉”——这份严厉正是后文“Harcourt 的妥协”所承受的压力。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把精英舆论写成“严厉”——正是这份严厉，为后文“Harcourt 的妥协”交代了压力来源：他得同时对付底层的同情与上层的重罚期待。

> **原句 4:** "On Friday, December 12, the home secretary, William Harcourt, reached his decision. He recommended that the queen commute Dudley's and Stephens's death sentences, but he stood up to members of the general public—and his own attorney general—who wanted the men to be set free immediately. He proposed that the men serve six months' imprisonment without hard labor, with the time running from the day of their conviction, December 4."

- **中文理解**：12 月 12 日星期五，内政大臣 William Harcourt 作出了决定。他建议女王对 Dudley 与 Stephens 的死刑判决予以减刑，但他顶住了公众——以及他自己的检察总长——要求立即释放这两人的压力。他提议让二人服六个月监禁，不带苦役，刑期自定罪之日 12 月 4 日起算。
- **句子结构**：三句递进：决定 → 建议减刑但顶住压力 → 六个月不配苦役。
- **关键词**：`"commute Dudley's and Stephens's death sentences"`、`"six months' imprisonment without hard labor"`
- **表达方式**：用“stood up to”把 Harcourt 写成“顶住压力”——这份“顶住”正是后文“妥协”的道德层面的根据。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Harcourt 写成“挺身顶住”——正是这份挺身，让后文的“妥协”不止是策略：即便承受双重压力，他仍坚持法律该有的样子。

> **原句 5:** "It was a compromise that embodied the Victorian reform spirit. Harcourt stuck to his conviction that Dudley and Stephens had committed a serious crime. In doing so, he was siding with the sick seventeen-year-old Parker, the lowliest person in the lifeboat, and insisting that taking his life was murder—"the highest crime known to the law." By calling for Dudley and Stephens to face serious punishment, Harcourt was sending the message that Victorian reformers hoped the case would deliver: that murder at sea in conjunction with survival cannibalism was still murder and would be punished as such—even if it was the captain doing it and the cabin boy being killed. If the queen adopted Harcourt's recommendation, a centuries-old tradition of allowing survival cannibalism would come to an end."

- **中文理解**：这是一个体现了维多利亚改革精神的妥协。Harcourt 坚持认为 Dudley 与 Stephens 犯下了严重罪行。这样做，等于站在生病的十七岁少年 Parker——救生艇上最卑微的人——一边，坚持夺走他的性命就是谋杀——“法律所知的最重罪行”。通过要求 Dudley 与 Stephens 面对严重惩罚，Harcourt 传达了维多利亚改革者希望此案带来的信息：海上杀人并伴随求生性食人，依然是谋杀，依然要按谋杀来惩处——哪怕下手的是船长，被杀的是 cabin boy。如果女王采纳 Harcourt 的建议，那项允许求生性食人的数百年旧习就将告终。
- **句子结构**：五句递进：妥协 → 坚持定罪立场 → 站在 Parker 一边 → 传达改革者期待的信息 → 数百年旧习将终。
- **关键词**：`"the highest crime known to the law"`、`"centuries-old tradition of allowing survival cannibalism would come to an end"`
- **表达方式**：用“centuries-old tradition…would come to an end”把 Harcourt 的决定写成“历史性的”——这份“历史性”正是后文“遗产”所标明的分量。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Harcourt 写成“终结旧习”——正是这份终结，为后文的“遗产”交代了分量：他一个人关上了那道几百年没关上的门。

> **原句 6:** "The next morning, at Holloway Prison, Dudley and Stephens learned of Harcourt's decision. The men were relieved that Harcourt was recommending mercy, but they had been hoping for more. Only a day after telling his wife he expected to be free on Sunday, Dudley had to send her a telegram saying that the home secretary was recommending six months' imprisonment. A disappointed Philippa Dudley told a reporter that she had thought her husband would receive a full pardon and be released immediately. She pointed out that he had originally assumed he would not be charged at all."

- **中文理解**：第二天早上，在 Holloway 监狱，Dudley 与 Stephens 得知了 Harcourt 的决定。他们因为 Harcourt 建议宽赦而松了口气，但原本指望的更多。才在告诉妻子自己预计周日就能获释的第二天，Dudley 就不得不发电报说，内政大臣建议的是六个月监禁。失望的 Philippa Dudley 告诉记者，她原以为丈夫会得到完全赦免并立即获释。她指出，他最初还以为自己根本不会被起诉。
- **句子结构**：五句递进：获知决定 → 松了口气但期望更多 → 发电报 → Philippa 的失望 → 他原本以为自己不会被起诉。
- **关键词**：`"relieved that Harcourt was recommending mercy, but they had been hoping for more"`、`"originally assumed he would not be charged at all"`
- **表达方式**：用“originally assumed he would not be charged at all”把 Dudley 写成“天真”——这份天真正是后文“上诉”反讽的来源。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Dudley 写成“天真”——正是这份天真，让后文那次注定失败的上诉带上了反讽：他从头到尾都低估了法律。

> **原句 7:** "Dudley had the shortest and most eventful life after the Mignonette. In her statement accompanying Lever's letter to the queen, Philippa Dudley had written that her husband was "eager to leave England as soon as possible: we trust soon to settle our affairs + make a fresh start in Australia." As soon as Dudley was released from prison, the family began making preparations. They traveled to Sydney in 1885 on the steamship Austral, completing the transoceanic journey the Mignonette had failed to. Dudley's aunt handed over her business, and Dudley began making and selling sails and other boating supplies near the waterfront, under the name T. R. Dudley and Co. The company prospered, and by 1890 it had a staff of forty. The Dudley family also thrived. Philippa gave birth to three more children in Australia. One, a son, died young, but two daughters survived. Two of Philippa's sisters, both teachers, also moved to Sydney to join the family."

- **中文理解**：在 Mignonette 号之后，Dudley 的人生既最短，也最多变故。在 Lever 致女王的信所附的声明中，Philippa Dudley 写道，丈夫“渴望尽快离开英国：我们希望很快料理好事务，在澳大利亚重新开始”。Dudley 一获释，家人便开始筹备。他们于 1885 年乘蒸汽船 Austral 号前往悉尼，完成了 Mignonette 号当年未能完成的跨洋航程。Dudley 的姑母把自己的生意交给了他，他以 T. R. Dudley and Co. 的名义，在滨水一带制作并出售帆与其他船具。公司生意兴隆，到 1890 年已有四十名雇员。Dudley 一家也过得兴旺：Philippa 在澳大利亚又生了三个孩子，其中一个儿子早逝，两个女儿活了下来；Philippa 的两个姐妹都是教师，也迁居悉尼与家人团聚。
- **句子结构**：十一句递进：人生最短多变 → Philippa 的声明 → 出狱后筹备 → 乘船赴悉尼 → 姑母让出生意 → 经营帆具 → 公司兴隆 → 家庭兴旺 → 三个孩子 → 两个姐妹移居。
- **关键词**：`"eager to leave England as soon as possible"`、`"completing the transoceanic journey the Mignonette had failed to"`、`T. R. Dudley and Co`
- **表达方式**：用“completing the transoceanic journey the Mignonette had failed to”把 Dudley 移居澳大利亚写成“完成”——这份“完成”正是后文“死于鼠疫”的反讽所在：航程完成了，人却没等到。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Dudley 的移居写成“完成”——正是这份完成，为后文的鼠疫之死备下反讽：他补上了那趟没走完的航程，却没走完自己的一生。

> **原句 8:** "Dudley's was the first official death of Sydney's bubonic plague epidemic of 1900. It went on to take 103 lives in the first nine months of that year and 535 in a series of outbreaks that recurred through 1925. To combat transmission of the disease, there were strict rules about the burial of victims. Dudley's body sat unattended for days, then was wrapped in sailcloth soaked in 5 percent sulfuric acid, placed in a watertight coffin with more acid, and wrapped again in acid-soaked sailcloth and a layer of asbestos cloth. The body was then taken by skiff to a cemetery in the city's remote quarantine station and unceremoniously deposited in a grave of "unusual depth.""

- **中文理解**：Dudley 是 1900 年悉尼腺鼠疫疫情中的第一例官方死亡病例。此后疫情在当年头九个月夺走 103 条生命，此后在反复爆发的一连串疫情中累计夺走 535 条生命。为阻断疾病传播，对死者的埋葬有严格规定。Dudley 的遗体无人看管地停了几天，随后被裹进浸有 5% 硫酸的帆布，放进一只内含更多酸液的水密棺木，再用浸酸的帆布和一层石棉布包裹。遗体接着由小艇运到城市偏远检疫站的墓地，被“不加仪式地”埋进一口“异常深”的墓穴。
- **句子结构**：五句递进：第一例官方死亡 → 疫情夺命数字 → 埋葬的严格规定 → 遗体的处理 → 运往偏远墓地草草安葬。
- **关键词**：`"first official death"`、`"unusual depth"`、`"unceremoniously deposited"`
- **表达方式**：用“unceremoniously deposited”把 Dudley 的安葬写成“无仪式”——这份“无仪式”正是后文“被抹去”的结局感所在。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Dudley 的安葬写成“毫不隆重”——正是这份潦草，为后文的“被抹去”备下终局感：他死得像一段被草草收掉的插曲。

> **原句 9:** "As the city's first official casualty, Dudley's name was widely reported in the news. If there was any larger meaning to be gleaned from Dudley's inglorious end, any sense that the score had been evened for what he had done years earlier to his cabin boy, the press did not notice it. With his move to Australia, Dudley had managed to put the killing of Richard Parker and his murder conviction behind him. Virtually none of the news accounts of Dudley's death mentioned his past life, and the official public health report referred to him simply as "Captain Dudley, a sailmaker.""

- **中文理解**：作为这座城市第一例官方死亡病例，Dudley 的名字在新闻中被广泛报道。可如果从 Dudley 这不光彩的结局里能读出任何更大的意义，如果有人觉得他多年前对 cabin boy 所做之事的账已经结清，媒体并没有注意到。随着移居澳大利亚，Dudley 成功地把杀害 Richard Parker 与那桩谋杀定罪抛在了身后。关于他死讯的报道几乎没有一篇提到他此前的人生，官方公共卫生报告只是简单地称他为“Captain Dudley, a sailmaker”。
- **句子结构**：四句递进：名字被广泛报道 → 媒体未读出更大意义 → 他已把往事抛在身后 → 官方报告只称他“Captain Dudley, a sailmaker”。
- **关键词**：`"Captain Dudley, a sailmaker"`
- **表达方式**：用“Captain Dudley, a sailmaker”把 Dudley 写成一个“普通人”——这份“普通”正是后文“渐渐淡出”所指向的抹去。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Dudley 写成“普通人”——正是这份“普通”，为后文“渐渐淡出”备下抹去的根据：他的过去被彻底删除，只剩一个职业称呼。

## 一句话总结

案件的尾声是两份“淡出”：一是舆论在精英的严厉与底层的同情之间裂开，Harcourt 的六个月与女王的赦免把法律收尾；二是人物的淡出——Dudley 移居悉尼、做成生意，最终在 1900 年的腺鼠疫中成了这座城市第一例官方死亡病例，遗体被酸与石棉层层裹好草草埋了，而关于他死讯的报道，几乎没有一篇提到他曾是那个杀人的船长。
