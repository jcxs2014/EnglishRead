# 09. 8 The Grand Jury Weighs In（大陪审团的表态）

## 概览

- **出处**：*The Captain's Dinner*，Adam Cohen 著
- **作者**：Adam Cohen
- **章节定位**：全书第 8 章。叙述 Exeter 巡回审判的大陪审团程序：Baron Huddleston 的指示（否定必要性抗辩、驳斥海难旧俗、否定自卫、援引维多利亚改革原则）、大陪审团返回起诉书，以及王室赦免权的预告
- **字符数**：约 23,500（提取件 `text/ch09_8_the_grand_jury_weighs_in.txt`）
- **一句话主旨**：Baron Huddleston 以“强势法官”的姿态把大陪审团推向起诉书——否定必要性抗辩（St. Kitts 案与 Holmes 案）、驳斥海难旧俗（近乎亵渎）、否定自卫、援引维多利亚改革（保护年轻人与弱者）、预告王室赦免；大陪审团当天返回起诉书，审判三日后开庭

## 论证结构

- **核心论点**：Baron Huddleston 以强势法官的方式把大陪审团推向起诉书——他否定必要性抗辩的先例、驳斥海难旧俗、否定自卫、援引维多利亚改革原则、预告王室赦免；大陪审团当天返回起诉书，三日后开庭。

- **证据链**：

  | 证据 | 类型 | 支撑什么 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Baron Huddleston 的“强势法官”声望 | 人物 | 显示他对陪审团的影响力 |
  | “似乎很清楚……构成故意杀人” | 法官引语 | 显示他的直率 |
  | St. Kitts 案被否定（荷兰医生的医学奇闻） | 法律 | 显示他对先例的否定 |
  | Holmes 案被否定（美国判例、无约束力；且未抽签） | 法律 | 显示他对必要性抗辩的否定 |
  | “这样的理由几乎要接近亵渎” | 法官引语 | 显示他对旧俗的驳斥 |
  | 自卫被否定（Parker 并未构成威胁） | 法律 | 显示他对自卫的否定 |
  | 年轻人的生命更有价值（西塞罗） | 维多利亚改革 | 显示他的改革原则 |
  | 保护弱者不被强者剥削 | 维多利亚改革 | 显示他的改革原则 |
  | 预告王室赦免权 | 法律 | 显示两段式审判制度 |
  | 大陪审团当天返回起诉书 | 事实 | 显示他的影响力 |
  | 大陪审团成员的拜访：“放心吧……我们确信会得到赦免” | 事实 | 显示大陪审团的同情与对死刑的畏惧 |

- **论证脉络**：公众利益 → 大陪审团程序 → Baron Huddleston 的指示（事实、法律、否定必要性、驳斥旧俗、否定自卫、维多利亚改革）→ 预告王室赦免 → 大陪审团返回起诉书 → 大陪审团成员来访

- **可质疑处**：
  1. **Baron Huddleston 的“强势法官”声望**：作者的“强势法官”写法可能夸大了他的影响力，大陪审团或许是独立得出同样结论。
  2. **“维多利亚改革原则”的援引**：作者把改革原则与司法推理相连接，但 Huddleston 法官的“道德化”也许出于个人立场，而非改革立场。
  3. **大陪审团成员的拜访**：作者把那句“耐人寻味的话”解读为 Baron Huddleston 的影响力所致，但也可能只是跨过了那道不高的门槛。

## 选择性精读

> **原句 1:** "Baron Huddleston began by telling the grand jury the facts of the case: the shipwreck, the time the men spent in the lifeboat, the killing of Parker, and the cannibalism. His account was neutral and nonjudgmental, and he was generous in describing Dudley. The captain was, he said, "a man of exemplary character, great experience, and courage." Baron Huddleston then explained the relevant law. This was standard, and he was right to do it, but Baron Huddleston was unusually direct in stating what he believed the facts and law added up to. "It seems clear," he said, "that the taking away of the boy's life was carefully considered and amounted to a case of deliberate homicide.""

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 先向大陪审团讲述案件事实：海难、水手们在救生艇上度过的那段时间、Parker 之死、以及食人。他的陈述中立而不带评判，在描述 Dudley 时还相当慷慨——船长是，他说，“一个品行典范、经验丰富且勇敢的人。”随后 Baron Huddleston 解释了相关法律。这是标准做法，他做得没错；但他异常直接地陈述了自己认为事实与法律合起来意味着什么。“似乎很清楚，”他说，“夺走那孩子性命之举是经过慎重考虑的，构成一桩故意杀人。”
- **句子结构**：六句递进：陈述事实 → 中立而慷慨 → 评价 Dudley 的引语 → 解释法律 → 异常直接 → “似乎很清楚……”的引语。
- **关键词**：`"a man of exemplary character, great experience, and courage"`、`"deliberate homicide"`
- **表达方式**：用“unusually direct”把 Baron Huddleston 写成“强势的法官”——这份“直接”为后文“我必须告诉你们”备下了一贯性。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Baron Huddleston 写成“强势法官”——正是这份强势，让后文“大陪审团当天就返回起诉书”有了影响力上的解释。

> **原句 2:** "The St. Kitts case, Baron Huddleston said, was not a precedent at all, because there was no reliable report of a legal decision. Everything that was known about it, he said, came from a Dutch physician's book of medical curiosities. There was no reason to believe, he added, that it was actually a holding of an English court. As for United States v. Holmes, Baron Huddleston said that it relied on the necessity doctrine, and unlike American law, British law did not provide any legal support for such a doctrine. Baron Huddleston also noted that Justice Henry Baldwin had only approved of sacrificing lives if lots were drawn. Dudley and Stephens had not drawn lots, so the Holmes case did not support their defense."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 说，St. Kitts 案根本不能算先例，因为没有可靠的法律判决记录。关于此案的一切认知，他说，都来自一本荷兰医生写的医学奇闻书。他补充说，没有理由相信那真的是英国法院的判决。至于 United States v. Holmes，Baron Huddleston 说它建立在必要性原则之上，而与美国法不同，英国法不为这一原则提供任何法律支持。他还指出，Henry Baldwin 法官只在抽签的前提下才认可牺牲生命。Dudley 与 Stephens 没有抽签，所以 Holmes 案并不支持他们的抗辩。
- **句子结构**：八句递进：St. Kitts 不算先例 → 荷兰医生的奇闻书 → 无从确认是法院判决 → Holmes 依赖必要性原则 → 英国法不支持 → Baldwin 只认可抽签 → 未抽签 → Holmes 无助。
- **关键词**：`"not a precedent at all"`、`"Dutch physician's book of medical curiosities"`、`"British law did not provide any legal support"`
- **表达方式**：用“Dutch physician's book of medical curiosities”把 St. Kitts 案写成“不可靠”——这份“不可靠”正是后文“驳倒先例”的根据。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Baron Huddleston 写成“逐一驳倒先例”——这份驳斥让后文“必要性抗辩薄弱”有了具体法律依据：英国法里根本没有可援引的先例。

> **原句 3:** "Baron Huddleston went further and directly repudiated the custom of the sea. He noted that Justice Baldwin had endorsed the drawing of lots as a basis for deciding who should be killed in an emergency at sea, seeing it as "in some sort, an appeal to God for selection of the victim." Baron Huddleston regarded Justice Baldwin's analysis as not only wrong but offensive. "Such a reason would seem almost to verge upon the blasphemous," he said."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 更进一步，直接驳斥了“海难旧俗”。他指出，Baldwin 法官曾认可抽签作为海上紧急情形下决定该杀谁的基础，视之为“在某种意义上是向上帝呼求以选定牺牲者”。Baron Huddleston 认为 Baldwin 法官的分析不仅错误，而且冒犯。“这样的理由几乎要接近亵渎了，”他说。
- **句子结构**：四句递进：直接驳斥 → Baldwin 认可抽签 → 认定其错误且冒犯 → “亵渎”的引语。
- **关键词**：`"directly repudiated"`、`"verge upon the blasphemous"`
- **表达方式**：用“blasphemous”把海难旧俗写成“令人反感”——这份“冒犯”让后文“否定旧俗”站上了道德层面。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Baron Huddleston 写成“否定旧俗”——这份否定让后文“必要性抗辩薄弱”多了一条道德依据：旧俗是亵渎，不是法。

> **原句 4:** "Having rejected the necessity defense, Baron Huddleston went on to explain why the doctrine of self-defense did not apply. It was true, he said, that the law sometimes permitted people to kill to prevent someone from killing them. But that had not been the case in the lifeboat, he said. Dudley and Stephens may have been in danger of dying of hunger and thirst, but Parker, "at the bottom of the boat, was not endangering their lives by any act of his," the judge pointed out. As a result, it was "impossible to say that the act of Dudley and Stephens was an act of self-defense.""

- **中文理解**：在否定了必要性抗辩之后，Baron Huddleston 接着解释为什么自卫原则同样不适用。他说，法律有时确实允许人们为阻止别人杀自己而杀人。但救生艇上的情形并非如此。Dudley 与 Stephens 或许面临饥渴而死的危险，但 Parker——法官指出——“在船底，他的任何行为都没有危及他们的性命。”因此，“不可能说 Dudley 与 Stephens 的行为是自卫。”
- **句子结构**：五句递进：否定必要性 → 自卫有时被允许 → 救生艇上不适用 → Parker 并未危及他人 → 不能说是自卫。
- **关键词**：`"at the bottom of the boat, was not endangering their lives by any act of his"`、`"impossible to say that the act of Dudley and Stephens was an act of self-defense"`
- **表达方式**：用“impossible to say”把自卫写成“不可适用”——这份“不可适用”让后文“已无法律抗辩”站得更稳。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Baron Huddleston 写成“否定自卫”——这份否定让后文“二人已无任何法律抗辩”有了法律依据：必要性与自卫都套不上。

> **原句 5:** "Baron Huddleston argued that it was especially wrong to take the life of a young person as Dudley and Stephens had done. They had decided that Parker's life was less valuable than their own because he had no wife or children. On the contrary, young people's lives were more valuable, Baron Huddleston argued, citing ancient Rome's most famous lawyer. If somebody had to be sacrificed in an emergency, Cicero wrote, it should be the person least likely to contribute to the Republic. Baron Huddleston insisted that Parker, as the youngest person in the lifeboat, could be expected to live the longest and provide more service to England than the others."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 论证说，像 Dudley 与 Stephens 那样夺走一个年轻人的性命尤其错误。他们认定 Parker 的性命不如自己值钱，理由是他没有妻儿。Baron Huddleston 恰恰相反地论证道：年轻人的生命更有价值，他援引了古罗马最著名的律师。西塞罗写道，若紧急情况下必须牺牲一人，那应当是对共和国贡献最少的那一位。Baron Huddleston 坚持认为，Parker 作为救生艇上最年轻的人，本可望活得最久、为英国贡献得最多。
- **句子结构**：五句递进：尤其错误 → Parker 价值较低的理由 → 反论年轻人更有价值 → 援引西塞罗 → Parker 最年轻、能活得最久。
- **关键词**：`"young people's lives were more valuable"`、`Cicero`、`"least likely to contribute to the Republic"`
- **表达方式**：借西塞罗的引语把“年轻人更值钱”写成“古典权威”——这份权威为后文“维多利亚改革”提供了哲学层面的依据。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Baron Huddleston 写成“援引西塞罗”——这份援引为后文“维多利亚改革”接上了古典传统：保护弱者不只是新潮主张，也是自古已有的原则。

> **原句 6:** "Another broad Victorian reform principle that Baron Huddleston invoked was the importance of protecting the weak from being exploited by the strong. Baron Huddleston noted that Dudley considered Parker's illness to be a reason for sacrificing him, but he maintained that the opposite was true. "One would have imagined that the state of his health, and the misery in which he was at the time, would have obtained for him more consideration," he said."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 援引的另一个广义的维多利亚改革原则，是保护弱者不被强者剥削的重要性。他指出，Dudley 把 Parker 的疾病当作牺牲他的理由，但他坚持认为事实恰恰相反。“人们本会以为，他的健康状况和他当时所受的苦难，应当为他赢得更多的体谅，”他说。
- **句子结构**：三句递进：保护弱者 → Dudley 把疾病当作理由 → “人们本会以为……”的引语。
- **关键词**：`"protecting the weak from being exploited by the strong"`、`"One would have imagined that the state of his health, and the misery in which he was at the time, would have obtained for him more consideration"`
- **表达方式**：用“One would have imagined…”把 Parker 的病情写成“更该得到体谅的理由”——这份“体谅”让后文“维多利亚改革”站上道德层面。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Baron Huddleston 写成“援引改革原则”——这份援引让后文“维多利亚改革”有了道德层面的落点：要保护的是弱者，不是淘汰弱者。

> **原句 7:** "Having covered both the facts and the law—with a strong dose of subjective moralizing—Baron Huddleston proceeded to firmly suggest to the grand jury what it should do. "I am bound to tell you that if you are satisfied that the boy's death was caused or accelerated by the act of Dudley, or by Dudley and Stephens, this is a case of deliberate homicide, neither justifiable or excusable," he said. "The crime is murder, and you, therefore, ought to find a true bill for that offense against both the prisoners.""

- **中文理解**：在把事实与法律都讲过一遍——并加上了大量主观的道德评判——之后，Baron Huddleston 又斩钉截铁地告诉大陪审团应该怎么做。“我必须告诉你们，”他说，“如果你们确信那孩子的死亡是由 Dudley 的行为、或 Dudley 与 Stephens 的行为造成或加速的，这是一桩故意杀人，既不能正当化，也不能原谅。”“这罪名是谋杀，因此你们理应对两名囚犯就这一罪名提出起诉书。”
- **句子结构**：三句递进：讲完事实与法律 → “我必须告诉你们……” → “这罪名是谋杀……”。
- **关键词**：`"I am bound to tell you"`、`"deliberate homicide, neither justifiable or excusable"`、`"ought to find a true bill"`
- **表达方式**：用“I am bound to tell you”把 Baron Huddleston 写成“斩钉截铁”——这份“强硬”正是后文“大陪审团当天返回起诉书”的影响力来源。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把 Baron Huddleston 写成“明确指示”——正是这份明确，让后文“大陪审团当天返回起诉书”读起来是法官推动的结果，而非陪审团独立判断。

> **原句 8:** "The day after the proceeding, a member of the grand jury paid an unofficial visit to Dudley. The visitor said he felt he and his fellow grand jurors had no choice but to return a true bill. It was not entirely clear what he meant by that cryptic comment. The whole point of the proceeding was that the grand jury was free to return a true bill or not. The grand juror may have been confirming what was so often said about Baron Huddleston, that he was adept at pushing jurors to his desired outcome. Or Dudley's visitor may have been saying that it seemed clear to the grand jurors that the defendants had in fact committed a crime—clear enough, at least, to meet the low bar of sending the case on to a jury trial."

- **中文理解**：第二天，一位大陪审团成员非正式地拜访了 Dudley。他说，他和他的大陪审团同僚别无选择，只能提出起诉书。这句耐人寻味的话究竟是什么意思，并不十分清楚。整个程序的要旨正在于：大陪审团完全可以选择提出起诉书或不提出。这位大陪审团成员或许是在印证人们常说的话——Baron Huddleston 很擅长把陪审员推向他想要的结果。也或许，他是在说，在大陪审团看来被告确实犯了罪，清楚到至少足以跨过把案件送交陪审团审理这道不高的门槛。
- **句子结构**：六句递进：有人来访 → 别无选择 → 耐人寻味 → 程序本可自由选择 → 或在印证 Huddleston 的强势 → 或在说罪行已然清楚。
- **关键词**：`"no choice but to return a true bill"`、`"cryptic comment"`、`"adept at pushing jurors"`
- **表达方式**：用“cryptic comment”把这位大陪审团成员的话写成“含混”——这份含混让后文“两种解读”有了不确定的余地。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把这次拜访写成“耐人寻味”——正是这份耐人寻味，让后文“两种解读”并存：Huddleston 的影响力，抑或只是跨过了那道不高的门槛。

> **原句 9:** "The visitor was aware that he and his fellow grand jurors had put Dudley and Stephens on a path toward the death penalty. But the purpose of his visit was not to dwell on the impending death sentence but rather to put the defendants' minds at ease. The grand juror went on to tell Dudley to "rest assured," because "you will be granted a free pardon we all are sure." But these words of reassurance were not actually reassuring. The grand juror was predicting that Dudley and Stephens would be sentenced to death. As for the royal pardon, Dudley knew there was no way his visitor, or anyone else, could know for certain what William Harcourt, head of the Home Office, and Queen Victoria would do. He was worried his visitor might be right about the verdict and punishment but wrong about the royal pardon. "From that hour," Dudley later said in a legal filing, "I prayed for strength to bear that awful sentence.""

- **中文理解**：这位访客清楚，他和他的大陪审团同僚已经把 Dudley 与 Stephens 推上了通往死刑之路。但他来访的目的不是纠缠于即将到来的死刑判决，而是要让被告们安心。他继续告诉 Dudley“放心吧”，因为“你们一定会得到赦免，我们都确信”。然而，这些安慰的话其实并不让人安心——这位大陪审团成员是在预告 Dudley 与 Stephens 将被判处死刑。至于王室赦免，Dudley 明白，他的访客或任何人都无法确知内政大臣 William Harcourt 与维多利亚女王会作何决定。他担心访客对判决与处罚的预测或许是对的，对王室赦免的预测却可能错了。“从那一刻起，”Dudley 后来在一份法律文书中说，“我就祈求有力量承受那可怕的判决。”
- **句子结构**：九句递进：知情 → 来访目的是安抚 → “放心吧……” → 并不令人安心 → 预告死刑 → 赦免无从预知 → 担心判断有对有错 → “从那一刻起……”的引语。
- **关键词**：`"rest assured"`、`"you will be granted a free pardon we all are sure"`、`"I prayed for strength to bear that awful sentence"`
- **表达方式**：用“not actually reassuring”把这位大陪审团成员的话写成“反而令人心惊”——这份心惊为后文“Dudley 的祈祷”提供情感层面的根据。
- **为什么这样写**：作者把这次拜访写成“令人心惊”——正是这份心惊，让后文“Dudley 的祈祷”不只是修辞：他真的在为承受判决祈求力量。

## 一句话总结

Baron Huddleston 用一次不留情面的开场白，把大陪审团推向起诉书：他否定 St. Kitts 案与 Holmes 案两个必要性抗辩的先例，斥抽签选人为“近乎亵渎”，否定自卫，又搬出西塞罗与“保护弱者”的维多利亚原则，最后直接说“你们理应提出起诉书”——当天大陪审团即返回起诉书，而那位来探视的成员那句“放心吧，我们确信会得到赦免”，让死刑第一次悬在 Dudley 头上。
