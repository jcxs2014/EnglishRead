# 09. 8 The Grand Jury Weighs In（大陪审团的 weigh in）

## 概览

- **出处**：*The Captain's Dinner*，Adam Cohen 著
- **作者**：Adam Cohen
- **章节定位**：全书第 8 章。叙述 Exeter Assize 的 grand jury proceeding：Baron Huddleston 的 instructions（dismiss necessity, repudiate custom, reject self-defense, Victorian reform principles）、grand jury 返回 true bill、royal prerogative of mercy 的 preview
- **字符数**：约 23,500（提取件 `text/ch09_8_the_grand_jury_weighs_in.txt`）
- **一句话主旨**：Baron Huddleston 作为 "strong judge" 把 grand jury 推向 true bill——dismiss necessity (St. Kitts/Holmes), repudiate custom of the sea (blasphemous), reject self-defense, invoke Victorian reform (protect young/weak), preview royal mercy；grand jury 返回 true bill，trial 三天后

## 论证结构

- **核心论点**：Baron Huddleston 用 "strong judge" 的方式把 grand jury 推向 true bill——他 dismiss necessity precedent，repudiate custom of the sea，reject self-defense，invoke Victorian reform principles，preview royal mercy；grand jury 返回 true bill，trial 三天后。

- **证据链**：

  | 证据 | 类型 | 支撑什么 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Baron Huddleston "strong Judge" reputation | 人物 | 显示 his influence on jury |
  | "It seems clear...deliberate homicide" | 法官引语 | 显示 his directness |
  | St. Kitts case dismissed (Dutch physician's medical curiosities) | 法律 | 显示 his dismissal of precedent |
  | Holmes dismissed (American, not binding; no lots) | 法律 | 显示 his dismissal of necessity |
  | "Such a reason would seem almost to verge upon the blasphemous" | 法官引语 | 显示 his repudiation of custom |
  | Self-defense rejected (Parker not endangering) | 法律 | 显示 his rejection of self-defense |
  | Young people's lives more valuable (Cicero) | Victorian reform | 显示 his reform principles |
  | Protect weak from strong | Victorian reform | 显示 his reform principles |
  | Royal prerogative of mercy preview | 法律 | 显示 two-stage system |
  | Grand jury returned true bill same day | 事实 | 显示 his influence |
  | Grand juror visit: "rest assured...free pardon we all are sure" | 事实 | 显示 grand jury's sympathy + fear of death penalty |

- **论证脉络**：public interest → grand jury proceeding → Baron Huddleston's instructions (facts, law, necessity dismissal, custom repudiation, self-defense rejection, Victorian reform) → royal mercy preview → grand jury returns true bill → grand juror's visit

- **可质疑处**：
  1. **Baron Huddleston 的 "strong judge"  reputation**：作者的 "strong judge" framing 可能 overstate his influence；grand jury 可能 independently agreed.
  2. **"Victorian reform principles" 的 invoke**：作者把 reform principles 与 judicial reasoning linked，但 Baron Huddleston 的 "moralizing" 可能是 personal, not reformist.
  3. **Grand juror's visit**：作者解读 "cryptic comment" 为 Baron Huddleston's influence，但可能只是 "low bar met"。

## 选择性精读

> **原句 1:** "Baron Huddleston began by telling the grand jury the facts of the case: the shipwreck, the time the men spent in the lifeboat, the killing of Parker, and the cannibalism. His account was neutral and nonjudgmental, and he was generous in describing Dudley. The captain was, he said, "a man of exemplary character, great experience, and courage." Baron Huddleston then explained the relevant law. This was standard, and he was right to do it, but Baron Huddleston was unusually direct in stating what he believed the facts and law added up to. "It seems clear," he said, "that the taking away of the boy's life was carefully considered and amounted to a case of deliberate homicide.""

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 首先向大陪审团讲述案件事实：海难、水手们在救生艇上度过的时光、Parker 的被杀、食人。他的叙述是中立且不带评判的，他在描述 Dudley 时相当慷慨。船长是，他说，"一个品行典范、经验丰富、勇敢的人。"Baron Huddleston 随后解释了相关法律。这是标准做法，他这么做是对的，但 Baron Huddleston 在陈述他所认为的事实和法律加起来意味着什么时，异常地直接。"似乎很清楚，"他说，"夺走男孩生命的决定是经过慎重考虑的，构成故意杀人。"
- **句子结构**：六句：facts → neutral/generous → Dudley quote → law standard → unusually direct → "It seems clear..." quote。
- **关键词**：`"a man of exemplary character, great experience, and courage"`、`"deliberate homicide"`
- **表达方式**：用 "unusually direct" 把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "strong judge"——这种 "direct" framing 为后文 "bound to tell you" 提供 "consistent" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "strong judge"——这种 "strong" framing 为后文 "grand jury true bill" 提供 "influence" basis。

> **原句 2:** "The St. Kitts case, Baron Huddleston said, was not a precedent at all, because there was no reliable report of a legal decision. Everything that was known about it, he said, came from a Dutch physician's book of medical curiosities. There was no reason to believe, he added, that it was actually a holding of an English court. As for United States v. Holmes, Baron Huddleston said that it relied on the necessity doctrine, and unlike American law, British law did not provide any legal support for such a doctrine. Baron Huddleston also noted that Justice Henry Baldwin had only approved of sacrificing lives if lots were drawn. Dudley and Stephens had not drawn lots, so the Holmes case did not support their defense."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 说，St. Kitts 案完全不是先例，因为没有可靠的法律决定报告。关于它的一切，他说，都来自一本荷兰医生的医学奇闻书。他补充说，没有理由相信这实际上是英国法院的判决。至于 United States v. Holmes，Baron Huddleston 说它依赖必要性原则，而与美国法律不同，英国法律不为这种原则提供任何法律支持。Baron Huddleston 还指出，Henry Baldwin 法官只在抽签的情况下才批准牺牲生命。Dudley 和 Stephens 没有抽签，所以 Holmes 案不支持他们的抗辩。
- **句子结构**：六句：St. Kitts not precedent → Dutch physician → no holding → Holmes relied on necessity → British law no support → Baldwin approved only if lots → Dudley did not draw → Holmes not support。
- **关键词**：`"not a precedent at all"`、`"Dutch physician's book of medical curiosities"`、`"British law did not provide any legal support"`
- **表达方式**：用 "Dutch physician's book of medical curiosities" 把 St. Kitts case 写成 "unreliable"——这种 "unreliable" framing 为后文 "dismiss necessity" 提供 "precedent" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "dismissing precedent"——这种 "dismissal" 为后文 "necessity defense weak" 提供 "legal" basis（no precedent for necessity in English law）。

> **原句 3:** "Baron Huddleston went further and directly repudiated the custom of the sea. He noted that Justice Baldwin had endorsed the drawing of lots as a basis for deciding who should be killed in an emergency at sea, seeing it as "in some sort, an appeal to God for selection of the victim." Baron Huddleston regarded Justice Baldwin's analysis as not only wrong but offensive. "Such a reason would seem almost to verge upon the blasphemous," he said."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 更进一步，直接驳回了"海难旧俗"。他指出 Baldwin 法官曾认可抽签作为在海上紧急情况下决定谁该被杀的依据，视之为"某种意义上是对上帝的呼吁，以选择牺牲者"。Baron Huddleston 认为 Baldwin 法官的分析不仅错误而且冒犯。"这样的理由似乎几乎要接近亵渎，"他说。
- **句子结构**：四句：repudiated → Baldwin endorsed → wrong and offensive → "blasphemous" quote。
- **关键词**：`"directly repudiated"`、`"verge upon the blasphemous"`
- **表达方式**：用 "blasphemous" 把 custom of the sea 写成 "offensive"——这种 "offensive" framing 为后文 "repudiate custom" 提供 "moral" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "repudiating custom"——这种 "repudiation" 为后文 "necessity defense weak" 提供 "moral" basis（custom is blasphemous, not legal）。

> **原句 4:** "Having rejected the necessity defense, Baron Huddleston went on to explain why the doctrine of self-defense did not apply. It was true, he said, that the law sometimes permitted people to kill to prevent someone from killing them. But that had not been the case in the lifeboat, he said. Dudley and Stephens may have been in danger of dying of hunger and thirst, but Parker, "at the bottom of the boat, was not endangering their lives by any act of his," the judge pointed out. As a result, it was "impossible to say that the act of Dudley and Stephens was an act of self-defense.""

- **中文理解**：在拒绝必要性抗辩后，Baron Huddleston 继续解释为什么自卫原则不适用。他说，法律有时确实允许人们杀人以阻止某人杀他们。但这种情况在救生艇上并不适用，他说。Dudley 和 Stephens 可能面临饥渴而死的危险，但 Parker，"在船底，没有任何行为危及他们的生命，"法官指出。因此，"不可能说 Dudley 和 Stephens 的行为是自卫。"
- **句子结构**：五句：rejected necessity → self-defense sometimes permitted → not case in lifeboat → Parker not endangering → impossible to say self-defense。
- **关键词**：`"at the bottom of the boat, was not endangering their lives by any act of his"`、`"impossible to say that the act of Dudley and Stephens was an act of self-defense"`
- **表达方式**：用 "impossible to say" 把 self-defense 写成 "inapplicable"——这种 "inapplicable" framing 为后文 "no legal defense" 提供 "legal" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "rejecting self-defense"——这种 "rejection" 为后文 "no legal defense" 提供 "legal" basis（neither necessity nor self-defense applies）。

> **原句 5:** "Baron Huddleston argued that it was especially wrong to take the life of a young person as Dudley and Stephens had done. They had decided that Parker's life was less valuable than their own because he had no wife or children. On the contrary, young people's lives were more valuable, Baron Huddleston argued, citing ancient Rome's most famous lawyer. If somebody had to be sacrificed in an emergency, Cicero wrote, it should be the person least likely to contribute to the Republic. Baron Huddleston insisted that Parker, as the youngest person in the lifeboat, could be expected to live the longest and provide more service to England than the others."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 论证说，像 Dudley 和 Stephens 那样夺走一个年轻人的生命尤其错误。他们认为 Parker 的生命不如他们自己的有价值，因为他没有妻子或孩子。相反，Baron Huddleston 论证道，年轻人的生命更有价值，他援引了古罗马最著名的律师。如果在紧急情况下必须牺牲某人，西塞罗写道，应该是对共和国贡献最少的人。Baron Huddleston 坚持说，Parker 作为救生艇上最年轻的人，可以预期活得最久，为英国提供的服务比其他人更多。
- **句子结构**：五句：especially wrong → Parker less valuable → contrary more valuable → Cicero → Parker youngest/longest service。
- **关键词**：`"young people's lives were more valuable"`、`Cicero`、`"least likely to contribute to the Republic"`
- **表达方式**：用 Cicero 引语把 "young people more valuable" 写成 "classical authority"——这种 "authority" framing 为后文 "Victorian reform" 提供 "philosophical" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "invoking Cicero"——这种 "invocation" 为后文 "Victorian reform" 提供 "classical" basis（protect young = Cicero's principle）。

> **原句 6:** "Another broad Victorian reform principle that Baron Huddleston invoked was the importance of protecting the weak from being exploited by the strong. Baron Huddleston noted that Dudley considered Parker's illness to be a reason for sacrificing him, but he maintained that the opposite was true. "One would have imagined that the state of his health, and the misery in which he was at the time, would have obtained for him more consideration," he said."

- **中文理解**：Baron Huddleston 援引的另一个广泛的维多利亚改革原则是保护弱者不被强者剥削的重要性。Baron Huddleston 指出 Dudley 认为 Parker 的疾病是牺牲他的理由，但他坚持认为事实恰恰相反。"人们会想象，他的健康状况和当时的痛苦，本应为他赢得更多关照，"他说。
- **句子结构**：三句：protect weak → Dudley considered illness as reason → "One would have imagined..." quote。
- **关键词**：`"protecting the weak from being exploited by the strong"`、`"One would have imagined that the state of his health, and the misery in which he was at the time, would have obtained for him more consideration"`
- **表达方式**：用 "One would have imagined..." 把 Parker's illness 写成 "reason for more consideration"——这种 "consideration" framing 为后文 "Victorian reform" 提供 "moral" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "invoking reform principle"——这种 "invocation" 为后文 "Victorian reform" 提供 "moral" basis（protect weak, not exploit them）。

> **原句 7:** "Having covered both the facts and the law—with a strong dose of subjective moralizing—Baron Huddleston proceeded to firmly suggest to the grand jury what it should do. "I am bound to tell you that if you are satisfied that the boy's death was caused or accelerated by the act of Dudley, or by Dudley and Stephens, this is a case of deliberate homicide, neither justifiable or excusable," he said. "The crime is murder, and you, therefore, ought to find a true bill for that offense against both the prisoners.""

- **中文理解**：在涵盖事实和法律——以及大量主观道德化——之后，Baron Huddleston 继续坚定地向大陪审团建议它应该做什么。"我必须告诉你们，"他说，"如果你们确信男孩的死亡是由 Dudley 的行为或 Dudley 和 Stephens 的行为造成或加速的，这是一个故意杀人案件，既不能正当化也不能原谅。""罪行是谋杀，因此你们应该对两名囚犯都提出这项罪名的正式起诉书。"
- **句子结构**：三句：covered facts/law → "I am bound to tell you..." → "The crime is murder..."。
- **关键词**：`"I am bound to tell you"`、`"deliberate homicide, neither justifiable or excusable"`、`"ought to find a true bill"`
- **表达方式**：用 "I am bound to tell you" 把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "firm"——这种 "firm" framing 为后文 "grand jury true bill" 提供 "influence" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 Baron Huddleston 写成 "firmly suggest"——这种 "firmly" 为后文 "grand jury true bill" 提供 "influence" basis（judge told them to return true bill）。

> **原句 8:** "The day after the proceeding, a member of the grand jury paid an unofficial visit to Dudley. The visitor said he felt he and his fellow grand jurors had no choice but to return a true bill. It was not entirely clear what he meant by that cryptic comment. The whole point of the proceeding was that the grand jury was free to return a true bill or not. The grand juror may have been confirming what was so often said about Baron Huddleston, that he was adept at pushing jurors to his desired outcome. Or Dudley's visitor may have been saying that it seemed clear to the grand jurors that the defendants had in fact committed a crime—clear enough, at least, to meet the low bar of sending the case on to a jury trial."

- **中文理解**：诉讼后的第二天，一位大陪审团成员非正式地拜访了 Dudley。这位访客说他觉得他和他的 fellow 大陪审团成员别无选择，只能返回正式起诉书。他这句话的 cryptic comment 到底是什么意思并不完全清楚。诉讼的全部要点是大陪审团可以自由返回正式起诉书或不返回。这位大陪审团成员可能在证实人们常说的话——Baron Huddleston 擅长把陪审员推向他想要的结果。或者 Dudley 的访客可能在说，对大陪审团来说似乎很清楚被告实际上犯了罪——至少清楚到足以满足把案件送交陪审团审判的低标准。
- **句子结构**：六句：visitor → no choice → cryptic → free to return → may have been confirming Huddleston → or saying clear crime。
- **关键词**：`"no choice but to return a true bill"`、`"cryptic comment"`、`"adept at pushing jurors"`
- **表达方式**：用 "cryptic comment" 把 grand juror's words 写成 "ambiguous"——这种 "ambiguous" framing 为后文 "two interpretations" 提供 "uncertainty"。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 grand juror's visit 写成 "cryptic"——这种 "cryptic" 为后文 "two interpretations" 提供 "uncertainty"（Huddleston's influence vs low bar met）。

> **原句 9:** "The visitor was aware that he and his fellow grand jurors had put Dudley and Stephens on a path toward the death penalty. But the purpose of his visit was not to dwell on the impending death sentence but rather to put the defendants' minds at ease. The grand juror went on to tell Dudley to "rest assured," because "you will be granted a free pardon we all are sure." But these words of reassurance were not actually reassuring. The grand juror was predicting that Dudley and Stephens would be sentenced to death. As for the royal pardon, Dudley knew there was no way his visitor, or anyone else, could know for certain what William Harcourt, head of the Home Office, and Queen Victoria would do. He was worried his visitor might be right about the verdict and punishment but wrong about the royal pardon. "From that hour," Dudley later said in a legal filing, "I prayed for strength to bear that awful sentence.""

- **中文理解**：访客意识到他和他的 fellow 大陪审团成员已将 Dudley 和 Stephens 推上死刑之路。但他拜访的目的不是 dwell on 即将到来的死刑判决，而是让被告安心。这位大陪审团成员继续告诉 Dudley "rest assured"，因为 "you will be granted a free pardon we all are sure"。但这些安慰的话实际上并不令人安心。这位大陪审团成员是在预测 Dudley 和 Stephens 将被判处死刑。至于 royal pardon，Dudley 知道他的访客或任何人都无法确定 William Harcourt（内政大臣）和 Victoria 女王会做什么。他担心访客对 verdict 和 punishment 的预测可能是对的，但对 royal pardon 的预测可能是错的。"From that hour," Dudley 后来在 legal filing 中说，"I prayed for strength to bear that awful sentence."
- **句子结构**：九句：aware → purpose → "rest assured..." → not reassuring → predicting death → no way know → worried → "From that hour..."。
- **关键词**：`"rest assured"`、`"you will be granted a free pardon we all are sure"`、`"I prayed for strength to bear that awful sentence"`
- **表达方式**：用 "not actually reassuring" 把 grand juror's words 写成 "alarming"——这种 "alarming" framing 为后文 "Dudley's prayer" 提供 "emotional" basis。
- **为什么这样写**：作者在此把 grand juror's visit 写成 "alarming"——这种 "alarming" 为后文 "Dudley's prayer" 提供 "emotional" basis（Dudley prayed for strength to bear sentence）。
