# 14. Jean Rhys（简·里斯）

## 概览

- **出处**：Stet: A Memoir（Granta Publications 2000 初版；Grove Press 2001 美版）
- **作者**：Diana Athill——叙述者「我」
- **章节定位**：Part Two 六篇作家肖像之**第二篇**，也是**全书最长的一章**（正文 110 段，58,901 字符）。分七部分：① 迟到的相遇（1964）与九年书信的空白（`six or nine months` → 1966 年 3 月）② 报偿之低（`so mean it was`）③ Dominica 的地理与政治，以及它如何解释她 ④ *Wide Sargasso Sea* 的完成（1966）⑤ Sonia Orwell 的二十八年资助（`the most active member of what we called ‘the Jean Committee’`）⑥ 女儿 Maryvonne 与那个**无法被写作修复**的伤害 ⑦ *Smile Please* 与她自题「Notes for a biography which will never be written」的遗作片段
- **人物**：Jean Rhys、`Francis Wyndham`（1950 年代那四本小说「very few admirers」之一；与 Athill 通信七年的编辑）、`Esther Whitby`（另一位 Deutsch 编辑，1964 年 volunteer 去 Cheriton 帮她整理稿子）、`Max`（Jean 的丈夫，后入狱，Jean 七十年代独自护理他）、`Sonia Orwell`（George Orwell 的遗孀，Jean 的资助者与「Jean Committee」最积极成员）、`Maryvonne Moerman`（Jean 与 Max 的女儿）、`David`（Sonia 的哥哥，与 Jean 的酗酒关系，`steering his way between the disintegration`）、`Colonel Rees-Williams`、`Francis Wyndham`、`Carole Angier`（Jean 传记作者）、`Peggy Ramsay`（戏剧agent，此后接手 Jean 全部影视广播版权）、`Selma vaz Dias`（演员，*The Exquisite Corpse* 一案）、`Arnold Goodman`（律师）、`Jo Batterham`、`Gini Stevens`
- **书名**（Jean）：前四部小说（1950 年代，`the four novels`，此处不点名）、*Wide Sargasso Sea*（1966）、*Smile Please*（最后一部）、*The Imperial Road*（未竟稿）
- **书名**（Athill 自己引的片段）：`Notes for a biography which will never be written`（Athill 自己给 Jean 未刊片段起的名字，节选含 THE MOTHER / THE FATHER / THEIR DAUGHTER 三节）
- **地名**：Cheriton FitzPaine（萨默塞特村，Jean 晚年住处）、Landboat Bungalows（多塞特海边，Jean 的住处）、Roseau（Dominica 首都）、Castries（St Lucia）、Portobello Hotel、Ring of Bells（村 pub）、Exeter
- **刊物/公司**：André Deutsch Limited、Dominica（`The Commonwealth of Dominica`）、UNRRA（Brian Moore 一节提到，此处不重）
- **字符数**：58,901（text/ch14_jean_rhys.txt）
- **一句话主旨**：这一章要同时做三件在写作史上极难的事——把一个**写不出**的人写成一个人（`how this hopelessly inept, seemingly incomplete woman could write with such clarity, power and grace remains a mystery`），把一个**岛屿**写成一种**性格**（`Her creed … was that she must tell the truth: must get things down as they really were`），并承认自己对她的**无能为力**（`I did almost nothing to help her during a long period of excruciating difficulty`）

## 论证结构

**核心论点**：本章的论证分三段推进，且每一段都推翻上一段的乐观读法。**第一段**（相遇与报偿）证明：这家出版社**没有**帮她，甚至没有见过她——`I did almost nothing to help her during a long period of excruciating difficulty`。**第二段**（Dominica）证明：她「不会生活」（`bad at life`）**不是性格缺陷而是地理后果**——`rich you did not get`，因此一个人从小得到的训练就是**无用**（`her abhorrence of what she saw as Englishness did make her embrace her own incompetence`）。**第三段**（完成与身后）证明：她终于写成了，但**代价由别人付**（Sonia 的二十八年），且**写作不能修复那个真正的伤害**（`the person she wanted forgiveness from was the abandoned child`）。

**论证脉络**：以**认识论的落差**开篇（读过她的人不会知道她多糟，见过她的人才会）→ 交代**七年只通信**的事实与自己的**无能为力**（`I did almost nothing to help her`）→ 交代**报偿之低**与她的三个辩护（三个 `which is true` 后跟一句 `it is inconceivable that anyone would have paid less`）→ 交代九年里她用 `gallant front` 掩盖的东西（`leaking pipes, or mice in the kitchen`）→ 交代崩溃机制（`incapacitate her for weeks … she would crack`）→ **提出信条**（`she must tell the truth: must get things down as they really were`）→ **立刻给出它的有效与失效**（Angier 的证明；`But it didn’t work in ‘The Imperial Road’`）→ 转向 Dominica 作为解释（`I think I have reached a better understanding of the bad-at-life side of Jean since coming to know Dominica`）→ 建立**两种人**的分类（Caribs vs 殖民者）→ 用农业史推出 `rich you did not get` → 落到肤色即阶层 → 落到英格兰的陌生感与 `It did not occur to her to learn, all she could do was hate` → 用 Castries 事件证明反应持续终生 → **给出本节最冷的一句**（`did make her embrace her own incompetence`）→ 写 1964 年 11 月的相遇与**被心绞痛打断**的仪式（`So there was no triumph over a bottle of champagne`）→ 写编辑的实际劳动（`I had to pack her into an ambulance`）→ **给出第二版友谊判据**（`it would be a mistake to suppose that meant trust`）→ 写 1966 年 3 月 9 日完成的信（四行，含 `it made no sense to me` 与 `fallen off`）→ 写稿子的物理状态与 Esther 的志愿 → 写**唯一的编辑介入**（`never needed ‘tidying up’`）→ 写 Sonia 的资助与其自我贬抑 → 写「Jean Committee」与 `Better the devil I know` → 写**女儿**与那句 `the person she wanted forgiveness from was the abandoned child` → 写 Maryvonne 来访与 `my heart sank` → 写（当快乐时的）年轻 Jean → 写 *Smile Please* 的极简 → 写她**看不到工作中的自己**（`Out of her eyes, then, looked a whole and fearless being`）→ 以未刊片段三节收束

**可质疑处**：

1. 全章对 Jean「不会生活」的解释最终落到一句 `her abhorrence of what she saw as Englishness did make her embrace her own incompetence`（她对「英国性」的憎恶**使她**接受了自己的无能）。这是一个**极强的心理因果**，而它与本章的**地理论证**（Dominica 教不出别的）其实**互相竞争**：如果地理决定了她学不会谋生，那她对英国性的憎恶是**结果**；如果憎恶导致了她的无能，那地理就只是**背景**。她把两条线都当作解释给出（`I have reached a better understanding of the bad-at-life side of Jean since coming to know Dominica`），却没有区分它们是**互补**还是**互斥**。而 her own incompetence 里的 `incompetence`（无能）这个词，把她早年**在 Dominica 受到的、具体的、无力**的处境，压缩成了一个**性格词**——这一步压缩做得太快，也太像 ch10 那句 `a specialized list … must be published by a specialist`（用一句格言代替一次说明）。
2. 她对 Jean 与 Dominica 的叙述里嵌入了大量**非虚构的地理与历史**（Caribs 的抵抗、殖民者的作物清单、白人即上层阶级），这些材料她都给了脚注来源（`Lennox Honychurch`、`Peter Hulme`），来源纪律是好的。但**她自己的**那一段解释（Castries 事件 → 反应持续终生 → 憎恶导致无能）**没有**任何证据来源，而它是全章的**结论性**环节。读者无法分辨：哪些是**考察**、哪些是**同情性重构**。
3. 「友谊 ≠ 信任」这个第二版判据（`it would be a mistake to suppose that meant trust`）与她自己的**后续行为**存在张力：她说 Jean **从不**信任任何人（`Jean never entirely trusted anybody`），却在接下来的**二十多年**里每年陪她过冬、组织委员会、替她处理财务与合同、追查 Selma 合同纠纷。这些都是**长期**的、**琐碎**的照护，而 ch12 那条判据说友谊以「结束时是否引起 disturbance」来判定。**没有被写出的**是：Jean 死时她是否在场、以及这段关系的结束是否引起了她的 disturbance——这正是判据唯一需要的输入。全章因此**测试**了那条判据，却**没有报告结果**。
4. `This Jean could never undo, whatever she wrote, because the person she wanted forgiveness from was the abandoned child` 这句是全章的伦理中心，也是全书最反道德律之一（写作能做什么、不能做什么）。但它的**举证**只有一件事（Maryvonne 来信、来伦敦、她心沉）。而她对 Maryvonne 的了解全部来自**转述**（`Maryvonne came to London and asked me to lunc…`），她本人从未与 Maryvonne 交谈过——她连 Jean 想要什么样的道歉都不知道。因此这句判断在结构上是**转述之上的转述**，而它被放在了「**无论她写什么**」这一**全称**位置上。

## 选择性精读

### ①

> **原句 1:** NO ONE WHO has read Jean Rhys’s first four novels can suppose that she was good at life; but no one who never met her could know how very bad at it she was. I was introduced to the novels quite early in the fifties, by Francis Wyndham, who was one of their very few admirers at that time, and I started corresponding with her in 1957; but I didn’t meet her until 1964; and as a result I did almost nothing to help her during a long period of excruciating difficulty.

**中文理解**：**读过**简·里斯前四部小说的人，不会以为她擅长生活；但**从未见过她**的人，不会知道她有多不擅长。五十年代初期我就被介绍读这几本小说，那是 Francis Wyndham 介绍的——他当时是它们**极少数**欣赏者之一——而我在 1957 年开始与她通信；但我直到 1964 年才见到她；结果是在一段**极其痛苦**的漫长时期里，我**几乎没做什么**来帮她。

**句子结构**：单句，是「**对照的两个分句**（NO ONE WHO has read … can suppose that she was good at life; but no one who never met her could know how very bad at it she was）+ 插入的施事说明（I was introduced to the novels … and I started corresponding with her in 1957; but I didn’t meet her until 1964;）+ 结果状语 and as a result I did almost nothing to help her during a long period of excruciating difficulty」。

**关键词**：NO ONE WHO has read … can suppose that（读过…的人不会以为）、no one who never met her could know how very bad at it she was（从未见过她的人不会知道她有多糟）、the novels quite early in the fifties（五十年代初期这几本小说）、one of their very few admirers（它们极少数的欣赏者之一）、I started corresponding with her in 1957（1957 年开始与她通信）、I didn’t meet her until 1964（我直到 1964 年才见到她）、I did almost nothing to help her（我几乎没做什么来帮她）、a long period of excruciating difficulty（一段极其痛苦的漫长时期）

**表达方式**：用一个 **`NO ONE WHO … ; but no one who …`** 的**对称否定结构**把「读者」与「认识者」的两种知识**分开**；用 **`can suppose`（会以为）** 与 **`could know`（会知道）** 两个**认知动词**的差别——一个是**推测**，一个是**知道**——来标记两种信息的**确定性不同**；用一个 **`almost nothing`**（几乎什么都不是）来写自己的**无功**；末句用 **`excruciating`**（极其痛苦的）这一**身体性的**词，而不是任何关于书的形容词。

**为什么这样写**：这是全章的**第一句**，而它做的事是**把「读」与「见」分开**。一个人可以在完全不知道作者生活的前提下读她四本小说——而那四本小说**恰恰不写**这种生活。于是全章的整个项目被这句定义：**她要写的是读者读不到的那部分**（`how very bad at it she was`），而那部分只有七年的**通信**和一个 **1964 年**的面面相对可作材料。末句的自我定罪值得特别留意：`I did almost nothing to help her during a long period of excruciating difficulty`——`almost nothing` 而不是 `nothing`（`almost` 承认她**确实做了一点**），`excruciating` 修饰的是 **difficulty** 而非任何事——也就是说，被**困难本身**困住的不是 Jean，是**他们之间的沟通**。这一句与 ch12 那句 `The person with whom the writer wants to be in touch is his reader` 构成一组对照：在 Jean 这件事上，**中间人（编辑）花了七年**才走到那位作家面前，而她与「读者」之间的关系则要等 1966 年那本书才成立。

### ②

> **原句 2:** When people exclaim at how mean this was I no longer blush simply because I have blushed so often. I tell myself that the pound bought much more in the fifties than it does now, which is true; that this was not, after all, an advance, only an advance on an advance, which is true; and that no one else in those days would have paid much more for an option, and that, too, is true. But it is inconceivable that anyone would have paid less – so mean it was. If we had known anything about Jean’s circumstances I am sure that Francis and I would have fought for more, but it would be a long time before we gained any idea of them.

**中文理解**：当人们惊呼这有多过分时，我已经**不再脸红**了——仅仅因为我脸红了太多次。我告诉自己：五十年代一英镑能买的东西比现在多得多，**这是真的**；这毕竟不是一笔预付款，只是**预付款的预付款**，**这也是真的**；而那个年代别人为一份期权也不会多付多少，**这同样是真的**。但**任何人都不可能付得更少**——就是这么过分。如果我们当时对 Jean 的处境有**任何**了解，我确信 Francis 和我会争取更多；但我们要过**很久**才对她的处境有哪怕一点概念。

**句子结构**：两句：首句是「时间状语从句 When people exclaim at how mean this was + 主句 I no longer blush + 原因状语 simply because I have blushed so often + 并列的自我辩护（三个 that 从句，各自以 `which is true` / `and that, too, is true` 结句）——`I tell myself that …, which is true; that …, which is true; and that …, and that, too, is true`」；次句是「转折句 But it is inconceivable that anyone would have paid less – so mean it was + 条件句 If we had known anything about Jean’s circumstances I am sure that Francis and I would have fought for more, but 转折句 it would be a long time before we gained any idea of them」。

**关键词**：I no longer blush simply because I have blushed so often（我不再脸红，仅仅因为我脸红太多次）、the pound bought much more in the fifties（五十年代一英镑能买的东西多得多）、an advance on an advance（预付款的预付款）、no one else in those days would have paid much more for an option（那个年代别人为一份期权也不会多付）、it is inconceivable that anyone would have paid less（任何人都不可能付得更少）、so mean it was（就是这么过分）、If we had known anything about Jean’s circumstances（如果我们对她的处境有任何了解）、it would be a long time before we gained any idea of them（我们要过很久才对她的处境有点概念）

**表达方式**：用**三个平行的 `that …, which is true`** 把自我辩护写成**三项清点**（每项都自己盖章「这是真的」）；用 **`so mean it was`**（就是这么过分）这一**倒装的强调**作转折的落点——把 `inconceivable` 的判断缩成**一个词**；用一个 **`it would be a long time before …`** 的将来完成式把「我们不知道」写成**一件未来的事**（而不是承认当时无知）。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章对**出版商的道德责任**最诚实的一次处理，而它的技术是**先认罪、再辩护、再放弃辩护**。三个 `which is true` 极其重要：她不是在**辩解**（pleading），而是在**清点事实**（`the pound bought more`、`it was only an advance on an advance`、`nobody would have paid more`）——而每一项都**只证明这不是我们的错**（我们没有少付**相对**而言），**不证明这是对的**。因此当她写下 `But it is inconceivable that anyone would have paid less – so mean it was` 时，她实际上是**自己拆了自己的辩护**——三个 `true` 全部为真，而结论仍是否定。末句那个 `it would be a long time before we gained any idea of them` 才是真正的关键：它把责任**从无知**移到了**距离**（`a long time`）与**渠道**（`gained any idea`——「获得一点概念」这个动词说明他们连**问**都没问到能问的地方）。这一句与 ch13 那条 `a specialized list … must be published by a specialist` 构成一组：Jean 的书需要一位**知道 Dominica 的**编辑，而 André Deutsch Limited 里没有。

### ③

> **原句 3:** The trouble was, she kept up a gallant front. In the letters we exchanged between 1957, when she said that her book would be finished in ‘six or nine months’, and March 1966, when she announced that it was finished, she would refer to being held up by domestic disasters such as leaking pipes, or mice in the kitchen, and she would make the disasters sound funny. Not until I met her did I understand that for Jean such incidents were appalling: they knocked her right out because her inability to cope with life’s practicalities went beyond anything I ever saw in anyone generally taken to be sane. Max’s health had given out, but her loyalty to him extended beyond keeping silent about his prison sentence to disguising his subsequent helplessness. It was years before I learnt how dreadful her seventies had been as she alternated between the struggle to nurse him and bleak loneliness when he was in hospital. She ate too little, drank too much, was frightened, exhausted and ill – and paranoid into the bargain, seeing the village of Cheriton FitzPaine (to which they moved during these years) as a cruel place. So any little horror on top of all this would incapacitate her for weeks. And when it passed a certain point she would crack.

**中文理解**：问题在于，她**硬撑着一副勇敢的门面**。在我们 1957 年（她说她的书会在「**六到九个月**」内完成）到 1966 年 3 月（她宣布完成了）之间的通信里，她会提到自己被**家中的灾难**耽搁——比如**漏水的管子**、**厨房里的老鼠**——而她会把这些灾难说得**很好笑**。直到我见到她，我才明白对 Jean 来说这类事有多可怕：它们会**把她整个击倒**，因为她应付生活实际事务的无能，超出我在任何**通常被认为神志正常**的人身上见过的一切。Max 的健康已经垮了，但她的忠诚不止于**对他的监禁保持沉默**，还延伸到了**掩饰他后来的无助**。直到过了好几年我才明白，她七十年代的那些日子有多可怕——她在**护理他**的挣扎与**他住院时**的 bleak 孤独之间来回切换。她吃得太少、喝得太多、害怕、疲惫、病着——**此外还**疑心，把 Cheriton FitzPaine 村（这些年他们住在那里）看作一个残忍的地方。所以在这一切之上再加任何一点小恐怖，都会让她**失去能力**好几周。而一旦过了某个**临界点**，她就会**裂开**。

**句子结构**：首句是「主句 The trouble was, she kept up a gallant front」；次句是「**时间状语（一个以 `between … and …` 框住的时间跨度，内含两个 when 从句）+ 主句 she would refer to being held up by … + 并列句 and she would make the disasters sound funny**」；末段是「倒装强调句 `Not until I met her did I understand that …`（内含 for Jean such incidents were appalling: they knocked her right out because …）+ 独立句 Max’s health had given out, but her loyalty to him extended beyond keeping silent about his prison sentence to disguising his subsequent helplessness + 独立句 It was years before I learnt how dreadful her seventies had been as she alternated between … and … + 排比句 She ate too little, drank too much, was frightened, exhausted and ill – and paranoid into the bargain, seeing … as a cruel place + 独立句 So any little horror on top of all this would incapacitate her for weeks + 独立句 And when it passed a certain point she would crack`」。

**关键词**：she kept up a gallant front（她硬撑着一副勇敢的门面）、between 1957 … and March 1966（1957 到 1966 年 3 月之间）、‘six or nine months’（「六到九个月」）、domestic disasters such as leaking pipes, or mice in the kitchen（漏水的管子、厨房里的老鼠）、she would make the disasters sound funny（她把灾难说得很好笑）、Not until I met her did I understand（直到见到她我才明白）、they knocked her right out（它们把她整个击倒）、her inability to cope with life’s practicalities went beyond anything I ever saw in anyone generally taken to be sane（她应付生活实际事务的无能超出我在任何通常被认为神志正常的人身上见过的）、her loyalty to him extended beyond keeping silent about his prison sentence to disguising his subsequent helplessness（她的忠诚延伸到掩饰他后来的无助）、alternated between the struggle to nurse him and bleak loneliness（在护理他的挣扎与bleak 孤独之间来回）、ate too little, drank too much, was frightened, exhausted and ill（吃得少、喝得多、怕、累、病）、and paranoid into the bargain（此外还疑心）、any little horror … would incapacitate her for weeks（任何一点小恐怖都会让她失去能力好几周）、when it passed a certain point she would crack（一旦过了某个临界点她就会裂开）

**表达方式**：用一个 **`gallant front`（勇敢的门面）** 这一军事化的词写**信件里的姿态**——而它与 `she would make the disasters sound funny`（把灾难说得很好笑）构成**同一姿态的两种表现**；用一个 **`between 1957 … and March 1966`** 的框把**九年**压成**一个状语**；用一个 **`Not until … did I …`** 的倒装把「**见面**」标为**认识的门槛**；用一个 **`went beyond anything I ever saw in anyone generally taken to be sane`** 这一**极端化比较**（`generally taken to be sane` = 通常被认为正常）来为一个**个人**的判断辩护；用五个**过去式动词的排比**（`ate too little, drank too much, was frightened, exhausted and ill`）加一个 **`and paranoid into the bargain`** 把**第五项**（疑心）写成**追加物**；末两句用 **`any little horror`** 与 **`a certain point`** 两个**量词**写**脆性的非线性**。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章对「**如何**生活」这件事最具体的一段，而它的形式是**一次翻译的失败**——她在信里写的是 `leaking pipes, or mice in the kitchen`（很具体、很小、很好笑），而真实原因（`her inability to cope with life’s practicalities`）在九年里**一个字也没有**。这个「九年只谈老鼠」的事实被写成一个**时间状语的框**，读者由此得到一个不舒服的推论：她与编辑之间**所有**通信都可以是关于**管道**的。而 `went beyond anything I ever saw in anyone generally taken to be sane` 是极重的判断——它把 Jean 放在「一般被认为正常」这条线**之外**——但它被写成**她自己的印象**（`anything I ever saw`），并且紧跟着 `Not until I met her did I understand` 这个**知识上的转折**，所以它是**观察报告**而非**诊断**。末两句（`would incapacitate her for weeks` / `she would crack`）把脆性写成**两级**（量的：几周；质的：裂开），而这个非线性正是 ch09 那条 `square peg`（不合模子的）分类在此的**最严重**形态——她不是难相处，她是**有一个阈值**，而阈值以下没事、以上就全部崩掉。

### ④

> **原句 4:** People are not, thank God, wholly explicable. Carole Angier’s biography of Jean does as much as anything ever will to explain the connections between the life and the work, but how this hopelessly inept, seemingly incomplete woman could write with such clarity, power and grace remains a mystery. I have long since settled for this fact; but I think I have reached a better understanding of the bad-at-life side of Jean since coming to know Dominica*, the island in the eastern Caribbean where she was born.

**中文理解**：**感谢上帝**，人不是全都能被解释的。Carole Angier 那本 Jean 的传记，做到了任何作品所能做的最好程度——解释了生活与作品之间的联系——但**这样一个无可救药地无能、看上去并不完整的女人，怎么能写得这样清晰、有力、优雅，却仍然是个谜**。我早就**接受**了这个事实；但我想，**自从我认识/了解了 Dominica**（她出生的那个东加勒比海岛屿）以来，我对 Jean「不擅长生活」的那一面有了更好的理解。

**句子结构**：两句：首句是「主句（形式主语 People + 插入语 thank God + 系动词 are not + 表语 wholly explicable）」；次句是「主句（主语 Carole Angier’s biography of Jean + 谓语 does as much as anything ever will + 不定式 to explain the connections between the life and the work）+ 转折连词 but + 主语从句 how this hopelessly inept, seemingly incomplete woman could write with such clarity, power and grace + 系动词 remains + 表语 a mystery + 并列句 I have long since settled for this fact; but I think I have reached a better understanding of the bad-at-life side of Jean since coming to know Dominica, the island in the eastern Caribbean where she was born」。

**关键词**：People are not, thank God, wholly explicable（感谢上帝，人不是全都能被解释的）、Carole Angier’s biography of Jean（Carole Angier 那本 Jean 的传记）、does as much as anything ever will to explain the connections between the life and the work（解释生活与作品的联系，已做到极限）、how this hopelessly inept, seemingly incomplete woman could write with such clarity, power and grace（这样一个无可救药地无能、看上去并不完整的女人怎么能写得这样清晰、有力、优雅）、remains a mystery（仍然是个谜）、I have long since settled for this fact（我早就接受了这个事实）、the bad-at-life side of Jean（Jean「不擅长生活」的那一面）、since coming to know Dominica（自从我了解 Dominica 之后）

**表达方式**：用 **`thank God`**（感谢上帝）这个**插入语**把一个关于人的判断标为**幸运**而不是**缺陷**；用 **`wholly explicable`** 的 `wholly` 作**限定**——不是「不能解释」，是**不是全都能**；用一个 **`hopelessly inept, seemingly incomplete`** 的双重形容词把「**无能**」与「**不完整**」并置，而第二个用 `seemingly`（看似）**自我松动**；用一个 **`how … could …`** 的**感叹性从句**（`remains a mystery`）把**反差**写成**谜**；用 **`I have long since settled for this fact`**（我早就接受了）把一个**未解的谜**写成**已归档的事实**。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章的**方法论声明**，而它处理的是一个**传记无法解决**的问题：Angier 的传记已经做了**所能做的最好**，而仍然剩下一个谜——`how this hopelessly inept … woman could write with such clarity, power and grace`。这个反差不是「她其实很能干」（那会取消观察），而是**两个同时为真**的事实。这一段的结构值得注意：`I have long since settled for this fact` 把**谜**降级为**事实**（我不再追问），而**下一句立刻用 `but` 打开一个新方向**（`I think I have reached a better understanding of the bad-at-life side`）。也就是说：她放弃的是**整体的谜**（为什么她能写），转向的是**局部**（为什么她不会生活）——而这个转向是**地理的**（`since coming to know Dominica`）。这个分工很重要：**「怎么写出那样的文字」她不解（本节全部写的是不解），「怎么过不好日子」她用 Dominica 解释。** 而末句那个 `the island in the eastern Caribbean where she was born` 用了全书少见的**地理学定义**（`where she was born`），把 Dominica 从一个地名变回**一个人的来处**。

### ⑤

> **原句 5:** Human beings have two ways of relating to such terrain. If, like the Caribs, who were there when Columbus turned up, you are the kind of human who lives with nature rather than on or against it, you find it hospitable: you can’t freeze in it, you can’t starve in it, there is plenty of material for building shelters and a vast number of mighty trees out of which to make canoes; and if hostile humans invade they find it extremely difficult to move about in, while you can very easily hide, and then ambush them. (There are still more Caribs living in Dominica than anywhere else, and it enabled escaped slaves to put up a more impressive resistance to vengeful slave-owners than they could do on any other island.) But if you are the kind of human who likes to control nature, and hopes to make a profit from it, then you must either leave such an island alone, as the Spanish sensibly did, or else steel yourself to work very hard for sadly little return. Dominica’s settlers have tried planting a variety of crops – coffee, cocoa, a very little sugar (not enough flat ground), lots of bananas and citrus fruit, vanilla, bay rum . . . all of them reasonably profitable for a time, then wiped out or greatly reduced by hurricanes, blights, or shifts in the market. In many parts of the Caribbean planters made fortunes; in Dominica with luck you got by, but rich you did not get.

**中文理解**：人类对**这样的地形**有两种相与的方式。如果你是那种**与自然共处**（而不是在它上面、或与它对抗）的人——像哥伦布到来时就在那里的 Caribs 人那样——你会发现它是**宜人的**：你在它里面冻不死、也饿不死，有足够的材料搭建庇护所，还有大量可以造独木舟的高大树木；如果有敌对的人侵入，他们会发现自己在其中行动极其困难，而你很容易藏起来，然后**伏击**他们。（**至今 Dominica 的 Caribs 人比任何别的地方都多**，而正是这一点使**逃奴**得以对**复仇的奴隶主**作出比在任何其他岛上都更令人印象深刻的抵抗。）但如果你是那种**喜欢控制自然**、并希望从中获利的人，那么你要么**不去打扰**这样一个岛——**西班牙人明智地**就是这么做的——要么就打定主意**非常辛苦地工作，换来少得可怜的回报**。Dominica 的定居者试过种各种各样的作物——咖啡、可可、很少量的糖（平地不够）、大量的香蕉和柑橘、香草、月桂油……它们全都**在一段时间里还算有利可图**，然后**被飓风、病害或市场转移 wiped out 或大幅减少**。在加勒比的许多地方，种植园主发了财；在 Dominica，**有运气的话你过得去，但你并没有发达**。

**句子结构**：首句是「主句（主语 Human beings + 谓语 have + 宾语 two ways of relating to such terrain）」；次段是一个超长条件句「If, like the Caribs …, you are the kind of human who lives with nature rather than on or against it, you find it hospitable: you can’t freeze in it, you can’t starve in it, there is plenty of material for building shelters and a vast number of mighty trees out of which to make canoes; and if hostile humans invade they find it extremely difficult to move about in, while you can very easily hide, and then ambush them.」（内含四个并列的 `you … / there is …` 分句 + 一个嵌套的 if 从句 + while 对比）」（括注给史实支撑）」（`But if you are the kind of human who likes to control nature, and hopes to make a profit from it, then you must either leave such an island alone, as the Spanish sensibly did, or else steel yourself to work very hard for sadly little return.`）」；末段是「主句（Dominica’s settlers have tried planting a variety of crops）+ 破折号插入作物清单 + 省略主语的并列句 all of them reasonably profitable for a time, then wiped out or greatly reduced by … + 对比句 In many parts of the Caribbean planters made fortunes; in Dominica with luck you got by, but rich you did not get」。

**关键词**：two ways of relating to such terrain（对这样的地形有两种相与的方式）、lives with nature rather than on or against it（与自然共处，而不是在它上面或与它对抗）、you find it hospitable（你会发现它是宜人的）、you can’t freeze in it, you can’t starve in it（在它里面冻不死、饿不死）、a vast number of mighty trees out of which to make canoes（大量可造独木舟的高大树木）、hide, and then ambush them（藏起来，然后伏击他们）、escaped slaves … resistance to vengeful slave-owners（逃奴对复仇的奴隶主的抵抗）、likes to control nature, and hopes to make a profit from it（喜欢控制自然并希望从中获利）、leave such an island alone, as the Spanish sensibly did（不去打扰这样一个岛，西班牙人明智地这么做）、steel yourself to work very hard for sadly little return（打定主意非常辛苦地工作，换来少得可怜的回报）、all of them reasonably profitable for a time, then wiped out or greatly reduced（全都一段时间内有利可图，然后被消灭或大幅减少）、with luck you got by, but rich you did not get（有运气你过得去，但并没有发达）

**表达方式**：用一个 **`Human beings have two ways`**（人类有两种方式）这种**教科书式的断言**开篇，随后**两条路都写得像自然史**；用 **`rather than on or against it`**（不是在它上面也不是与它对抗）三个介词并置作**分类轴**；用 **两个否定式的平行**（`you can’t freeze in it, you can’t starve in it`）写「宜人」；用 **`steel yourself to work very hard for sadly little return`**（打定主意辛苦工作换少得可怜的回报）里的 **`sadly`** 一词把「少」写成**哀叹**；末句用 **`with luck you got by, but rich you did not get`** 这一**押韵式的对句**（`got` / `get`）作**判词**。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章对 Dominica 的**最完整论证**，而它的修辞策略是**先分类，再归纳，最后落到一句格言**。分类的轴不是「贫富」而是**人与自然的关系方式**（`lives with nature` vs `likes to control nature`）——这使 Dominica 的贫困不是**道德失败**，而是**人与地形的相与方式**的直接后果。而最有分量的是那个括注（`escaped slaves … resistance`）：它承认 Dominica 给过**真实的胜利**（Caribs 人至今比别处多，逃奴的抵抗优于其他岛），因而这段论证**不是**「那个岛无可救药」，而是「**那个岛不适合被榨取，但适合被抵抗**」。这一区分是 Jean 后来那部小说的全部材料。末句 `with luck you got by, but rich you did not get` 之所以有力，是因为它用 **`got by`（过得去）与 `rich`（发达）** 这一个**程度差**写完了整段论证，而**全段的重量**（加勒比许多地方种植园主发财、此岛作物周期性被清空）都压在这两句的**对照**上——这一句可以直接用作 Jean 家境的**定义**。

### ⑥

> **原句 6:** In England everyone she met knew things she didn’t know – not just the things taught in schools, but baffling ordinary, everyday things. Many young women are nimble face-savers, able to learn ways out of difficult situations, but Jean was not. Already, for whatever reason, she was in some ways trammelled in childishness; already paranoia threatened. It did not occur to her to learn, all she could do was hate. She hated this country which was so far from resembling her dream, and even more fiercely its inhabitants, for despising (as she was sure they did) her ignorance and her home. This feeling persisted into her old age: I saw it flare up when a woman spoke of Castries, in St Lucia, as ca shanty town’. Instantly Jean assumed that this sneering woman – these sneering English – would see Roseau in the same way, and Roseau was not a shanty town – it was not – they were not seeing it right. She sprang to defend it against strangers – hateful strangers. She had always hated them with their damned cold competence and common sense: never would she dream of trying to be like them. Probably she could not have been, anyway; but her abhorrence of what she saw as Englishness did make her embrace her own incompetence.

**中文理解**：在英格兰，她遇见的**每个人**都懂得她不懂的事——不只是学校教的东西，还有那些**令人困惑的、日常的、极其普通**的东西。许多年轻女人是**灵活的对场者**，能学会从困难处境脱身的办法，但 Jean 不是。不管什么原因，她已经在某些方面被**幼稚**束缚住了；疑心**已经在逼近**。她**压根儿没想过**去学，她能做的**只有恨**。她恨这个离她的梦那么远的国家，更恨它的居民——因为（她确信）他们**鄙视**她的无知与她的家乡。这种感情**一直持续到她老年**：我看见它发作过一次，是当一个女人把 St Lucia 的 Castries 说成「一个棚户区」的时候。Jean **立刻**认定这个**讥笑的女人**——这些**讥笑的英国人**——也会用同样的眼光看 Roseau，而 **Roseau 不是一个棚户区**——它**不是**——他们**看错了**。她**跳起来**替它向陌生人辩护——**可恨的陌生人**。她一直**恨**他们那**该死的冷静的 competent 与常识**：她**绝不会**梦想去学他们那样。**大概**她反正也**做不到**；但她对自己所看到的「英国性」的**憎恶**，确实使她**接受了自己的无能**。

**句子结构**：首句是「地点状语 In England + 主句 everyone she met knew things she didn’t know + 破折号引出限定 `not just the things taught in schools, but baffling ordinary, everyday things`」；次句是「主句（主语 Many young women + 系动词 are + 表语 nimble face-savers, + 不定式 able to learn ways out of difficult situations）, but 主句 Jean was not」；末段是「并列的独立句（Already, for whatever reason, she was in some ways trammelled in childishness; already paranoia threatened. / It did not occur to her to learn, all she could do was hate. / She hated this country …, and even more fiercely its inhabitants, for despising … her ignorance and her home.）+ 冒号引出例证 This feeling persisted into her old age: I saw it flare up when … + 独立句（Instantly Jean assumed … / and Roseau was not a shanty town – it was not – they were not seeing it right. / She sprang to defend it against strangers – hateful strangers. / She had always hated them with their damned cold competence and common sense: never would she dream of trying to be like them.）+ 独立句 Probably she could not have been, anyway; but her abhorrence of what she saw as Englishness did make her embrace her own incompetence」。

**关键词**：everyone she met knew things she didn’t know（她遇见的每个人都懂她不懂的事）、baffling ordinary, everyday things（令人困惑的、日常的、极其普通的东西）、Many young women are nimble face-savers（许多年轻女人是灵活的对场者）、Jean was not（但 Jean 不是）、trammelled in childishness（被幼稚束缚住）、paranoia threatened（疑心已在逼近）、It did not occur to her to learn（她压根儿没想过去学）、all she could do was hate（她能做的只有恨）、even more fiercely its inhabitants（更恨它的居民）、despising her ignorance and her home（鄙视她的无知与她的家乡）、This feeling persisted into her old age（这种感情持续到她老年）、Castries, in St Lucia, as ca shanty town’（把 St Lucia 的 Castries 说成「一个棚户区」）、Instantly Jean assumed（Jean 立刻认定）、Roseau was not a shanty town – it was not – they were not seeing it right（Roseau 不是棚户区——它不是——他们看错了）、She sprang to defend it against strangers – hateful strangers（她跳起来向陌生人辩护——可恨的陌生人）、their damned cold competence and common sense（他们那该死的冷静的 competent 与常识）、never would she dream of trying to be like them（她绝不会梦想学他们那样）、Probably she could not have been, anyway（大概她反正也做不到）、her abhorrence of what she saw as Englishness did make her embrace her own incompetence（她对所看到的「英国性」的憎恶确实使她接受了自己的无能）

**表达方式**：用 **`baffling`**（令人困惑的）修饰 `ordinary, everyday things`——把**普通**写成**难以理解**；用一个 **`Many young women … but Jean was not`** 的对照把一个**群体特征**用**否定**钉在一个人身上；用 **`It did not occur to her to learn, all she could do was hate`** 这一**极简的对句**（该做的没想／唯一能做的）把「**学不会**」写成「**不想学**」；用 **`it was not – they were not seeing it right`** 的**重复与强调**（破折号插入的短促否定）写**被激怒的语法**；用 **`Probably she could not have been, anyway`** 把一个**宽恕**放在 `anyway` 之后——**先承认能力上可能做不到，再说明是憎恶使她不去做**；末句用 **`did make her`** 这一强调结构把「憎恶」写成**原因**。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章对 Jean **「不会生活」**最具体也最不留情的一段，而它的技术是把**一个道德判断拆成两步**。第一步（`it was not – they were not seeing it right`）把她的反应写成**一次正义的愤怒**——她确实是对的：Roseau 不是棚户区，而那个女人确实在讥笑。第二步（`Probably she could not have been, anyway; but her abhorrence … did make her embrace her own incompetence`）才下那个冷的结论。`Probably … anyway` 这个让步极其重要：如果她**本来能**做到而不做，那是**骄傲**；如果她**本来就做不到**，那憎恶只是**给了她一个不去做的理由**——而理由本身是**选择**。末句 `did make her embrace her own incompetence`（**确实使她**接受了自己的无能）用 `embrace`（拥抱）这个主动动词——她不是**陷于**无能（`be trapped in`），是**抱住了它**。这正是本节对 Jean 最准确也最令人不适的一句：她的无能**被当作一个身份来持有**，而这使它**免于被修正**。对照 ch14 那句 `It did not occur to her to learn, all she could do was hate`——「不想学」与「学了也没用」之间，她选择了前者，而这一选择被伪装成了后者。

> **原句 7:** Jean and I met for the first time in November 1964, when, after the support she had received from Francis, and also from another Deutsch editor, Esther Whitby, who had volunteered to spend a weekend at Cheriton FitzPaine to help her sort out and arrange what she had written, she felt able to bring the finished book to London. Or rather, the almost finished book: there were still a few lines which she would have to dictate to the typist to whom we had given the material brought back by Esther. We would meet, Jean and I, for a celebratory lunch the day after she arrived . . . Instead, I was called to her hotel by an agitated manageress, who reported that she had suffered a heart attack during the night. So there was no triumph over a bottle of champagne. I had to pack her into an ambulance and take her to hospital. This, followed by three or four weeks of hospital visiting, with all the usual intimacies of nightdress washing, toothpaste buying and so on, plunged us into the deep end of friendship – though I soon learnt that it would be a mistake to suppose that meant trust. Jean never entirely trusted anybody. But she was never thereafter to show me an unfriendly face.

**中文理解**：Jean 和我第一次见面是 1964 年 11 月——在**她从 Francis 那里**、以及从**另一位 Deutsch 编辑 Esther Whitby** 那里得到支持之后（Esther 自愿去 Cheriton FitzPaine 住一个周末，帮她把她写的东西**分类整理**），她才觉得能把**写完的书**带回伦敦。**或者说，几乎写完的书**：还有几行字她得**口述**给那位打字员——我们把 Esther 带回来的材料交给了她。我们本来，Jean 和我，打算在她到达的第二天一起吃一顿庆祝午餐……**结果**，我被一位**焦急的女经理**从她住的旅馆叫去，她报告说她在夜里**犯了心绞痛**。于是**没有**那瓶香槟的胜利。我**不得不**把她塞进一辆救护车送去医院。这一件——接着是**三四个星期的探病**，连同那些通常的亲密事：**洗睡衣、买牙膏**等等——把我们**一下子推进了友谊的深水区**；尽管我很快明白，**以为那等于信任是错的**。Jean **从不完全**信任**任何人**。但从那以后，她**再也没有**对我露出不友善的面孔。

**句子结构**：首句是「主句（主语 Jean and I + 谓语 met + 状语 for the first time in November 1964）+ 时间状语从句 when, after the support she had received from Francis, and also from another Deutsch editor, Esther Whitby（内含 who 引导的定语从句 who had volunteered to spend a weekend at Cheriton FitzPaine to help her sort out and arrange what she had written）, she felt able to bring the finished book to London」；次句是「修正句 Or rather, the almost finished book: there were still a few lines which she would have to dictate to the typist to whom we had given the material brought back by Esther」；末段是「主句（I was called to her hotel by an agitated manageress, who reported that …）/ 独立句 So there was no triumph over a bottle of champagne. / 独立句 I had to pack her into an ambulance and take her to hospital. / 主句 This, followed by three or four weeks of hospital visiting, with all the usual intimacies of nightdress washing, toothpaste buying and so on, plunged us into the deep end of friendship – though I soon learnt that it would be a mistake to suppose that meant trust. / 独立句 Jean never entirely trusted anybody. / 转折句 But she was never thereafter to show me an unfriendly face.」。

**关键词**：met for the first time in November 1964（1964 年 11 月第一次见面）、Esther Whitby（Esther Whitby，另一位 Deutsch 编辑）、who had volunteered to spend a weekend at Cheriton FitzPaine to help her sort out and arrange（自愿去 Cheriton FitzPaine 住一个周末帮她分类整理）、the finished book … Or rather, the almost finished book（写完的书……或者说，几乎写完的书）、there were still a few lines which she would have to dictate to the typist（还有几行她得口述给打字员）、We would meet, Jean and I, for a celebratory lunch（我们本来打算吃一顿庆祝午餐）、I was called to her hotel by an agitated manageress（我被一位焦急的女经理从旅馆叫去）、she had suffered a heart attack during the night（她夜里犯了心绞痛）、So there was no triumph over a bottle of champagne（于是没有那瓶香槟的胜利）、I had to pack her into an ambulance（我不得不把她塞进救护车）、three or four weeks of hospital visiting（三四个星期的探病）、nightdress washing, toothpaste buying and so on（洗睡衣、买牙膏等等）、plunged us into the deep end of friendship（把我们推进了友谊的深水区）、it would be a mistake to suppose that meant trust（以为那等于信任是错的）、Jean never entirely trusted anybody（Jean 从不完全信任任何人）、she was never thereafter to show me an unfriendly face（她再也没有对我露出不友善的面孔）

**表达方式**：用 **`Or rather, the almost finished book:`** 这一**自我修正**加冒号，把**九年的成果**从「写完了」降为「几乎写完」；用一个 **`Instead, I was called …`** 把**庆祝**的句子换成一个**被动句**（我被叫去）；用 **`So there was no triumph over a bottle of champagne.`** 这一**极短句**作**最重的一击**——`no triumph` 取消了**仪式**；用三个**动名词的排比**（`nightdress washing, toothpaste buying and so on`）写**友谊的构成材料**（它们是洗衣与牙膏）；用一个 **`– though I soon learnt that …`** 的**插入让步**把「深水区」立刻**限定**；末句用一个 **`never thereafter`**（从那以后再没有）写**关系的下限**。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章的**相认仪式**，而它的形式是**两次取消**。第一次取消在 `Or rather, the almost finished book:`——九年之后仍有几行要口述，而那几行还得通过一个打字员转手（`to whom we had given the material brought back by Esther`）。第二次取消更狠：`So there was no triumph over a bottle of champagne.`——**没有庆祝**，因为庆祝的那个人在夜里**心绞痛**。而这句之后紧跟的 `I had to pack her into an ambulance`（我不得不把她塞进救护车）是全书最不浪漫的一句，而它的宾语是 `pack`（**塞**）——一个把**人当行李**的动词。这一连串取消把「见面」写成了**一次事故处理**，而正是在这个**事故处理**里（探病、洗睡衣、买牙膏）他们变成了朋友——这一安排极为精确：**友谊不是从交谈开始的，是从洗衣开始的**。末两句的对照是全段最好的收束：`Jean never entirely trusted anybody`（她从不完全信任任何人）与 `But she was never thereafter to show me an unfriendly face`（但她再没对我露出不友善的面孔）——两句合起来给出了 ch12 那条判据的**修订版**：**信任不可得，但关系可以**；而她随即明白 `it would be a mistake to suppose that meant trust`。

> **原句 8:** She financed long winter holidays in London for Jean every year from the publication of Wide Sargasso Sea in 1966 to the end of her life, and she gave her many expensive presents. When I remarked on the amount she was spending she told me that she had always felt embarrassed at having inherited George Orwell’s literary income, and had decided that she must use it to help writers who were hard up. This she said shyly and apologetically, to stop me thinking she was more generous than she was, not to take credit for it. And more impressive than the money she spent was the sensitivity she showed in her determination to give Jean a good time. She didn’t just pay hotel bills: she did all the tipping in advance, she explained to the management the special kinds of attention this old lady would need, she booked hairdressers and manicurists, she bought pretty dressing-gowns, she saw to it that the fridge was full of white wine and of milk for Jean’s nightcap, she supplied books, she organized visitors . . . From time to time she even did the thing she most hated (as I did too): took Jean shopping for clothes. This was so exhausting and so boring that eventually we both went on strike – and it was Sonia who then saw to it that younger and stronger spirits took our place. It was also she who was the most active member of what we called ‘the Jean Committee’ – the meetings at which she, Francis and I discussed ‘Jean problems’, such as getting her finances in order, or trying to find her somewhere to live nearer London, and less mingy, than Landboat Bungalows. (In this we did not succeed: whenever we came up with a real possibility Jean would jib: ‘Better the devil I know’, she would say.)

**中文理解**：从 1966 年 *Wide Sargasso Sea* 出版起一直到她去世，**她**（Sonia）**每年**都资助 Jean 在伦敦过冬的假期，还送她许多昂贵的礼物。当我提到她花的钱有多少时，她告诉我，她一直**因为继承了 George Orwell 的文学收入而感到难为情**，并且认定自己**必须**用它去帮助那些**手头紧的**作家。这话她说得**羞怯而认错**，以便**阻止我**把她想得比实际**更慷慨**，**不居这个功**。而比她花的钱**更令人印象深刻**的，是她在**决心让 Jean 过得好**这件事上显出的**敏感**。她不光是付旅馆账单：她**提前把所有小费都付了**，她向管理层**说明这位老太太需要哪些特别的照顾**，她**预约理发师和修指甲的**，她**买好看的晨袍**，她**确保冰箱里满是白葡萄酒、以及 Jean 夜里要喝的那杯牛奶**，她**提供书**，她**安排来访的人**……**时不时地她甚至还去做了那件她最恨的事**（**我也是**）：**带 Jean 去买衣服**。这件事**如此耗人、如此无聊**，以至于后来**我们俩都罢工了**——**于是是 Sonia 安排**由**更年轻、更精力充沛的人**接替我们。而**也是她**是我们称作「**Jean 委员会**」的成员里**最积极**的一个——那些会上，她、Francis 和我讨论「**Jean 的问题**」，比如**理清她的财务**，或者**设法在离伦敦更近的地方**、**比 Landboat Bungalows 更不寒酸**的地方给她找个住处。（**这一点我们没有成功**：每当我们想出一个真正可行的方案，Jean 就会**抗拒**：「**我认识的魔鬼总比不认识的好**」，她会说。）

**句子结构**：首句是「主句（主语 She + 谓语 financed + 宾语 long winter holidays in London for Jean）+ 时间状语 every year from the publication of Wide Sargasso Sea in 1966 to the end of her life + 并列句 and she gave her many expensive presents」；次句是「时间状语从句 When I remarked on the amount she was spending + 主句 she told me（that 宾语从句内含两个并列分句 she had always felt embarrassed at having inherited George Orwell’s literary income / and had decided that she must use it to help writers who were hard up）+ 主句 This she said shyly and apologetically, to stop me thinking she was more generous than she was, not to take credit for it（不定式表目的）」（反身代词 she 指 Sonia）；末段是「主句（and more impressive than the money she spent was the sensitivity she showed in her determination to give Jean a good time）+ 七个 `she + 过去式动词` 构成的排比（`She didn’t just pay hotel bills: she did all the tipping in advance, she explained …, she booked …, she bought …, she saw to it that …, she supplied books, she organized visitors`）+ 独立句 From time to time she even did the thing she most hated (as I did too): took Jean shopping for clothes. + 独立句 This was so exhausting and so boring that eventually we both went on strike – and it was Sonia who then saw to it that younger and stronger spirits took our place. + 独立句 It was also she who was the most active member of what we called ‘the Jean Committee’ – the meetings at which she, Francis and I discussed ‘Jean problems’, such as … or …（括号补充）」+ 括注（In this we did not succeed: whenever we came up with a real possibility Jean would jib: ‘Better the devil I know’, she would say.）」。

**关键词**：financed long winter holidays in London for Jean every year（每年资助 Jean 在伦敦过冬）、from the publication of Wide Sargasso Sea in 1966 to the end of her life（从 1966 年出版到她去世）、embarrassed at having inherited George Orwell’s literary income（因为继承了 George Orwell 的文学收入而难为情）、help writers who were hard up（帮助手头紧的作家）、shyly and apologetically（羞怯而认错地）、to stop me thinking she was more generous than she was（阻止我把她想得更慷慨）、not to take credit for it（不居这个功）、more impressive than the money she spent was the sensitivity（在敏感上更令人印象深刻）、did all the tipping in advance（提前把所有小费付了）、explained to the management the special kinds of attention this old lady would need（向管理层说明这位老太太需要哪些特别照顾）、booked hairdressers and manicurists（预约理发师和修指甲的）、the fridge was full of white wine and of milk for Jean’s nightcap（冰箱里满是白葡萄酒与夜宵的牛奶）、organized visitors（安排来访的人）、the thing she most hated (as I did too)（她最恨的事，我亦是）、took Jean shopping for clothes（带 Jean 去买衣服）、we both went on strike（我们俩都罢工了）、younger and stronger spirits took our place（更年轻更强的人接替）、the most active member of what we called ‘the Jean Committee’（「Jean 委员会」里最积极的成员）、‘Jean problems’（「Jean 的问题」）、less mingy, than Landboat Bungalows（比 Landboat Bungalows 更不寒酸）、Jean would jib（Jean 就会抗拒）、‘Better the devil I know’（我认识的魔鬼总比不认识的好）

**表达方式**：用一个 **`more impressive than the money … was the sensitivity`** 的比较结构把**金钱**与**敏感**对调，使**后者**成为评价的对象；用**七个 `she + 过去式`** 的排比把**照顾**写成**一串小动作**（其中 `She didn’t just pay hotel bills:` 冒号后的清单是关键，它把「付账」降为其中**最不重要**的一项）；用 **`as I did too`**（**我亦是**）这个括注把自己**放进同一份清单**；用一个 **`we both went on strike`**（我们俩都**罢工了**）把照护写成**可以罢工的工作**（劳动词汇）；用一个 **`younger and stronger spirits`**（更年轻更强**的 spirits**）——**spirits** 一词指人，却原是「精力」的旧义；用一个 **`Better the devil I know`**（**我认识的魔鬼**）把 Jean 的抗拒写成一句**谚语**——而这句话是全章对 Jean 最精确的一次描述：**她怕的不是坏的处境，是未知的处置**。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章对**照顾**这件事最详尽的一段，而它的修辞核心是**颗粒度**。Sonia 的做法被拆成一串**极小的动作**（提前付小费、向管理层说明需要哪种照顾、预约理发师、买晨袍、塞满冰箱），而这一串动作之所以有力，是因为它全部**不可见于账本**——`more impressive than the money she spent` 一句就说明了这一点：读者能看见的是**钱**，而她在意的（并写下来的）是**敏感**。这一对照与 ch13 那段「广告」论证是**同一个结构**的两次应用：那里她论证「**报告**（你的书登在报纸 A、B、C 上）不能替代**版面**（多大、第几版）」，这里她记录「**付了多少钱**不能替代**冰箱里有没有白葡萄酒**」。而最有分寸的一处是 `we both went on strike`（我们俩都罢工了）——她与 Athill 都**承认自己受不了**（`so exhausting and so boring`），**并且写下**了，而这一承认不损害她们对 Jean 的关系，反而是它**能持续二十八年**的原因：照顾一个人若不许自己疲惫，那个人会先累垮。

### ⑨

> **原句 9:** Her creed – so simple to state, so difficult to follow – was that she must tell the truth: must get things down as they really were. Carole Angier, in her biography, has demonstrated how this fierce endeavour enabled her to write her way through to understanding her own damaged nature; and it also enabled her, in her last novel, to show Dominica’s racial pain as it really was. But it didn’t work in ‘The Imperial Road’.

**中文理解**：她的**信条**——**说出来如此简单，遵它而行如此困难**——是：**她必须说出真相**：必须把事情**按它们真正的样子**记下来。Carole Angier 在她的传记里已经**证明**，这种**猛烈的努力**如何使她**一路写下去**、直至**理解自己受损的本性**；而它也使她在最后一部小说里，把 Dominica 的**种族痛苦**按它**真正的样子**呈现出来。但它在《The Imperial Road》里**没有奏效**。

**句子结构**：两句：首句是「**主句（主语 Her creed + 破折号插入的对照 `– so simple to state, so difficult to follow –` + 系动词 was + 表语从句 that she must tell the truth: must get things down as they really were`（内含两个平行的 must））」；次句是「主句（主语 Carole Angier, in her biography, has demonstrated + 宾语从句 how this fierce endeavour enabled her to write her way through to understanding her own damaged nature）+ 并列句 and it also enabled her, in her last novel, to show Dominica’s racial pain as it really was + 转折句 But it didn’t work in ‘The Imperial Road’.」。

**关键词**：Her creed（她的信条）、so simple to state, so difficult to follow（说出来如此简单，遵它而行如此困难）、she must tell the truth（她必须说出真相）、must get things down as they really were（必须把事情按它们真正的样子记下来）、Carole Angier, in her biography, has demonstrated（Carole Angier 在传记里已证明）、this fierce endeavour（这种猛烈的努力）、write her way through to understanding her own damaged nature（一路写下去直至理解自己受损的本性）、to show Dominica’s racial pain as it really was（把 Dominica 的种族痛苦按真正的样子呈现）、But it didn’t work in ‘The Imperial Road’（但在《The Imperial Road》里没有奏效）

**表达方式**：用一对 **`so simple to state, so difficult to follow`**（**说**简单、**做**困难）把一条信条写成一道**格言**——这一对也正是 ch13 那句 `so simple to state, so difficult to follow` 在她本人身上的应用；用 **`must … : must …`** 的平行祈使（第二句省略主语与情态词）把「说出真相」拆成**两步**（`tell` 与 `get things down`），即**口头**与**文字**；用 **`write her way through to understanding`**（**一路写下去直到理解**）这一隐喻把写作写成**一条通道**；末句用一个 **`But it didn’t work`** 的极短转折句作**全章的方法论收束**。

**为什么这样写**：这一段是全章的**方法论核心**，而它的形式是**一条不可行的信条**。`so simple to state, so difficult to follow`（说出来简单，做起来难）把「如实记录」从一个方法降为一个**理想**，而紧接着的 `must get things down as they really were` 又把它**升高**（两个 must 平行，第二个还省略了主语与情态词，使它读成**不可协商**的）。然后是**证据**（Angier 证明它如何让她**理解自己**、如何让她**呈现** Dominica 的种族痛苦）——注意这两项证据的**对象都是别人**（她自己的本性、Dominica 的痛苦），也就是说她的「如实」**服务于外部**。而末句 `But it didn’t work in ‘The Imperial Road’.` 用**最短的句子**给出**最强的限定**——一个未竟稿的名字。这一句因此与 ch12 那条判据（`not impossible, but rare`）同族，也与 ch11 那句 `it took quite a long time to work`（要很久才见效）呼应：**如实**是一条**有效但有时失效**的方法，而她的诚实之处在于**把这个失效写进结论句**——不是「但她没有写完」，而是「**它在那部作品里没有奏效**」。

### ⑩

> **原句 10:** Perhaps she thought that true. She could not see herself when she was working. Out of her eyes, then, looked a whole and fearless being, without self-pity, knowing exactly what she wanted to do, and how to do it.

**中文理解**：**大概她自己也以为那是真的。**她在工作的时候**看不到她自己**。那么，从**她的眼睛里**看出来的那，就是一个**完整的、无所畏惧的存在**——**没有自怜**，**确切地知道自己想做什么**，以及**怎么做**。

**句子结构**：两句：首句是「情态动词 Perhaps + 省略主语的插入语 she thought that true（that 为指示代词，指前面那件事）」；次句是「独立句 She could not see herself when she was working.」；末句是「独立句 Out of her eyes, then, looked a whole and fearless being, without self-pity, knowing exactly what she wanted to do, and how to do it.」（内含三个并列的否定/确切状语：`without self-pity` / `knowing exactly what she wanted to do` / `and how to do it`；主句为倒装 `Out of her eyes … looked …`）。

**关键词**：Perhaps she thought that true（大概她自己也以为那是真的）、She could not see herself when she was working（她在工作的时候看不到自己）、Out of her eyes, then, looked a whole and fearless being（从她眼睛里看出来的是完整的、无所畏惧的存在）、without self-pity（没有自怜）、knowing exactly what she wanted to do, and how to do it（确切地知道自己想做什么，以及怎么做）

**表达方式**：用一个 **`Perhaps … that true`** 的极短插入句把一个判断**降为可能**并**立刻关闭**；用 **`could not see herself`**（**看不到自己**）这个**否定能力**（`could not`）替代 **`did not see`**（没看见）——不是**没看**，是**看的能力缺失**；用一个 **`Out of her eyes, then, looked …`** 的**倒装**（把「来源」提到句首）写出「**眼睛成了窗口**」；用三个**平行的状语**（`without self-pity` / `knowing exactly what she wanted to do` / `and how to do it`）给那个存在**三项属性**。

**为什么这样写**：这三句是全章的**落点**，而它们的力量来自**一次视角的切换**：全章的叙述者是**看见她的人**（`her inability to cope`、`hardly ever said a word`、`this hopelessly inept woman`），而这三句忽然说：**她看不见她自己**，而**从她的眼睛里**看出的是另一个人。这不是矛盾，而是本节对「写作」这一活动的**定义**——ch05 里她说 `So the decision was to be mine`，ch08 里她说好的编辑让 `the author’s voice` 显现；这里她给出同一件事在**作家身上的形式**：写作时的人**是完整的**，而生活中的那个人**看不到这份完整**（`could not see herself`）。末句的三个状语正是 ch12 那条「必须面对自己」的要求的**正面版本**：`without self-pity`（无自怜）——这一项与 ch13 里 Moore 那封信的 `a fever of self-righteous spite`（自以为正义的怨毒）正好相反；`knowing exactly what she wanted to do, and how to do it`（确切知道自己想做什么、怎么做）——这一项与本节开头那个 `bad at life` 形成对照。**生活里她不知道自己要什么；工作时她知道。** 而 `Out of her eyes, then, looked …` 这个倒装把这一切**归因于她的眼睛**——也就是说，读者是通过**她的目光**才看见那个完整的人的，而这正是全章一开头那句认识论（`no one who never met her could know how very bad at it she was`）的反面：**见过她的人，恰好在她写作时看见了另一个她。**

## 一句话总结

这一章是全书最长的一章，也是她**唯一一次**用地理来解释一个人；它的开头是一个**认识论的裂缝**（`no one who never met her could know how very bad at it she was`），中间是**九年**的等待与一个被心绞痛打断的相认（`So there was no triumph over a bottle of champagne`），而落点是**写作时的她与生活中的她的裂缝**：`She could not see herself when she was working. Out of her eyes, then, looked a whole and fearless being, without self-pity, knowing exactly what she wanted to do, and how to do it.`
