# 《The Burnings》概述

## 全书梗概

**一五七八年，丹麦，一笔交易定下了全书的引火方式。** 弗朗西斯·斯图尔特走进德拉格斯霍姆城堡的塔顶牢房，去看那个被关了近十年的囚犯——他自己的舅父。刀抵着喉咙，舅父交出的不是越狱办法，而是一套政治纲领：‘You need to make him, and everyone else, see he’s a pathetic, craven sliver of a man. Make him afraid. Terrified of his own shadow. Your greatest weapon will be that boy’s fear.’（ch01） 这句话的效力不在于它有多毒，而在于它**不出现在任何一份文件里**——叔父本人此后再未现身，纲领的完整表述全书只此一处；到第四十六章，由一个苏格兰贵族当着国王把同一套意思转述出来，而那时国王已经照着它活了很多年。

**一五八九年丹麦赫尔辛格，一个女人在码头上闻到了烟味。** 玛格丽塔被人拖去看一场烧巫——她本来是要走的，是她的乳姐妹伊尔莎把她留住了。伊尔莎要她去苏格兰，去见一个叫格丽丝·邓肯的助产士学徒，理由是「像我们这样的人若肯帮忙，就都安全了」。玛格丽塔拒绝了，甩身就走。临走前伊尔莎把话说到底：Witchcraft is in your blood. You can never be rid of it.（ch02） 这句话在第四十六章由伊尔莎对玛格丽塔说了第二遍，措辞几乎一样，而两人都没有解释它是什么意思。

**（她真正倒下是后来的事，在苏格兰的宫廷里，不在丹麦的火刑现场。）** 她昏迷过，醒来时约翰只说了三个字。关于那一晚她自己也说不准，只能用「也许」——而她最该指认的那个名字，恰恰是她没有说出口的那个。

**同一年苏格兰，另一条线开始了。** 草药婆格丽丝住在地主塞顿家的辖地上，郡守的视线像茅屋顶上的滴水：What to be done? Nothing. He was the bailie. He had noble kin. Girls like Geillis, with no husbands or fathers or brothers, were expected to endure, endure, and endure again. She’d considered complaining to Mistress Katherine, but what wife wanted to be told about her husband’s roving eye, still less asked for help restraining him? Besides, Seton’s wife was no more approachable than a swan; she glided through the house, cutting conversations in two like water, the slightest arching of her neck a silent warning.（ch05） 她没有丈夫、没有兄弟、没有靠山，唯一能保护她的是一个连自己都护不住的朋友艾格尼斯。这本书的全部张力都长在这道落差上：**在一个「有没有人肯开口」决定生死的地方，谁替谁说过话，就是全部。**

**沉船。** 一艘从伯蒂斯兰出发的船在风暴里没了，船上有一位王后的侍女。‘The Margaret,’ Fian announced, as if lecturing small children. ‘Sailing from Burntisland to Leith at King James’s request. He wishes Jean Kennedy, sister-in-law to his adviser, Sir James Melville, to become Anna of Denmark’s lady-in-waiting.’（ch09） 沉船把玛格丽塔和格丽丝两条线短暂地拉到同一片水域边上。但**原文从未把这艘船和第十章那艘断了桅杆的船画等号**，也从未把第十三章那条「不点灯的船」算进来——同一片海域，账要分开算。

**一五九〇年到一五九一年，苏格兰学会了另一件事：
- 不是杀人，是让人指认。** 双头牛、下海的女孩、被雪埋掉的小屋——流言先在村镇上跑：A calf was born with two heads in Fife. Three girls went into the sea in Galloway and never emerged – but three seals had watched their village ever since, black heads gleaming beneath the moon.（ch17）
- 然后是那些**过于清白**的人：Conspiracies were one thing. Trying to save your own neck another. But letting innocent men take the blame? One more sin Geillis was too frightened to stop herself committing.（ch17）
- 酷刑机器在托尔博特的地下运作，夹棍与拇指夹让口供变成唯一能流通的货币：He took her hand, terrifyingly gentle. ‘They call them pilliwinks, or thumbikins. Such silly names for something so … damaging.’ He slipped the device around her thumb. ‘You might not think it now. Look how small it is! Look what a fraction of you a thumb is! And yet, I’m told, our hands are among the most sensitive parts of our bodies. And, of course, the most useful. Whatever would a girl like you do with crushed thumbs?’（ch33）
- 而女巫案卷自己说出了这套机器的用途：‘He wanted us to spread fear.’ Agnes leaned forward, barely wincing now. ‘The only thing that frightens men more than witchcraft is a woman they cannot control. They want us to confess to witchcraft? We shall not. Do you hear me? We will not confess.’（ch33）

**公堂上，机器第一次对准了国王的耳朵。** 罪状被念出来的时候，念的不是罪名，是**只有国王和王后可能知道的话**：
- ‘Agnes Sampson has spoken words only I and Queen Anna could possibly have known. This is all the evidence needed to prove her a witch. I call upon the dempster to pass sentence.’（ch35）
- 被告人在台上把话说完了：Except this. And I don’t regret it. I want James to know what I’ve done.’（ch35）
这是本书里最接近「自陈」的一刻——她做的与国王要的，是同一件事的两半，而她自己知道。

**城堡山上的绞架，和一句流亡。** 判决词念完，被告说了全书最不像辩护的一句：
- ‘They weren’t witches,’ Geillis said. ‘They were frightened women hoping to ease their childbed, to keep their own property, to regain their stolen positions. This isn’t justice – this is vengeance and spite, and scores of Scots have died for it.’（ch44）
- 三周之后，一个还活着的女人闻到烤肉味就反胃：Even three weeks after Geillis’s death, Margareta grimaced whenever the smell of roasting meat writhed out of Holyrood’s kitchens. Whenever she donned a necklace, Margareta shivered as cold stones closed around her neck. She’d seen Anna constantly loosening her pearls, as if she too were desperate to avoid constriction, even if those pearls could have bought a hundred guards to help her escape. But there was nowhere for Anna or Margareta to go.（ch45）
- 至于那个从一五七八年起就在布置这一切的人，原文只写到「流亡」两个字，和一句回指：Exiled. The word was a sigh of relief. Bothwell was gone at last. It had taken years, but James had built higher walls with laws and trials and royal decrees than those of any castle, and Bothwell had been unable to breach them. John’s evidence had done its work. Bothwell’s erstwhile supporters now vehemently pretended they had been no such thing, to little avail. There was nothing left in Scotland for Bothwell now. Like his uncle before him, he’d fled overseas.（ch47）
罪名、受审与否、判决日期、刑期——**全书都没有写**，不要替他补。**处决发生在哪一年，原文也没有给**（这一段里唯一出现的年份是尾声表头那一五九五年），所以只能说「在尾声之前」。

## 三个主题

**一、同一套工具，两种用法。** 格丽丝的草药是救孩子的：
- ‘I couldn’t do it,’ Geillis said quietly. ‘I meant to save children, not kill them. Most herbs can heal or hurt depending on how much you take. I thought it would make a difference. But …’（ch40）
- 博斯韦尔的「巫术」是造恐惧的：‘You need to make him, and everyone else, see he’s a pathetic, craven sliver of a man. Make him afraid. Terrified of his own shadow. Your greatest weapon will be that boy’s fear.’（ch01）
两人用的是同一个词，方向相反——而这本书的全部篇幅，就是看这套工具如何从救人的手里滑到杀人的手里，中途每一环都有人说「我只是照做」。

**二、信念本身就是刑具。** 塞顿一家相信女儿被巫术害死，于是那条信念一路变成罪状：
- Belief matters, Agnes had said. The Setons believed their daughter had been bewitched and that Bothwell and Agnes were to blame. Geillis could only tremble in her coward’s silence, bereft of words to damn herself or defend Agnes.（ch22）
- 而作者早就把判词交到读者手里了——「巫术在你的血里」，**它不需要被证明，只需要被相信**。到第四十六章，同一句话被用来解释一个王后的整个婚姻：‘God chose your mother,’ John pointed out. ‘Scotland chose you. All along, Bothwell has wanted you to destroy yourself, to be terrified either into submission or into behaving so rashly that Scotland turns away from you and raises Bothwell onto the throne.’（ch46）

**三、共谋的沉默，和它的报偿。** 序章里最安静的一句是守卫那句：
- How easy it was for the guards to pretend they heard nothing.（ch01）
- 这套秩序不靠暴力维持，靠所有人共同决定不听。后来在圣吉尔斯教堂，它变成了另一种说法：‘There’s nothing holy about this place anymore.’ He looked her in the eye. ‘If I wished to repent, I’d go to a kirk in Edinburgh. St Giles’, perhaps, on the Canongate.’（ch30）
- 而全书最后，人物给出的判词仍然是同一件事的反面：‘No.’ He pulled her closer against his chest. ‘As long as men are afraid, there will be people accused of witchcraft.’（ch46）

## 人物弧光

**玛格丽塔（丹麦贵族，嫁约翰·韦米斯）——**

- 起点：Margareta could smell the smoke from the wharf, no matter how close to the oyster sellers she stepped; caught by the dawn boats, the shellfish were now drying into brackish dullness, but even salted rot was better than the smell of a burning. Only wood so far, but Margareta knew what would follow. She’d be smelling it for hours, if not days; every time she brushed her hair or smoothed her skirts she’d gag at the stench of seared meat. Dear God, if there were suckling pigs roasting on spits in Kronborg Castle’s great hall, she’d be sick.（ch02）
- 转向：‘Normal cruelty,’ Margareta repeated. ‘Maybe that’s how everyone sees a burning – until it happens to someone they love.’（ch28）
- 最低点：But they didn’t need dark clouds to remind them of Euphame’s end. They didn’t even need to have witnessed it themselves: her screams could be heard across the city. Margareta, remembering Johanna’s burning, knew someone had built Euphame’s pyre with drawn-out suffering in mind. John, who’d witnessed the execution, looked a decade older than the man Margareta had danced with in Kronborg; the shadows in his eyes had thickened and the furrows around mouth and brows were carved deeper.（ch40）
- 落点：Margareta smiled up at him, remembering what a simple lemon cake had meant to a girl long ago. ‘In a moment. Let’s be alone here a little while longer.’（ch47）
- 她的弧线不是「变得勇敢」，是**从目击者变成当事人**：她先是闻到烟味的人，然后是交出药的人，最后是站在绞架下举起手的人。原文始终没写她知道多少——她的丈夫知道多少——所以她的弧线只能用「在场」来度量，不能用「知情」。

**格丽丝（草药婆，艾格尼斯的密友）——**

- 起点：She’d tried to ignore it at first, like a drip in the thatch that wakes you at night – intruding and icy – and yet, undealt with, it grows more insistent, bringing the promise of rot. Thus it was with the bailie’s gaze, slithering down her back as Geillis draped wet sheets over rosemary bushes, seeping through her bodice as she reached up to oil the leather-panelled walls, running across her briefly bared wrists as she poured his wine at dinner.（ch05）
- 转向：There was an explanation. But if true, it damned Geillis forever. Witchcraft.（ch22）
- 最低点：‘Now then, Geillis,’ Maitland said softly. ‘Tell me everything you know about John Wemyss.’（ch42）
- 落点：‘They weren’t witches,’ Geillis said. ‘They were frightened women hoping to ease their childbed, to keep their own property, to regain their stolen positions. This isn’t justice – this is vengeance and spite, and scores of Scots have died for it.’（ch44）
- 她一生都在替别人说话，最后一句仍然是在替别人说话。她与艾格尼斯之间那条「师徒／同谋」的界线原文从未画清——**不必替它画**。

**博斯韦尔（弗朗西斯·斯图尔特）——**

- 起点：‘To raise the Hepburns to the throne of course. Why else did I have Jenny marry your father? You’re a king’s grandson – if I can’t be king, you should be.’（ch01）
- 中段：‘Playing my role as best I can.’ His eyes were grim. ‘I was instructed to make Bothwell believe I’m helping him. If I stood by while he was dragged off to a dungeon, would he ever trust me again?’（ch38）
- 落点：Exiled. The word was a sigh of relief. Bothwell was gone at last. It had taken years, but James had built higher walls with laws and trials and royal decrees than those of any castle, and Bothwell had been unable to breach them. John’s evidence had done its work. Bothwell’s erstwhile supporters now vehemently pretended they had been no such thing, to little avail. There was nothing left in Scotland for Bothwell now. Like his uncle before him, he’d fled overseas.（ch47）
- 他是全书唯一从头到尾都在计划的人。他的弧线是**计划如何脱离他的控制**：他要的是一个恐惧的国王，最后他面对的是他自己一手制造的恐惧——以及一个不肯再怕的人。原文到最后一页也没给他定罪。

**艾格尼斯（农场主之女）——**

- 起点：‘Best to say it,’ Agnes advised. ‘Then it becomes something you own, a tale you can control. If not, all the unsaid pain will eat away from within. Besides, better to speak of it before he does. Then he’ll be the one defending himself. The second version of any story is the one people assume is the lie.’（ch09）
- 落点：‘Agnes Sampson has spoken words only I and Queen Anna could possibly have known. This is all the evidence needed to prove her a witch. I call upon the dempster to pass sentence.’（ch35）
- 她是格丽丝与这套机器之间唯一的中间层。她从「教你讲故事」走到「用自己的名字顶下罪名」，而她自己是不是女巫——**原文从头到尾没有说过**。
