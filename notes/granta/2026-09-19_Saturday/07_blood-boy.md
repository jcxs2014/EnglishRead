Lurking below the pavilion deck, I felt the propitious future I’d seen so clearly when I arrived dissipate.


After the applause died down, Alex was still lingering onstage. Rex went on for another minute about Alex’s ‘preternatural ability to connect with the world’s best AI talent’ before announcing that starting next week, she would be Sutton Hale’s youngest partner.


I choked down a bitter laugh. There was that piss-warm feeling of indignation again.


Rex told everyone to enjoy themselves, and that in a few minutes a well-known dance troupe from Hong Kong was going to perform their lion dance on the pavilion. Then for the finale of the evening, Alex would light the fireworks.


The crowd dispersed across the lawn while I paced back to the patio to compose myself. But as soon as I got there, the sliding door opened and Colter walked out from the house.


He was wearing a dark suit, a couple of the buttons on his white shirt were undone and he was already drunk, clearly having come from another event. After scanning the crowd, Colter rolled up to the red lacquer bar and ordered two negronis. After fixing my hair, I positioned myself two feet away from him and willed him to say hello. But then he leaned his elbow against the counter to check his phone so that his back was facing me. The bartender placed the drinks on the counter. As Colter reached across to grab them, I pressed my body against his so that when he turned we collided with each other. The splash left a satisfying ruby stain on my white shirt.


‘Oh shit, I’m so sorry, bro!’ Colter cried, his eyes flicking between my face and the red stain on my shirt.


You’re so fine! Wait, didn’t we take a class together in college? Your name is Colter, right?


All good bro. Colter, right? Nice work on Zelion, by the way – Kelvin is a close friend.


Déjà vu! Haha remember when this exact sequence happened last time? How crazy is that!


But before I could get any words out, he’d mouthed a ‘my fault bro’ and slipped away.


I watched Colter saunter over the garden bridge toward Alex, who was holding court with a circle of admirers. Smirking, he interrupted their conversation, handed Alex the other negroni, and raised his glass to ‘the competition getting hotter’. Alex rolled her eyes and they clinked their glasses together. I watched as Colter launched into a whole routine, practically groveling to get a laugh out of Alex, until I couldn’t take it anymore.


I whipped my phone out and took a zoomed-in picture of Colter whispering in Alex’s ear.


The caption sparked out from my fingertips like electricity. ‘Who could’ve guessed everyone’s favorite golden child is just another MARCO worshipper!!’ I sent the picture to the group chat confidently, waiting for the heart and haha reacts to roll in. A few seconds later, my phone began to buzz.















I stood alone in the gloam, numb to everything except the haptic feedback from my phone pulsing in my palm. At that exact moment, Rex started tapping his glass again to gather the partygoers.


The crowd applauded and took their seats facing the pavilion, Alex next to Rex, his peerage, and Colter in the front row. While the lion dancers performed, I retreated to the patio bar and got another yuzu seltzer. The only people in this part of the backyard were myself and a drunk guest who was tossing leftover dumplings to the jumbo koi. This sent the koi into a feeding frenzy; they leapt and thrashed against each other to snatch the dumplings, splashing big waves onto the patio. I stared at the back of Rex’s and Alex’s heads and wondered whether I should hurl myself over the cliff behind the pavilion to ruin the party. Instead, while everyone was watching the lion dancers, I made my way over to the firework setup and poured what was left of the yuzu seltzer down the mortar funnel.


After the dancers cleared the stage, the event staff moved the fireworks to a grassy area next to the pavilion and invited Alex to come up while Rex looked over her shoulder like a proud parent. I felt almost giddy with excitement.


‘Oh goodness, I’m kinda nervous,’ Alex said, which drew a couple laughs. She crouched down by the base of the mortar and squeezed the barbecue lighter next to the fuse. On her third try, the fuse ignited – the crowd cheered as Alex dashed back to safety and the spark ate its way down the fuse and disappeared into the mortar shaft. Alex was performatively covering her ears and ten, then twenty long seconds passed, but nothing happened. The silence became awkward as we all stood there staring at the dead firework.


‘Sorry, that’s really weird!’ Alex stammered. As I watched her flounder, a cold satisfaction seeped through my entire body.


‘No worries, let’s move the party inside for now,’ Rex said in an unrecognizably gentle voice.


‘No! Ah, sorry let me just check,’ Alex said. Before anyone could stop her, Alex crouched down beside the firework, inspecting it with her face directly next to the mortar. Even as I felt my body instinctively backing up, a queasy but thrilling anticipation locked me in place – I couldn’t look away.


‘I think it’s dead!’ Alex said, looking up with an embarrassed smile. Then there was a flash and a terrible wet bang.
