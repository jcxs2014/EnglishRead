# People Keep Finding Bodies in Joshua Tree

Source: Granta
URL: https://granta.com/people-keep-finding-bodies-in-joshua-tree/
Published: Thu, 10 Sep 2026 06:44:25 +0000

---

It’s hard, in the era of pig,

to remember what it felt like.

Topanga Boulevard winds up

Topanga Canyon

from Topanga Beach.

Charles Manson saw a spaceship

land here while Kenneth Anger

was exorcizing the Pentagon.

The only problem with California

is the fire thing.

And the cars, the mouthwash,

the aerospace industry,

the tech sector, the think tanks,

the subdivisions, the landfills.

The real estate, the scene.

I learned punk rock in Colorado,

Straight Outta Compton

on a janky tape deck

down 285 outside Fairplay.

Grassland basin, bison

nosing turf along the highway.

In Joshua Tree I’m trying

to find a desert tortoise

in the road so I can follow

the Fish and Wildlife Service

instructions for helping

a tortoise cross the road

so they don’t get creamed.

Quietly approach the tortoise

is step one.

Last step is wash your hands.

I feel that all my life

without knowing it

I’ve been perfecting

my quiet tortoise approach.

But I’d like to know how

I’m supposed to wash my hands

in the desert.

I like step two, note the direction

the tortoise was heading.

If only things were that simple.

I decide it makes more sense

to note the direction

before I quietly approach.

But I don’t see any tortoises.

I see: rocks, dirt, Joshua trees,

various birds, one jackrabbit.

I only see the jackrabbit

for a second.

And the sky. I see the sky.

I don’t know where I’m heading,

but I note the direction

in which some clouds

are crossing the road.

I carefully pick up a cloud

holding it upright

in its normal walking position.

I carry it carefully across the road

no more than a few hundred yards

into the desert

and place it in the shade.


 


On a dark desert highway

heading in the direction

of Twentynine Palms

I play the song.

Anton LaVey was rumored

to be visible in the high window

on the inner gatefold

just as he was rumored

to play the hairy devil

in Rosemary’s Baby.

Susan Atkins danced topless

in LaVey’s Witches’ Workshop

before murdering

Polanski’s wife, Sharon Tate,

who was photographed

at Joshua Tree in 1968.

Still those voices are calling

from far away. The desert

is full of old metal

shafts jabbing up

from the void.

Mötley Crüe learned punk rock in Hollywood.

Of course they covered ‘Helter Skelter’.

This was long after Charles Manson

stole Doris Day’s green telescope.

When Nikki Sixx was working on

‘Shout with the Devil’

an Elektra A&R man witnessed

a knife and fork rise from the table

and stick in the ceiling.

He recommended Sixx change

the title to ‘Shout at the Devil’

forthwith. It’s a perfect song,

and in eighth grade

when two metalheads came to my house

looking to beat me up

for insulting their musical taste,

as I had in fact done,

I invited them in,

showed them my metal tapes,

and we all grooved for a while

to Def Leppard and the Crüe,

though they advised me to get serious

and check out Slayer and Iron Maiden,

advice which has served me well

down to the present day.

I don’t believe in spontaneous

demonic levitation of flatware.

As Slayer’s drummer put it,

‘I don’t care about all these things.

I play drums. That’s it.’

Image is everything, as Andre Agassi

and Baudrillard understood.

I just wanna go real hard.


 


I’m in Joshua Tree reading an article

called ‘People Keep Finding

Bodies in Joshua Tree’.

The author claims that ‘bodies

don’t bury themselves’,

but the late ’90s taught me

otherwise. I resolve to find

and quietly approach no more

than one body. Let the dead

raise the dead. I’ll put my hand

to the plough and look back.

I like John 8, where they bring

the adulteress to Jesus. They’re like,

the law says we should stone her,

and he just stoops down

and writes in the dirt with his finger.

I assume he was writing

lyrics from his favorite bands.


 


Some assholes cut down

a bunch of Joshua trees.

I hate U2, but I saw them

at the Garden for free,

because I’d never been

to the Garden. I posted

about how bored I was,

and to entertain me, Joshua,

no relation to the trees,

posted a long thing

about how he quit Spin,

which blew up online,

and now Joshua’s gone, too.

The last song he turned me on to

says ‘If you’re resourceful

anything can be the right weapon,’

which reminds me of Ani’s

‘Every tool is a weapon

if you hold it right.’


 


You can see why all the freaks

end up in the desert.

Saint Anthony, Manson,

Brigham Young. Hard-luck

scrabblers out for gore

or God or both. Rock

pictograph, coyote scrub,

brimstone cowboys.

Strike-slip along the Eureka.

Adrianne Lenker’s out here

in a trailer. She just wants to go

real soft. Athanasius says the devil

threw a silver plate in Anthony’s path

when he lit out for the desert.

But Anthony had his number:

‘May your silver plate go to hell with you.’

These rocks were star-born.

Caught in the devil’s bargain.

I’ve got to get back to the Garden

to see the Knicks play basketball

as the Sugarhill Gang put it in ’79.

The Garden is the opposite of the desert.

There are no numbers in the rafters

and no rafters. No emo teens

along 8th Ave. Neither snow

nor rain nor gloom of night.

Just the greatest hits

of the Precambrian Supereon

rolling on like REO Speedwagon.

I wrote the previous line for the sound

but as an afterthought

did a search for the band

and guess where the album cover

with the tuna fish was shot.

People keep finding bodies

in Joshua Tree.


 


Gram Parsons saw spaceships

out here. The internet says

‘Joshua Tree park guides

are given the option

to tell the story

of Parsons’ cremation

during tours.’

It should be Parsons’s.

My grandparents had a sign in their barn

that said The Robbins,

which drove me crazy as a teen.

It’s in my kitchen now.

Beneath the solecism

is a fairly good painting

of my grandfather’s horse,

Shane, named for Shane.

Frosty, my grandmother’s horse,

was kind of a dick

and once bit her ‘on the tit’

as she liked to tell visitors.

Those summers outside

Rose Hill, Kansas, dirt roads

for light years – the Delines

have a song that says

‘It was the only time

I ever felt right

about anything.’

And now, visions of bodies

being burned. Every star

is a death star in the end.


 


Who’s gonna pay for all this?

We used to break into the high school,

drink in the classrooms, write

stupid shit on the blackboards.

The Jeep got stuck in the bush.

This was before classrooms

were killing fields, before

footage and stills and drills.

Before they made a desert

and called it peace.

Tacitus says ‘the earliest humans,

having no perverse desires,

lived without fault and crime,

without punishment and coercion’.

You shouldn’t sit in an exit row

if you’re unwilling or unable

to help, but actually every row

is an exit row, and no one knows

whether they can help

until they’re called on to do so.

So no one should be punished,

ever, for anything. I don’t know

much, but I know that.


 


I hardly ever notice birds,

then one catches my eye –

that ladder-backed woodpecker

on the Joshua tree that looks like

a giant asterisk had babies,

for instance – and birds are all

I think about for the next hour.

In this respect birds are like

other people. And other people

are like the desert tortoise,

floating in the sky, changing

shape, blotting out the sun.


 


Bury the dead, they say.

Have faith in the war.

A desert is a warm gun,

and any rock is the first tool.

It’s not killing that’s wrong

but choosing the wrong target.

Lisa told me the bully

I fantasized about taking apart

fell out of a tree, high,

and died, decades ago.

So I found another body,

without knowing it,

and it buried itself.

And when we were kids

we walked all over town

by ourselves. Our parents

never had a clue where we were.

It’s not that they trusted the world

but they consented to live in it.

And my brain is still a map

of that town, and I still name

tornadoes after metal girls

I let copy my homework.

And I learned then that loss

is a thread and the soul

is a needle.


 


The songs know. Yesterday

I got so old. I want to hurt

the hand inside me. I hate

children, but now, past fifty,

I get why people have them.

They’re an anti-loneliness drug.

You can take them to the desert

and they’ll scare away

the interesting birds

but you’ll have a grand old time

and take pictures

they’ll look at when they’re old

and lonely and wondering

what the point of anything is.

Maybe I should put it plainly

for once. I want the feeling back.

Making out with a witch

in a coffee truck. Four hundred

cigarettes on Mary’s floor.

My old Ford, a fire road.

The little things I didn’t know

were everything. But it sounds

stupid when I put it like that.

Midlife baby mourns lost youth.

At least I’m not boring

someone in some dark café.

The wise course would be acceptance.

But I hate wisdom.
