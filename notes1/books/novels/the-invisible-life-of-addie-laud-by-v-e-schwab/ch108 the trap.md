> **原句 1:** The bookstore is about to close. It gets dark early this time of year, and there's snow in the forecast, which is rare for London. The various clerks bustle about, dismantling old displays and putting up new ones, trying to finish their work before the mist outside turns to frost. She lingers nearby, thumb skating along the ring at her throat as a pair of teenage girls restock a wall in New Fiction. "Have you read it yet?" asks one. "Yeah, this weekend," says the other. "I can't believe the author didn't put their name on it," says the first. "Must be some kind of PR stunt." "I don't know," says the second. "I think it's charming. Makes the whole thing feel real. Like it's really Henry, telling her story." The first girl laughs. "You're such a romantic." "Excuse me," cuts in an older man. "Could I grab a copy of Addie LaRue?" Her skin prickles. He says the name with so much ease. Sounds tripping off a foreign tongue.

书店快关门了。每年这个时候天黑得早，而且预报有雪，这在伦敦很罕见。各个店员忙着拆除旧展示，放上新的，试图在外的薄雾变成霜之前完成工作。她徘徊在旁边，拇指沿着喉咙上的戒指滑行，一对青少年女孩在小说区重新上架。"你读过了吗？"一个问。"是的，这个周末，"另一个说。"我不敢相信作者没有把他们的名字放在上面，"第一个说。"一定是某种公关噱头。""我不知道，"第二个说。"我觉得它很迷人。让整件事感觉很真实。就像真的是 Henry 在讲她的故事。"第一个女孩笑了。"你真浪漫。""打扰一下，"一个年长男人插话。"我能拿一本《Addie LaRue》吗？"她的皮肤刺痛。他说这个名字如此轻松。听起来像从外语中跳出来。

> **原句 2:** She waits until the three of them have moved off to the till, and then, at last, she approaches the display. It is not just a table, but a full shelf, thirty copies of the book, faced out, the pattern repeating down the wall. The covers are simple, most of the space given over to the title, which is long and large enough to fill the jacket. It's written in cursive, just like the notes in the journals by the bed, a more legible version of her words in Henry's hand. The Invisible Life of Addie LaRue. She runs her fingers over the name, feels the embossed letters arc and curve beneath her touch, as though she had written them herself. The shop girls are right. There is no author's name. No photo on the back. No sign of Henry Strauss, beyond the simple, beautiful fact that the book is in her hands, the story real. She peels back the cover, turns past the title to the dedication. 她等到他们三个走到收银台，然后，最后，她走近展示。它不仅是一个桌子，而是一个完整的书架，三十本书，面朝外，图案沿着墙重复。封面很简单，大部分空间给了标题，它又长又大到填满封面。它是用草书写的，就像床边笔记本里的笔记，亨利手中她的话的一个更清晰的版本。《The Invisible Life of Addie LaRue》。她把手指放在名字上，感觉凸起字母在她的触摸下弧线和弯曲，好像她自己写过它们。店员们是对的。没有作者的名字。背面没有照片。没有 Henry Strauss 的迹象，除了这本书在她手中这个简单、美丽的事实，故事是真实的。她翻开封面，翻过标题到献词。三个小词在页面中央休息。我记得你。

She does not know if it was love, or simply a reprieve. If contentment can compete with passion, if warmth will ever be as strong as heat. But it was a gift. Not a game, or a war, not a battle of wills. Just a gift. Time, and memory, like lovers in a fable. She thumbs through the chapters of the book, her book, and marvels at the sight of her name on every page. Her life, waiting to be read. It is bigger than her now. Bigger than either of them, humans, or gods, or things without names. A story is an idea, wild as a weed, springing up wherever it is planted.

> **原句 6:** Three hundred years she's had to learn the color of his moods. She knows them all by now, the meaning of every shade, knows his temper, wants, and thoughts, just by studying those eyes. She marvels, that in the same amount of time, he never learned to read her own. Or perhaps he saw only what he expected: a woman's anger, and her need, her fear and hope and lust, all the simpler, more transparent things. But he never learned to read her cunning, or her cleverness, never learned to read the nuances of her actions, the subtle rhythms of her speech. And as she looks at him, she thinks of all the things her eyes would say. That he has made a grand mistake. That the devil is in the details, and he has overlooked a crucial one. That semantics may seem small, but he taught her once that words were everything. And when she carved the terms of her new deal, when she traded her soul for herself, she did not say forever, but as long as you want me by your side. And those are not the same at all. If her eyes could speak, they would laugh. They would say that he is a fickle god, and long before he loved her, he hated her, he drove her mad, and with her flawless memory, she became a student of his machinations, a scholar of his cruelty. She has had three hundred years to study, and she will make a masterpiece of his regret. Perhaps it will take twenty years. Perhaps it will take a hundred. But he is not capable of love, and she will prove it. She will ruin him. Ruin his idea of them. She will break his heart, and he will come to hate her once again. She will drive him mad, drive him away. And then, he will cast her off. And she will finally be free. Addie dreams of telling Luc these things, just to see the shade it turns his eyes, the green of being bested. The green of forfeit, and of losing. But if he's taught her anything, it's patience. So Addie says nothing of the new game, the new rules, the new battle that's begun. She only smiles, and sets the book back on its shelf. And follows him out into the dark.

三百年她不得不学习他情绪的颜色。她知道它们全部到现在，每种色调的含义，知道他的脾气、欲望和想法，只是通过研究那些眼睛。她惊叹，在相同的时间里，他从来没有学会读她自己的。或者也许他只看到他所期望的：一个女人的愤怒，和她的需要，她的恐惧和希望和欲望，所有更简单、更透明的事情。但他从来没有学会读她的狡猾，或她的聪明，从来没有学会读她行为的细微差别，她讲话的微妙节奏。当她看着他时，她想她的眼睛会说的所有事情。他犯了一个大错误。魔鬼在细节中，他忽略了一个关键的。语义可能看起来很小，但他曾经教过她文字就是一切。当她雕刻她的新交易的条款，当她用灵魂交易自己，她没有说永远，而是只要你想让我在你身边。而那些根本不是一样的。如果她的眼睛能说话，它们会笑。他们会说他是一个善变的在他爱她之前，他恨她，他让她疯狂，凭借她完美的记忆，她成为他阴谋的学生，他残忍的学者。她有三百年去学习，她会制造他遗憾的杰作。也许需要二十年。也许需要一百年。但他没有能力去爱，她会证明。她会毁了他。毁掉他对他们的想法。她会伤他的心，他会再次恨她。她会让他疯狂，赶走他。然后，他会抛弃她。她将终于自由。Addie 梦想告诉 Luc 这些事情，只是看看它把他眼睛变成的颜色，被打败的绿色。失败和失去的绿色。但如果他教了她任何东西，那就是耐心。所以 Addie 没有说任何关于新游戏，新规则，新战斗已经开始的事情。她只是微笑，把书放回书架。跟着他走进黑暗。
