# 54. Author's Note（作者附言 · 5,078 字节 · 13 段）

## 本章导航

**一句话概括**：**全书最后一章换了说话人，而它的技术是**先给自己看一张旧照片，再把这本书的道德立场交出去**——开头是 `There's a photograph of me and my sister in front of a table crowded with bottles of wine and liquor.`（有一张我和我姐姐的照片，背景是一张摆满葡萄酒和烈酒瓶子的桌子。）**而全章的落点是作者指着 `Fran` 的一首诗说的最后一句**（⑤ `You will still be` / `You.` / `You must live in spite of all this.`）

**情感弧线位置**：**这一章不在弧线上，它是弧线的注释**。**注意它是全书唯一一个由「写这本书的人」而不是「书里那个人」说话的章节**（`Author's Note`）——**而它的技术是**把自己的经历与 `Bella` 的经历用同一个词串起来**（④ `Like Bella, when that initial buzz hit, I felt like I was home.`／④ `Like most of them, Bella doesn't think she has a problem; she's just trying to survive.`）

**人物弧线**（**这一章没有「她」，只有作者；所以这里记的是作者的三个动作**）：① **先交出自己的照片**（①）——**而这张照片的说明是「我四五岁」，而照片里已经有一桌酒**；② **先说出书里那句格言的出处**（④ `Like Fran says in this book, you can have years of sobriety and suddenly the littlest thing will trigger that buried need;`）——**而这一句是 ch38 ⑦ 与 ch49 ⑥ 那两次「事后理解」的最终来源**；③ **最后用 `Fran` 的诗作结**（⑤）——**而那三行是 ch12 那一章 `Fran` 讲的东西的书面版**。

**叙事手法**：**全章是散文体（13 段）**＋ **叙述者是作者本人**＋ **0 处 `—` 分节**＋ 0 处 U+00AD ＋ **5,078 字节**。**而这一章的形式有一个特点：它引用的唯一一段文字不是自己的，而是书里 `Fran` 的诗**（⑤ `You will still be / You. / You must live in spite of all this.`）——**而这段诗在原文里是被分行排的，因此这一章的最后一个「引语块」是全书唯一一个以分行结束的引语块。**

## 故事梗概

**那张照片（①②）** —— `There's a photograph of me and my sister in front of a table crowded with bottles of wine and liquor.` —— `The photograph is worn and blurred with age, but there we are:` —— `my sister with her beautiful red hair, me with my tangled blond hair.` —— `We're fancied up; it looks like I'm in some sort of pretty, zippered velvet dress.` —— `Maybe it was Christmas and a family party, lots of cousins and aunts and uncles and tumult.` —— `I'm four or five in the photo. I know this because my kindergarten picture is the last one of me with long hair. After that, it was cut short, never to be long again.`

**从那瓶香槟开始（③④）** —— `Was it some portent of what my life would someday be?` —— `a long time ago, I really, really, really, really liked drinking. And I was good at it, until I wasn't.` —— `everything began innocently for me: a game of quarters with cousins and a bottle of champagne at eleven.` —— **`Like Bella, when that initial buzz hit, I felt like I was home.`** —— `The world smoothed out in impossibly velvety ways. My anxiety lessened.` —— `Like Bella, I became an expert self-medicator` —— **`The problem is when you have a problem and that problem becomes an even bigger problem: where to get alcohol, how much to get, how to hide it, how to recover from it, how to keep doing it. It's amazing to me now (and sometimes quite funny; people in recovery develop extremely dark senses of humor) the lengths to which I was willing to go to make sure I could keep doing that thing that I thought was helping me function in the world but was actually erasing me, bit by bit.`** —— `(The anecdote in The Glass Girl about a kid duct-taping alcohol to their body to sneak into rehab? Let's have coffee sometime and I'll tell you a story about underage me, a trip to Juàrez, duct tape, and rum.)`

**为什么会开始（④）** —— `I've always been fascinated by how addictions begin (a side note here: some studies suggest alcohol addiction is 50 percent inherited; the percentage rises for other addictions).` —— **`For Bella, it was a bonding moment with her grandmother and an immediate salve for her anxiety, which increased with her parents' divorce, her grandmother's death, the pressures of school, and a bad breakup with her boyfriend.`** —— `But lots of kids go through those things and don't abuse drugs or alcohol.` —— **`But also…lots of kids do. Those are the kids Bella meets in rehab.`** —— `Like most of them, Bella doesn't think she has a problem; she's just trying to survive.` —— `After all, who's an alcoholic at fifteen?`

**那条线不是直的（④）** —— **`And lots of people who aren't, or can't be. They deserve our support when they need help, not judgment. Recovery is not a straight line.`** —— **`Recovery can take years and require many efforts. Relapse happens. Like Fran says in this book, you can have years of sobriety and suddenly the littlest thing will trigger that buried need; something inside you will take over, something you thought you'd successfully outrun.`**

**关于那家机构（④）** —— `Sonoran Sunrise is run by a team of addicts in recovery doing their best to teach kids to value themselves, confront their fears, earn some successes, believe in themselves.` —— `(If you're reading this and you've been to rehab: Bella's experience might not mirror your own. All rehabs are different. This is a work of fiction.)`

**`Fran` 的诗（⑤）** —— `Can I tell you how much I loved writing Fran's poem? Because I did. Because that poem encapsulates addiction for me:` —— `you can smooth out the edges, you can try to drown whatever's inside you that's eating you alive, you can bury shame and anxiety and trauma by dropping shitloads of pills on them or dousing them in alcohol…but in the end, those things are still there. You have to face yourself. And that's the toughest part, like Fran says:` —— **`You will still be`** —— **`You.`** —— **`You must live in spite of all this.`**

**最后（⑤）** —— `Bella is at the very beginning of addressing her addiction. I don't know what the future holds for her, but I have hope.` —— `By the end of the book, she's trying to commit to a sober life. She has support in friends like Dawn and Amber.` —— `She's discovering photography as a way to connect with her grief over her grandmother. She's going to try.` —— `That's all you can ask of someone, and that's all they can do: try.` —— `The other side of addiction is recovery, and sometimes it really, really, really sucks here, but also…there are great and shining moments, and believe me, you want to be able to experience them clearly.` —— **`You want to be able to see yourself, like Fran says, in all your heart-shaped glory.`**

## 精读

### ①

> "There's a photograph of me and my sister in front of a table crowded with bottles of wine and liquor. The photograph is worn and blurred with age, but there we are: my sister with her beautiful red hair, me with my tangled blond hair. We're fancied up; it looks like I'm in some sort of pretty, zippered velvet dress. Maybe it was Christmas and a family party, lots of cousins and aunts and uncles and tumult. I'm four or five in the photo. I know this because my kindergarten picture is the last one of me with long hair. After that, it was cut short, never to be long again."

**中文理解**：——**有一张我和我姐姐的照片，背景是一张摆满葡萄酒瓶和烈酒瓶的桌子。照片被岁月磨旧、磨模糊了，但我们就在那儿：我姐姐一头漂亮的红发，我一头乱蓬蓬的金发。我们都打扮过了；看起来我穿着某种漂亮的、拉链的丝绒裙子。也许是圣诞节、一个家庭聚会，叔叔婶婶哥哥姐姐一大群，乱哄哄的。照片里的我四五岁。我知道这一点，因为我的幼儿园照片是我最后一张长头发的照片。在那之后，头发就被剪短了，再也没留长过。**

**关键词**：a photograph of me and my sister / in front of a table crowded with bottles of wine and liquor / worn and blurred with age / my sister with her beautiful red hair / me with my tangled blond hair / We're fancied up / some sort of pretty, zippered velvet dress / Maybe it was Christmas and a family party / lots of cousins and aunts and uncles and tumult / I'm four or five in the photo / my kindergarten picture is the last one of me with long hair / After that, it was cut short, never to be long again

**为什么这样写**：**这一章的第一句是全书唯一一张不在书里、也不属于任何角色的照片，而它的技术是**把酒写成了背景（①）。** —— `There's a photograph of me and my sister in front of a table crowded with bottles of wine and liquor.`（**有一张我和我姐姐的照片，背景是一张摆满葡萄酒瓶和烈酒瓶的桌子。**）—— **而它的技术是**一个 `in front of`（在……前面）把两个人放在了一堆瓶子后面**；**——而 `crowded with`（挤满）这个分词短语的技术是**它让那桌酒成了画面里最实的东西，而两个孩子是「在前面」的**；**——也就是说这张照片的技术是：**孩子在前，酒在后**。

**而关于年龄的那两句是这一段的技术核心（①）** —— `I'm four or five in the photo. I know this because my kindergarten picture is the last one of me with long hair. After that, it was cut short, never to be long again.`（**照片里的我四五岁。我知道这一点，因为我的幼儿园照片是我最后一张长头发的照片。在那之后，头发就被剪短了，再也没留长过。**）—— **而它的技术是**用一张幼儿园照片推断出了年龄**——**也就是说作者没有说她记得这件事，她说她是从**另一张照片**推出来的**；**——而 `After that, it was cut short, never to be long again.`（在那之后，头发就被剪短了，再也没留长过。）的技术是**被动语态（`it was cut short`）加上 `never to be long again`（再也没留长过）**——**而这一句里的「被剪短」是这一章唯一一次有人替她做一个决定。**

**读者视角提示**：**`never to be long again.`（再也没留长过。）这一句与 ch03 那张 `Those Polaroids` 之后的一切是一条暗线** ——**而 ch48 那一封信里她父亲说 `Because I don't want to be a watercolor. I don't want to wash off, or away.`（ch53 ⑤ 她自己说的同一句）——**也就是说这一章的作者与书里的女孩，都用同一句话（`wash off`）来描述自己最怕的那件事，而那句话在 ch51 ① 是一句诗、在 ch53 ⑤ 是一个决定、在 ch54 ① 是一段被剪短了的头发。**

### ②

> "I think about that photograph a lot. Was it some portent of what my life would someday be? Because a long time ago, I really, really, really, really liked drinking. And I was good at it, until I wasn't. Like it did for Bella in The Glass Girl, everything began innocently for me: a game of quarters with cousins and a bottle of champagne at eleven. Like Bella, when that initial buzz hit, I felt like I was home. I could talk and be funny. The world smoothed out in impossibly velvety ways. My anxiety lessened. Like Bella, I became an expert self-medicator and felt like as long as I was doing what I was supposed to be doing (school, work, all the things that are supposed to make a life) and doing it reasonably well…well, what was the problem?"

**中文理解**：——**我常常想着那张照片。它会不会是我这一生某个日子的某种预兆？因为很久以前，我非常、非常、非常、非常地喜欢喝酒。而且我喝得很好，直到我喝不好了。就像这件事对《玻璃女孩》里的 `Bella` 那样，一切对我来说都是清白的开始：和表亲们玩二十五美分硬币游戏，一瓶香槟，才十一岁。就像 `Bella` 那样，最初那阵晕眩上来的时候，我觉得自己到家了。我能说话，我能逗人乐。世界以不可思议的丝绒般的方式变得平滑。我的焦虑减轻了。就像 `Bella` 那样，我成了一个自我用药的行家，并且觉得只要我一直在做我该做的事（上学、工作，那些据说构成一个人生的事）并且做得还不错……那么，问题是什么呢？**

**关键词**：I think about that photograph a lot / Was it some portent of what my life would someday be? / I really, really, really, really liked drinking / And I was good at it, until I wasn't / everything began innocently for me / a game of quarters with cousins / a bottle of champagne at eleven / Like Bella, when that initial buzz hit, I felt like I was home / I could talk and be funny / The world smoothed out in impossibly velvety ways / My anxiety lessened / Like Bella, I became an expert self-medicator / as long as I was doing what I was supposed to be doing / and doing it reasonably well / well, what was the problem

**为什么这样写**：**这一段的技术是**三次 `Like Bella`（②）**——**而这三次的间距一次比一次远**（② `Like it did for Bella in The Glass Girl`（就像这件事对《玻璃女孩》里的 `Bella` 那样）／`Like Bella, when that initial buzz hit`（就像 `Bella` 那样）／`Like Bella, I became an expert self-medicator`（就像 `Bella` 那样））——**——也就是说作者用三次「像」把自己的经历一件一件地叠到 `Bella` 的经历上，而叠上去的三件是：起点、那一下、以及**成为行家**。**

**而那句自问是全书最好的一次反讽（②）** —— `and doing it reasonably well…well, what was the problem?`（**并且做得还不错……那么，问题是什么呢？**）—— **而它的技术是**一个 `well…well`（那么……那么）把同一个词用了两次，而前一个在收句、后一个在提问**；**——而这一句正是 ch54 ③ 那段 `The problem is when you have a problem`（问题在于你有了一个问题，而那个问题变成一个更大的问题）的提问版**——**——也就是说全书最后一章的结构是：先问「问题是什么呢」，再答「问题是……」**

**而 `felt like I was home`（觉得自己到家了）是这一段里最狠的一句，而它与 ch39 ① 那个毛衣比喻是同一个感觉（②）** ——ch39 ① `You feel like you're home.`（你觉得到家了。）——**——也就是说「酒＝回家」这个想法在书里出现过两次，而作者在 ch54 ② 承认这是**两个人**共有的**；**——而这一句在原文里是被 `Like Bella,` 领着的，也就是说作者写下它的时候，她已经知道书里有一句一模一样的话。**

**读者视角提示**：**`a game of quarters with cousins and a bottle of champagne at eleven`（和表亲们玩二十五美分硬币游戏，一瓶香槟，才十一岁）是全书对「第一次」最轻的一次描述（②）** ——**而它与 ch08 那一夜（`My brain says: Take it. You can pour it over ice, it'll look like soda.`／`My brain says: Bella, do it.`）是同一件事的两种年龄**——**也就是说 `Bella` 的第一次被写成了一次斟酌，而作者自己的第一次被写成了一次生日派对。**

### ③

> "The problem is when you have a problem and that problem becomes an even bigger problem: where to get alcohol, how much to get, how to hide it, how to recover from it, how to keep doing it. It’s amazing to me now (and sometimes quite funny; people in recovery develop extremely dark senses of humor) the lengths to which I was willing to go to make sure I could keep doing that thing that I thought was helping me function in the world but was actually erasing me, bit by bit. (The anecdote in The Glass Girl about a kid duct-taping alcohol to their body to sneak into rehab? Let's have coffee sometime and I'll tell you a story about underage me, a trip to Juàrez, duct tape, and rum.) … I've always been fascinated by how addictions begin (a side note here: some studies suggest alcohol addiction is 50 percent inherited; the percentage rises for other addictions). For Bella, it was a bonding moment with her grandmother and an immediate salve for her anxiety, which increased with her parents' divorce, her grandmother's death, the pressures of school, and a bad breakup with her boyfriend. But lots of kids go through those things and don't abuse drugs or alcohol. … But also…lots of kids do. Those are the kids Bella meets in rehab. Kids reeling from trauma. Kids neglected or abused by their parents. Kids with anxiety. Like most of them, Bella doesn't think she has a problem; she's just trying to survive. After all, who's an alcoholic at fifteen? Drinking is everywhere. Everyone does it; or at least, everyone does something. There are plenty of people who are responsible drinkers throughout their lives."

**中文理解**：——**问题在于你有了一个问题，而那个问题变成一个更大的问题：到哪里去弄酒，弄多少，怎么藏起来，怎么从一场醉里缓过来，怎么继续做下去。现在回头看我觉得很惊人（有时候还有点好笑；在康复中的人会发展出极其黑暗的幽默感），当年我为了能继续做那件我以为在帮我在这个世界上运转、实际上却在一 bit 一 bit 地把我抹掉的事，愿意走到多远。（《玻璃女孩》里那个把孩子把酒用布基胶带缠在身上带进戒治所的轶事？改天一起喝杯咖啡，我给你讲讲未成年的我、一趟华雷斯城之旅、胶带，还有朗姆酒。）**——**我一直对成瘾是怎么开始的着迷。（这里插一句：有些研究提示酒精成瘾有 50% 是遗传的；对别的成瘾，这个比例更高。）对 `Bella` 来说，那是与她外婆之间建立感情的一个时刻，也是她焦虑的一剂即时慰藉，而那焦虑随着她父母的离婚、外婆的去世、学校的压力、以及一次糟糕的分手而加重。可是很多孩子经历了这些事，并没有滥用药物或酒精。**——**但也……很多孩子会。那些就是 `Bella` 在戒治所遇到的孩子。被创伤甩得晕头转向的孩子。被父母忽视或虐待的孩子。有焦虑的孩子。和他们大多数一样，`Bella` 不认为自己有问题；她只是在求生。毕竟，谁在十五岁就是个酗酒者呢？酒到处都是。人人都喝；或者至少，人人都在做点什么。这辈子都做负责任的饮酒者，多的是。**

**关键词**：The problem is when you have a problem and that problem becomes an even bigger problem / where to get alcohol, how much to get, how to hide it, how to recover from it / that I thought was helping me function in the world / but was actually erasing me, bit by bit / duct-taping alcohol to their body to sneak into rehab / a story about underage me, a trip to Juàrez, duct tape, and rum / I've always been fascinated by how addictions begin / some studies suggest alcohol addiction is 50 percent inherited / For Bella, it was a bonding moment with her grandmother / an immediate salve for her anxiety / which increased with her parents' divorce / her grandmother's death, the pressures of school, and a bad breakup with her boyfriend / But lots of kids go through those things and don't abuse drugs or alcohol / But also…lots of kids do / Those are the kids Bella meets in rehab / Kids reeling from trauma / Kids neglected or abused by their parents / Kids with anxiety / Like most of them, Bella doesn't think she has a problem; she's just trying to survive / After all, who's an alcoholic at fifteen? / Drinking is everywhere. Everyone does it / or at least, everyone does something / There are plenty of people who are responsible drinkers throughout their lives

**为什么这样写**：**这一段的技术是**把一个问题写成五个问句都不必问的清单（③）。** —— `The problem is when you have a problem and that problem becomes an even bigger problem: where to get alcohol, how much to get, how to hide it, how to recover from it, how to keep doing it. It’s amazing to me now (and sometimes quite funny; people in recovery develop extremely dark senses of humor) the lengths to which I was willing to go to make sure I could keep doing that thing that I thought was helping me function in the world but was actually erasing me, bit by bit.`（**问题在于你有了一个问题，而那个问题变成一个更大的问题：到哪里去弄酒，弄多少，怎么藏起来，怎么从一场醉里缓过来，怎么继续做那件我以为在帮我在这个世界上运转、实际上却在一 bit 一 bit 地把我抹掉的事。**）—— **而它的技术是**一个同位语的自我重复（`a problem`／`an even bigger problem`）＋ 四个 `how` ＋ 一个 `how to keep doing that thing`——**而那最后半句的技术是**它从句子里伸出去了（`that I thought was helping me…`），把「问题」的定义换成了「我一直以为在帮我、其实在抹掉我的那件事」**；**——而 `bit by bit`（一 bit 一 bit）这个短语是全书唯一一次它以**逐字形式**出现，而它出现在 ch41 ① 那句 `I feel my body splitting into a million little pieces`（我感觉我的身体裂成了一百万个小碎片）与 ch49 ⑧ 那句 `And pick up all the broken parts of me`（捡起我所有的碎片）的下游。**

**而那个括号是全书最轻的一次自曝（③）** —— `(The anecdote in The Glass Girl about a kid duct-taping alcohol to their body to sneak into rehab? Let's have coffee sometime and I'll tell you a story about underage me, a trip to Juàrez, duct tape, and rum.)`（**（《玻璃女孩》里那个把孩子把酒用布基胶带缠在身上带进戒治所的轶事？改天一起喝杯咖啡，我给你讲讲未成年的我、一趟华雷斯城之旅、胶带，还有朗姆酒。）**）—— **而它的技术是**一个把「我们改天喝杯咖啡吧」和一个未成年人去华雷斯走私酒的故事放进同一个括号**；**——而那个括号里最重的是三个并列的名词**（`underage me`／`a trip to Juàrez`／`duct tape and rum`）**——也就是说作者用一个玩笑句式交代了一件刑事级别的事。**

**而 `For Bella, it was a bonding moment with her grandmother`（对 `Bella` 来说，那是与她外婆之间建立感情的一个时刻）是这一段里最温柔的一句，而它紧跟的半句是 `and an immediate salve for her anxiety`（也是她焦虑的一剂即时慰藉）（③）** —— **而它的技术是**一个连词把「亲情」与「止痛药」并列**；**——而 `salve`（慰藉／药膏）这个词的技术是**它同时属于医学与感情这两个词表**；**——而紧跟的 `which increased with her parents' divorce, her grandmother's death, the pressures of school, and a bad breakup with her boyfriend.`（**那焦虑随着她父母的离婚、外婆的去世、学校的压力、以及一次糟糕的分手而加重。）**这一句的技术是**四个名词短语、零个动词**——**——也就是说这本书把一个女孩的焦虑史写成了四个条目。**

**而那一段三连是全书最冷的一次分类（③）** —— `Kids reeling from trauma. Kids neglected or abused by their parents. ### ⑤

> "Can I tell you how much I loved writing Fran's poem? Because I did. Because that poem encapsulates addiction for me: you can smooth out the edges, you can try to drown whatever's inside you that's eating you alive, you can bury shame and anxiety and trauma by dropping shitloads of pills on them or dousing them in alcohol…but in the end, those things are still there. I don't know what the future holds for her, but I have hope. By the end of the book, she's trying to commit to a sober life. She has support in friends like Dawn and Amber. She's discovering photography as a way to connect with her grief over her grandmother. She's going to try. That's all you can ask of someone, and that's all they can do: try. **中文理解**：——**我能告诉你我有多喜欢写 `Fran` 那首诗吗？因为我真的很喜欢。因为那首诗对我来说浓缩了成瘾这件事：你可以把边磨平，你可以试着淹掉那个正在吃掉你的、在你里面的东西，你可以把羞耻和焦虑和创伤埋起来——往它们身上丢一大堆药，或者用酒精浇上去……但到最后，那些东西还在。你得面对你自己。而那是最难的部分，就像 `Fran` 说的：你仍然会是／你。／你必须尽管有这一切，还是活下去。**——**`Bella` 才刚刚开始处理她的成瘾。我不知道未来对她意味着什么，但我抱有希望。到这本书结束的时候，她正努力把自己托付给一种清醒的生活。她有 `Dawn` 和 `Amber` 这样的朋友在支持她。她正在发现摄影是一种与她对祖母的悲伤相连接的方式。她会去尝试。这就是一个你能向一个人要求的全部，也是一个人能做的全部：尝试。成瘾的另一面是康复，而在这里它有时候真的、真的、真的很糟，但也有……那些 great and shining（又亮又好的）时刻，相信我，而你希望自己能清清楚楚地经历它们。你希望自己能够看见自己，就像 `Fran` 说的，以你全部那心形的荣光。**

**关键词**：Can I tell you how much I loved writing Fran's poem / Because I did / that poem encapsulates addiction for me / you can smooth out the edges / you can try to drown whatever's inside you that's eating you alive / you can bury shame and anxiety and trauma / by dropping shitloads of pills on them or dousing them in alcohol / but in the end, those things are still there / You have to face yourself / You will still be / You. / You must live in spite of all this / Bella is at the very beginning of addressing her addiction / I don't know what the future holds for her, but I have hope / she's trying to commit to a sober life / She has support in friends like Dawn and Amber / She's discovering photography as a way to connect with her grief over her grandmother / She's going to try / That's all you can ask of someone, and that's all they can do: try / sometimes it really, really, really sucks here / but also…there are great and shining moments / you want to be able to experience them clearly / You want to be able to see yourself / in all your heart-shaped glory

**为什么这样写**：**这一章的最后一处引语是全书唯一一段由作者**引用**而不是**她自己写**的文字，而它的技术是**把一个人称代词单独放成一行（⑤）。** —— **`You will still be`（你仍然会是）／`You.`（你。）／`You must live in spite of all this.`（你必须尽管有这一切，还是活下去。）**—— **而中间那个 `You.`（你。）独立成行，而它的技术是**它前面是 `You will still be`（你仍然会是），而这一行把那个系动词 `be`（是）拿掉，只剩下主语与句号**；**——也就是说这五行诗的技术是**先把「你」变成一个句子，再让它变成一个词，最后再让它带上一整个谓语**。**

**而作者解释她为什么喜欢这三行（⑤）** —— `Because that poem encapsulates addiction for me: you can smooth out the edges, you can try to drown whatever's inside you that's eating you alive, you can bury shame and anxiety and trauma by dropping shitloads of pills on them or dousing them in alcohol…but in the end, those things are still there. You have to face yourself.`（**因为那首诗对我来说浓缩了成瘾这件事：你可以把边磨平，你可以试着淹掉那个正在吃掉你的、在你里面的东西，你可以把羞耻和焦虑和创伤埋起来——往它们身上丢一大堆药，或者用酒精浇上去……但到最后，那些东西还在。你得面对你自己。**）—— **而它的技术是**三个 `you can`（你可以）并列**，**——而三个 `you can` 之后接的是 `but in the end, those things are still there.`（但到最后，那些东西还在。）**；**——而这一组是全书对「戒断为什么难」最技术性的一次说明**（`smooth out`／`drown`／`bury` → `still there`）。

**而那三句 `really` 是这一章里唯一一次情绪外露（⑤）** —— `sometimes it really, really, really sucks here, but also…there are great and shining moments,`（**在这里它有时候真的、真的、真的很糟，但也有……那些 great and shining（又亮又好的）时刻，**）—— **而它的技术是**三个 `really` 与两个 `but`（但是）**，**——而 `here`（在这里）这个词的技术是**它把「康复」限定成了一个地点**。

**而全章的最后一句是全书唯一一次作者直接对读者说话（⑤）** —— `You want to be able to see yourself, like Fran says, in all your heart-shaped glory.`（**你希望自己能够看见自己，就像 `Fran` 说的，以你全部那心形的荣光。**）—— **而它的技术是**一个 `like Fran says`（就像 `Fran` 说的）**第三次出现在全章**，而这一次的宾语是**读者**；**——而 `heart-shaped glory`（心形的荣光）这六个字是全书最后六个实词，而它们形容的不是成就，是**形状**。**

**读者视角提示**：**`She's discovering photography as a way to connect with her grief over her grandmother.`（她正在发现摄影是一种与她对祖母的悲伤相连接的方式。）这一句与 ch53 ① 那句 `I think Laurel would like that.`（我想 `Laurel` 会喜欢那样。）是一组（⑤）** ——**而 ch53 那一章的最后一格是 `Click.`（咔哒。）；也就是说这本书最后交给读者的是两个动作而不是两句话**：**一个是 ch53 ⑤ 的一个拟声词，一个是 ch54 ⑤ 的一个希望（`but I have hope.`）**。**

## 一句话总结

**这一章换了说话人，而它最值得记的技术是**把一个人称代词单独放成一行**（⑤ `You will still be` ／ `You.` ／ `You must live in spite of all this.`）——**而这三行是 `Fran` 在 ch12 那一章讲的东西的书面版，而作者在附言里解释了它们为什么对自己成立**（⑤ `Because that poem encapsulates addiction for me`）——**全书最后一句是作者对读者说的**（`You want to be able to see yourself, like Fran says, in all your heart-shaped glory.`），**而它与 ch53 那一章的最后一句是同一件事**：**ch53 `Because I don't want to be a watercolor.`（我不想变成一张水彩画）／ch54 `you want to be able to see yourself`（你希望自己能够看见自己）**——**一个是不想被洗掉，一个是想被看见。**
